The Hellephant is the volcanic twin of the [Mammoth](/taming/how-to-tame-a-mammoth) — the same animal, the same food bill, one crucial difference: **the Mammoth is neutral and the Hellephant attacks you on sight**. That single change is what turns an identical recipe into a much harder tame.

This page covers what it wants, where it lives, and the tactics that make a hostile apex tame possible.

Last verified: **2026-09-30**.

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|

| Flute | **Strong Taming Flute** (Tier 3) |

## The One Difference That Matters

Everything about the tame is the same as the Mammoth's. What changes is the approach.

**The Mammoth stands still and lets you play a flute at it.** The Hellephant does not. It is a hostile entity: it wanders until it notices you, then charges, and its hit puts you in **ragdoll** — on the floor, briefly helpless, in the middle of the Volcanic Biome.

That collides with the one rule that governs every tame: **an animal must be undamaged to be tamed**. So you cannot soften it up with a Rifle and then walk in. You have to survive it while you do the mini-game.

It gets worse before it gets better: the Hellephant **spawns near cultists**, particularly around the Ritual Circle. Expect to be taming while something else is shooting at you.

The Hellephant tame leans on the campfire; [Kiwi](/entities/the-kiwi) is the other campfire-dependent tame, and ours is the one where the fire level is the entire price.

## The Campfire Trick

Here is the mechanic that makes this possible, and it is the most useful thing on this page.

**The Hellephant stops pursuing if you get too close to a lit campfire.** It breaks off and returns to wandering — and reportedly teleports back to its original spawn if it ends up too far away with no target. So a lit campfire is a **reset button**. You can pull it, break the aggro, heal, eat, and come back to try again instead of fighting to the death.

The second exploitable weakness: **it gets stuck on large structures** — trees, buildings — and can lose interest in you while still standing in your detection radius. That is your window: put a solid object between you and it, and approach from the side it cannot path around.

Between those two, the practical loop is:

1. **Put a campfire down near the Ritual Circle.** This is the set-up step nobody mentions and the one that turns a coin-flip into a farmable attempt.
2. **Pull the cultists first** and clear them, so you are not doing the mini-game under crossbow fire.
3. **Break the Hellephant's aggro at the campfire** whenever your health drops, then re-engage. Repeat rather than trading hits.
4. **Start the mini-game as soon as you get a clean window.** One guide reports that the mini-game **temporarily freezes the target** while you balance the bar — which would be exactly what makes a hostile apex tame viable. That claim is single-source, so verify it in your own run rather than counting on it.
5. **Feed 2 Pumpkin + 1 Cake** per successful cycle, five times.

- **Where the Steak comes from.** [Chef and Cook](/classes/chef-class) — that 8-9 Cooked Steak is the raw material for the Chef's Steak Dinner.

## What It Wants

Identical to the Mammoth, and — as with the Mammoth — the sources agree:

| | Per cycle | × 5 cycles |
|---|---|---|
| **Pumpkin** | 2 | **10** |
| **Cake** | 1 | **5** |

**Cake is the bottleneck, not Pumpkin**, for exactly the same reason as the Mammoth: plots scale Pumpkin throughput, and Cake does not keep up. Our [Pet Food](/taming/pet-food) page has the ingredient sources.

You also need a **Strong Taming Flute** before any of this works, which means the same flute-levelling route as the Mammoth: tame Bunnies and Wolves for XP, upgrade at the Upgrade Station, then take on apex animals. The three flutes and the route between them are on the [Taming Flute](/items/taming-flute) page. The full route is on the [Taming Guide](/taming/taming-guide).

**And here is the payoff for the Cake farm:** the Mammoth, the Hellephant and the Jungle's Mossy Mammoth all want the **same 10 Pumpkin + 5 Cake**. One farm, three biomes' worth of apex pets.

## What It Drops

The Hellephant pays out better than most things in the biome, and the detail that makes it unusual is that the food is **already cooked**:

| Drop | Amount | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Cooked Steak** | 8–9 | Guaranteed |
| **Cooked Ribs** | 0–2 | Chance |
| **Mammoth Tusk** | 0–1 | Chance |

One source notes it is **the only entity that drops cooked food with no cooking required** — which means killing one in the middle of a Volcanic run refills your hunger bar immediately. If you were going to fight it anyway, that alone makes the trip worthwhile; the tame is the bonus, not the other way round.

## HP: A Number Worth Flagging

Sources disagree, so treat this as the honest version rather than a clean stat:

| Source | HP |
|---|---|
| 99-nights-in-the-forest.com | **500** |
| Pocket Tactics | **500** |
| Sportskeeda | **500** |
| rblxguide | 800 |

We have since re-checked this for [The Hellephant](/entities/the-hellephant), which lists the same split in full — the three-versus-two reading below is the current one there too.

**500 is the three-source figure**, so that is what we use. The 800 listing looks like the Mammoth's own HP carried across — and it would make the Hellephant the joint-highest-HP entity in the game, which nothing else claims. Its damage (30) and walkspeed (20) are unanimous across all four listings.

For comparison: the [Mammoth](/taming/how-to-tame-a-mammoth) is 800 HP but neutral. **The hostile volcanic version has less health than the peaceful snow one** — which is the trade the designers made.

## Naming, and Why You See Two

You will find this animal written as **Hellephant** and as **Lava Mammoth**, sometimes in the same game.

The short version: players called it Lava Mammoth first, and a tester later reported the in-game name as **Hellephant**. It is a red reskin of the Mammoth — red glowing eyes, no head fluff — and the name is a pun on "hell" and "elephant". One source also notes it was **never mentioned in the official update log**, so the creature's official status rested on community reporting for a while.

Either name gets you to the same animal. Our [Taming Guide](/taming/taming-guide) lists it as the Hellephant, which is the name the game itself uses.

## FAQ

**Is the Hellephant the same as the Lava Mammoth?**
Yes — same animal, two names. Players said Lava Mammoth first.

**What food does it need?**
10 Pumpkin and 5 Cake across five cycles — 2 Pumpkin + 1 Cake per cycle. Identical to the Mammoth.

**Which flute?**
Strong Taming Flute (Tier 3). Nothing lower engages it.

**Where do I find one?**
The Volcanic Biome, at the Hellephant Ritual Circle, usually with cultists nearby.

**Do I have to fight it first?**
No — but you will have to survive it. It attacks on sight, and you cannot damage it before taming, because a damaged animal cannot be tamed.

**How do I survive the charge?**
Get near a **lit campfire** — it breaks off pursuit. Structures also break its pathing, and it gets stuck on them.

**How much HP does it have?**
Three sources say 500 (99-nights-in-the-forest.com, Pocket Tactics, Sportskeeda), one usable source says 800. We use 500; the split is laid out in full on [The Hellephant](/entities/the-hellephant).

**Does it drop good food?**
Very — 8–9 **cooked** Steaks, plus Cooked Ribs and a chance of a Mammoth Tusk. The only entity that drops cooked food with no fire needed.
