The **Wolf** is the first real combat pet most players ever tame in 99 Nights in the Forest — and still one of the best value. It is common, cheap to feed, and gives you genuine fight backup against the cultists and bears that show up later. This page is the practical how-to; stats and drops live on [The Wolf](/entities/the-wolf), and the full per-animal food table is on [Taming Guide](/taming/taming-guide).

Last verified: **2026-09-29**.

## What You Need Before You Start

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Old Taming Flute** | The Tier 1 flute (see below) |
| **6 Steak** | 2 Steak per cycle × 3 cycles — get it from [Pet Food](/taming/pet-food) |
| **A free, undamaged forest wolf** | Not a night-spawned wolf, not a collared cave guard |

## Step 1 — Get the Old Taming Flute

The Old Taming Flute is the **Tier 1** flute. You start finding it at the **Domestication Cabin** (also called the Abandoned Animal Shelter) near your camp at the beginning of a run — look for the red tent with a pot next to it; flutes sit inside the pot. It also drops from rare chests.

The Old Flute tames the entry-level animals: **Rabbit, Wolf, [Scorpion](/entities/the-scorpion), [Arctic Fox](/entities/the-arctic-fox)**. Anything stronger (Alpha Wolf, Bear, Mammoth) needs a higher-tier flute — the tiers and where to get them are on [Taming Guide](/taming/taming-guide).

## Step 2 — Pick the Right Wolf

Only a **free forest wolf** tames normally. Two types will not take the flute:

- Wolves that **spawn on you during a night raid**.
- The **collared guard wolves** outside the missing-kids caves (see [Missing Kids Locations](/locations/missing-kids)).

Find a calm wolf away from the camp — and, crucially, **do not damage it before you start**.

## Step 3 — Play the Mini-Game

Walk up and step into the **green circle** around the wolf. A vertical bar appears with a light-green target zone and a red marker. Hold to push the marker up, release to let it fall; keep it inside the green zone until the progress bar fills. A wolf is easy compared to higher-tier animals — the **Zookeeper** class used to make this mini-game even easier, but that perk has been replaced; see [Zookeeper class](/classes/zookeeper-class) for the current list.

Each completed attempt pacifies the wolf long enough to feed it and flips one "face" above its head to happy.

## Step 4 — Feed It

After each successful mini-game, the wolf asks for food. Feed it **2 Steak**, then repeat. Three cycles total:

| Cycle | Food |
|---|---|
| 1 | 2 Steak |
| 2 | 2 Steak |
| 3 | 2 Steak |
| **Total** | **6 Steak** |

When every face flips to hearts, the wolf is yours.

## Rules That Break Taming

- **The wolf must be undamaged when you start.** If it has already taken a hit, the flute will not work — let it reset or find another.
- **No night-spawned or collared wolves** (see Step 2).
- **Match the prompt exactly.** The food a wolf asks for can shift between cycles or patches, so always read the speech bubble in your own run before you commit your stack.

## The Two-Pet Limit

You can keep **two tamed pets** at once. To tame a new one, **release** an existing pet first (it despawns). Wolves are cheap enough that rotating one in and out is normal — tame one for a fight, release it when you want a Bear instead.

## Sources

The **3 cycles × 2 Steak = 6 Steak** figure is consistent across the fandom wiki, sportskeeda and allthings.how, all checked 2026-09-29. Exact food amounts can shift with balance patches, so always confirm with the on-screen prompt in your own run.
