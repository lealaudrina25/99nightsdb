# Hunter Class in 99 Nights in the Forest

The Hunter is the entry-level way to make the forest give you more of what you already kill. It costs **40 Diamonds**, hands you **two bear traps** at the start, and then spends three levels on two drop-rate bonuses and one extra trade with the Pelt Trader.

That last one — the **extra Wolf Pelt trade** — is the reason this page exists. Our own [Pelt Trader](/items/pelt-trader) page says plainly that nobody found a source naming the fifth rung's pelt or its reward. The answer has been sitting on the Hunter's level 3 perk description the whole time. We deal with it below.

The Hunter's pelt economy and the ammunition economy meet at the Pelt Trader, and our [Ammo and Guns](/guides/ammo-guide) guide draws that line.


| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| **What it is** | A resource class, not a combat class. It has no weapon and no armour |
| **Cost** | **40 Diamonds** |
| **Starter tools** | **2 Bear Traps** (one source says 1) |
| **Level 1** | + **Increased meat drop rate** |
| **Level 2** | + **Slightly higher pelt drop rate** — needs 120 bunnies and 120 wolves killed |
| **Level 3** | + **The Pelt Trader offers an extra Wolf Pelt trade** — needs 120 Alpha wolves and 250 wolves killed |
| **Do the drop rates have numbers?** | **No.** Not one source we found gives a percentage |
| **Added with** | The **Cultist Stronghold Update, 4 July 2025** — two independent sources agree |
| **Worth it?** | See [Is It Worth 40 Diamonds](#is-it-worth-40-diamonds). Sources disagree, so we lay them out |

## Hunter Names Four Different Things

Before anything else, because this is where most confusion starts: **the word "Hunter" is attached to four unrelated things in this game**, and only one of them is the class on this page.

| The name | What it actually is |
|---|---|
| **Hunter** | The class on this page — 40 Diamonds, bear traps, drop rates |
| **Big Game Hunter** | A different class at **600 Diamonds**. See [Hunter vs Big Game Hunter](#hunter-vs-big-game-hunter) |
| **Hunter level** | The class's own 1-to-3 counter. "Hunter 3" means level 3, not a rank outside the class |
| **Cultist Hunter / Hunter Camp** | Cultist-side names — [the Cultist Hunter Camp](/locations/cultist-stronghold) and the cultists in it. Nothing to do with this class |

Searching for "Hunter" in a guide will hand you the other three as often as this one.

120 bunnies and 120 wolves, then 120 alpha wolves and 250 wolves — the only counter in the game that does not go up between rungs. The rest are in our [class levels guide](/guides/class-levels).

See [Pumpkin](/items/pumpkin) for the crop itself: the only four-fork food the game grows, the two-day yield, the Farmer class guarantee, and the arithmetic behind the 10 Pumpkin and 5 Cake bill.

Hunter turns pelts into drops rather than pets; [Kiwi](/entities/the-kiwi) is a pet you never have to kill anything for, which is the trade our page spells out.

The Hunter turns in drops for gear, and the Steak coming off the same animals is what our [Steak](/items/steak) page tabulates.

## What Every Level Gives You

There is no passive-on-unlock beyond the traps. The whole class is three upgrades:

| Level | Perk | Requirements |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Increased meat drop rate | Unlocked on purchase |
| **2** | Slightly higher pelt drop rate | Kill **120 bunnies**, kill **120 wolves** |
| **3** | The Pelt Trader offers an extra Wolf Pelt trade | Kill **120 Alpha wolves**, kill **250 wolves** |

Two wikis and one secondary write-up all give the same perk text. The **requirement numbers** are the less common half of the information — we found them stated identically on two sources (one wiki and one secondary guide), and the third wiki we checked publishes the perks without the counters.

That count is almost entirely Steak-driven, and the only animal on it not paid for with meat is billed in [Chili](/items/chili).

The 120 bunnies in that count are the cheapest half to fill, and our [Bunny](/entities/the-bunny) page carries the farming routes.

## What We Can Add Up: 610 Animals to Max Out

Here is our arithmetic, and we mark it as ours because no source adds it up.

The two level requirements are separate counters:

- Level 2: **120 bunnies + 120 wolves**
- Level 3: **120 Alpha wolves + 250 wolves**

Add the wolf counts together within one run and maxing the Hunter means killing roughly:

> **120 bunnies + 370 wolves + 120 Alpha wolves = about 610 animals**

That is our multiplication of published figures, not a number any source states. Two things make it an estimate rather than a fact:

1. **We assumed the counters accumulate.** If each requirement is counted fresh, the wolf total is 370 across both levels; if the wolf counter carries over, the two requirements overlap and the real total is lower. No source says which.
2. **Nobody publishes whether the counter is per-run or permanent.** A class level does not say "this run only" or "forever," so we cannot tell you whether 610 animals is a single-run commitment or a lifetime one.

The comparison worth making is not against other classes' costs — it is that **the 40-Diamond Hunter asks for more animals than the 600-Diamond Big Game Hunter does** (that one needs 220 wolves and 150 pelts used). We spell that out below, because the instinct that cheap means quick to max is exactly backwards here.

## The Drop Rates Nobody Published

Both payoffs are drop rates, and **every source describes them in words only**:

- Level 1: an **increased** meat drop rate
- Level 2: a **slightly higher** pelt drop rate

We checked the Hunter pages of two wikis, a secondary guide, a Russian-language tier list and a secondary round-up. Not one of them prints a percentage. So **we are not going to invent one**, and neither should you plan around a number that does not exist.

What we can say, because it follows from mechanics rather than from a statistic:

- **The pelt drop rate is the useful one.** Pelts are what the [Pelt Trader](/items/pelt-trader) asks for, and his chain runs Bunny Foot to Wolf Pelt to Alpha Wolf Pelt to Bear Pelt. A higher pelt rate shortens that chain.
- **The meat drop rate is the early-game one.** It smooths hunger before the trader exists, which is most of Day 1 and 2.
- **Neither applies outside killing animals.** Drop-rate perks act on animal drops. They do not make cultists or chests give you more.

reaching that fifth rung is what puts the Giant Sack in reach at the third, and the prerequisite attached to that sack is explained on our [sacks and inventory capacity](/guides/inventory-capacity) page.

## Level 3: What the Fifth Trade Actually Is

This is the part our Pelt Trader page has to leave open, and it does not have to.

Reaching **Hunter level 3** takes the Pelt Trader chain from four trades to five. Every source agrees that happens. What they say the fifth one *is*, consistently across three sources, is:

> **an extra Wolf Pelt trade**

That settles the question our Pelt Trader page raised. The fifth rung is not a fifth pelt — it is **one more Wolf Pelt rung**, a repeat of the second rung of the chain. Practically, it means you can walk away with one more thing from the Wolf Pelt rung than a non-Hunter can.

One honest limit: **"extra Wolf Pelt trade" tells you which rung repeats, not what that rung awards.** On our [Pelt Trader](/items/pelt-trader) page, the Wolf Pelt rung awards a choice of the **Spear or the Revolver**; whether the fifth rung lets you take one of those or adds a different item is not stated by any source.

### The Good Axe Claim Does Not Add Up

Here is a conflict worth flagging rather than papering over. Two wiki pages carry the same tip in their tips section: that the Hunter's level 3 lets you obtain a **Good Sack, a Good Axe and a Spear**, with the Good Sack "from the Bunny Foot trade" and the Good Axe and Spear "from the Wolf Pelt trades."

That list does not fit the trader's own table. On both wikis, the **Good Axe is a Bunny Foot rung reward** — the first rung, the pelt the chain opens with — not a Wolf Pelt reward. The same pages list the Wolf Pelt rung as Spear or Revolver and say nothing about an axe there. So the tip contradicts the table printed on the very same pages.

We go with the table. The tip is a summary someone wrote from memory, and it collapsed two rungs into one.

## Hunter vs Big Game Hunter

These two are not a cheap version and an expensive version. They solve different problems, and one of the differences is surprising.

| | **Hunter** | **Big Game Hunter** |
|---|---|---|
| **Cost** | **40 Diamonds** | **600 Diamonds** |
| **Starter** | 2 Bear Traps | Rifle + 12 Rifle ammo + a **Pelt List** |
| **Level 1** | Increased meat drop rate | Can eat animal pelts for permanent buffs |
| **Level 2** | Slightly higher pelt drop rate | Higher pelt drop rate |
| **Level 3** | An extra Wolf Pelt trade | Infinite Mammoth Tusk consumption for permanent health |
| **Upgrade cost** | ~610 animals (our arithmetic) | 220 wolves killed, 150 pelts used (our addition of the two level requirements) |
| **What it builds toward** | The [Pelt Trader](/items/pelt-trader) chain | Endurance runs that outlive the trader's list |

Two things fall out of that table:

**Both classes' level 2 is a pelt drop rate.** One is "slightly higher," the other "higher," but the perk text is near-identical and the sources do not publish a number for either. So the pelt farm is not what separates them.

**The cheap class is the slower one to max.** Hunter level 3 needs around 610 animals. Big Game Hunter level 3 needs 220 wolves and 150 pelts eaten. If your frame is "what gets maxed fastest," the 600-Diamond class is the quick one and the 40-Diamond class is the grind. That is our comparison of two published requirement sets, and no source states it.

At 50 Diamonds the [**Fisherman**](/classes/fisherman-class) is the cheapest alternative of the same kind: no traps or combat value, but food that never costs ammo. If you are deciding between the two, that is the trade.

## Is It Worth 40 Diamonds?

The sources genuinely disagree, so here they are side by side rather than averaged:

- **For it:** the Hunter is cheap, flexible solo or with a team, and pairs unusually well with the Big Game Hunter class — one wiki lists that synergy explicitly. The bear traps let you take wolves and bunnies without standing in their bite range. The per-animal hit counts that go with those traps are collected on our [Bear Trap](/items/bear-trap) page.
- **Against it:** once the Pelt Trader's list is complete, the class stops earning its keep. The same wiki lists that as its main downside, along with the time the levels take.
- **More against it:** a tier list written in Russian puts it in the low tier for a blunt reason — with an active run you already get plenty of meat, pelts drop sooner or later anyway, and the extra trade may simply never be needed. A separate round-up calls the perks minor and says the traps are fine for base defence but not strong enough to justify the class.
- **For it, team-wise:** several sources cast it as the class that feeds the camp. Another player does not have to think about food, which is a real cost saving in a four-player run.

We are not adjudicating this. What the disagreement tells you is that **the Hunter is a class that pays off inside a specific plan** — one built around the Pelt Trader chain — and that outside that plan the 40 Diamonds are better spent on something that answers a question you actually have.

## How to Level It Without Dreading It

Every source that addresses leveling says the same two things:

1. **Farm bunnies and wolves near camp during the day.** They are the fastest targets and they cover the level 2 requirement.
2. **Keep going at night from a high spot.** Stand on a tree branch or similar ledge and shoot down into the night's wolf spawns. Three sources give this same tip; it is the standard wolf-farming position in this game, not a Hunter-specific trick.

For the Alpha wolf requirement at level 3: Alpha wolves need a **Level 3–5 campfire** in the Forest and show up in the Snow biome at night, in Locked Caves and at the [Cultist Hunter Camp](/locations/cultist-stronghold). Our [Alpha Wolf](/entities/the-alpha-wolf) page has the spawn conditions spelled out.

Bear traps are worth using early — they are the reason the class starts with anything at all — but they are a limited resource in a run, and you cannot farm 610 animals on traps alone.

## FAQ

**How many bear traps does the Hunter start with?**
**Two**, per two wikis and a secondary guide. One source list says one. We record two as the majority.

**Does the Hunter give a percentage bonus to drops?**
No source publishes one. Both perks are described in words only, and we are not going to guess a number.

**How many animals to max the Hunter?**
About **610** — 120 bunnies, 370 wolves and 120 Alpha wolves — if the two counters accumulate in one run. That is our arithmetic on published requirements, not a stated figure.

**How do I get the Pelt Trader's fifth trade?**
Reach **Hunter level 3**. It is the same fifth rung described on the [Pelt Trader](/items/pelt-trader) page, and it is an extra Wolf Pelt trade rather than a new pelt.

**Do class levels keep across runs?**
Unknown. No source states whether the Hunter's kill counters are per-run or permanent.

**Should I buy Hunter or Big Game Hunter?**
Different jobs. Hunter sharpens the [Pelt Trader](/items/pelt-trader) chain, Big Game Hunter builds permanent health and damage over a long run — see the comparison table above, [All Classes](/classes/all-classes) for the full pricing tier, and our [Big Game Hunter class](/classes/big-game-hunter-class) page for the nine-row Pelt List those numbers come from.

**Is the Hunter a combat class?**
No. It starts with traps and nothing else. Bring your own weapon.
