The **Berserker** is a 100-Diamond class in 99 Nights in the Forest whose whole identity is one perk: **you come back from your first death automatically**. Everything else it does is built around that moment.

It is also the most argued-about class in the game. Across the tier lists we checked it lands anywhere from **S-tier** to **C-tier**, and the reason for the spread is simple — people are grading two different things. As a *second chance*, the Berserker is genuinely unique and cheap. As a *damage class*, it underperforms a class that costs the same. This page separates the two claims instead of averaging them into a grade.

Last verified: **2026-10-02**.

## Berserker Class at a Glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cost** | 100 Diamonds |
| **Starting item** | 1× Medkit |
| **Type** | Solo survivability / high-risk melee |
| **Level-up requirement** | Kills made below 40% HP — 50, then 150 |
| **Added in** | Owl Update, **8 August 2025** |
| **Best at** | Solo runs, boss attempts, surviving one mistake |
| **Weakest at** | Consistent damage, early game, beginner play |

The Berserker arrived on **8 August 2025** in the Owl Update, alongside Base Defender, The Owl itself and the Mammoth Helmet. That patch date is corroborated by the fandom Update Log, droidgamers and noleep.

## Perks by Level

| Level | Perk | Requirement to reach it |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | After your **first death**, auto-revive with low HP and hunger | Unlocked on purchase |
| **2** | On reviving, receive a **strength boost** for a short duration | **50** kills while below 40% HP |
| **3** | Deal **higher damage the lower your HP** is | **150** kills while below 40% HP |

Two things about that table decide whether the class is for you.

**Level 1 is the entire purchase.** The auto-revive is available the moment you buy the class — no grinding, no level requirement. Levels 2 and 3 make the revive *better*, but the thing you actually paid for works immediately.

**Levels 2 and 3 are gated behind the most awkward requirement in the game.** You have to get kills while sitting under 40% HP. That means deliberately playing injured, which is exactly the state the class is trying to rescue you from. See the leveling section below for how players actually grind it.

## What the Auto-Revive Actually Does

The wording matters here, because several guides are vague about it.

**It fires once, on your first death of the run.** Three sources — the fandom wiki, BloxBoom's revive guide and noleep — all state it does not repeat. After that you are back to normal revive rules: a teammate with a Bandage or Medkit, the Respawn Capsule, or Robux. So it is a **single insurance policy per run**, not a sustain mechanic.

**You come back weak.** You return with low HP *and* low hunger. The revive saves the run, it does not restore you to fighting condition — plan to retreat, not to re-engage.

**Level 2 turns the revive into an escape window, and the numbers are specific.** On reviving with Level 2 unlocked you become **unseen by entities for 5 seconds** (unless you attack them), and you get a damage buff plus a **speed buff comparable to a chilli pepper**, lasting about **30 seconds**. That is your window to run back to your dropped items and leave. Treat it as a get-out tool, not a counterattack.

One note that trips people up: the fandom wiki lists a Medkit as the starting item, and says some players mistake the Berserker for a healer class because of it. It is not one — the Medkit is a single consumable, not a healing perk.

## How Big Is the Level 3 Damage Bonus, Really?

This is the number nobody publishes cleanly, and it is where most of the disagreement about the class comes from.

**What is published.** One community breakdown on the fandom wiki gives concrete figures: Level 2 is a **+25% damage boost** with **2× sprint speed** for its duration, and Level 3 adds **+0.25% damage per 1 HP lost**, totalling **+24.75% at 1 HP**. Another player in the same thread describes the Level 3 ceiling as roughly **30% with melee weapons**. No wiki or major guide publishes a percentage, so treat **+25% to +30% at near-zero HP** as the working estimate rather than a confirmed value.

**What that means in practice — our calculation, not a published figure.** If both bonuses stack and you are at 1 HP inside the Level 2 window, the combined effect is roughly **×1.25 × 1.25 ≈ 1.56**. But that window lasts about 30 seconds and only happens after a revive. The number you actually live with is the Level 3 bonus on its own: roughly **+25%**, at 1 HP, which is a health total you should not be casually sitting at.

**The comparison that settles it.** [Brawler](/classes/all-classes) also costs 100 Diamonds and grants **+30% melee damage at Level 1**, permanently, at full health, with no setup. Berserker's maximum — reached only at the brink of death — is roughly the same or slightly less. That is the complaint players have been making since launch, and it is correct: **Berserker's damage ceiling is approximately Brawler's damage floor.**

The one place Berserker may win is ranged. Brawler's bonus is explicitly melee-only and Brawler cannot use ranged weapons at all; whether Berserker's HP-scaled bonus applies to guns and alien tech is not stated anywhere we could find. If you want the bonus on a [Raygun](/weapons/how-to-get-raygun) or [Laser Cannon](/weapons/how-to-get-laser-cannon), Berserker is the only one of the two that can possibly deliver it — but we cannot confirm it does.

## How to Level It Up

Both requirements are **kills made while your HP is below 40%**. Fifty for Level 2, one hundred fifty for Level 3.

**The method players use.** Drop your own HP below 40% first — by any means, including letting something weak hit you — then use a weapon with **infinite ammo** so you are not also managing resources. The fandom wiki names the [Raygun](/weapons/how-to-get-raygun) and [Laser Cannon](/weapons/how-to-get-laser-cannon) for exactly this reason, and points at low-HP enemies like [Wolves](/entities/the-wolf) as the efficient target. Finish weakened enemies rather than opening on healthy ones.

**Do the two levels together.** The requirement is cumulative, so every low-HP kill counts toward both thresholds. There is no reason to stop at 50.

**One detail worth knowing:** a reply in the same fandom thread states that kills must be on enemies and that **Bunnies do not count**. That is a single source and we could not corroborate it, but it matches how similar kill-count requirements behave elsewhere in the game.

**A specific warning from the wiki:** its own tip list says **do not go for the Durability badge with this class.** The class wants you to take damage; that badge does not.

## Berserker vs the Other 100-Diamond Classes

If you have exactly 100 Diamonds, these are the three you are choosing between, and they want completely different players.

| | Berserker | Brawler | Undead |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Headline perk** | Auto-revive on first death | +40% HP, +30% melee damage | Permanent bonus every time you are revived |
| **Always on?** | No — once per run | Yes | Yes, but needs deaths to stack |
| **Level 2** | Strength boost after reviving | 10% health regen, regenerates sooner | Instant revive |
| **Level 3** | More damage at lower HP | 5% chance to block damage | Move for 8 seconds after death |
| **Requirement style** | Kills below 40% HP | Passive play | Be revived 30, then 60 times |
| **Best for** | Solo players who want one free mistake | Melee players who stay healthy | Groups that expect to go down |

**Pick Berserker if** you play solo and the thing that ends your runs is one bad night with nobody to pick you up.

**Pick Brawler if** you want damage you can rely on every minute of every run, and you are happy fighting in melee. It is the safer purchase.

**Pick Undead if** you play in a group that revives each other often — its bonus stacks with every revive, which turns a team habit into permanent stats. Its requirements (be revived 30, then 60 times) are close to unachievable solo.

## Is It Worth 100 Diamonds?

**Yes, if you play solo and you die once per run.** No other cheap class converts a fatal mistake into a continuation. In solo play nobody is coming with a Bandage, and the auto-revive is the only thing in the game that replaces that.

**No, if you want it to make you hit harder.** Levels 1 and 2 give you no passive damage at all, and Level 3 only pays out when you are nearly dead. The fandom wiki's own cons list is blunt about this: until Level 3 the class is weak, and once the revive has been used it has no permanent passives left.

**Buy it if:** you run solo, you are attempting bosses or the [Cultist Stronghold](/locations/cultist-stronghold) where one mistake is fatal, or you already have a combat class and want a second cheap safety net.

**Skip it if:** you are new (every source that addresses this says the same — it is not a beginner class), you play in a coordinated group where a Medic is better value at 40 Diamonds, or you were expecting a damage class and should buy Brawler instead.

## Tips That Change How It Feels

**Do not spend the revive early.** The perk is worth least on night 3 and most on night 40. Sources are explicit that using it early is a waste.

**After the revive, leave.** You come back with low HP and hunger. The Level 2 window (unseen for 5 seconds, chilli-pepper speed for 30) exists so you can grab your items and go — not so you can re-engage the thing that killed you.

**Stop healing to full if you are using Level 3.** The bonus scales with missing HP and is reported to peak under about 20% health. Sitting at half health is a reasonable compromise; sitting at full health turns the perk off.

**Carry food, not just Bandages.** The revive restores you with low hunger, and hunger is what kills you in the minutes after.

**Pair it with ranged weapons for the grind.** Infinite-ammo weapons let you farm the low-HP kill requirement without spending a second resource.

**Do not fight The Deer or The Owl expecting the bonus to save you.** Both are unkillable and their damage scales; being at 1 HP against them is just dying.

## Pros and Cons

**Pros**
- Only class that gives a free second life, and it works from Level 1
- Cheapest meaningful solo survivability perk in the shop
- Level 2 revive window is genuinely strong: 5 seconds unseen plus a large speed boost
- Level 3 has the highest theoretical damage ceiling of any cheap class
- Starts with a Medkit
- Level-up requirement is passive once you commit to playing injured

**Cons**
- The auto-revive works **once per run**
- You revive with low HP *and* low hunger, not at fighting strength
- Levels 1 and 2 give no permanent passive damage
- Level 3 only pays off at dangerously low HP — roughly +25%, which Brawler matches for free
- Level-up requirement forces you to play in the state the class exists to rescue you from
- Not recommended for beginners by every source that addresses it

## Where Sources Disagree

**Tier placement is genuinely split, and the spread is the story.** We found **S-tier** (rblxguide, which calls it the best first purchase), **B-tier** (ofzenandcomputing in two separate articles, and PC Gamer), **C-tier** (fandomwire, which says flatly that it is not worth it and to buy Brawler instead), and **3/5 stars** from beebom. That is almost the full width of the scale on one 100-Diamond class.

Our read: the S-tier and C-tier verdicts are both defensible because they are grading different things. S-tier grades the revive in solo play; C-tier grades the damage. Neither is wrong about its own axis, and no list we found states which axis it is using.

**Nobody publishes the Level 3 damage percentage.** The only concrete figures we found come from a community post on the fandom wiki (+0.25% per HP lost, +24.75% at 1 HP) and a reply in the same thread describing it as about 30% with melee weapons. Both are single sources and they do not agree. We are publishing them as an estimate range, not as a stat.

**A note on the Berserker damage figures elsewhere on this site.** Several of our weapon pages list a "fully boosted Berserker" number that implies roughly **+60%** over base (for example 25 to 40, or 35 to 56). No source we have found supports a bonus that large — the only published figures put the ceiling near +25–30%. Those weapon numbers came from aggregator damage tables and should be treated as unverified; we are flagging them here rather than quietly restating them, and we will reconcile them once a second source publishes a percentage.

## FAQ

**How many times does the Berserker auto-revive?**
Once. It triggers on your first death of the run and does not repeat.

**What do I come back with?**
Low HP and low hunger. It saves the run, not your condition.

**Is Berserker better than Brawler?**
Not for damage. Brawler gives +30% melee damage permanently at full health; Berserker's HP-scaled bonus only reaches a similar size at 1 HP. Berserker's advantage is the revive, which Brawler does not have.

**How do I unlock Level 2 and Level 3?**
Get 50, then 150, kills while your HP is below 40%.

**When was the Berserker added?**
8 August 2025, in the Owl Update — the same patch that added The Owl, Base Defender and the Mammoth Helmet.

**Is it good for beginners?**
No. Every source that addresses the question says the same thing: it rewards playing at low health and punishes the mistakes beginners make.

**Are Diamonds and Gems the same thing?**
Yes — one currency, two words. Full explanation on the [Diamonds farming page](/items/how-to-get-diamonds-fast).
