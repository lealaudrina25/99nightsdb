The **Cyborg** is the most expensive way to shoot things, and the only class that sells you a *power plant* instead of a weapon.

For **600 Diamonds** you spawn with a **Laser Cannon** and an **Alien Armour**, you can see at night, and your alien tech punishes you for overheating. That sentence is on most tier lists. What those tier lists skip is the one thing that decides whether this class feels strong or feels unleveled: **the Cyborg has two separate upgrade counters, and neither one is "use the laser cannon."** One of them counts how many times a *single shot* hits several enemies. Buy the class expecting a damage class and you will spend your first two nights farming a number that has nothing to do with killing things faster.

Where it differs from the [Alien class](/classes/alien-class) is the whole story. Both are alien-tech classes on the same charge bar. The Alien is a **mobility** class — its perks move you. The Cyborg is a **standing still and deleting a cluster** class — its perks keep a cannon fed. Same fuel, opposite playstyles, and the more expensive one is not obviously the better one.

## What 600 Diamonds Actually Buys

Two starter items, and the armour is the one people under-rate.

The **Laser Cannon** is the reason the class exists. Its damage, its area effect and its 8-second recharge are documented on our own [Laser Cannon page](/weapons/how-to-get-laser-cannon), where we also cover the overheat penalty in detail — this page will not repeat those numbers.

The **Alien Armour** is the part that quietly carries the run. Our [Alien Armour page](/items/how-to-get-alien-armour) covers its tier position (above the Iron Body, below the Thorn Body) and its four acquisition routes. The one fact that matters for a Cyborg is the **shared charge bar**: the Raygun, the Laser Cannon and the Laser Sword all draw from the same Alien Tech meter, and the armour hands energy back **when a melee hit lands on you**, scaled by the damage taken. A Cyborg standing in a wolf pack is not being gang-rushed; every bite is a partial recharge.

It is also the strongest single recommendation on [Hard Mode](/guides/hard-mode), where infinite ammo and alien armour cover the rift-clearing rotation that decides whether the run can reach day 99.

So the 600 Diamonds buy a **closed loop**: take melee hits, get energy, fire the cannon, kill more things that reach you and feed you again. It is the only class built around refuelling from damage taken rather than from time.

50 multi-target shots and 200 alien-tech kills, then 100 and 350. How every other class scales its second rung is in our [class levels guide](/guides/class-levels).

## The Perks, and the Two Counters Behind Them

| Level | Perk | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | If alien tech overheats, **you take damage and your armour is temporarily lowered** — plus **excellent visibility at night** | Unlocked on purchase |
| **2** | Alien tech deals **increased damage** | Damage multiple targets with 1 shot: **50**; kills with alien tech: **200** |
| **3** | Kills restore **some** alien tech energy | Damage multiple targets with 1 shot: **100**; kills with alien tech: **350** |

The perk text is agreed by **three independent sources** — rblxguide, the BreezeWiki (fandom) page and wikiHow — all published in 2026 and all stating the same three-line list. The **upgrade numbers** come from the same three, and the BreezeWiki lists them exactly as *Damage multiple targets with 1 shot: 50 / 100* and *Get kills with alien tech: 200 / 350*.

Two clarifications the sources state but never spell out:

**The Level 3 perk is not limited to the Laser Cannon.** BreezeWiki is explicit that it activates **even when you kill with a non-alien weapon** and **even while wearing different armour** — the page names the Thorn Body as an example. It is a kill-streak reward, not a cannon reward. In practice, that makes Level 3 a *survival* perk: clearing a room with whatever is in your hands refills the meter you need for the next room.

**The Level 2 damage buff applies to alien tech generally** — Laser Cannon included — and that is the level where the class's damage jumps. BreezeWiki notes the practical edge: with Level 2, the Laser Cannon can **one-shot a Wolf**.

## The Upgrade Wall Nobody Mentions

This is the section the tier lists omit, and it is the difference between a strong class and a mid class.

**The Level 2 perk needs two things at once: 50 multi-target shots and 200 alien-tech kills. Level 3 needs 100 and 350.** That is a *pair* of counters running in parallel, which is unusual — most classes in the game gate levels on one number. Compare the [Alien class](/classes/alien-class), which gates everything on a single kill counter (150, then 300 kills with alien tech).

Three honest unknowns sit on top of those numbers:

**What "damage multiple targets with 1 shot" counts.** The wording implies one shot hitting several enemies, but no source we can find states whether it counts the *number of shots that hit multiple enemies*, the *number of enemies hit in total*, or the *number of enemies beyond the first*. The counter is published; its unit is not. Treat 50 as "a lot of clustered shots" rather than as a number you can plan exact sessions around.

**Whether "kills with alien tech" needs the kill to be *finished* by alien tech.** Our own Alien class page documents a finisher trick — soften an enemy with a high-damage weapon and land the last hit with the Raygun, and the kill still counts as an alien-tech kill. Whether that trick also satisfies the Cyborg counter is **not documented anywhere** we can check, and it is the difference between grinding kills and grinding them cleverly.

**How long the counters persist.** Neither wiki says whether they are per-run or banked across runs like the class's cost is. Both wikis do describe the grind shape, which implies the numbers are meant to be farmed rather than earned in one very good night.

The farming answer is published and it is not subtle: **raid the Cultist Stronghold.** Both BreezeWiki and the rblxguide analysis point there, because the Stronghold is the only place that reliably serves you a **cluster of enemies** — which is exactly what both of the Cyborg's counters consume. Multi-target shots *are* a Stronghold stat, not just a kill stat.

The Cyborg's energy loop is a recharge rather than a reload, and our [Ammo and Guns](/guides/ammo-guide) guide is the page that compares the two honestly.


Our arithmetic on the published figures — this is ours, not a sourced claim.

The Laser Cannon spends roughly **75% of the bar per shot** (BreezeWiki's figure, and consistent with our [Laser Cannon page](/weapons/how-to-get-laser-cannon), where one shot leaves you about 25%). The bar refills in about **8 seconds**.

| State | Energy | What you can do |
|---|---|---|
| Full bar | 100% | Fire once, immediately |
| After one shot | ~25% | Not enough for a second shot |
| After waiting ~4 s (25% → 75%) | ~75% | Second shot, then overheat |
| After waiting the full ~8 s | 100% | Back to a clean double |

So the play pattern is **one shot, wait, one shot** — and the Cyborg's Level 1 penalty turns that required pause from a wait into a **cost**, because overheating damages you and drops your armour. BreezeWiki puts it flatly: firing twice in a row almost always overheats.

What the Level 3 perk changes is the only thing that can shortcut that pause. **Kills refund energy.** The refund size is **not published by any source** we can find, so we will not put a number on it — but the mechanism is documented, it works with any weapon, and it is the reason the class is described as sustaining itself in a long fight rather than in a burst.

The one synergy most guides list and few explain: the Alien Armour refills the same bar from melee hits. Overheating is a Cyborg mistake, a low-energy fight is a positioning mistake, and the armour is what makes the second one survivable.**

## Overheating Is a Class Mechanic, Not a Gun Mechanic

The penalty is frequently mis-attributed. Our [Laser Cannon page](/weapons/how-to-get-laser-cannon) covers the mechanics; the Cyborg-relevant point is the scope.

- A **non-Cyborg** player who loots the cannon out of a chest gets the same 8-second recharge but **no self-damage** on overheat — the gun simply will not fire.
- A **Cyborg** player at Level 1 takes **damage and a temporary drop in armour** when the alien tech overheats.

That is why the class is rated "Intermediate–Advanced" difficulty by rblxguide rather than "beginner friendly": the same weapon is strictly safer in the hands of someone who did not buy the class. The class does not give you more damage capability so much as it gives you a **reason to be careful**, and then charges you for every second you are not careful.

## Cyborg vs Alien: The 100-Diamond Brother

Both classes share the alien-tech charge bar, and both were added in the same 18 July 2025 patch. They are not alternatives.

| | **Alien** | **Cyborg** |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | 100 Diamonds | 600 Diamonds |
| Starter | 1× Raygun | 1× Laser Cannon + 1× Alien Armour |
| Level 1 | Night vision; **sprints slower while overheated** | Night vision; **takes damage and loses armour when overheated** |
| Level 2 | Alien tech recharges **faster** | Alien tech deals **more damage** |
| Level 3 | Sprints **faster at full charge** | **Kills** restore some alien tech energy |
| Level-up counter | Kills with alien tech: 150, then 300 | Multi-target shots 50/100 + alien-tech kills 200/350 |
| Playstyle | Move, disengage, chip | Stand, cluster, delete |

The trade is legible: the **Alien converts energy into mobility**, and the **Cyborg converts energy into deleted enemies**. If your problem is "I die chasing things," the Alien class is the cheaper fix. If your problem is "night five will not end," the Cyborg is the answer — at six times the price.

## Is 600 Diamonds Worth It?

The sources genuinely split here, and the split is about playstyle rather than numbers.

**The pro side** is the consensus. wikiHow's best-class guide names the Cyborg **the best overall class** in the game for both solo and group play, and rblxguide calls it one of the strongest offensive options available, noting that Level 3 kills feeding energy make it bristle with momentum. BreezeWiki's own list of reasons to buy it is long: one of the best 600-Diamond classes, spawns with two top-tier items that work well together, Level 2 lets the cannon one-shot wolves, and the night vision makes rescuing Missing Children considerably easier.

**The honest counter-arguments**, most of them from BreezeWiki's own Trivia section:

- **It is the most expensive tier, and some players consider it not worth it** because of the cost, the overheat debuff and the slow weapon recharge.
- **Low damage against heavy single targets** — BreezeWiki's cons list calls out mammoths, bears and other strong enemies specifically. The area cannon shines against clusters and disappoints against one long health bar.
- **It is described as ideal mainly for mobile play**, with the claim that a PC player who is good at melee would get more from the [Vampire class](/classes/vampire-class).
- The armour **cannot be taken off once worn** and can only be swapped for something strictly better, so the Alien Armour you spawn in is a run-long commitment unless you craft upward (see our [Alien Armour page](/items/how-to-get-alien-armour) for the ladder and the permanence rule).

An October 2026 tier list that ranks the [Beastmaster](/classes/beastmaster-class) in **A** puts the Cyborg **above it**, and the [Zookeeper](/classes/zookeeper-class) in **C** — so the 600-Diamond tier is where the tier lists still stack up.

Our read, which is our opinion and not a source: **the Cyborg is worth 600 Diamonds if you play in packs of enemies, and roughly worthless 600 Diamonds if you play in single long fights.** That is the same split as the weapon it sells — see the [Laser Cannon page](/weapons/how-to-get-laser-cannon) for the damage table behind it.

## Where Older Guides Still Go Wrong

The Cyborg has been reworked, and the pre-rework version is still circulating.

BreezeWiki's Trivia records both changes plainly:

- **Level 1 used to not include the excellent night vision.** Older guides list the Cyborg's Level 1 as a single drawback with no visibility perk, which is why some pages describe the class as purely a penalty bundle.
- **Level 2 used to give a healing buff when alien tech was at full charge**, before it was changed to an **unconditional damage buff**.

If a guide tells you the Cyborg **heals you at full charge**, it is describing the old Level 2. If a guide tells you the Cyborg has **no night vision**, it is describing the old Level 1. Neither is current; both are in circulation.

The only point of agreement, stated by all three sources we checked, is the **600-Diamond cost, the two starter items, and the three perk names**.

## Tips

- **Fire once, then reposition.** One shot is roughly 75% of the bar. The second shot is a deliberate decision, not a reflex.
- **Let them come to you.** The [Alien Armour](/items/how-to-get-alien-armour) refunds energy on melee hits, so letting a pack close is not a mistake — it is the recharge.
- **Grind the Stronghold, not the woods.** Both upgrade counters want clusters; the Cultist Stronghold is the only reliable cluster in the game.
- **Do not spend your bar on movement.** Unlike the Alien class, a Cyborg has no sprint escape while overheating, and overheating costs you HP and armour.
- **Level 3 pays off with any weapon.** If your cannon is dry, killing the room with a rifle still refills the meter for the next room.
- **Night vision is a rescue-tool.** It is the difference between finding the last Missing Child hours late and finding them in time.

## Pros and Cons

**Pros**
- Spawns with **two top-tier items** that synergise more than anything else in the shop
- Genuinely the best **cluster-clear** tool most classes cannot match
- Level 2 **one-shots wolves**, which is a real step change in clearing speed
- Level 3 **refunds energy from kills**, with any weapon and any armour
- **Excellent night vision** from Level 1, which carries into every other part of the run

**Cons**
- **600 Diamonds** — the top of the shop, and the hardest number to farm
- Overheating is a **dual penalty**: HP loss plus a temporary loss of armour
- **Poor against single heavy targets** — mammoths and bears are where the class disappoints
- Two upgrade counters, one of which measures **multi-target shots**, are awkward to grind
- The Alien Armour you spawn in **cannot be removed**; it defines your run

## FAQ

**How much does the Cyborg cost?**
**600 Diamonds.** Stated identically by rblxguide, BreezeWiki and wikiHow, and consistent with our own [Laser Cannon](/weapons/how-to-get-laser-cannon) and [Alien Armour](/items/how-to-get-alien-armour) pages.

**What do you spawn with?**
A **Laser Cannon** and an **Alien Armour**, per shot. No other class starts with an Alien Armour.

**What are the Level 2 and Level 3 requirements?**
Level 2: **50** shots that damage multiple targets, plus **200** kills with alien tech. Level 3: **100** and **350**. All three sources list the same pair.

**Does the Level 3 energy refund work with a non-alien weapon?**
Yes. BreezeWiki states it activates when killing with any weapon and even when wearing a different armour, naming the Thorn Body.

**Is the Cyborg better than the Alien class?**
Different jobs. The Alien spends energy on **movement** at 100 Diamonds; the Cyborg spends it on **area damage** at 600. If you want to disengage, buy the Alien. If you want to hold ground, buy the Cyborg.

**Is the Cyborg the best class in the game?**
wikiHow says yes. BreezeWiki's Trivia says a lot of players think it is not worth its cost, and that PC players strong at melee do better as a Vampire. Both positions are published, and both concern playstyle.

**Why does my Laser Cannon hurt me when someone else's does not?**
The self-damage and armour drop on overheating are **Cyborg Level 1 perks**, not properties of the gun. A non-Cyborg using the same weapon just waits.

**Were the Cyborg perks always these?**
No. Level 1 **used to not include night vision**, and Level 2 **used to be a heal at full charge**; both were changed. Older pages still describe the old versions.
