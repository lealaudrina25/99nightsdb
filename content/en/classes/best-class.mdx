A class decides how you survive in 99 Nights in the Forest. This page ranks them by what they actually do in a run — not by rarity — and tells you which one to buy first.

## How Classes Work

Classes are bought with **Diamonds** and unlocked permanently. They cannot be refunded, and you cannot respec, so the first class you buy is the one you play for a long time.

Each class grants starting gear and a survival speciality, with **perks unlocking at levels 1, 2 and 3**. The level-3 perk is usually the reason a class is worth its price — reading only the first perk is the most common way players end up with a class they dislike.

Prices run from about **10 Diamonds** for the cheapest classes up to **600 Diamonds** for endgame picks. A brand-new player can realistically afford one cheap class and should not expect more.

- **The food role, split in two.** [Chef and Cook](/classes/chef-class) — the Cook stretches every dish you already cook; the Chef builds a station nobody else can use.

## Ranking by Role, Not by Rarity

Rarity is not the same as usefulness. A cheap class that matches your job beats an expensive one that does not. Pick by the role you want to fill:

| Role | Class | What it does for the camp |
|---|---|---|
| Sustained wood supply | Lumberjack | Keeps the fire alive past night 20 |
| Food at scale | Chef | Cooks unique recipes that buff the whole camp |
| Healing | Medic | Turns survivable mistakes into non-events |
| Ranged defence | Ranger | Handles threats before they reach the walls |
| Monster defence | Pyromaniac | [Flamethrower](/weapons/how-to-get-flamethrower) clears night-wave pressure |
| Crafting | [Blacksmith](/classes/blacksmith-class) | Unlocks the next crafting bench tier until your bench hits Tier 4 |
| Solo survivability | [Berserker](/classes/berserker-class) | Auto-revives after your first death |
| Pets and scouting | Beastmaster | Summons tamed wolves to fight with you |
| Team revive scaling | [Undead](/classes/undead-class) | Permanent buffs every time a teammate revives you |
| Army you feed yourself | [Necromancer](/classes/necromancer-class) | Raises dead cultists as six summons — and grows weaker if your team clears too fast |
| Melee sustain you bank and spend | [Vampire](/classes/vampire-class) | Lifesteal on any melee weapon and faster at night — but only for players who fight up close |
| Cluster clearing | [Cyborg](/classes/cyborg-class) | Laser Cannon wipes a pack in one shot, and the Alien Armour refills its energy on melee hits — worth 600 Diamonds, and weak against single heavy targets |

## Best Class for Solo Play

If you are playing alone or with one other person, survivability beats team utility. Two classes stand out:

**[Berserker](/classes/berserker-class)** — after your first death you auto-revive with low HP and hunger. In solo play, where nobody is coming to revive you, this single perk is worth more than any damage bonus. Note that this is a survivability pick, not a damage one: at the same 100-Diamond price, Brawler gives more reliable damage.

[**Beastmaster**](/classes/beastmaster-class) — you can summon tamed wolves and start with steak in your sack, and you can keep up to five pets. Pets act as a bodyguard while you gather, which is exactly when solo players are most vulnerable.

## Best Class for a Full Camp

In a group of 20+, stop thinking about yourself and start thinking about what the camp is missing. A balanced group needs:

- **Medics** for healing
- **Lumberjacks** for wood production
- **Chefs** for food at scale
- **Rangers or Pyromaniacs** for monster defence

One of each does more for a camp than four players all running the same combat class.

Add an [Undead](/classes/undead-class) if your camp already has a Medic — it collects a permanent buff every time someone revives it, so the bandages a Medic burns stop being pure overhead. Outside of that pairing it sits in the C-tier bracket on most tier lists, because without a steady reviver almost nothing in its kit fires.

- **[Assassin](/classes/assassin-class)** — faster sprint at the cost of some HP. Good for scouting runs where getting home matters more than winning a fight. It is the one expensive class that pays out immediately rather than after a grind.

**Alien** deserves a mention: it grants strong visibility at night, which directly addresses the thing that kills most players. Sprint slows while the alien tech overheats, so it is a trade, not an upgrade.

## Tips and Common Mistakes

**Read the level-3 perk before buying.** Most regret comes from players who bought on the strength of level-1 alone.

**Save codes for Diamonds.** Check the active codes page before you buy — free Diamonds are the difference between a cheap class and a good one.

**Do not buy two half-classes.** Thirty Diamonds buys one class. Committing to a role beats dabbling in two.

**Match the class to your group, not your ego.** In a full camp, a Lumberjack contributes more than a fifth combat class, every single time.

**Check after updates.** New classes arrive with patches — most recently Escape Artist, Elemental and Woodsman — and balance shifts with them. This page is updated alongside each patch.
