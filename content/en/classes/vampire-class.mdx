The **Vampire** is the class for players who want to stop buying Medkits. It is 600 Diamonds, it arrives with a **Vampire Scythe** and a **Vampire Cloak**, and from Level 1 onward it turns melee hits into health — on *any* melee weapon, not just the scythe it hands you.

It is also the one class documented to conflict with Hard Mode loot. A Corrupted Thorn Armour should not be put on if you are depending on the Vampire Cloak to heal, and the reason is on [Hard Mode](/guides/hard-mode).

This page is about the parts of the Vampire that the weapon guides leave out: what the level counters actually cost in swings, why the published numbers imply a lifesteal percentage nobody publishes, and one question that decides whether this class has a floor or a ceiling.

The sustain comparison worth making is against the cheapest suppliers rather than the other sixth-hundred-Diamond options. [**Fisherman Class**](/classes/fisherman-class) is the baseline for permanent food.

## At a Glance

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cost** | **600 Diamonds** |
| **Released** | **1 November 2025** (the Hedge Maze update — Halloween Part 3: Carnival Games & Haunted Maze) |
| **Starter tools** | **Vampire Cloak ×1, Vampire Scythe ×1** |
| **Level 1** | Lifesteal energy when you attack with **any melee weapon**; **run faster at night**. No requirements. |
| **Level 2** | **Better vision at night**. Requires **450 HP healed through lifesteal** *and* **5,000 damage dealt**. |
| **Level 3** | **Increased healing from lifesteal**. Requires **1,000 HP healed** (one source: **900**) *and* **10,000 or 15,000 damage dealt**. |
| **Armour on spawn** | **Vampire Cloak — rated below the Iron Body** |
| **Best at** | Solo pushes into the [Cultist Stronghold](/entities/the-cultist), boss kills, any run where you are the only melee |
| **Weakest at** | Anything that ends the fight before your next swing |

**The one-line version:** the Vampire is not a glass cannon and not a support class. It is a melee class whose entire defensive kit is "keep swinging" — which makes it one of the strongest classes in the game for a player who enjoys melee, and one of the hardest for a player who does not. PC Gamer puts it plainly: good for solo and co-op, "but incredibly difficult for players uncomfortable with melee combat."

## The Starter Armour Is Below Iron — Which Is the Good News

Every source agrees the Vampire spawns with a **Vampire Cloak**. Almost every source also agrees, on this site at least, that the Vampire Cloak is **weaker than the Iron Body** — roughly Leather-tier protection, sitting one rung below the Iron Body on the armour ladder described in our [Iron Body](/items/how-to-get-iron-body) and [Thorn Body](/items/how-to-get-thorn-body) pages.

At first glance that looks like a flaw in a 600-Diamond purchase. It is the opposite.

The game's armour rule, which the fandom wiki and rblxguide both state and which this site uses throughout the armour pages: **once a piece of armour is equipped it cannot be unequipped — not even by dying and reviving — and it can only be swapped for a strictly better piece.** Applied to classes, the consequences are:

- A **Brawler** spawns in a **Leather Body**, the weakest armour in the game. That sounds like a handicap, but it is the cheapest possible starting state: there is nothing to un-equip and nothing locking them out of a better piece.
- A **Cyborg** ([full class here](/classes/cyborg-class)) spawns in an **Alien Armour** that the [Thorn Body](/items/how-to-get-thorn-body) outranks, so they are committed for the run unless they craft upward.
- A **Vampire** spawns in a **Vampire Cloak** that the Iron Body also outranks — so, like the Brawler, they start in a state that costs them nothing and blocks nothing.

So buying the Vampire does not trap you in weak armour. It is only a trap in one specific case, and that case is the next section.

Three published numbers for one meter and a fourth that attaches it to the wrong object; our [Healing, Bandages and Medkits](/guides/healing-guide) guide treats lifesteal as a heal source that costs nothing per use.

## The Blood Meter: Three Published Numbers and an Owner Nobody Names

The Vampire does not heal per hit. It **banks** healing: you keep landing melee hits, a meter fills, and when it tops out it pays out in one chunk. Every source agrees on the shape. None of them agree on the size, or on what is holding the meter.

| Source | What it says |
|---|---|
| Our [Vampire Scythe](/weapons/how-to-get-scythe) page | The scythe's blood bar heals **~30% of your health** when full, and **~50% at Vampire Level 3** |
| Our [Thorn Body](/items/how-to-get-thorn-body) page | The **Vampire Cloak's** blood meter heals **~25%**, **at night**, and **pauses in combat** |
| 99nightsintheforest.wiki | A hidden "Blood Well" heals **10% of max HP** when it procs at night |

Three numbers, and a fourth split: the scythe page attaches the meter to the **weapon**, the thorn-body page attaches it to the **armour**, and the wiki attaches it to a third thing entirely. What all three agree on is the behaviour that matters — the heal arrives in a lump, not a trickle, so the Vampire is built around *disengaging with a full bar* rather than topping up continuously.

**The question this leaves open, and it is a big one:** if the blood meter lives on the **cloak**, then the moment you upgrade to an Iron Body — which is exactly what the armour rule tells you to do — your healing may quietly disappear along with the armour. If it lives on the **class**, an Iron Body costs you nothing.

No source we can find states which it is. We are not going to pick one and present it as fact, because the difference between "upgrade your armour" and "upgrade your armour and lose your sustain" is the difference between a good class and a trap class. What we can say is that the two readings are both compatible with everything published so far, and that a Vampire who swaps into an Iron Body and finds the healing gone has not done anything wrong.

Note also that the **[Iron Body](/items/how-to-get-iron-body) is the standard mid-run target** for most players, and it is reachable from the Pelt Trader's fourth rung. If you are going to be a Vampire long-term, establish which of these is true before you spend a Bear Pelt.

450 over 5,000 and 1,000 over 10,000. That shared damage counter is published for three classes; the [class levels guide](/guides/class-levels) works out what it means for the other nineteen.

## How the Levels Actually Tick: Two Counters, One of Them Arithmetic

Every level past the first requires **two things at once** — a healing counter and a damage counter. That is easy to skim past and worth reading properly, because the two counters are not independent.

Level 2 needs **450 HP healed** and **5,000 damage dealt**. Level 3 needs **1,000 HP healed** (or 900) and **10,000 or 15,000 damage dealt**. The healing counter is a **percentage of damage dealt** — that is what lifesteal means — so the question "which counter finishes first?" is really "what percentage is the lifesteal?"

**Our reading of the published pairs:**

| Pair (heal : damage) | Implied percentage |
|---|---|
| 450 : 5,000 (Level 2, universally agreed) | **9%** |
| 900 : 10,000 (99-nights-in-the-forest.com, Level 3) | **9%** |
| 1,000 : 10,000 (PC Gamer, Level 3) | **10%** |
| 1,000 : 15,000 (the wiki, Sportskeeda, Spot.Monster, Level 3) | **6.7%** |

Two things follow. First, the heal counter is a **fixed fraction** of the damage counter at every level, which is exactly what you would expect if a single hidden percentage drives it. Second, that fraction lands at **9–10%**, and **nobody publishes the number**. Which brings us back to the Assassin: on that class we could show that its critical-hit chance is not published anywhere and refused to invent it. The same rule applies here. The 6.7% row is the odd one out, and it is the most-published Level 3 row — worth knowing when you read a tier list written from it.

**What the damage counter costs in swings.** The [Vampire Scythe](/weapons/how-to-get-scythe) does **45 damage** on a **0.7-second** swing. Taking those two published numbers:

- **10,000 damage** = about **222 swings** = about **2.6 minutes** of continuous, uninterrupted, every-swing-hits melee.
- **15,000 damage** = about **333 swings** = about **3.9 minutes** of the same.

That is our arithmetic on published figures, not a sourced claim, and it assumes every swing connects against one target with no misses and no interruptions. Even so, the conclusion is durable: **the damage requirement is a few minutes of work, not a wall.** The wall is that all of it has to be you, melee, while you are standing still enough to swing — and the healing counter is a fixed multiple of the same damage.

## Where Sources Disagree

**Level 3 damage: 10,000 vs 15,000.** PC Gamer and 99-nights-in-the-forest.com give **10,000**. The wiki, Sportskeeda and Spot.Monster give **15,000**. That is an even 2:2 split, so we publish both and do not choose. It is not a cosmetic difference: at 10,000 the Level 3 requirement is a little over two minutes of scythe swings; at 15,000 it is nearly four.

**Level 3 healing: 1,000 vs 900.** Four sources give **1,000** and one gives **900**. On a straight count that looks settled, but see the table above — it is the **900** figure that sits at a clean 9%, the same fraction the Level 2 pair implies, which makes it the internally consistent number even though it is the minority one. We are not adopting it. We are pointing out that counting sources and checking ratios give different answers here, and the honest position is that the Level 3 healing requirement is not established.

**The lifesteal percentage.** Not published by any source we can verify. Every guide says the Vampire *heals*; none says how much per hit.

**Night speed.** "Run faster at night" is agreed, the **percentage is not published** by any source. Do not borrow the Assassin's +10% — that number belongs to a different class.

**A source we do not use, and why.** The wiki at `99nightsintheforest.wiki` publishes a large stack of Vampire mechanics as "hidden requirements" and data-mined stats: 5–8% lifesteal at Level 0, 12–15% at Level 3, a Blood Well, +20% walk speed and +10% attack speed at night, a "Red Outline" that shows enemies through trees, an over-heal shield of 50 damage, half-speed health regen during the day, +10% damage against Entities, and a Blood Amulet. **None of it has a second source**, three of those numbers contradict the figures the same page publishes in its own table, and one of its "requirements" (10,000 melee damage for Level 2) contradicts its own stated Level 2 requirement of 5,000 on the same page. We list it here rather than silently ignoring it, because its numbers are the ones that float around tier-list comments. None of it appears in this article as fact.

## Vampire vs Necromancer: the Two 600-Diamond Sustain Classes

At 600 Diamonds you are choosing between the Vampire and the [Necromancer](/classes/necromancer-class), and our own [best class by role](/classes/best-class) page puts both of them in the slot it calls "sustain". They are not the same purchase, and the [Necromancer](/classes/necromancer-class) page has its own version of this comparison.

| | **Vampire (600)** | **Necromancer (600)** |
|---|---|---|
| **What sustains you** | Your own melee hits | An army of resurrected cultists |
| **Where you have to stand** | In melee range, personally | Wherever the kills happen — a positioning problem, not a distance problem |
| **Scales with** | Your weapon and your uptime | The number of corpses your team leaves |
| **Weak when** | The fight ends before your next swing | Your team clears faster than you can harvest |
| **Upkeep** | Nothing — healing is free | Souls: 3 per resurrection |
| **Team effect** | Makes its own survival better | Makes the whole run better |
| **Armour situation** | Starts below Iron, may or may not lose its meter on upgrade | No starter armour to lose |

If you are playing solo, the Vampire is the more reliable of the two, because its sustain depends only on your own hands. If you are on a large camp that already clears the map, the [Necromancer](/classes/necromancer-class) is the one that takes pressure off everyone. The trade-off is the whole decision.

The lifesteal is why the Vampire is the game's solo boss killer, and our [Bosses](/guides/boss-guide) guide is where the two killable fights are listed.

## Is It Worth 600 Diamonds?

Tier lists split on this class, and the split falls exactly where you would expect — on whether the player fights in melee.

| Source | Placement |
|---|---|
| dinokid.wiki | **S** — "a lifesteal bruiser that sustains through fights... excellent for solo boss kills and [Cultist Stronghold](/entities/the-cultist) clears" |
| theclick.gg | **S** |
| earnaldo | **S** bracket |
| gamegeekfusion | **A** |
| bloxboom | **B** |

Compare that with the [Necromancer](/classes/necromancer-class), which five published 2026 tier lists all put in **S** — and note that the Vampire's disagreement is not about strength but about **fit**. PC Gamer's own verdict is the honest version of the argument: the Level 3 lifesteal "feels great, but doesn't mean you can charge into massive mobs and forgo kiting." The B-rank writers are describing a player who wants to be handed a class and play at range. The S-rank writers are describing a player who wants to be in the middle of a fight and stay there.

**Our verdict:** if you already like melee — if you are the one who runs at the cultists with a [Morningstar](/weapons/how-to-get-morningstar) — the Vampire is one of the best Diamonds you can spend, and the Level 1 perk means you can keep using whatever melee weapon you like rather than being pinned to the scythe. If you buy it hoping for a tanky ranged class, it will feel like a downgrade, and that is on the purchase, not the class.

## Tips

- **Your weapon is a choice, not the scythe.** Level 1's lifesteal applies to **any melee weapon**, so take the [Morningstar](/weapons/how-to-get-morningstar) or the [Infernal Sword](/weapons/how-to-get-infernal-sword) if they suit you better. The [Vampire Scythe](/weapons/how-to-get-scythe) is the starter, not the requirement.
- **Disengage to bank the heal.** Because the meter pays out in a lump rather than per hit, the value comes from backing off before it fills, spending the heal, and going back in. Running away mid-pursuit is the mistake.
- **The first two nights are the weak part.** Level 3 is where the healing gets meaningfully better; until then you are a melee class with a middling cloak. Do not judge the class on night 1.
- **Kite anyway.** PC Gamer's warning is not cosmetic: the sustain does not replace positioning, and it does nothing while you are dead.
- **Sort out your armour early, not late.** The permanent-armour rule means if an Iron Body is going to delete your blood meter, you will not find out by swapping at night 30.

## Pros

- Lifesteal works on **any melee weapon**, so the class does not lock you into one weapon
- Free healing with no Medkit economy — saving you real coins over a long run
- Faster at night, plus **sharper** night vision at Level 2
- Excellent solo carry; one of the few classes that can hold a Stronghold push alone
- Permanent class — unlike event gear, the scythe and cloak are yours every run

## Cons

- **Ranged does nothing for you.** No gun, no bow, no ranged lifesteal.
- Your defence is conditional on being in melee range, which is where the damage is
- The blood meter's size, owner and day/night behaviour are all unresolved
- You spawn with below-Iron armour, and the upgrade path may cost you your sustain
- Level 3 is the payoff, which means the early nights of the class are the weak ones

## FAQ

**What does the Vampire class cost?**
**600 Diamonds.** It sits in the 600-Diamond tier alongside Cyborg, Pyromaniac, [Big Game Hunter](/classes/big-game-hunter-class), Necromancer and the Gunslinger.

**What do you spawn with?**
A **Vampire Cloak ×1** and a **Vampire Scythe ×1**. That is the entire starter list.

**Does the lifesteal only work with the scythe?**
No. Every source states it applies when attacking with **any melee weapon**. The scythe is what you start with.

**What are the Level 2 and Level 3 requirements?**
Level 2: **450 HP healed through lifesteal plus 5,000 damage dealt.** Level 3: **1,000 HP healed** (or 900, per one source) **plus 10,000 or 15,000 damage dealt** depending on the source. Both counters must be filled.

**How much do you heal per hit?**
Nobody publishes it. The published level requirements *imply* roughly 9–10%, but that is arithmetic on two counters, not a stated figure.

**Can I take the Vampire Cloak off?**
No — armour cannot be unequipped once worn; you can only swap it for a strictly better piece. Iron Body, Alien Armour and Thorn Body all outrank the Cloak, so they can replace it. Whether that removes your blood meter is the question we cannot answer from published sources.

**When was the Vampire added?**
**1 November 2025**, in the Hedge Maze update (Halloween Part 3). The same update brought the Carnival mini-games and the Haunted Maze.

**Is Vampire better than Necromancer?**
Different jobs. Vampire sustains you personally; [Necromancer](/classes/necromancer-class) sustains through an army that depends on kills happening near you. Solo: Vampire. Big camp: Necromancer.
