The **Poison Master** is the class for players who want the forest to do the killing. It costs **200 Diamonds**, it arrives holding a **Blowpipe** and wearing **Poison Armour**, and every one of its three levels makes poison better — stronger ticks, a chance to hand the poison to whoever is standing next to your victim, and finally a damage buff against anything already poisoned.

This page is about the parts of the class that the weapon and armour guides leave out: why the "infinite ammo" label on the Blowpipe sends people into the wrong fight, what Level 2 actually triggers, why Level 3 makes this a team class instead of a solo one, and the one mechanic that decides whether the Poison Armour is defence or a trap.

For the four ways to get the armour itself — class, Frog Chests, the Frog Cave pond, and the Mother Temple — see [How to Get Poison Armour](/items/how-to-get-poison-armour). This page starts from the assumption that you already own the class, or are deciding whether to buy it.

Some Chests hand out seeds; the complete seed list is on our [Seed Packs](/items/seed-packs) page.

**The one-line version:** the Poison Master is the cheapest class in the game that hands you both a ranged weapon and a defensive armour in one purchase, and the strongest of the mid-priced classes at turning a crowd into a self-destructing crowd. It is also a class where two of the three perks get described as something they are not.

## What You Actually Own When You Spawn

Two items, and both of them matter:

**The Blowpipe** — a ranged weapon that poisons whatever it hits. Every source we checked describes it the same way: a **three-dart magazine**, **unlimited reserve ammo**, and a **slow reload**.

**The Poison Armour** — a chestplate that poisons anything that hits you in melee. Fandom describes it as "reduces damage players receive and inflicts poison damage on enemies that attack the wearer," and puts it in the same family as the [Thorn Body](/items/how-to-get-thorn-body): different effect, same trigger.

That is the whole starting kit. There is no melee weapon, no shield, and no second armour. Compared with the 500-Diamond [Assassin](/classes/assassin-class), who arrives with a [Katana](/weapons/how-to-get-katana) *and* 120 [Kunai](/weapons/how-to-get-kunai), the Poison Master's opening is narrower — but it comes with a defensive item that genuinely changes how a run can go.

The poison route is advertised as unlimited ammunition, and it is worth separating that claim from the way our [Ammo and Guns](/guides/ammo-guide) guide prices ranged fire: the real question is what a shot costs you, not whether the label says unlimited.


This is the first thing to get straight, because the guides disagree with themselves on it.

Fandom's items list says the Blowpipe has "a magazine capacity of 3, an unlimited reserve ammo, and a slow reload time." Droid Gamers says "Infinite ammo, but can only fire 3 shots before entering a 10-second cooldown." Sportskeeda's own FAQ answers "How many shots can a Blowpipe fire?" with "A Blowpipe can fire three darts before requiring a reload" — in the same article that calls it a weapon with "unlimited darts." Another weapons guide lists it as "unlimited reserve ammo but has a slow reload speed."

Read all of that together and one thing is constant: **you never run out of darts, but you are limited to three shots before a reload.** "Unlimited ammo" means you are not rationing resources. It does *not* mean the weapon is spammable. The bottleneck is the reload, and the reload is slow — Droid Gamers is the only source we found that puts a number on it (**10 seconds**, single source, worth taking as a rough figure rather than an established value).

So the class's actual loop is not "stand at the edge and spray." Sportskeeda describes the intended play as: hit the enemy with the Blowpipe to poison them, then "follow up with a few quick strikes from a melee weapon." That is the build — the Blowpipe opens and applies the effect, the melee weapon finishes. Without a melee weapon in the other hand, the Poison Master spends most of a fight waiting.

> **What this means in practice:** the Poison Armour is not decoration. Slow reloads are exactly when melee attackers reach you, and every one of them that lands a melee hit is going to eat a poison stack on its way in. The armour is what covers the reload window.

## What the Poison Armour Actually Does

Three separate questions, three separate answers — and one of them is a live disagreement.

**Does it trigger on melee only?** Yes, and this is the most consistent claim on the whole page. Fandom: "anything that attacks the wearer (with a melee attack) gets inflicted with poison damage." The 99 Nights in the Forest wiki's item entry: "automatically applies a potent poison DoT effect to any entity that deals melee damage to the wearer." Deltia's Gaming: "any enemy that attacks the wearer is inflicted with a poison debuff." Melee is the trigger.

**How long does the poison last?** One source gives a figure — the site above rates the armour's damage reduction as "Medium-High" and says the poison "lasts for approximately 3 seconds." That duration, and the "Medium-High" rating, come from a single source with no corroboration. Treat the 3-second figure as a rough one.

**Does the armour reduce incoming damage?** This one is genuinely unsettled:

| Source | On damage reduction |
|---|---|
| Fandom (items list) | "reduces damage players receive" — but gives no number |
| 99-nights-in-the-forest.com | "Damage reduction: Medium-High" |
| Deltia's Gaming | "Poison Armor doesn't reduce incoming damage, so it's less defensive than armors like Iron Body or Alien Armor" |

Two of three say it reduces damage. Nobody publishes a number for how much. Deltia's is the more specific claim and the odd one out, so if you are theory-crafting, the reasonable reading is "some reduction, amount unknown" — not "a Iron Body in green" and not "paper." What we would not do is pick a side and present it as fact.

**How it differs from the Thorn Body.** Both counterattack, but they counterattack on different clocks. Fandom's armour page puts the Thorn Body as redirecting "a small portion of damage to the attacker when the wearer is hit with a melee attack," while the Poison Armour inflicts a poison DoT. Thorn is a small immediate refund; Poison is a delayed tick that keeps working after the attacker has already backed off. Thorn is the simpler defensive armour, Poison is the one that scales — and it is the only one of the two that the Poison Master's levels touch.

**The catch:** because the trigger is melee, the Poison Armour does nothing about anything that shoots at you. The same wiki entry that rates the reduction says the armour "offers no protection against ranged attacks, so be cautious around archers." Combine that with a slow-reloading weapon and the class's weakness stops being abstract.

100 then 250 poisoned kills. The ratios behind that pair, across all twenty class counters, are in our [class levels guide](/guides/class-levels).

## The Three Levels, Line by Line

| Level | Requirement | Perk |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | None on spawn | "Your poison effects are stronger" |
| **2** | Kill enemies that are poisoned: **0 / 100** | "25% chance for enemies to spread their poison on death" |
| **3** | Kill enemies that are poisoned: **0 / 250** (one source: **0 / 200**) | "Attacks deal increased damage to enemies afflicted with poison" |

Two things stand out.

**First: the counter is on kills, not on applications.** You cannot poison your way to Level 3 — the enemy has to actually die while poisoned. That means every point of progress requires finishing what you poisoned, which interacts badly with the reload window and well with the Poison Armour (anything that dies to your own counterattack *while poisoned* still counts, and a swarm chewing on you counts for you).

**Second: the counter is heavily back-loaded.** Level 2 costs 100. Level 3 costs 250. The last level is **2.5× the first one**, so almost every Poison Master you meet who is still Level 2 or below is not a bad run — they are one standard grind away from max, and that grind is where players quit the class. Droid Gamers frames the same thing as the class's cost: "easy to level up compared to other high-tier Classes," but it requires killing **350 poisoned enemies in total** between Level 1 and Level 3.

Neither the Fandom wiki, Sportskeeda, Droid Gamers, nor the other guide publishes a poison damage figure for the Blowpipe that we are willing to repeat. Every detailed number in circulation for the poison itself traces back to a single background wiki we treat as unreliable on class mechanics (see below). **The poison's damage is not published. Do not let anyone sell you a damage-per-second table for it.**

Dying is what this level is built around, so it is worth knowing what dying actually does. See [**Death and Respawn**](/guides/death-and-respawn).

## Level 2 Is a Death Trigger, Not a Steady Area Effect

This is the most-misread perk on the class, and the difference matters for how you fight.

The wording that repeats across Droid Gamers, Sportskeeda and the background wiki is the same: **25% chance for enemies to spread their poison on death.** Droid Gamers renders it as "Defeated enemies have a 25% chance to spread their poison effect on death to nearby mobs."

A minority read it as a continuous effect — the Russian-language translation of the guide list simply says "poisoned enemies have a 25% chance to spread poison," with no death condition, and one character profile site gives it a 5-metre radius and says the poison "retains full potency" and can chain. That version would make the Poison Master a genuine crowd-clear machine.

The death-conditional version is the one published by the largest number of independent guides, and it has an important consequence: **poison does not walk to your next victim. It only jumps when a poisoned enemy dies.** So the chain does not happen while you are shooting — it happens at the moment of each kill, at a 25% rate, one hop at a time. In a tight wolf pack where everything dies at once, that averages out to roughly one extra infected enemy per four kills. In a fight where enemies scatter, it is close to nothing.

Plan around that and Level 2 stops being a letdown: it rewards fights you win fast and close, in packs, and it does nothing for you in a long sniping duel.

That team amp is what puts the Poison Master in a boss fight rather than a solo one, per our [Bosses](/guides/boss-guide) page.

## Level 3 Makes This a Team Class

"Attacks deal increased damage to enemies afflicted with poison" reads like a selfish damage perk. It is not.

Every source carries the same phrasing, and the deepest version of it — from the background wiki — spells out the part the short versions hide: poisoned enemies "take increased damage from ALL sources," "become vulnerable to teammates' attacks," and "are significantly easier to kill during boss fights."

That is a team buff. Your job at Level 3 is not to be the person landing the biggest hits; it is to be the person who makes everything else land harder. Two carry implications:

- **A poisoned Frog King dies faster to a [Katana](/weapons/how-to-get-katana) than to more Blowpipe darts**, because the buff applies to melee too.
- **In a solo run you are denied your own best use of the perk.** Nothing applies the buff to you if nothing else is hitting the target. Level 3 is worth more with a partner than alone — which is the opposite of how most 200-Diamond classes behave.

Sportskeeda's patch notes go further and call the armour comparable to the [Alien Armour](/items/how-to-get-alien-armour) that the [Cyborg](/classes/cyborg-class) spawns with. That is one source's opinion, stated flatly, and we would not bank on it — but it does tell you where the community's expectation for this class sits.

The page we do not use: **99nightsintheforest.wiki** prices the Poison Master at 100 Diamonds, invents a blowpipe of "10 initial damage + 5-second poison (6 damage/sec)," a Level 2 and Level 3 gated on XP rather than kills, and a Level 3 that boosts blowpipe base damage by 20%. It is the only source that contradicts the price, and the perks it describes do not match the other four. We list it here so you can see what we rejected rather than take it on trust.

## How to Actually Play It

**The core loop.** Poison, then finish. Blowpipe to apply, melee to close the kill, repeat. The reload is the gap and the Poison Armour is what you survive the gap with.

**Grinding the counter.** Sportskeeda is straightforward about it: "Farming frogs is the best way to level up this Class. Such enemies only deal five damage per hit and are slower than wolves, bears, and [arctic foxes](/entities/the-arctic-fox)." That matches our own reading of the frogs — see [The Frog](/entities/the-frog) — and it doubles as the Poison Armour's showcase, since frogs attacking you is exactly the counterattack you want.

**Our reading, flagged as such:** because the counter only counts *poisoned* kills, and the Poison Armour poisons *every* melee attacker that connects, a nest of melee enemies is also your counter. Standing inside a frog wave lets their own attacks poison them, and their deaths count. That is our inference from the trigger plus the counter — no source states it — but it follows directly from the two mechanics.

**Where the counterattack does not cover you.** Anything that shoots. The [Cultist](/entities/the-cultist) stronghold mixes melee with elites that keep their distance, and a Poison Master who only trades at range is trading with one hand tied behind their back. There is no second armour to swap to — the Poison Armour, like every armour in the game, cannot come off, and it can only be replaced by something strictly better. The [Riot Shield](/items/how-to-get-riot-shield) is the holdable option if you need to buy space during reloads.

**Upgrades worth pairing with it.** Fandom's own tip for the armour is to pair it with poison sources — it names the Poison Spear in our [own guide](/weapons/how-to-get-poison-spear) and the Blowpipe. It also names a **Poison Claws** item in its tips; we do not have a page for it and have not verified its behaviour, so we are only recording the name.

## Where It Wins, Where It Falls Apart

| Situation | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Frog swarms, [Frog King](/entities/the-frog-king) | **Wins hard** | Melee-only attackers feed the counterattack, the counter, and Level 2's spread at once |
| Wolf packs, [Scorpion](/entities/the-scorpion) nests | **Wins** | Anything walking into you eats poison; the class is built for this |
| Team boss fights | **Wins** | Level 3's damage amp applies to your teammates' weapons, not just yours |
| Ranged pressure, archers | **Loses** | Poison Armour ignores ranged attacks entirely |
| Fast enemies that kill inside a reload | **Loses** | Three darts and a slow reload is not burst defence |
| Solo runs, no melee in the second slot | **Mediocre** | Half the loop is missing and Level 3 has nobody to buff but you |
| Endgame after you can craft [Thorn Body](/items/how-to-get-thorn-body) | **Changes** | Thorn counters too, and it does not wait for a reload — but it does not scale with levels |

That last row is worth pausing on. The Poison Master's armour is the best *starting* armour in the game for a swarm-heavy run, but it is also replaceable, and when it is replaced you lose the only scaleable part of the kit. If you buy the class for the armour rather than the poison, the armour stops mattering the moment you find something better.

## Poison Master Against the Other 200-Diamond Options

At 200 Diamonds the shop forces a choice, and the two other names at that price are meaningfully different:

- **Blacksmith** — per Droid Gamers and Sportskeeda, it is the crafting-bench option with no combat value. If your run is plan-driven rather than fight-driven, that is the trade. See the [Blacksmith class page](/classes/blacksmith-class) — and note that it trades away precisely the thing this class is built on.
- **Fire Bandit** — the other kind of 200-Diamond purchase: it hands you an [Infernal Crossbow](/weapons/how-to-get-infernal-crossbow) and a Wildfire, and its perks hang off the weapons *other* than that crossbow. Same price, immediate ranged damage, no poison synergy either way. The counter arithmetic is on the [Fire Bandit class page](/classes/fire-bandit-class); the comparison itself is in the FAQ below.

And the comparison players actually ask about: the Poison Master is the anti-crowd class, while the [Vampire](/classes/vampire-class) is the anti-damage-taken class and the [Necromancer](/classes/necromancer-class) is the anti-death class. If the problem is "too many things are hitting me at once," Poison Master. If the problem is "I keep dying to one big thing," that is a different purchase.

## Is It Worth 200 Diamonds?

**Yes, with one condition: you have to want to fight in melee range.** The class is not a ranged class despite owning a ranged weapon. Its defence is a melee-triggered counterattack, its damage window is three shots at a time, and its entire scaling depends on enemies dying while poisoned. Played as a sniper, it is the worst version of itself; played as a poison-baiting frontliner with a melee weapon in the other hand, it is the best value in the 200-Diamond slot.

Sportskeeda calls it "a meta for both solo and team runs" and says it is "easier to level up compared to other high-tier Classes." Droid Gamers is more measured: "200 Diamonds is quite the steep price," but you get both items "with relative ease" by defeating the Frog King. Both are true — the class is cheap in diamonds and expensive in patience.

The honest risk column: the poison's damage is not published anywhere we trust, so **you cannot compute this class's real damage output before buying it**. What you can predict is the mechanism, the trigger, and the shape of the grind. If being good at the mechanism matters more than a number on a stat panel, 200 Diamonds buys it.

## Tips That Change How It Feels

- **Carry a melee weapon from the first minute.** The Blowpipe applies; the melee finishes. Without the second weapon the class has a hole in it.
- **Reload during movement, not during standing still.** The reload is slow and continues whether or not the weapon is equipped, so swapping weapons mid-fight keeps poison applied and lets you cover the gap.
- **Do not re-shoot a target that is already poisoned unless the tick has expired.** One source we treat as unreliable insists poison overwrites rather than stacks; that specific claim is unverified, but overwriting is the safer assumption to play against.
- **Save the swarm fights for when you are poison-armoured.** The counterattack and the Level 2 spread both need bodies standing next to you, and Level 3 needs a poisoned boss with a teammate nearby.
- **Chase Level 2 before you chase anything else.** It is 100 kills and it is the perk that turns poison into crowd control. Level 3 is 250 and saves itself for the runs where you are still alive that deep.

## Pros and Cons

**Pros**
- 200 Diamonds for both a ranged weapon and a working defensive armour
- Unlimited reserve ammo — no hunting for ammo boxes mid-fight
- Poison Armour punishes every melee attacker, which is the class's answer to the reload gap
- Level 3 buffs *your* damage and your teammates' at the same time
- Best level-3 requirement-to-power ratio of any class we have priced, if you can finish the counter
- Odds-on best class in the game for [Frog Cave](/locations/frog-cave) runs

**Cons**
- Three shots per reload on a slow cycle, in a game where wolves do not wait
- Poison Armour does nothing about ranged attackers
- Level 2's spread only fires on death — it is a bonus, not an area effect
- The grind is 350 poisoned kills, and the last 250 are most of the work
- Level 3 has little solo value
- Poison damage is unpublished, so you cannot benchmark it against other classes

## FAQ

**Is the Blowpipe really unlimited?**
It has unlimited *reserve* ammo — you never run out of darts — but the magazine holds three and the reload is slow. Several guides write "unlimited ammo" as though it means infinite output. It does not.

**Does the Poison Armour count as armour for tier purposes?**
It is one of the seven wearable armours, it cannot be removed, and it can only be replaced by a strictly better piece. Exactly where it sits in the tier order depends on which source you ask — see the disagreement table above. Note that a Poison Master who upgrades to another armour loses the poison scaling.

**Can you level the class without killing anything?**
No. Both requirements are "kill enemies while they are poisoned."

**Does the armour's poison benefit from the class levels?**
Unconfirmed. One site says yes and that it can spread with the rest. Single source, no corroboration, and it is the claim that decides how strong the class gets late.

**Is Poison Master better than Fire Bandit?**
Different jobs. Fire Bandit hands you an immediate strong ranged weapon; Poison Master hands you a defensive counterattack and a slow-poisoning ranged opener. For a run where you want to shoot your way out, the Fire Bandit's crossbow is more immediately useful — see the [Fire Bandit class page](/classes/fire-bandit-class) for its side of that trade. For a run where enemies come to you, the Poison Master is the better engine.

**Should I buy it just to get the Poison Armour?**
That is the single most common reason people buy it, and it is a legitimate one — the class is the fastest guaranteed route to the armour. But understand that you are buying the class, not the armour: the Poison Armour's counterattack is the main thing keeping you alive through slow reloads, so replacing it does not solve your problem, it removes your safety net.
