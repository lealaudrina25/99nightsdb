# Big Game Hunter Class in 99 Nights in the Forest

The Big Game Hunter is the most self-funded class in the game. It costs **600 Diamonds**, and it does not hand you a weapon to win with. It hands you a **Pelt List** — a menu of nine rows — and then lets you spend the animals you already have to kill into stats that stay with you for the rest of the run.

That is the whole class, and it is also the reason the page is worth reading. Almost every guide that covers this class lists the perks as three short lines ("can consume pelts", "higher pelt drop rate", "infinite mammoth tusks") and stops there. The numbers people actually need — how many of each pelt, which ones are locked to a biome, which row is level-gated — are scattered across three wikis that do not agree with each other.

This page collects all nine rows, prints the disagreement rather than averaging it, and separates what is published from what is arithmetic we did ourselves.

## Quick Answer

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| **What it is** | A self-scaling class. It grows with what you hunt, not with what you loot |
| **Cost** | **600 Diamonds** — every source agrees |
| **Availability** | The Class Shop, and it is only there when the shop is stocked. See [All Classes](/classes/all-classes) for how the shop works |
| **Starter kit** | **1 Rifle + 12 Rifle ammo + a Pelt List** |
| **Level 1** | You can consume animal pelts for permanent bonuses |
| **Level 2** | Higher pelt drop rate — needs **70 wolves killed and 50 pelts used** |
| **Level 3** | Infinite Mammoth Tusk consumption for permanent health — needs **150 wolves killed and 100 pelts used** |
| **How many Pelt List rows?** | **Nine.** That is our count of the published rows, and three sources list different numbers |
| **Biome-gated rows?** | **Yes.** Three of the nine only appear in a Snow or Volcano run |
| **Worth it?** | See [Is It Worth 600 Diamonds](#is-it-worth-600-diamonds). Sources genuinely disagree |

Big Game Hunter sells you the right to hunt; [Kiwi](/entities/the-kiwi) is the one animal in the game where hunting is not the cost and the campfire is.

The pelts this class trades away all come from animals that also drop Steak, and our [Steak](/items/steak) page lists the count each one drops.

## What You Are Actually Buying

The **600 Diamond** price is stated identically by the fandom wiki, RBLXGuide, Sportskeeda and Gamermovil. It puts the class in the joint-most-expensive tier, alongside the [Cyborg](/classes/cyborg-class), the [Necromancer](/classes/necromancer-class) and the [Vampire](/classes/vampire-class).

Two sources add the detail most guides skip: the class is bought from the **Class Shop**, and it is only available when the shop is carrying it. That means the 600 Diamonds is the price of gaining access to the class the next time it rotates in, not the price of owning it forever.

To assemble 600 Diamonds, the routes our own [Diamonds page](/items/how-to-get-diamonds-fast) covers are the ones sources name: raiding the [Cultist Stronghold](/locations/cultist-stronghold), completing badges, and opening high-rarity chests. One guide adds a specific figure — roughly **5 Diamonds per Stronghold run** — but that is a single source and we do not carry the number.

You also spawn with a **Rifle and 12 rounds of Rifle ammo**. That is not much. It is the subject of a section below, because this class has a longer ammo problem than its price suggests.

Fifty pelts is the Hunter event badge; the pelt table behind it, and the Hunting badge it completes, are cross-checked on our [All Badges](/guides/all-badges) page.

The Arctic Fox pelt sits at the top of that chain and arrives behind a [Chili](/items/chili) bill, priced on our Chili page.

Chili only ever arrives from a Seed Pack, as our [Seed Packs](/items/seed-packs) page explains.
Three [Bunny](/entities/the-bunny) feet at the top of that list buy the 15% sprint, and our Bunny page is the farm that keeps them coming.

That Bunny Foot row is the fastest sprint in the game, a claim our [Sprint Speed](/guides/sprint-speed) guide makes against the Assassin.

## The Pelt List, Row by Row

This is the core of the class and the thing nobody collects in one place. The fandom wiki is the only source we found that prints a full row set; Sportskeeda and Backyard Drunkard both publish a shorter version, and they are missing three of the nine.

| Row | How many | What it gives | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bunny Foot** | **3** | Sprint **15% faster** | Bunnies, anywhere |
| **Wolf Pelt** | **3** | **+5% damage** | Wolves, anywhere |
| **Arctic Fox Pelt** | **3** | Permanent warmth, stacks with warm clothing | **Snow Biome only** |
| **Alpha Wolf Pelt** | **3** | **10% chance** ammo is refunded on a shot | Alpha wolves, anywhere |
| **Bear Pelt** | **5** | **+25 HP** permanently | Bears, anywhere |
| **Polar Bear Pelt** | **3** (2 sources) / **5** (1 source) | **+10% damage** | **Snow Biome only** |
| **Mammoth Tusk** | **1 each, no limit** | **+5 HP** per tusk, stacking | [The Mammoth](/entities/the-mammoth), deep in the snow |
| **Scorpion Shell** | **10** | Attacks have a **15% chance to poison** | [The Scorpion](/entities/the-scorpion), **Volcano Biome only** |
| **Cultist King Antler** | **1** | **+10% damage** | [The Cultist King](/entities/the-cultist-king) |

Two rows deserve their own treatment because they are the ones that quietly decide whether a run was worth starting.

### Mammoth Tusks Are the Whole Late Game

The Mammoth Tusk row is the only row with **no cap**. Every tusk you eat is +5 max HP, and the fandom wiki's own strategy section says this is the class's ceiling: for a run of hundreds of days, hunting [Mammoths](/entities/the-mammoth) for tusks can make you close to unkillable — at the cost of being slow to set up. Nothing else on this class scales without limit, which is why guides describe reaching Level 3 as the moment the class stops being average.

Note the level gate: the fandom wiki marks the tusk row as **Level 3 exclusive**. Before Level 3, it is not on the list.

### Three Rows Are Biome-Locked, and That Is the Real Cost

Of the nine rows, **three only exist in a specific biome**:

- **Arctic Fox Pelt** and **Polar Bear Pelt** — Snow Biome
- **Scorpion Shell** — Volcano Biome

So the effective Pelt List in any given run is **six to eight rows**, not nine. A Snow run gives you all nine except the Scorpion Shell. A Volcano run gives you all nine except the two Snow rows. A Jungle or normal-Forest run gives you **six** — the fandom wiki lists the Jungle outcome explicitly as a downside of the class.

This is why the class's value is a variable, not a constant, and why "is it worth 600 Diamonds" has a different answer depending on the biome you spawn into.

The **Cultist King Antler** row is the loosest of the three. It is not marked as biome-locked, but it is also the row that appears on only one source — see the disagreement section below.

70 wolves and 50 pelts, then 150 and 100. Every other class's Level 2 and Level 3 sit beside those in our [class levels guide](/guides/class-levels).

## What Each Level Costs

The perks are agreed. The requirements are printed by only two of the guides we found, and they disagree about which level owns which.

| Level | Perk | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Consume animal pelts for permanent bonuses | None published |
| **2** | Higher pelt drop rate | **70 wolves killed, 50 pelts used** |
| **3** | Infinite Mammoth Tusk consumption for permanent health | **150 wolves killed, 100 pelts used** |

**Our arithmetic:** those two rungs add up to **220 wolves killed and 150 pelts consumed** before the class is maxed. We print it because the two requirements are published separately by two guides and never added up anywhere, and because it makes the comparison with the cheap [Hunter class](/classes/hunter-class) honest — our Hunter page works out that class at roughly 610 animals total, so the cheaper class is the one that grinds longer.

Worth saying plainly: **doing nothing to level the class still leaves you with a Rifle and 12 rounds.** Every one of these buffs is something you go and earn.

## "Permanent" Means One Run

Every source uses the word **permanent** for these buffs, and every source leaves it undefined.

The only source that defines it — and we flag it rather than carry it — says the buffs last for the duration of your current run and do not carry into a new game. That is consistent with how the counters behave in practice: the kill numbers and the eaten-pelt number are per-run, so the buffs they unlock behave the same way.

We state that as a reading of undefined wording, not as a published fact. No source we accept says it outright.

The three ways out of an ammo drought are all cheaper than they look once our [Ammo and Guns](/guides/ammo-guide) guide prices them per round.


Twelve rounds per Rifle magazine is the class's weak point, and the sources say so independently.

**The three published outs, in rough order of value:**

1. **The Alpha Wolf Pelt row.** A 10% chance that a shot refunds its own ammo. That is the row that turns a 12-round gun into a sustainable one, and it is the row most guides skip past.
2. **The Volcanic Biome ammo furnace.** If you spawn in the Volcano, fuel-fed furnaces produce boxes of ammo. fandom names this as the counter to ammo scarcity.
3. **The Ammo Crate** at [Crafting Bench](/locations/crafting-bench) tier 4, and fishing, which occasionally turns up ammo boxes.

The honest summary: the class is strongest if you can convert 12 rounds into a lot of shots. If you cannot, the run becomes a melee run.

## Where This Class Fights Itself and Your Team

The fandom wiki's own cons list is unusually blunt about this, and it is the part new buyers miss.

**Against it, in a team: the Pelt Trader.** The [Pelt Trader](/items/pelt-trader) wants the same pelts this class eats. For good items, teammates need pelts you consume. The wiki says the class does not work well with others — especially in four- and five-player runs — and that the higher pelt drop rate can be spent the other way round, feeding teammates so they can complete trades.

**It is a solo class that happens to work in duos and trios.** That is the fandom wiki's framing and we keep it.

**Against itself: the grind.** Getting all nine rows is time-consuming even with the Level 2 drop-rate perk, and that makes the class average-to-weak early. One guide's diagnosis is that most of the class's value arrives late, which is the same verdict our [Hunter page](/classes/hunter-class) reaches from the other direction.

## How to Level It Without Dreading It

The fandom wiki's tips section has a small set of practical routes, and unlike most tips they are consistent with its own numbers:

- **The fast Level 1 to Level 3 route** is several runs with a *small campfire*, clearing the bunnies and wolves near camp, and not expanding into the Alpha wolf or bear areas. That is a deliberate decision to farm the cheap counters and skip the expensive ones.
- **For the wolf counter specifically**, stand on a tree branch or another elevated camping spot and shoot down at the wolves that spawn at night.
- **Three Bunny Feet beat the Assassin's sprint**, with no health penalty attached. That is the fandom wiki's line and it is the cheapest way this class shows off.
- **Biome detection is free:** the Pelt List tells you at the start of a run whether you are in Snow or Volcano, and so does the back of the missing kids noticeboard. This matters, because it decides whether three of nine rows are reachable.

## Where Sources Disagree

Four genuine conflicts. We print them rather than pick one, because in two of the four cases the evidence is close.

**1. Which level owns which perk — one rung apart.** The fandom wiki, Sportskeeda and RBLXGuide all put the higher pelt drop rate at Level 2 and the infinite tusk consumption at Level 3. Backyard Drunkard shifts both up one: it gives Level 2 the drop rate and Level 3 the tusks, with the requirement numbers attached to those rungs. **The requirement numbers are identical in both readings — 70 wolves / 50 pelts, then 150 wolves / 100 pelts. Only the level labels move.** We go with the three-source reading because it is three against one, and because our own Hunter page treats the tusks as the Level 3 payoff.

**2. Polar Bear Pelt: 3 or 5?** Two independent sources (the fandom wiki and Backyard Drunkard) say **3**. One — Sportskeeda, reprinted near-verbatim by Sports Rant — says **5**. That is 2 against 1, so the table carries 3.

**3. Are three rows biome-locked?** Only the fandom wiki gates the Arctic Fox Pelt, Polar Bear Pelt and Scorpion Shell to Snow and Volcano. Sportskeeda and Backyard Drunkard list those same pelts with no biome restriction, which implies they are grabbable in any run. We carry the fandom wiki's version because it is the only one that says anything at all, and because the biome check it describes is a real in-game screen.

**4. The Cultist King Antler row.** It appears on the fandom wiki alone, in a list where every other row has at least one second source. It is therefore single-source and flagged as such. If you are planning a route around that +10% damage, know that nothing corroborates it.

**One source we do not rely on at all** publishes this class at **300 Diamonds**, with a four-star rating and an entirely different perk list — Precision Shot, Track Prey, Forest Camouflage, Kill Shot, Hunter's Mark, plus a Compound Bow, a Hunting Knife, a Trap Kit and Scouting Binoculars, and a Level 2 requirement of 0/70 wolves that is confusingly close to the real one. Not one of those abilities appears in any other source we found, and the price is contradicted by four. We mention it only so you recognise it if you hit it.

## Is It Worth 600 Diamonds?

**For it,** per the fandom wiki and Gamermovil: strong solo, decent in duos and trios with the right partners, excellent for long runs and leaderboard grinding. It spawns with a genuinely good ranged weapon. Every perk is different from the last, and together they let it beat other classes at their own jobs — faster than the [Assassin](/classes/assassin-class) on sprint, tankier than the [Brawler](/classes/brawler-class) on health.

**Against it:** the cost, the early-game weakness, and the pelt competition with your own team. Gamermovil rates it S-tier; the fandom wiki's own con list is longer than its pro list.

**Our read:** this is the class to buy if you intend to play single runs deep into the hundreds of days, and the class to skip if you play five-player lobbies, because in five-player lobbies you will be eating pelts your teammates needed. That is our synthesis of the published pros and cons, not a source's verdict.

For the 600-Diamond tier as a whole, see [Best Class](/classes/best-class). For the cheap alternative that does a different-but-related job, see [Hunter Class](/classes/hunter-class) — our Hunter page already runs the full side-by-side.

## FAQ

**How much does the Big Game Hunter cost?**
**600 Diamonds.** Stated identically by four independent sources.

**Do you get the rifle every run?**
Yes — **1 Rifle and 12 Rifle ammo**, plus the Pelt List each time. The Pelt List is not a permanent item you own; it is what the class brings.

**How many rows does the Pelt List have?**
**Nine** published rows in the fullest source. In a given run you can only reach **six to eight** of them, because three rows are locked to the Snow and Volcano biomes.

**Can I eat pelts before Level 3?**
The fandom wiki marks the **Mammoth Tusk** row as Level 3 exclusive. Whether the other rows are gated is not stated by any source we accept.

**What unlocks Level 2 and Level 3?**
Level 2 needs **70 wolves killed and 50 pelts used**; Level 3 needs **150 wolves killed and 100 pelts used**. Both are single-guide figures, and the two guides that print them disagree about which rung they belong to.

**Does the class work in a team?**
The fandom wiki says no, especially at four and five players, because of the Pelt Trader competition. It suggests the opposite use in team runs — feed your teammates the pelts.

**What is the fastest way to max it?**
Clear bunnies and wolves around a small campfire across several runs without expanding into Alpha wolf or bear territory, and shoot down night wolves from an elevated spot. That is the fandom wiki's tip, not ours.
