The **Lumberjack** is the quiet workhorse class of 99 Nights in the Forest. For **70 Diamonds** it starts you with a **Good Axe** and turns wood — the resource the whole game runs on — from a bottleneck into a surplus. This page covers its cost, its L1–L3 perks, its role, and whether it is the right first Diamond spend.

## The Perks, Level by Level

- **Level 1 — 20% bonus Log.** Every tree you fell has a 1-in-5 chance to drop an extra Log. Small on its own, but it compounds across a whole run.
- **Level 2 — 25% double Sapling.** A quarter of your trees drop *two* saplings instead of one. This is the sustainability perk: your sapling farm refills itself faster than you plant.
- **Level 3 — 20% 1–2 bonus Logs.** The L1 bonus returns, now 1–2 Logs instead of one. Late-game, this is a meaningful per-swing bump.

These line up exactly with what [How to Get Wood Fast](/items/how-to-get-wood-fast) describes: at full level, a 25% double-sapling chance and bonus logs of 1–2.

## Why Wood Matters

Wood feeds the campfire, and the campfire's level gates the map. More wood → higher fire → deeper terrain → better chests and axes. The Lumberjack attacks that loop at the source: it makes the bottleneck (wood) simply go away, so the rest of the team can build and explore instead of foraging.

It is also one of the **cheaper classes** (70 Diamonds sits in the mid tier), which is why it is such a common first pick. See [How to Get Diamonds Fast](/items/how-to-get-diamonds-fast) for the Diamonds, and [Best Class](/classes/best-class) for the full ranking.

## The Trade-Off

Lumberjack has **no combat ability** and levels slowly. It is a specialist: on a team it shines when someone else fights and it keeps the base supplied; solo it is fine too, because wood scarcity is the most common early-game wall and Lumberjack erases it. If you want to fight, look at [Best Class](/classes/best-class) instead — but most runs want at least one wood specialist keeping the fire fed.
