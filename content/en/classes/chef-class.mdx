# Chef and Cook in 99 Nights in the Forest

Two classes in this game sell you the same promise: food that does more than stop your hunger bar emptying. One charges **40 Diamonds** and works through a pot that already exists. The other charges **150 Diamonds** and brings its own station. They share a family, they share a mechanic, and almost every guide on the internet folds them into one subsection.

This page separates them, prices them honestly, and walks the whole food chain — raw meat, the campfire, the Crock Pot, the Chef's Station, and the six recipes that come off it.

Last verified: **2026-10-04**.

Raw Steak is one of the three meats that hurt you before you can cook it, and our [Steak](/items/steak) page is where the raw and cooked fork values sit next to the dish it becomes.

## Quick Answer

| | |
|---|---|
| **Chef** | **150 Diamonds** |
| **Cook** | **40 Diamonds** |
| **Chef starting items** | 1× Chef's Station Blueprint, 1× Recipe Book |
| **Cook starting item** | 1× Seasoning |
| **Chef's signature** | A station of its own, plus recipes nobody else can cook |
| **Cook's signature** | Every cooked dish restores more hunger, permanently, from level 1 |
| **Both buy you** | Food that scales with the run instead of food that only fills a bar |
| **Both fail at** | Solo play. Every current source says so in as many words |
| **Best with** | Hunter, Farmer, Fisherman — all three exist to hand you ingredients |

If you own only one of them and you play alone, you probably own the wrong one. The **Cook line is the cheaper, quieter, single-player-friendly half**; the Chef is a four-player decision dressed up as a class.

## The Two Classes Are One System, Split in Two

The food economy in this game has three tiers of cooking, and the two classes sit on different rungs:

1. **The campfire** — cooks raw meat into cooked meat, nothing more. Every run has one.
2. **The Crock Pot** — a bench item (Tier 3 on the **[Crafting Bench](/locations/crafting-bench)**) that turns three ingredients into a Stew. The **[Cook](/guides/campfire) class makes this station faster and occasionally luckier.**
3. **The Chef's Station** — a separate, class-locked station that produces dishes with timed buffs. **Only the Chef can build it or cook from it.**

That third rung is the whole reason the Chef exists. The Cook improves tier two. The Chef is tier three.

The important consequence: **the Cook does not remove the need for the Chef, and the Chef does not replace the Crock Pot so much as outrank it.** One source puts it plainly — the Chef's Station cooks faster than the Crock Pot, which means a Chef running their own station has good reasons to never build a pot at all.

## Cook: 40 Diamonds, and What Forty Actually Buys

The Cook is one of the oldest classes in the game — one source notes it was among the first batch added in the Classes Update, which is why so many beginner guides mention it without ever explaining it.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cost** | 40 Diamonds |
| **Starting item** | 1× Seasoning |
| **Level 1** | Seasoned food restores more hunger |
| **Level 2** | Crock Pots cook 20% faster |
| **Level 3** | 25% chance to produce a Hearty Stew |
| **Level 2 requirement** | 200 meat cooked, 50 stews cooked |
| **Level 3 requirement** | 400 meat cooked, 200 stews cooked |
| **Difficulty** | Beginner to intermediate |
| **Type** | Support / food utility |

### The perks, one at a time

**Level 1 — Seasoned food restores more hunger.** This is the only perk that fires in solo play, and it is the reason the class is worth 40 at all. Seasoning is a starter item, so you own this on day one with zero grinding. The effect is qualitative: seasoned dishes fill more of the hunger bar than the unseasoned version of the same dish. No source gives a percentage, which we flag below.

**Level 2 — Crock Pots cook 20% faster.** In raw numbers, the Crock Pot's cycle goes from about **10 seconds down to 8**. Be honest about what that is worth: every source that discusses it reaches the same conclusion, which is that **food output is bottlenecked by how many ingredients you own, not by how fast the pot runs**. The perk is a convenience. It becomes noticeable only when you run several pots side by side or cook in bulk for a four-person camp.

**Level 3 — 25% chance of a Hearty Stew.** Cooking an ordinary Stew has a one-in-four chance of producing a Hearty Stew instead, which heals about **15 health**. That is a small heal, and the sources are blunt about it: decent, not useful, except in **[Hard Mode](/guides/hard-mode)** where free healing is scarce enough that 15 HP genuinely matters.

200 meat and 50 stews, then 400 and 200. Both cooking ladders, and the Level 2 and Level 3 of all twenty classes, are on one table in our [class levels guide](/guides/class-levels).

### How the Cook levels

Two mechanics that are not obvious:

- **An auto-cooking setup still counts**, provided the Cook personally drops the ingredients in. You build the loop, it runs, but your hand has to go in the pot at least once per cycle.
- **Cooking raw meat at the campfire first speeds up the counter.** The "meat cooked" track is fed by fire-cooked meat, so pre-cooking raw drops at the fire is the shortcut.

The requirements are long. One source calls the Cook one of the slower classes to level, and lists the third tier as genuinely hard to finish inside a normal run.

## The Chef's Station: Blueprint, Materials, Prerequisites

The Chef does not spawn holding a built station. They spawn holding a **blueprint** and a **recipe book**, and the station is a construction project with gates on it.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Item** | Chef's Station Blueprint |
| **Rarity** | 3 stars |
| **Ranked** | A tier |
| **Price listed** | 60 Diamonds |
| **Class gate** | Chef only |
| **Camp gate** | Base Camp Level 2 |
| **Run gate** | Survive 15 nights |
| **Materials** | 25 Wood, 15 Stone, 5 Rope, 1 Crude Tools, 1 functional Campfire nearby |
| **Build time** | 2.5 minutes, once the materials are in |
| **Blueprint category** | Crafting Blueprint — places a crafting station at your Campfire |
| **Main advantage** | +15% food restoration, buffed dishes, faster than a Crock Pot, several dishes at once, lower raw-food poisoning risk |
| **Main weakness** | Expensive to set up, destroyable in a night raid, useless in a fight |

Two things in that table deserve attention.

**The 60 versus the 150.** The blueprint carries a separate listed price of 60 Diamonds, and the class costs 150. Whether that 60 is a real purchase, a bench-tier cost, or a legacy number the site never updated is not stated anywhere we can find — treated as an open question below.

**The three gates.** The station is not simply "unlock Chef and build it." It also wants **Base Camp Level 2** and **survived 15 nights**. If you are building on day 3, you cannot place it yet, and no source explains what happens to a Chef who reaches level 3 before night 15 — the recipe book presumably unlocks the knowledge, but the station itself stays unavailable.

### Where it goes

The station must sit near a working campfire, which is the same constraint most base furniture obeys. One community trick is worth stealing: **embed the Chef's Station halfway into the front wall of a Wood Rain Storage, seal the opening with a log wall or similar, and fill the storage with ingredients.** Items fall into the station and it stacks finished meals. Manually taking the finished dishes out speeds the process up.

That is the closest this game gets to a passive kitchen, and it is the single most useful Chef tip in circulation.

Jar o' Jelly is a full heal with five minutes of speed — the strongest heal in the game that is not a drop; our [Healing, Bandages and Medkits](/guides/healing-guide) guide ranks it against the rest.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Cost** | 150 Diamonds |
| **Starting items** | 1× Chef's Station Blueprint, 1× Recipe Book |
| **Level 1** | New unique recipes with large bonuses; all cooked items seasoned |
| **Level 2** | Unlocks a new recipe |
| **Level 3** | Unlocks a new recipe |
| **Level 2 requirement** | 200 food cooked, 50 special dishes cooked |
| **Level 3 requirement** | 400 food cooked, 100 special dishes cooked |
| **Added** | Listed among the five classes from the 27 June 2025 update |
| **Best with** | Hunter, Farmer, Fisherman |

**Level 2 is the Carrot Cake. Level 3 is the Jar o' Jelly.** Those are the two named unlocks, and both are healing dishes — which is the honest shape of the class: two of its three levels buy you food that patches you up rather than food that feeds you.

The upgrade loop has a shortcut the community uses for every cooking class: **cook any meat at the campfire before you cook it on the Chef's Station.** The "food cooked" counter is fed the same way for both stations.

That is more of a [Pumpkin](/items/pumpkin) question than a fuel one, and the crop page carries the yield nobody publishes plus the break-even point between Pumpkin and Cake.

The Carrot Cake at level 2 wants two [Carrot](/items/carrot) and a Cake, and it is the only recipe in the game that adds night vision to a dish.

## Every Chef Recipe, Ingredient by Ingredient

Six dishes are attributed to the Chef across the sources. Four are available from level 1; the other two arrive at levels 2 and 3.

| Dish | Ingredients | Unlocked | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Seafood Chowder** | Fish + Any + Any | Level 1 | Restores about 140 hunger |
| **Steak Dinner** | Steak + Any + Any | Level 1 | Restores about 150 hunger |
| **Pumpkin Soup** | Pumpkin + Any + Any | Level 1 | Keeps you warm for about 2 minutes |
| **BBQ Ribs** | Ribs + Any + Any | Level 1 | Restores hunger, heals some health |
| **Carrot Cake** | Cake + Carrot + Carrot | Level 2 | Night vision ~9 minutes, plus a small heal |
| **Jar o' Jelly** | Jellyfish + Any + Any | Level 3 | Full heal, plus ~5 minutes of speed |

Read the "Any + Any" column honestly. It means two free slots in a three-slot dish, so the class is not as ingredient-starved as the fixed items suggest — Steak Dinner, for instance, costs you one Steak and two things you were eating anyway.

**Pumpkin Soup is the conditional one.** Its warm effect only means anything while the Snow Biome is active. In a run that never enters the cold, that dish is a Steak Dinner with worse flavour.

**The three carryable heals.** Carrot Cake, BBQ Ribs and Jar o' Jelly can all be eaten to heal yourself, and the wiki's advice is to hold them — eat one when you are in danger, not when you are merely hungry. Jar o' Jelly in particular is flagged as slow to make, so it is a stock item, not a snack.

## Passive Seasoning, and Who It Actually Applies To

Seasoning is the thread running through both classes.

- The **Cook** spawns with 1× Seasoning.
- The **Chef's** level-1 perk states that **all cooked items are seasoned**.

Seasoned food restores more hunger than the unseasoned version, and the bonus applies in solo play as well as team play. That is the one claim in this whole system that every source makes without qualification.

The practical read: the Cook's seasoning is a **replaceable consumable-starter**, the Chef's is a **permanent class property**. If you lose your single seasoning jar as a Cook, you have to re-buy or re-find one. As a Chef, every dish leaving the station is seasoned whether or not you had anything in your sack.

The two with this class are the starting points for anything the Chef actually wants to cook, including Seafood Chowder and Jar o' Jelly. The [**Fisherman**](/classes/fisherman-class) is the supply line that keeps both stocked.

Underneath the cooking chain sits the bar it is all filling: roughly a quarter of an hour from full to death, measured in forks. See [**Hunger in 99 Nights in the Forest**](/guides/hunger) for the timings and the full food table.

## Raw Meat, the Campfire, and the Pot Hierarchy

Before either class matters, the base rule:

- **Raw Morsel, raw Steak and raw Rib damage you.** All three hurt when eaten straight from a kill. Cooking them at a campfire removes the damage and roughly doubles their hunger value.
- **The campfire is the only cooker you start with.** See **[Campfire](/guides/campfire)** for the fuel costs that keep it lit.
- **The Crock Pot needs Tier 3 on the bench** and costs 15 Wood and 10 Scrap. It **rejects berries and peppers**.
- **Three of anything goes in.** Three carrots in a Crock Pot is cited as the cheapest, easiest Stew in the game. A Stew made with meat is not vegetarian.
- **Stews equip to your toolbar**, so they ride along without eating your sack slots.

There is a shortcut that bypasses the campfire entirely, and several classes and weapons lean on it: **animals killed by fire drop cooked meat.** The **[Fire Bandit](/classes/fire-bandit-class)** class, the **[Flamethrower](/weapons/how-to-get-flamethrower)**, the **[Infernal Crossbow](/weapons/how-to-get-infernal-crossbow)** and the **[Infernal Sword](/weapons/how-to-get-infernal-sword)** all produce cooked drops, and a weapon with an auto-cook perk cooks anything you drop. The **[Infernal Sack](/items/how-to-get-wood-fast)** also cooks raw food held inside it.

If you are playing a fire class, the Cook's raw-meat-counted progress is easy to grind for free.

Fish belong in that category too: both Seafood Chowder and Jar o' Jelly take the catch of a rod, and no other class produces the raw material. [**Fishing in 99 Nights in the Forest**](/guides/fishing-guide) shows which water holds which fish.

Storm and meteor nights are the reason a stocked kitchen empties, which our [Weather and Night Events](/guides/weather-and-night-events) guide explains.

## Team Play, Solo Play, and the Day-60 Wall

**In a team.** The Chef becomes the camp's kitchen. The correct division of labour is that teammates drop ingredients near the station and never cook themselves, because the Chef converts them more efficiently. The Chef should be cooking for the group, not for themselves.

**In solo play.** Both classes are weak, and for the same reason: nearly every perk in the kit scales with a group or with a station you have to keep alive.

**After Day 60 in Hard Mode, the arithmetic changes sharply.** At the 60% Corruption threshold most stored food rots in about **90 seconds**, even in your sack or hotbar, and cooking does not save it. Map-spawned food never rots. That means stockpiling and cooking lines stop paying off *at the same moment* the two classes get strongest on paper — see **[Hard Mode](/guides/hard-mode)** for the full threshold table. Foraging beats kitchens there, which is the opposite of what the Chef's upgrade list promises.

## Building a Timeline Around the Kitchen

- **Day 1–3.** Eat carrots and berries. Cook raw meat at the fire. Do not worry about either class.
- **Day 4–8.** Build the Crock Pot at bench Tier 3 if you bought the Cook. Start cooking Stews in bulk; they are the first food that scales.
- **Day 8–15.** Survive 15 nights. If you are a Chef, this is when the Base Camp Level 2 and night-15 gate on the Chef's Station can be satisfied. Build it behind a wall.
- **Night 10 at the latest.** One source says the Chef's Station should exist by then, with an "advanced station" upgrade around night 30.
- **Level 2 and 3.** Carrot Cake and Jar o' Jelly arrive, and with them the first genuinely strong healing food in the game.
- **Day 60+.** If Hard Mode is on, expect the kitchen to stop mattering.

## Where Sources Disagree

**1. Two completely different recipe lists.** One source publishes the six-dish table above (Seafood Chowder, Steak Dinner, Pumpkin Soup, BBQ Ribs, Carrot Cake, Jar o' Jelly). Another publishes four different dishes for the same station — Forest Stew (meat ×2, vegetables ×3, water ×1, +40 HP and +10% damage resistance for 2 nights), Hunter's Rations (grains ×2, preserved meat ×1, +25 HP and +15% speed for 1 night), Energizing Brew (herbs ×3, fruits ×2, water ×1, +30 stamina and +5% critical chance for 2 nights), and Survival Feast (meat ×3, vegetables ×3, grains ×2, herbs ×2, +60 HP and +15% to all stats for 3 nights, gated on Chef level 10). The ingredient count differs — four items against three — and not one is shared. We list both rather than pick.

**2. Carrot Cake's heal.** One source says 10 HP. Another says 40. A third does not state a number at all and only says it heals. We cannot resolve this; the 10 figure appears in the recipe table, the 40 in the consumable list.

**3. What the Cook actually spawns with.** Three sources say 1× Seasoning and nothing else. One source covering the "Chef" in detail lists the starting kit as Chef's Station Blueprint, Recipe Book **and** Seasoning, and prices the whole class at 40 Diamonds. Either that page means the Cook and mislabels it, or Chef and Cook share a starter kit. We flag it rather than choose.

**4. The 60 versus the 150.** The Chef's Station Blueprint carries its own listed price of 60 Diamonds while the class is 150. No source states what the 60 buys, whether it is a separate purchase, or whether it is stale.

**5. Hearty Stew's numbers.** Three sources agree it heals about 15 HP and that the Cook's 25% chance is the route to it. One consumable list describes Hearty Stew as +45% hunger and ×5 nutrition with no heal attached. The two descriptions are not compatible.

**6. What a Stew restores.** Half a hunger bar, or +45% hunger, or a four-fork rating. Those are three encodings of roughly the same idea, but only one of them is a number you can plan with.

**7. The Crock Pot's cost.** Listed as 15 Wood + 10 Scrap by most sources; the crafting cheat sheet repeats the line as "15 Wood + 10 Scrap + 15 Wood + 10 Scrap," which reads like a duplicated column header rather than 30 Wood and 20 Scrap.

**8. Carrot Cake's buff length is consistent, its value is not.** All sources agree on roughly 9 minutes of night vision. Whether that is "enormous" or merely good is a disagreement about the player, not the dish.

## FAQ

**Do I need the Chef class to use the Chef's Station?**
Yes. The station is class-locked to the Chef, and its blueprint starts in the Chef's inventory.

**Can I build it without the other gates?**
No. The listing adds Base Camp Level 2 and survived 15 nights on top of owning the Chef.

**Is the Chef or the Cook better?**
Different jobs. The Cook is 40, works with a station you already build, and its level-1 perk helps even alone. The Chef is 150, brings a station, and is the only route to the six buffed dishes and the two carryable heals.

**Does the Crock Pot beat the Chef's Station?**
No. The Chef's Station cooks faster, and it is the only thing that can make the Chef-only recipes.

**Does seasoning work on raw food?**
No. It applies to cooked food.

**Why is my cooking slow to level?**
Both classes feed off "food cooked," and pre-cooking raw meat at the campfire is the fastest known shortcut. Auto-cooking loops count only if the Cook or Chef personally places the ingredients.

**Can I eat raw meat to save time?**
You take damage. It roughly doubles in value once cooked, and the damage is not worth the three seconds you save.

**What happens to my food if Hard Mode turns on?**
At the 60% threshold, most stored food rots in about 90 seconds. The kitchen lines stop paying off and foraging takes over.
