Every other crop in 99 Nights in the Forest is already there when you get to it. Carrots are in the grass, berries are on bushes, apples hang off trees you can buy from the Grocery Store. You walk out, you fill a Sack, you come back. The Chili is the only food in the game that is not there until you make it be there.

That single difference decides almost everything else about it. A Chili cannot be found, farmed with a plot, or stolen from a chest on the way to somewhere else — its entire supply runs through a Chili Seed Pack and one in-game day of waiting. Which means the Chili is not a hunger item that happens to have a buff. It is a **scheduled** item, and the only thing in the game that genuinely asks you to place an order in advance.

What makes it worth a page is that the guides treat it the same way they treat the carrot: "1 hunger, speed boost, farm plots." Two of those three are contested, and the third is wrong in a way that costs you a tame. This page is about the difference between a crop you harvest and a crop you order.

Last verified: **2026-10-05**.

## Quick Answer

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What it is | A **crop plant** and a raw food. **One fork** of hunger plus a **short movement-speed boost** |
| Where it comes from | **Chili Seed Pack only.** Chili does not spawn on the map by itself — plant the packet, and plants appear the next in-game day |
| Where the seed comes from | Grocery Store, the Fairy (flower trade), Chests, the Food Warehouse, Farmer Cletus |
| Farm Plot? | **No** in every published crop table we can find — Chili is a seed-pack plant, not a plot crop |
| Who wants it | **The Arctic Fox**, the only animal in the game billed in Chili: **Good Flute, 3 cycles, 3 Steak + 3 Chili** |
| Regrows? | **No.** Once the plants are picked there is nothing left to come back |
| Rarity | Uncommon (fandom) |
| Worth carrying? | Yes — it is the cheapest way to break a Deer chase that already started going badly |

## The One Thing That Makes Chili Different

Try to answer this without planting anything: **how many Chili are on the map right now?** The honest answer is that the number is zero, and it is zero until you change it.

Carrot, berry and apple are *found* food. Each of them has a standing population that the map maintains, so even a player who has never built a Farm Plot is not starving. Chili has no standing population. Every Chili in the game was placed there by somebody's seed packet. The fandom wiki states the rule flatly: "Chili's only spawn when the player places seeds all over the map."

That moves Chili out of the food economy and into the **scheduling** economy. You cannot grind for Chili the way you grind for Steak — Steak drops from wolves and bears, and more wolves exist every night. You cannot shortcut Chili either. There is no second route, no side farm, no trader who keeps a standing stock. There is one action (plant a packet) and one delay (an in-game day).

The practical consequence is that the Chili is the only thing in a long run where being right about *when* matters more than being right about *how much*. Three Chili will not appear because you wanted them on Day 5. They appear because somebody planted a packet on Day 4.

## Where Chili Actually Comes From

The route is one step long and one step slow:

1. **Get a Chili Seed Pack.**
2. **Use it.** The packet plants Chili all over the map (fandom Chili).
3. **Wait one in-game day.** Bloxodes and the 99nights-intheforest.net seed guide both give the growth time as **1 in-game day**, with no conditions about biome, campfire level or placement.
4. **Harvest.** The plants show up near large trees and out in the open (fandom). Pick them.
5. **Stop.** Chili "do not grow back naturally, but more can be grown with Chili Seeds" (fandom Items). After the plants are cleared there is nothing regenerating in the background.

| Step | Cost | Who agrees |
|---|---|---|
| Seed Pack | Found, bought or traded | bloxodes, deltiasgaming, fandom |
| Planting | Free, one action | fandom, breeze |
| Growth | **1 in-game day** | bloxodes, 99nights-intheforest.net, deltiasgaming |
| Location | Near large trees, in the open | fandom |
| Regrowth | **None** | fandom Items, breeze, deltiasgaming |

Note what is *not* on that list. A Farm Plot is not on it. Neither is the Grocery Store selling Chili directly, which would be the obvious assumption and is not what the sources describe — the store sells you the **packet**, not the fruit.

## Every Route to a Chili Seed

The seed is the bottleneck, so it is worth knowing all the doors into it. They are not equivalent: some are repeatable, one is a one-time structure, and one costs you twenty flowers.

| Route | What it takes | Repeatable | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Grocery Store** | Buy with Mossy Coins | Yes | bloxodes, 99nights-intheforest.net, deltiasgaming |
| **The Fairy** | Trade flowers for packets | Yes, if you keep picking flowers | fandom Chili; bloxodes lists the Fairy as a seed source |
| **Chests** | Legendary Chests carry Chili Seeds | Unbounded by luck | our own [Chest Tiers](/items/chest-tiers); bloxodes lists "Chests" |
| **Food Warehouse** | Loot the building; it holds Boxes of Chili Seeds | One-time per run | fandom Food Warehouse |
| **Farmer Cletus** | Buys Chili pepper and berry seed boxes for Mossy Coins | Yes | fandom Locations thread |

The Fairy route is the one the fandom guide spells out, and it is more specific than "trade flowers": pick **20 flowers**, ideally in clumps — a circle of eight counts as one group, and the Fairy's Home itself holds about ten — then hand them over for Chili Seeds. Twenty flowers is roughly a morning's picking, and the Fairy's Home is a fixed stop, so this is the most reliable door on the list if you reach it early.

The Food Warehouse is the interesting one, and it is also the most fragile claim on this page. The fandom Food Warehouse page is blunt about it: "The Food Warehouse is the only structure to give Seed Packs." That would make it the single most important stop in the game for a Chili order, and no other source we found mentions the building at all. Treat it as a strong single-source lead rather than a rule.

## The One-Day Lag: Why Chili Is a Scheduling Problem

Because the growth time is one in-game day and the harvest is not stored anywhere you can buy it back, the Chili behaves like a delivery with a lead time. The arithmetic is simple and it is the whole strategy:

| You want the Chili on | Plant the packet on | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Day 2 (the earliest sane tame) | **Day 1** | One in-game day of growth, no exceptions in any source |
| Day 5, for an Arctic Fox tame | **Day 4** | Three Chili, one per cycle — three cycles cannot start before the first one lands |
| The moment a Deer starts chasing you | **Yesterday** | This is what makes Chili a stock item rather than a rescue item |

The second row is the one that gets runs lost. An Arctic Fox tame asks for **1 Steak + 1 Chili on each of three cycles**. The cycle that needs Chili #1 cannot start until Chili #1 exists. So "I'll tame a fox after Day 5" is not a plan — "I planted chili on Day 4" is the plan, and the tame is a consequence of it.

By contrast, look at what the [Carrot](/items/carrot) page prices against this: the Bunny costs **one Carrot and one cycle** with no lead time at all, because Carrots are on the map already. The Bunny is a snap decision. The Arctic Fox is an appointment.

## Planting It: Where the Plants Show Up

Two details matter when you actually go looking.

**Where.** The fandom Chili article says the plants "will appear near large trees and in the open." That is a search pattern — walk the tree line and the clearings, not the middle of dense canopy. Other write-ups are vagger, saying only that Chili is scattered "throughout the map" or "all over the map," which is why players conclude they have found none.

**How much one packet gives.** Nobody publishes a number, and the sources disagree on the shape of the mechanic itself. Fandom's older Chili article lists a Seed Pack as placing one of **four** things — Chili, Flowers, Fireflies, Berries. The Breeze wiki lists **six**, adding Strawberries and Trees. If your packet lands on a Tree, you did not get Chili, and the only honest answer is to plant again.

So budget a packet per three Chili plus a spare. Planting in a known area is worth more than planting at random, because you need to know where to walk on the morning you harvest.

## What Eating One Does

| Effect | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Hunger | **1 fork** | Every crop table we found agrees — 1 |
| Speed | **Short boost** | The reason to carry one |
| Health | **None** | Not a heal, not a Hearty Stew substitute |
| Duration | **Not published** | fandom says "a couple of seconds"; 99-nights-in-the-forest.com says "slight... short duration" |

The important claim is the second one. The Chili is the **only plant in the game that gives a speed boost when eaten** — a fact both the fandom and the Breeze wiki call out in their trivia sections. That makes it a utility item wearing a food costume. One fork of hunger plus an escape is a better trade than three forks of Pumpkin, which is why the Chili shows up in [speedrun](/guides/speedrun-guide) plans and not in meal plans.

The obvious use is breaking a Deer chase. Eat it at the moment the Deer is closer than you can sprint, not after stamina and spacing are gone — the boost is short, and a boost used at the end of a chase is worth nothing.

The other comparable speed source is the [Ice Temple](/locations/ice-temple), which our own page benchmarks at "roughly triple normal sprint speed" and explicitly compares to eating a Chili. The trade is worth stating plainly: the Chili is repeatable and costs an inventory slot, the Ice Temple is a location you can use once per visit. If you have not been to the Temple, its effect may be the better pickup. If you have, Chili is the one you stack.

## The Arctic Fox: The Only Animal That Bills You in Chili

There is exactly one taming bill in the game with Chili on it, and it is the Arctic Fox.

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Flute | **Good Taming Flute** |
| Cycles | **3** |
| Per cycle | **1 Steak + 1 Chili** |
| Total | **3 Steak + 3 Chili** |
| Where | Snow Biome |
| Drops back | No Steak — the Fox does not refund the Steak you spend |

The flute, the cycle count and the food are consistent across games.gg, Sportskeeda, 99-nights.wiki and Bloxodes, all cross-checked on 2026-09-30. Our own [How to Tame a Fox](/taming/how-to-tame-a-fox) page reaches the same figures independently, and our [Pet Food](/taming/pet-food) page lists Chili as the Arctic Fox's taming food. Where the argument between our pages and the outside world happens is downstream of the bill, not at the bill: the Steak half is farmable from wolves and bears, and the Chili half is not farmable at all.

This is also why our [Best Pet](/taming/best-pet) page calls the Arctic Fox "situational — the Chili grind is annoying." Read that line as a statement about the seed, not the fox. The fox is cheap. The Chili in front of it is the expensive part, and it is expensive in *days*, not in materials.

## Chili Versus Carrot: The Two Taming Foods, Ranked by Friction

The cleanest way to see what the Chili costs is to put it next to the only other food that buys a tame.

| | **Chili** (Arctic Fox) | **Carrot** (Bunny) |
|---|---|---|
| Exists on the map by itself? | **No** | Yes |
| Supply route | Seed Pack, 1 in-game day | Wild spawns + Farm Plot (10 Wood), 6 crops per 2 days |
| Number needed | **3** | **1** |
| Wait before the first one | One in-game day | None — pick it now |
| Hard Mode, at 60% Corruption | Not on the never-rot list | Wild ones never rot; plot ones rot in 90 seconds |
| Bottleneck | **Planning** | **Wood** |

The last row is the point. The [Carrot](/items/carrot) page spends its Hard Mode section on the fact that a map carrot is rot-proof while a plot carrot is not — you can always fall back on the wild one. The Chili has no such fallback, because the wild one does not exist. When the rot spores come, the Carrot player walks outside and picks. The Chili player walks outside and finds nothing, because the Chili was grown and the Chili rots.

That is the whole argument for taking Chili seriously as its own subject rather than as a footnote in a fox guide.

## Hard Mode: The Food Chili Has No Safety Net For

Our own [Hard Mode](/guides/hard-mode) guide sets out the Corruption ladder: Hungry Deer from 15%, passive healing reduced at 25%, stronger cultist raids at 35%, **Food Rot Spores at 60%**, extreme raids at 75%, healing stopped at 80%, and poison spores at 100%.

At the 60% step the fandom Hard Mode page is specific about which food survives it, in one sentence that splits every food in the game into three buckets:

- Food dropped by normal animals **rots after 90 seconds**.
- Food grown in farm plots **rots after 90 seconds**.
- Food that spawns on the map — **carrots, berries, strawberries, apples** — **never rots**.

Three things follow, and they are the reason this section exists.

**First, Chili is not on the never-rot list.** It is not a carrot, a berry, a strawberry or an apple, and it is not animal-dropped. So it sits in the same bucket as the farm plot food, which means the Chili you grew is exposed to the same 90-second clock.

**Second, and this is the part nobody writes down: the Chili has nowhere to run.** Carrot fruit is still out there in the grass. Berry bushes are still out there. When the spores start, you harvest the wild stock that was already there. Your Chili supply was entirely yours — grown, picked, in a Sack — and a Sack does not get an exemption.

**Third, Chili is the one food where that exposure is unavoidable rather than a choice.** If you have 30 Pumpkins in the plot and the spores come, you lost the plot and you still have the map. If you have 3 Chili banked for a fox tame and the spores come, the Chili is the loss.

What to do about it, in order of how much it hurts:

1. **Plant the packet early and harvest it early.** Lead time is your friend in every other mode; here it is the only lever you have.
2. **Do not bank Chili for a tame you will not start inside 90 seconds of a bad moment.** Eat one, use the boost, or hand it over — do not carry it.
3. **Know the escape hatch.** Rotten food still damages you if you eat it, and cooking does not fix it, but rotten food **can still go into the Biofuel Processor** for campfire fuel (Game8). A lost Chili is a lost tame, not a lost fire.
4. **Take the rot-proof option when it is offered.** The Hard Mode research chests contain **3 MREs, described as super-stews that do not rot**, alongside a medkit and two bandages (fandom Hard Mode). If Chili is the sand in your taming gear, MREs are the answer the game hands you.

## Where Sources Disagree

Nine places where the published material contradicts itself or us. We are not resolving these; they are the state of the sources as of 2026-10-05.

1. **Is Chili a Farm Plot crop?** Our own [Pet Food](/taming/pet-food) page says "Farm Plots grow Carrot, Corn, Pumpkin, Chili and Cake," and our [All Locations](/locations/all-locations) page repeats the list. Sports Rant's crop table answers the question in its own column — Chili, "Farmed Via Plot? No." Rblxguide's Farm Plot article lists only **Carrot, Corn, Pumpkin, Cake**. And the fandom Locations thread says an empty plot can grow "a Carrot, corn, grape, chili pepper, or pumpkin," which says yes. Three answers to one question; we list them rather than pick, but the seed-pack route is the only one everyone agrees on.
2. **Does Chili grow on the map already?** Our [Hunger](/guides/hunger) page lists its source as "Across the map, and from Chili Seeds," and our Chef page says "Around the map and chili seeds." Fandom's Chili article says the opposite flatly: "Chili's only spawn when the player places seeds all over the map." Breeze splits the difference with "Natural Spawn? Kinda — you need to plant it."
3. **How much hunger does a Chili give?** Every external food table — fandom Items, Deltia's Gaming, Sports Rant, Finding Dulcinea — puts it at **1 fork plus a boost**. Our Hunger page puts it at **none**: "Chili and Jellyfish both trade their hunger value for a speed or energy boost," and its table reads "None — speed boost instead." We cannot close this, and it changes whether a Chili is a snack or a pure utility.
4. **How many things can one Seed Pack place?** Four — Chili, Flowers, Fireflies, Berries — in fandom's earlier Chili article. Six — the same four plus Strawberries and Trees — in the Breeze wiki.
5. **At what Corruption does Food Rot Spores start?** 60% in fandom's Hard Mode table and in our own Hard Mode page. 50% in the BloxEarn hard mode guide. "Roughly 75%" in RBLXGuide, and 75% in Game8.
6. **Are there Chili recipes at all?** The 99-nights-in-the-forest.com item page claims two Thanksgiving recipes — 2 Chili + 1 Stuffing for Stuffed Peppers, and 2 Chili + 1 Swordfish for Spicy Swordfish. No other source we found mentions a Chili recipe, and every published Crock Pot and Chef list excludes it (Stew takes Carrot, Corn, Pumpkin, Apple, Morsel, Steak, Rib, Cake and Meat Sandwich; Chili is not on it).
7. **Where planted Chili appears.** "Near large trees and in the open" (fandom Chili) versus "throughout the map" (Deltia's Gaming) versus "all over the map" (Breeze).
8. **How big is the boost?** Nobody publishes a number. Fandom: "a couple of seconds." 99-nights-in-the-forest.com: "a slight speed boost for a short duration." Our own Ice Temple page estimates the comparable Temple effect at "roughly triple normal sprint speed," which tells you nothing useful about the Chili itself.
9. **Where you buy seeds.** Bloxodes and 99nights-intheforest.net give the Grocery Store, the Fairy and Chests. Fandom adds the Food Warehouse — described there as the only structure in the game that gives Seed Packs — and Farmer Cletus, who sells Chili pepper and berry seed boxes. The Food Warehouse and Farmer Cletus appearances are single-source.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Where do I get Chili?**
Plant a **Chili Seed Pack** and wait one in-game day. The packet plants Chili all over the map and the plants appear near large trees and in the open. Seeds come from the Grocery Store, the Fairy, Chests, the Food Warehouse and Farmer Cletus.

**Can I grow Chili in a Farm Plot?**
In our own [Pet Food](/taming/pet-food) listing, yes. In Sports Rant's crop table, Rblxguide's plot list and Breeze's write-up, no. Plant it with a seed pack instead — that route is agreed on by every source.

**Does Chili heal?**
No. It gives **1 fork of hunger** and a **short speed boost**. For health you want a [Bandage](/guides/healing-guide), a Medkit, or a dish like Hearty Stew.

**How many Chili do I need for an Arctic Fox?**
**Three**, one per cycle across three cycles, alongside 3 Steak, on a **Good Taming Flute**. Plant the packet the day before you intend to start, because nothing else can substitute.

**Can I just buy Chili at the Grocery Store?**
You can buy the **seed**, not the fruit, per Bloxodes, Deltia's Gaming and 99nights-intheforest.net. If your source says "Grocery Store" without qualifying it, that is what it means.

**Does Chili go bad in Hard Mode?**
It is not on fandom's never-rot list, which names only carrots, berries, strawberries, apples and wild spawns. Farm plot food and animal drops rot after 90 seconds at 60% Corruption, and Chili is neither wild nor animal-dropped. Do not plan a tame around Chili once the spores are up.

**Is the Chili the same as the Rage Pepper?**
No. The Rage Pepper is a separate item from the Wildlife Update update, described as giving +25% damage for 5 seconds. It is not a Chili and is not accepted for taming.

**If one Seed Pack might not give Chili, how many should I plant?**
Plant at least two. Four-way packets (Chili, Flowers, Fireflies, Berries) mean a third of plantings are not Chili, and six-way packets mean fewer still.
