The Carrot is the only food in the game that has a job in every stage of a run. On Day 1 it is the thing you kick out of the ground when you have nothing else. By Day 3 you are handing them to a Bunny to buy your first tame. Later you are cooking them into a dish that gives you night vision, burning the surplus in the Biofuel Processor, and in the Snow Biome putting one into the hands of a snowman who is missing a nose. Nothing else in the crop list does all four.

What makes it worth a page is that the game explains none of that. Every guide that mentions the Carrot mentions it as "1 hunger, farm plots, grocery store" and stops. The two numbers that decide what a carrot is worth — what a Farm Plot actually yields, and which carrots survive Hard Mode — are published on pages about other subjects, and both of them are more useful than the number the pages do print.

Last verified: **2026-10-05**.

## Quick Answer

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What it is | A **crop** and a raw food. **One fork**, no buff, and no penalty for eating it raw |
| Where you find it | Scattered across the map, [Farm Plots](/locations/crafting-bench), and the Grocery Store |
| Where you do not find it | Nowhere else. There is no Crafting Bench recipe that makes a Carrot |
| The fork scale | **1** is the bottom. **4** is the ceiling, held by Pumpkin, cooked Ribs, cooked Shark and every Crock Pot dish |
| Raw or cooked | Raw is safe. No source we can find gives a cooked Carrot a different value or an effect |
| The best thing it makes | [Carrot Cake](/classes/chef-class) at Chef level 2 — **Cake + Carrot + Carrot** |
| What Carrot Cake gives | **4 forks**, roughly **9 minutes of night vision**, and a heal |
| The cheapest tame in the game | The **Bunny** — Old Flute, one cycle, **one Carrot**, no other ingredient |
| The hidden sink | **One Carrot** to the noseless Snowman in the Snow Biome returns **1 Cultist Gem**, once |
| In Hard Mode | Map-spawned carrots **never rot**. Carrots grown on a plot rot after 90 seconds |
| The one thing it never does | Kill anything. A carrot run is a zero-kill run, which is what makes the Vegetarian badge possible |

## One Fork, and Nothing Else

The food scale is measured in forks, and the Carrot sits at 1. Our own [Hunger](/guides/hunger) page prints the whole table and the order stands: one fork is a Carrot, and four forks is the top of the scale. A Carrot is not a small Pumpkin. A Pumpkin is four forks, Corn is two, and a Carrot is the floor of the system.

That floor is not a criticism. Three things about it are advantages:

1. **It is the only four-way food you can pick up without killing, cooking, or a bench.** A Morsel needs an animal dead and a fire. A Steak needs a wolf. A Carrot needs you to walk over it.
2. **It is safe raw.** The raw-food rule that catches players is written about meat, and the fork table puts Carrot on the safe side of it. A carrot in a hurry costs you nothing.
3. **It never decays into a worse version of itself.** There is no cooked Carrot with a higher number. Whatever a carrot is worth at noon, it is worth at night.

The honest cost of those three things is that a Carrot is a top-up, not a meal. The useful shape is: eat one when the bar is low and there is nothing better in your sack, and save the rest for the four jobs further down this page.

## Where Carrots Come From

Three published sources, and they agree on the short list:

| Source | Published obtain methods |
|---|---|
| rblxguide | Scattered through the map, farm plots, and the grocery store |
| Pocket Tactics | Farm plots, the grocery store, and throughout the map |
| Rosenberry | Throughout the forest, farm plots, grocery store |

Two details are worth separating out, because they are the difference between finding carrots and having a carrot supply.

**The map is the supply.** Carrots spawn on the ground across the forest, which is why every beginner guide says "just pick up carrots" and why a Day 1 run can be fed entirely from the floor. The spawn is also why some guides describe a carrot in the Snow Biome as a *strawberry* instead — the Hard Mode rules list the map-spawned foods as "carrots, berries, strawberries, apples", and players see more than one root in the same ground.

**The Grocery Store is the bulk buy.** Our own [All Locations](/locations/all-locations) page lists Market and Grocery Store as the two places that sell carrots, apples, occasional seeds and healing items. That is the difference between picking up four carrots on your way somewhere and walking in with a stack.

**Farm Plots are the renewable version**, and they are the subject of the next section.

## The Farm Plot Question: Six Plants, Two Days, Unknown Contents

A Farm Plot is a Tier 2 Crafting Bench recipe at **10 Wood**, placeable anywhere on the map, stackable, with a cap of **20 per player**. Once placed it produces **6 plants every 2 days**.

The problem is not the yield. The problem is that the six plants are not guaranteed to be carrots, and the three sources that say so do not agree on what the pool is:

| Source | What the plot grows |
|---|---|
| Our [Crafting Bench](/locations/crafting-bench) page | "Grows carrots automatically every 2 days" |
| Our [Pumpkin](/items/pumpkin) page | A random pool of **four** — Carrot (most likely), Corn, Pumpkin, Cake (least likely) |
| The community wiki's crop page, as quoted by our Pumpkin page | Three crops — Carrot, Pumpkin, Corn — with roughly a 30% chance of Corn, and **no Cake** |
| rblxguide and the wiki Farm Plot page | Four crops — Carrot (most likely), Corn, Pumpkin, Cake (least likely) |

We print those four and do not pick one, for the reason the section below spells out: two of the four are written on this site, and they contradict each other. What the table does support is the ordering, which every source that gives an ordering puts **Carrot first**: a carrot is the single most likely thing a plot gives you, and cake is the rarest.

Two practical consequences:

- **A plot is a carrot farm before it is anything else.** If your goal is taming food, plots are still the right buy, because carrot comes off the top of the pool more often than corn, pumpkin, or cake.
- **A plot is not a carrot pantry.** If a source is wrong in the direction that matters, you can put 10 Wood into a plot and harvest six unknowns, which is not the same as six carrots.

One item of disagreement we cannot close: our own [Pet Food](/taming/pet-food) page says Farm Plots grow Carrot, Corn, Pumpkin and Cake from seeds, while the published seed packet lists circulated by Pocket Tactics contains Berry, Chili and seven others and **no Carrot Seed at all**. We are printing both rather than choosing.

The Bunny is billed in Carrot with no wait at all; the Arctic Fox is billed in [Chili](/items/chili) with a day's lead, and our Chili page sets the two bills side by side.

The animal on the other side of that bill now has its own page: the [Bunny](/entities/the-bunny), one cycle and about twenty seconds end to end.

## The Cheapest Tame in the Game Is Billed in Carrots

Every tame has a bill, and the bills are what a carrot is for. The short version, with our [Pet Food](/taming/pet-food) page and the animal tables as the source:

| Tame | Flute | Cycles | Bill | Carrot share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bunny** | Old | 1 | **1 Carrot** | 1 |
| Wolf | Old | 1 | Carrot ×1, then Steak | 1 of 2 |
| Kiwi | Old | 1 | Carrot ×1 | 1 |
| Arctic Fox | Good | 3 | 3 Steak + **3 Chili** | 0 |
| Bear | Good | 3 | Steak + Morsel | 0 |
| Mammoth | Good | 3 | 10 Pumpkin + 5 Cake | 0 |

That first row is the whole taming economy of the early game. One carrot, one cycle, one pet — our [Taming Guide](/taming/taming-guide) page answers "easiest animal?" with exactly that: Bunny, one carrot, one cycle. The [Zookeeper](/classes/zookeeper-class) page makes the same point from the other side, because the class' tame counter is measured in Bunnies: sixty tames is sixty carrots, and the published shortcut for it is to join a run, tame Bunnies with the carrots that spawn naturally, and reset the run.

This is also where the Carrot and the [Steak](/items/steak) page finally meet. The Steak is the taming currency of everything that fights back; the Carrot is the taming currency of everything that does not. If your plan is a farm of passive animals, your bills are carrots. If your plan is an Arctic Fox or a Bear, a carrot is not on the bill at all — and the Chili on that bill is its own grind, which is why our [Best Pet](/taming/best-pet) page calls the Chili requirement the annoying part.

## Carrot Cake: the Only Dish That Turns a Vegetable Into a Carryable Heal

At **Chef level 2** the Chef unlocks Carrot Cake, and the recipe is **Cake + Carrot + Carrot**. That is the entire dish: two carrots and one cake.

What it returns is the reason to make it:

| Value | Figure |
|---|---|
| Food | **4 forks** — a full top-of-scale meal |
| Effect | **Night vision for roughly 9 minutes** |
| Heal | Reported as **40** health by two sources; one other table says **10** |
| Carryable | Yes. It is one of the three dishes our [Healing](/guides/healing-guide) page lists as a Chef carry-heal, with Jar o' Jelly and BBQ Ribs |

The heal number is genuinely unresolved, and it is worth saying why it matters here rather than on the Chef page. Two of the three published figures cannot both be right, and the difference between them is the difference between a top-up and a real heal. We print the disagreement rather than averaging it.

What is not in dispute is that **Carrot Cake is the only recipe in the game that attaches night vision to a dish**, which is why our Mammoth page points at it specifically: the Mammoth tame wants 5 Cake, and the Chef's Carrot Cake is the only recipe that adds the buff on top. Two carrots is the cheapest way to turn a cake you found in a clinic into something that does two jobs.

## Stew, the Crock Pot, and the Line That Decides Your Badge

The Crock Pot is where the four-fork dishes live, and Carrot is one of the four ingredients in the one that matters most: **Stew is Carrots + Morsels + Cakes + Steaks**, and it returns four forks with the warmth effect attached. Our [Steak](/items/steak) page has the full argument for why that warmth clause is easy to miss — a Stew made in the Snow Biome warms you.

The reason that sentence has a clause in it is the Vegetarian badge, and the Stew is where the badge is won or lost. Our own [Steak](/items/steak) page records the in-site argument plainly: a Stew made with meat does not count as vegetarian. The published rule is the same. So the stew you cook is a diet decision, not a cooking decision, and the vegetable half of the pot is the only half that is unambiguously vegetarian.

The honest summary: **carrots are what a no-meat run eats, and they are also what makes the mixed pot possible.** You cannot make a Stew without one meat ingredient, so a vegetarian run and a normal run both want carrots, for opposite reasons.

## Carrots as Fuel, and as Bait

Two sinks that almost no guide connects to the carrot.

**Fuel.** Our [Biofuel](/items/how-to-get-biofuel) page is explicit that any food works — carrots, corn, steak, raw or cooked — and that uncooked food works as well as cooked. That makes a surplus carrot a fire problem rather than a hunger problem, and it is the honest use for the carrots that a plot hands you when you did not want a plot. Note the ordering on our Biofuel page: pelts are worth more than one unit each, so a pelt beats a carrot as fuel. Carrots are the fuel you fall back on, not the fuel you plan around.

**Bait.** The Bunny Trap is a **5 Wood** Tier 2 craft, placed near a bunny burrow, and a **carrot bait can yield 4 Morsels**. Put next to the fork table that is a real trade: one carrot is 1 fork, and four raw Morsels are 4 forks — or 8 if you cook them. The catch is obvious and worth stating: the trap consumes the carrot, so the maths only works if the burrow is busier than the bait, and the Morsels arrive raw.

## The Carrot the Snowman Wants

In the Snow Biome there is a snowman with no nose, and one source reports that **bringing it a carrot returns 1 Cultist Gem**.

This is a small fact with a large shape, and our own pages already disagree about how large:

- Our [All Badges](/guides/all-badges) page lists **Reconstruction** as "Bring a carrot to the noseless snowman", rates it 4, and notes three sources do. Its text adds that one guide walks through it and reports **4 diamonds plus a Cultist Gem**.
- Our [Cultist Gem](/items/cultist-gem) page lists the carrot-to-snowman exchange as one of its eight routes, states plainly that it is **1 carrot for 1 gem**, and marks it as a one-off interaction, not a farm.

So the same carrot is on one page a badge and four diamonds, and on another a gem with no diamonds in it. We print both. What is not in dispute is the shape: it is a single interaction that does not repeat, it costs one carrot, and it is the only use of a carrot that pays in something other than food. Our Cultist Gem page is the one to read if you are budgeting gems.

## Living Without Meat: What a Carrot Budget Actually Looks Like

The Vegetarian badge is **survive 99 days without eating any meat**. Our [Badges](/guides/all-badges) page has the badge and our [Steak](/items/steak) page has the argument for why the first bite locks it — the badge and the meat diet are mutually exclusive for the length of a run, so it is decided on Day 1.

If you take it, Carrot stops being a snack and becomes the entire food budget. What that looks like mathematically:

- The bar is a **12-to-17-minute** measure, and a single Carrot resets it.
- Everything else in the game that fills that bar either comes from an animal (Morsel, Steak, Rib), from a pond (every fish), or from a Chef (Carrot Cake, Jar o' Jelly, Seafood Chowder).
- Carrot is the only one of those four lists that is available to you on Day 1 without a fire, a rod, or a class.

So the budget is not really "one carrot every fifteen minutes". It is "one carrot every fifteen minutes until the Chef arrives, and after that, however many the Chef's table needs but no more than the ones you picked up". The Carrot is the only food in the game whose supply does not depend on a system being built first, and that is the entire reason a no-meat run is survivable to day 99 instead of day 4.

## What Hard Mode Does to a Carrot

This is the section the rest of the page is built around, and the reason a Carrot is worth more in Hard Mode than in any other mode.

At the **60%** Corruption threshold, Hard Mode turns on Food Rot Spores. The published rule, in full, is:

| What the source is | What happens at 60% |
|---|---|
| Food dropped by corrupted animals | Always rotten immediately |
| Food dropped by normal animals | Rotts after **90 seconds** |
| Food grown in farm plots | Rotts after **90 seconds** |
| Food that spawns on the map — **carrots, berries, strawberries, apples** | **Never goes rotten** |

That last row is the whole point. Two consequences follow, and neither is obvious until you put them together with the sections above.

**The Farm Plot stops being a pantry.** Carrots you grew rot in 90 seconds. Carrots you kicked out of the ground do not. So a plot is a 10-Wood purchase whose output expires unless you eat it immediately, while the carrots under the map keep their value for the entire run. In Hard Mode, a plot is a liability, not a farm.

**Spend map carrots, not plot carrots.** The Bunny tame costs one carrot, and only a Wild carrot is guaranteed to survive to the moment you need it. If you are holding a stack for a quieted rift, the stack that matters is the one you picked up, not the one in the plot.

There is a third consequence that applies to everything on the page: the Carrot Cake is a cooked dish, and cooked dishes rot. Night vision for nine minutes is worth buying at the moment you need it, not stockpiling for the night you hope to need it.

Our own [Hard Mode](/guides/hard-mode) page carries the same table, and our [Hunger](/guides/hunger) page repeats the short version. We are not adding a claim to them; we are pointing out what the table means for one specific item.

## Where Sources Disagree

Eight disagreements, in order of how much they change what you do.

1. **What a Farm Plot grows — four crops or one.** Our Crafting Bench page says the plot grows carrots, automatically, every 2 days. Our Pumpkin page says the crop pool is four with carrot most likely. The wiki's crop page quoted on the same site says three with no cake. The wiki Farm Plot page and rblxguide both say four. **Cannot be resolved from published sources.** What every ordering has in common is that carrot is top of it.
2. **Whether there is a Carrot Seed.** Our Pet Food page lists Carrot among the plot crops grown from seeds. The published seed list contains no Carrot Seed. **Unresolved.**
3. **Carrot Cake's heal — 40 or 10.** Two sources say 40, one table says 10. Our Chef page has printed this same fight already. **Unresolved, and the page that prints it is the Chef page.**
4. **Where Carrot Cake comes from.** Most sources say the Chef's station. Pocket Tactics lists it under trick-or-treating and the Chef class, which would make it an event dish as well. **The event path is single-source.**
5. **The Snowman carrot.** One set of sources reports the Reconstruction badge as 4 diamonds plus a Cultist Gem. Our Cultist Gem page reports the exchange as the gem alone. **Unresolved, and the two pages are on this site.**
6. **Corruption threshold for Food Rot Spores.** The wiki says 60%. Two secondary guides say 50%. We print 60%, because that is what the wiki says and what our own Hard Mode page prints.
7. **Map-spawn foods.** The wiki lists carrots, berries, strawberries and apples as the never-rot group. Nothing we can find lists them as a group anywhere else, and one secondary guide mentions "carrots and berries" only. **The apple and strawberry inclusion is single-source.**
8. **How many carrots a plot is worth.** One guide describes a two-plot setup producing "up to 16 carrots every few days", which does not divide by either the 6-per-plot or the 2-day figure. **Unresolved, and it is the reason the yield table above exists.**

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is a raw Carrot safe to eat?**
Yes. The raw-food penalty is written about meat, and the fork tables put Carrot on the safe side. A carrot costs you nothing raw; the same is not true of raw Morsel.

**Is there a Carrot Seed?**
Not in any seed list we can find. The published packets are Berry, Chili, Cavevine, Dripleaf, Firefly, Flower, Mandrake, Moonflower and Stareweed. Our own Pet Food page disagrees and lists Carrot among the seeded crops, so treat this as open.

**Does cooking a Carrot do anything?**
Nothing we can find. There is no cooked Carrot with a different fork value and no effect attached to it. Cook the carrot if it is going into a dish; cook it for itself and you have changed nothing.

**Which carrot should I feed a Bunny in Hard Mode, a plot carrot or a wild one?**
A wild one, once you are past the 60% threshold. Plot-grown carrots rot after 90 seconds, so a tame fed from your plot is fine at the moment it happens and worthless an hour later.

**Does a Carrot break the Vegetarian badge?**
No. A Carrot kills nothing. What breaks it is the meat ingredient in a dish — a Stew made with meat does not count as vegetarian — which is why the vegetable half of the pot is the safe half and the meat half is the decision.

**Why does the Chef page call Carrot Cake the unlock and not Jar o' Jelly?**
Both. Chef level 2 is Carrot Cake, level 3 is Jar o' Jelly, and our Chef page treats them as the two named unlocks of a three-level class. The Carrot Cake arrives first and it is the one that needs carrots.

**Is the carrot still worth picking up late in a run?**
Three times over: as fuel in the Biofuel Processor, as bait in a Bunny Trap, and as the ingredient requirement in a Carrot Cake if you want the night vision. Also, if you are in the Snow Biome, one of them buys a gem.

**Do carrots spawn anywhere else?**
The published answer is three places — map, farm plots, grocery store. Nothing we can find lists a fourth.
