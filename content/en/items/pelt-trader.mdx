The Pelt Trader is the fastest legitimate route from "I am swinging a starter axe on Day 2" to "I am wearing the Iron Body by Day 15." Four pelts, one NPC, no crafting — and unlike chest farming, the path is fixed. You can plan it down to the day.

What makes him worth a dedicated page is not his rewards. It is that **almost nothing about him is public**, and the things that go wrong go wrong silently: there are trades where he takes a pelt, closes the menu, and never gives you anything back. This page covers the whole mechanic. Which page for which reward stays on — see [How to Get the Iron Body](/items/how-to-get-iron-body) for the armor decision itself.

Last verified: **2026-10-03**.

## Quick Answer

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Who is he | An NPC who runs a **one-at-a-time chain**: he asks for a pelt, you hand it over, then he asks for the next one |
| First appearance | **Day 2** |
| How often | Back **every 3–4 days**; not affected by any in-game multiplier |
| Closing time | He **leaves the camp at night** — do the trade in daylight |
| Where | **Around your campfire**, at camp — not a fixed building, no map icon |
| How many trades | **4 pelts** by default; **5** if you are **Hunter class level 3** |
| Do other players steal it | No — the whole chain is **client-sided**, everyone has their own offers |
| What finishes it | Handing over the **Bear Pelt** ends the line; he asks for nothing after that |

He is camp-side rather than out in a biome, and one of the traders beside him only stays for the whole run once you reach campfire level 2. That ladder is on [Campfire](/guides/campfire).

He arrives on real Day 2 no matter what your multiplier says — a rule that shapes the opening of a [speedrun in 99 Nights in the Forest](/guides/speedrun-guide).

## Where He Is and When

Every source agrees on the schedule, and it is unusually consistent for this game: **first visit on Day 2**, then a return roughly **every 3 to 4 days**, and **he leaves at night**. Our own [How to Get the Iron Body](/items/how-to-get-iron-body) page and the fandom and Miraheze wikis all state the same numbers, and the fandom page explicitly notes the interval is **not influenced by multipliers** — no event or bonus shortens or lengthens it.

Two practical consequences:

- **The trade menu closes when he leaves.** He disappears at nightfall, and if the reward menu is open at that moment it shuts — see the failure modes below, because this one costs a pelt.
- **You cannot "save" a pelt for him.** Wolf pelts rot in your bag, not his inventory. If he shows up unannounced and you are out hunting, you miss the whole visit; the next one is 3–4 days out.

**Where exactly:** around the campfire at camp. He is not tied to a building or a dungeon — there is no structure you can learn the coordinates of, and unlike the Furniture Trader (who drags a cart and is audible) he does not announce himself. If he is not at camp when you check, he is not in the forest; he is simply not visiting that day.

**Multiplayer and you:** the trader is **client-sided** — every player runs their own chain against the same NPC. Another player handing in a pelt cannot take your offer away, and you cannot take theirs. That single detail is why the entire chain is worth planning around: you are never racing anyone.

## How the Chain Works

He does not list four trades up front. He runs them **one at a time**: he asks for the easiest pelt first, and after you hand it over and pick your reward, he asks for the next, harder one. Each rung is harder to obtain than the last, in a fixed order:

**Bunny Foot → Wolf Pelt → Alpha Wolf Pelt → Bear Pelt**

That order is the whole strategy. A Bunny Foot is a rabbit kill you can farm on Day 1; a Bear Pelt is a hunt you should not start until you have a weapon worth the fight. Budget your run around the order rather than hunting everything you see.

Note also that **the pelt is spent the moment you hand it over**, and the reward is chosen from a menu afterwards. Those are two separate moments — and the gap between them is where the trader's bugs live.

## The Full Trade Table

This is the complete chain, including the conditional rewards that most guides leave out, because several of them only appear depending on what you already own. The fandom and Miraheze wikis agree cell-for-cell on the base table.

| Trade | He asks for | What you can pick |
|---|---|---|
| 1st | **Bunny Foot** | Good Axe · Good Sack · Old Flashlight |
| 2nd | **Wolf Pelt** | Spear · Revolver · Good Axe **or** Good Sack (only if you already have a Revolver) · **Leather Body** (listed on the current fandom table) |
| 3rd | **Alpha Wolf Pelt** | Rifle · Strong Flashlight · Giant Sack **or** Good Sack (both require you to already own an Old Flashlight) · Old Flashlight (only if you do not already own one) |
| 4th | **Bear Pelt** | **Iron Body** · Strong Axe · Strong Flashlight (only if you do not already own one) |

Read the conditional cells carefully — they are where the value of this page sits:

- **The Alpha Wolf Pelt rung is the one that changes shape.** If you already own an Old Flashlight, the Giant Sack and Good Sack options both become available; if you do not, he substitutes an Old Flashlight instead. So the same trade can offer three things or five things depending on your loadout.
- **The Bear Pelt rung is a genuine fork.** The Iron Body and the Strong Axe share one pelt — see [How to Get the Iron Body](/items/how-to-get-iron-body) for the framing, and [How to Get Wood Fast](/items/how-to-get-wood-fast) if logging speed is your bottleneck. These pages agree; the pelt is spent once.

**"Wolf Pelt" is the one rung where sources genuinely disagree**, and it is worth seeing both before you commit:

| Source | Wolf Pelt rung offers |
|---|---|
| Fandom wiki (current) | Leather Body · Spear · Revolver · Revolver Ammo (if you have the Revolver) — otherwise the Revolver · Good Axe or Good Sack |
| Miraheze wiki | Old Rod · Spear · Revolver · Revolver Ammo |
| Old fandom snapshot | Leather Body · Spear · Revolver Ammo |

The reliable reading is the current fandom table: **Spear, Revolver and the Ammo substitute are the stable core**. The **Leather Body** appears on the fandom table and on the old snapshot but not on Miraheze, and the **Old Rod** appears on Miraheze and on older guides but not on the current fandom table. See the note below on why the Old Rod is not something to plan around.

## Where Each Pelt Comes From

The chain is only as good as your ability to fill it, and the four pelts come from four different animals in four different places:

| Pelt | Animal | Where you will actually find it |
|---|---|---|
| Bunny Foot | Bunnies | The small wildlife around the map — the easiest to farm, and the reason the first trade is never a bottleneck |
| Wolf Pelt | Wolves | The Forest, in packs (see [The Wolf](/entities/the-wolf)) |
| Alpha Wolf Pelt | **Alpha Wolves** | A rarer, tougher wolf — see [The Alpha Wolf](/entities/the-alpha-wolf) |
| Bear Pelt | Bears | Bears and the tougher [Polar Bear](/entities/the-polar-bear) — the deepest hunt on the chain |

Two notes that save runs. First, **Bunny Foot is not exclusive to this chain**: it also buys [warm clothing](/items/warm-clothing), and a Chainsaw or Riot Shield recipe wants six of them. Farming for the trader is also farming for your cold-weather survival, so the two rarely fight — but decide which one you want before the trader shows up. Second, a **Polar Bear pelt is not the same as a Bear Pelt**; they are separate drops with separate sinks, and only the regular Bear Pelt closes the trader's line.

## The Trades That Quietly Cost You a Pelt

This is the section that does not exist anywhere else, and it is the reason this page is worth reading before your first visit. The fandom Pelt Trader page lists these under Bugs; the practical effect is that several of them **take the pelt and skip the reward**.

**1. He leaves (or you walk away) while the menu is open.** If the trader leaves at night with the reward menu open, **the menu closes but the quest still advances**. Translation: you handed over a Bear Pelt and are now holding nothing. The same happens if you walk too far away from him. There is no recovery step — the chain moved on without paying you.

**2. Another player hands something in.** If you are sitting in the reward menu and a second player gives the trader an item, **your menu will sometimes close**. In a four-player run this is a real risk, and it is the single most avoidable loss on the list: pick your reward the moment the menu opens.

**3. Carrying certain items downgrades the offer.** If you have picked up certain items — the **Ice Axe** is the documented example — the trader may stop offering the same rewards and **give lower-value items in their place**. Do not farm that item in the same run you are trading.

**4. Your existing weapon can be overwritten.** Two documented interactions work in opposite directions:

- If you already hold the **Poison Spear** or a **Morningstar** and take the Spear from the trader, **you keep the Poison Spear** — the poison damage makes it the better weapon.
- If you have the **Shadow Dagger** equipped and you buy the Spear, **the Spear replaces the Shadow Dagger**, because the dagger is worse. That one is a straight loss if you were using it.

And the mild upside: if you pick a **ranged weapon you already have**, you get a duplicate in your inventory rather than nothing.

Hunting is the badge that line ends with, and its reward sits in the exploration block on our [All Badges](/guides/all-badges) page.

The fourth tier of that chain is where the class question surfaces — the cheapest food class and the pelt economy solve the same problem two ways. See [**Fisherman Class**](/classes/fisherman-class) for the other route.

## When the Line Ends, and the Hunter Question

**Handing over the Bear Pelt finishes it.** The trader will not ask for anything afterwards — the chain is closed for that visit, and after that visit you start again from the Bunny Foot rung on his next appearance. Cleaning up his full chain is also what the **Hunting badge** is for.

**Alpha Wolf Pelt can end the chain early.** A known bug, stated identically by both wikis: after you hand over an Alpha Wolf Pelt, **he may not ask for the Bear Pelt at all**. The quest line stops there and you never see the Iron Body rung. If that happens you fall back on a chest or wait for the next visit.

**The Hunter level 3 fifth trade is real, and its shape is known even though its reward is not.** Every source agrees that reaching **Hunter class level 3** takes the chain from four pelts to five, and the perks published on the Hunter pages name what the fifth rung repeats: **an extra Wolf Pelt trade**. So it is not a fifth pelt — it is a repeat of the second rung, which means the fifth rung awards one more of the same thing the Wolf Pelt rung awards. See the [Hunter class](/classes/hunter-class) page for that sourcing and for the conflict in the wiki tips sections. What still is not published is which item that rung hands you when you take it.

Every other rod source on that list still has a working route, and the reliable one is a different NPC entirely. [**Fishing in 99 Nights in the Forest**](/guides/fishing-guide) lists them in reliability order.

## The Old Rod Trap

If you read older guides, you will see the Wolf Pelt rung offering an **Old Rod** or an **Old Taming Flute** (the [Taming Flute](/items/taming-flute) page covers where a first flute actually comes from). Those are no longer obtainable from the trader. That reward used to be a Revolver, was switched to the old taming flute after the taming update, and was then changed again — the tool is now something you buy from the Tool Trader for 20 coins. So **do not plan a run around the Old Rod**; it is a museum piece, and the current fandom table has dropped it.

## Pelt Trader vs. the Other Traders

He is not the only trader in the forest, and knowing which is which saves a lot of confused hunting:

| Trader | Takes | Gives |
|---|---|---|
| **Pelt Trader** | Animal pelts | Weapons, armor, sacks, flashlights — the whole progression chain |
| Furniture Trader | Mossy Coins | Decorations, walls, elevated platforms around camp |
| Fairy | Flowers | Seed Boxes (chillies, strawberries, trees) |
| Bird Watcher | Feathers / Forest Fragment Gems | Fuel Canisters — **no longer in the game** |

Only the first two come and go. The **Fairy** stays for the whole run once you have reached Level 2 campfire, and sits outside the Mushroom House. If you were hunting for a Bird Watcher to finish a chain, stop — he has been removed.

The honest answer depends partly on whether your camp can already feed itself, and that is a hunger question before it is a trading one. See [**Hunger**](/guides/hunger).

## Should You Plan Around Him?

**Yes — this is the most plannable progression in the game.** The order is fixed, the days are predictable, and nothing you do to prepare is wasted:

1. **Day 1: farm Bunny Foot and Wolf Pelts.** They are the two cheapest rungs and the only ones you can safely prep before he arrives.
2. **Day 2: do the first trade immediately.** Do not save the pelt for a better reward — the chain only advances when you spend it.
3. **Use the early rungs to fix your loadout**, not to hoard. If the trader offers a Good Axe or a Giant Sack on an early rung and your inventory is starving for one, take it; the later rungs are where the Iron Body lives.
4. **Spend the Bear Pelt on one thing only.** Armor or logging, not both — the [Iron Body](/items/how-to-get-iron-body) page lays out the trade-off.
5. **Never let the reward menu sit open.** Most of the losses above are one missed second.

## FAQ

**When does the Pelt Trader first appear?**
Day 2, and he returns every 3–4 days after that, leaving at night.

**Can another player take the item I want?**
No. The chain is client-sided — everyone has their own offers and their own menu.

**Can I get the Iron Body and the Strong Axe from one Bear Pelt?**
No. You pick one. See [How to Get the Iron Body](/items/how-to-get-iron-body).

**Why did he not ask for a Bear Pelt after the Alpha Wolf Pelt?**
That is a known bug, not a bad luck. The chain may simply stop there.

**Does the fifth Hunter trade give anything special?**
Reaching Hunter level 3 does add a fifth rung, and the published Hunter perks say it repeats the **Wolf Pelt** rung rather than adding a fifth pelt. Which item that rung awards when you take it is still not published by any source we found, so we cannot tell you.

**Is the Old Rod still available from the trader?**
No. It was replaced, and the replacement is now bought at the Tool Trader for 20 coins.

**Can I trade while he is leaving at night?**
You can try, but if the reward menu is open when he leaves it closes and the quest advances without giving you the item.
