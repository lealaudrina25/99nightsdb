The Steak is the only food in 99 Nights in the Forest with four prices, and the only one you will spend without noticing. It is the ingredient every first pet is built on, the currency the Beastmaster class fires, a dish in the Chef's book, and a valid fuel for the Biofuel Processor. Every page on this site that mentions it mentions it as a number inside someone else's table — a drop row on an animal page, a taming cost on a tame page, a fork value on the hunger table. None of them put the numbers together, and the one number nobody has printed is the one a player actually wants: how much Steak a whole run eats.

That is what this page does. It prices the item four ways, adds up the pet arithmetic in one table, and separates the animals that pay for a tame from the ones that only appear to.

Last verified: **2026-10-05**.

Meat is one food route; the renewable one runs through the [Fairy](/entities/the-fairy)'s seed packets, covered on her page.

## Quick Answer

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What it is | A **meat drop** from larger animals. It never spawns in the world |
| What it gives you eaten | **1 fork raw, 2 forks cooked** — and raw costs you health |
| The raw rule | Morsel, Steak and Ribs all **damage you raw**. Cooking roughly doubles them and removes the damage |
| Where it comes from | **Killing animals only.** Wolf, Alpha Wolf, Bear, Polar Bear, Mammoth, Hellephant, Horse, and per one source the Boar |
| Best single source | The **Mammoth**, at **8–9 Steak** in one kill |
| The one that pays nothing | The **Arctic Fox** drops no Steak at all, yet its tame costs 3 of them |
| Four things it buys | Food, a pet feed or summon, a Chef dish, and **Biofuel** |
| The badge it locks you out of | **Vegetarian** — which our own badges page already notes treats a Stew, and therefore a Steak, as meat |
| Earliest cook | A **campfire**; the **Infernal Sack** cooks it for you with no fire at all |

## One Fork Raw, Two Forks Cooked

The entire value of a Steak is two numbers, and they are agreed on by every source we could find: **one fork eaten raw, two forks cooked.** That doubling is not a bonus, it is the reason the raw version is a trap rather than a downgrade. Raw costs you health on top of giving you half the food.

Six of the seven sources that print fork values for the meat family — our own [hunger](/guides/hunger) table, plus Rosenberry Rooms, Stealthy Gaming, Sports Rant, BreezeWiki and the rblxguide item page — state the same shape for all three meats. Morsel is 1 raw and 2 cooked, Rib is 2 raw and 4 cooked, and Steak sits between them at 1 and 2. The only page we found that lists Steak without mentioning the raw penalty is rblxguide's item page, which prints the fork values and the drop list but skips the damage clause. It is the outlier, and the clause is too widely printed to doubt.

One wiki adds the conversion to the bar itself: a cooked Steak fills roughly **30%** of the hunger bar, the raw version 20–24%. That reading comes from BreezeWiki alone, so treat the percentage as a single-source figure and the fork count as the settled one.

The practical shape of the rule is that **a Steak you cannot cook is worse than a Steak you can.** On night one, with no fire, a raw Steak is a health tax with a small meal attached. The same Steak over a campfire is double the food and free of the tax. Every guide we read says the same, and our own [hunger](/guides/hunger) page calls it the mistake nearly every new player makes once.

The wolf in a Large Cabin drops this too, and our [Cabins](/locations/cabins) page says where it stands.

## The Animals That Drop It, and How Much

There is no farm, no trader, no chest. BreezeWiki puts it well: it is **not a natural spawn**, you have to kill something. What varies is which something, and how much.

| Animal | Steak | Where it lives |
|---|---|---|
| **Wolf** | **1** | Forest, Snow, Volcanic |
| **Alpha Wolf** | **1** | Snow, at night |
| **Bear** | **3–4** | Deep forest, from campfire level 3 upward |
| **Polar Bear** | **3–4** | Snow Biome |
| **Mammoth** | **8–9** | Snow; **neutral** until you hit it |
| **Hellephant** | **8–9, already cooked** | Volcanic Biome |
| **Horse** | **1–2** | Travels with the Furniture Trader |
| **Boar** | **1** | Jungle Biome — **one source only** |

The Wolf and the Alpha Wolf are the farm nobody appreciates: both drop exactly **1 Steak** per kill, which sounds like nothing until you remember that a Wolf is the cheapest kill in the game and that wolf packs travel in groups. Two hundred Steak is two hundred wolves, and a wolf pack is a few minutes of a spear.

The Mammoth is the opposite end. It drops **8–9 Steak** in a single kill, which our own [Mammoth](/entities/the-mammoth) page calls the largest single meal drop in the game, and it is neutral until you damage it — you choose when the run starts. That is why the Mammoth, not the Wolf, is the answer to "I am out of food between nights".

The Hellephant deserves its own row in the table rather than a footnote, because of one word: **cooked.** It drops **Cooked Steak**, and it is the only entity in the game that hands you ready-to-eat food without a fire, which the [Hellephant](/entities/the-hellephant) page covers at length. If you are hunting in the Volcanic Biome, the Steak you carry home is safe to eat the moment you pick it up.

The Boar is listed last and hedged on purpose. Our own [Boar](/entities/the-boar) page checked three sources, found Morsel agreed on all three, and found **Steak and Boar Tusk resting on a single source** with nobody corroborating them. We keep the row because the Steak drop is printed on the Boar page and because the wiki listings include the Boar, but we would not plan a route around it.

Two animals belong in this section by absence. The **Arctic Fox drops no Steak at all** — its drop table is Morsel and Pelt, confirmed by our own [Arctic Fox](/entities/the-arctic-fox) page and by two independent mob tables. The **Lava Mammoth and Mossy Mammoth** are listed as Steak sources by both wikis and by rblxguide, but nobody publishes a count for them, so they sit in the source list and not in a column.

## Four Ways to Spend a Steak

A Steak is not a currency with one exchange rate. It converts into four different things at four different rates, and which rate you are spending at decides whether the trade was good.

1. **Into your own hunger — 2 forks cooked.** The baseline. It is a mid-tier food, and BreezeWiki ranks it the second-worst meat in the game on hunger alone, with Morsel last. That ranking is worth knowing: converting a Mammoth into your own belly is a downgrade on the Mammoth.
2. **Into a pet — one tame, or one summon.** Wolf 6, Bear 5, Arctic Fox 3, Alpha Wolf 4, and 3 per Beastmaster summon. This is where the arithmetic below lives, and it is by far the largest sink in the game.
3. **Into a dish.** The Chef's **Steak Dinner** is Steak plus two free slots and restores about **150 hunger** — several Steaks' worth of food in one plate. The [Chef](/classes/chef-class) page carries the recipe.
4. **Into fire.** The **Biofuel Processor** accepts any food including raw, and one Biofuel holds the campfire at max intensity for about **five minutes**. That is the [biofuel](/items/how-to-get-biofuel) page's number, and it is the reason a Steak you cannot cook is still worth something.

The fourth one is the one players miss. If your fire has gone out and you have raw Steak and no time to cook, the Processor does not care whether the meat is raw. Burning a cooked Steak buys roughly five minutes of rain-proof burn for the price of two forks of food — a bad trade if you are starving and a good one if the storm is on top of you.

The other half of that bill is [Chili](/items/chili), and our Chili page argues it is the expensive half even though it reads as the cheaper food of the two.

Chili only ever arrives from a Seed Pack, as our [Seed Packs](/items/seed-packs) page explains.
The zero rows in that table are the [Bunny](/entities/the-bunny) and the Kiwi, the two tames with no meat on the bill.

The bottom of that ladder is not a Steak bill at all, it is the [Berry](/items/berry), one of which buys the Kiwi on the Old Flute.

## What a Tame Actually Costs You

Every taming page on this site prints the cost of a tame. None of them compare that cost to what the same animal would have dropped. Do that once and the whole pet economy changes shape.

The column that matters is the last one: **Steak you have to find somewhere else.** A tame does not kill the animal, so the Steak you feed it cannot be the Steak that animal would have dropped. You have to go and get it.

| Pet | Steak you spend | Steak one kill of the same animal would have paid | You must find |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bunny** | 0 | 0 | **0** — a cycle and a Berry |
| [Kiwi](/entities/the-kiwi) | 0 | 0 | **0** — one cycle, one Berry |
| **Wolf** | 6 | 1 | **5** |
| **Arctic Fox** | 3 | 0 | **3** — and the Chili on top |
| **Bear** | 5 | 3–4 | **1–2** |
| **Alpha Wolf** | 4 | 1 | **3** |
| **Beastmaster summon** | 3 | 0 | **3**, again, every run |

Three things fall out of that table.

**The Wolf is the most expensive common tame in the game.** It costs 6 Steak and pays 1 back, and it is the pet every beginner is told to get first. Taming one Wolf means killing six Wolves' worth of Steak to own the seventh animal. That is not a reason to skip it — a tamed Wolf is real DPS for the whole run — but it is the number to know before you decide the Wolf is cheap.

**The Bear is almost free.** It costs 5 Steak and drops 3–4 of them. Whatever counts as finding those, the Bear is the only common tame where the animal is paying most of its own bill. This is why our [Bear](/entities/the-bear) page and our [taming cost table](/classes/zookeeper-class) both print the Bear at 5, and why the Bear, not the Wolf, is the animal you route toward once your weapon can survive it.

**The Arctic Fox is the trap.** It costs 3 Steak and drops none, because it does not drop Steak. It is not a bad pet — it is fast and it packs — but it is the cheapest tame in the mid-game that gives you nothing back in the currency you are short of.

And then there is the option the table cannot hold: the **Beastmaster**. Its Level 1 perk starts the run with 3 Steak and spends 3 per summon, which puts it on the same 3-per-unit line as the Fox for a power that leaves when the run does. Our [Beastmaster](/classes/beastmaster-class) page works out the summon counters — 50 summons at Level 2 and 150 at Level 3 are roughly 150 and 450 Steak of spend — and the Zookeeper comparison page makes the tiebreaker plain: the Beastmaster's army is a food cost that resets every run, and the Zookeeper's pack is animals you own. If your runs are short, or the Steak is not reliable, the 400-Diamond class does not keep anything.

One animal sits outside this table entirely, and it is the one worth remembering. **The Mammoth's published tame bill is Pumpkin and Cake, with no Steak in it** — that is the Zookeeper cycle table's per-cycle attribution, not our invention. A Mammoth tame therefore costs you farm output rather than meat, while a Mammoth kill pays out 8–9 Steak. It is the only apex animal on this site where the food economy and the tame economy run in opposite directions.

## When the Steak Is Fuel, Not Food

The **Biofuel Processor** turns any food into Biofuel, and our [biofuel](/items/how-to-get-biofuel) page is explicit that raw works as well as cooked. Steak is on that list, and BreezeWiki independently notes that Steak can go in the Processor.

What that gives you is an escape valve for the Steak you cannot use. A run that is burning through meat faster than it can cook it has a second outlet, and a run with no campfire yet can still convert Steak into the fire it does not have.

The trade, stated plainly: **five minutes of max-intensity fire, for the price of two forks of food.** Whether that is good depends entirely on which of the two you are short of. It is not a strategy, it is a valve, and the valve only matters in the runs where the fire dies on the wrong night.

## The One Badge a Steak Locks You Out Of

Eating meat and chasing one badge are mutually exclusive, and the consequences are decided by the first bite. Our own [badges](/guides/all-badges) page lists **Vegetarian** as surviving 99 days eating no meat, and adds the clause that catches people out: **a Stew counts as meat.** A Stew built from Carrots, Morsels, Cakes and Steaks is therefore a Steak in the badge's eyes, which closes the loop the badge page appears to have opened.

BreezeWiki says the same thing from the other direction: Steak is non-vegetarian and players who want the Vegetarian badge must not eat it, which also rules out feeding it to a pet as part of the same run's plan.

So a Vegetarian run has a hard constraint most guides never state in the same sentence as the food system: **every Steak you pick up is a decision about the badge**, not just about dinner.

## Where the Steak Goes: Steak Dinner and the Crock Pot

Two dishes carry Steak, and both are worth more than the Steak they contain.

**Steak Dinner** is a Chef-only plate made from **Steak + Any + Any** — two free slots out of three — and it restores about **150 hunger**. Our [Chef](/classes/chef-class) page is the authority on that recipe, and it is the cheapest way in the game to turn one Steak into several forks' worth of food, because the two free slots can be crops you were eating anyway.

**Stew** is the Crock Pot dish that takes Carrots, Morsels, Cakes and Steaks and returns **four forks** with the warmth effect attached. That warmth clause is easy to miss: a Stew made in the Snow Biome warms you, which no other four-fork dish you can build from Steak does. The [hunger](/guides/hunger) page owns the full dish table.

## Where Sources Disagree

**1. How much Steak a Bear drops.** Sportskeeda, Saber Tooth Games, Roonby and our own [Bear](/entities/the-bear) page all print **3–4**. Stealthy Gaming and TechWiser both print **2**. We print 3–4 and we note that the difference is enough to decide whether a Bear tame is nearly free or nearly as bad as a Wolf tame, which is why this one is not a rounding detail. Nothing we found explains the gap; the two lower figures look like a floor rather than a range.

**2. How many animals drop Steak.** Our [hunger](/guides/hunger) table lists seven: Wolf, Alpha Wolf, Bear, Horse, Polar Bear, Mammoth, Hellephant. BreezeWiki and rblxguide both list **nine**, adding the **Boar**, the **Lava Mammoth** and the **Mossy Mammoth**. The two lists disagree in an interesting way: the Hellephant is in ours and its Steak is *cooked*, so it is not a source of raw-meat exposure at all, while the Boar is in theirs and rests on a single source here. We print both lists and let the per-animal rows carry the confidence.

**3. How Steak Dinner is made.** Our Chef page says **Chef Level 1** with **Steak + Any + Any**. Rosenberry Rooms, Stealthy Gaming and Sports Rant all describe the same 150-hunger dish as a **Crock Pot** recipe with no fixed combination. Every one of the six sources agrees the result is worth about 150 hunger; they disagree entirely about the station and the ingredient count, and we have not resolved it.

**4. Whether raw Steak damages you.** Five sources say yes, and one — rblxguide's item page — prints the fork values without the penalty. The penalty is too widely attested to treat that page as a disagreement so much as an omission, but it is the one place the rule is missing.

**5. The cooking methods.** BreezeWiki claims a Steak can also be brought to cooked state by killing a Steak-dropping animal with an **Infernal Sword, Infernal Crossbow, Flamethrower or Wildfire Potion**. That is one source and we have not corroborated it. The version of this we can back is the one our own [inventory](/guides/inventory-capacity) page publishes: the **Infernal Sack** cooks raw food placed inside it automatically, which achieves the same result in the sack rather than at the moment of the kill.

**In-site disagreement.** Our [hunger](/guides/hunger) page lists Steak's sources as seven animals and omits the Boar, while our own [Boar](/entities/the-boar) page reports a Steak drop from the Jungle Boar. We leave both as they are: the headcount on the hunger table is a curation, and the Boar row carries a single-source caveat that belongs to the Boar page. This is the eighth place where two pages of ours describe the same mechanic at different levels of confidence, and the difference is a matter of sourcing rather than of fact.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How do I get Steak in 99 Nights in the Forest?**
By killing animals. There is no spawn, no shop and no chest. Wolves and Alpha Wolves each drop 1, Bears and Polar Bears 3–4, Mammoths 8–9, and the Hellephant 8–9 in *cooked* form.

**Does raw Steak hurt you?**
Yes. It is one of the three meats — with Morsel and Rib — that damages the player when eaten raw, on top of giving you half the hunger of the cooked version. Cook it at a campfire first.

**How much Steak does it take to tame a Wolf?**
**6 Steak**, across three cycles at 2 per cycle. That is the figure our Wolf page prints and the fandom, Sportskeeda and allthings.how agree on. Taming a Bear costs 5, an Arctic Fox 3, and an Alpha Wolf 4 plus Stew and Corn.

**What is the fastest way to farm Steak?**
Killing Mammoths, if you can survive them — 8–9 in one kill, and they are neutral until you hit them. In the early game it is wolf packs, which drop 1 each and travel in groups; the Wolf is also the cheapest kill in the forest.

**Does Steak have to be cooked before feeding it to a pet?**
Our [pet food](/taming/pet-food) page says to cook everything first, because raw meat damages you and raw feeding is not a thing the taming pages recommend. The animal drops the requirement, not the meals you eat.

**Is Steak used in the Biofuel Processor?**
Yes. The Processor accepts any food including raw meat, and one Biofuel holds the campfire at max intensity for about five minutes. See [how to get biofuel](/items/how-to-get-biofuel).

**Will eating Steak stop me getting the Vegetarian badge?**
Yes. The badge requires eating no meat, and our own badges page notes that a Stew counts as meat, which closes the Stew loophole. One Steak eaten — or one Stew made from Steak — ends the badge's eligibility for that run.

**Does the Hellephant drop raw Steak?**
No. It drops **Cooked Steak**, and it is the only entity in the game that hands you cooked loot with no fire involved.
