The Pumpkin is the only four-fork crop in the game, and it is grown on a Farm Plot or not at all. That last clause is the whole page. Older guides tell you to look for pumpkins under trees, which was true for one update and has been false ever since; two independent wikis agree that the Halloween Update spawned them in the world and that the spawn was then nerfed into the plot system. So every "where do I find pumpkins" answer published before that change is describing a game that no longer exists, and every answer published after it says "farm plots, low chance" and stops there.

Crops that are not on the Farm Plot list come from Seed Packs — our [Seed Packs](/items/seed-packs) page explains which.
What sits in the gap is worth a page. One source publishes the only yield count anybody has printed — **6 plants per plot every 2 days**, drawn from a pool of four crops. None of the sources that call Pumpkin "rare" publish an odds figure at all, and none of them connect that count to the thing people actually save pumpkins for: the Mammoth, which wants 10 of them plus 5 Cake. This page does that connection, prices the Farmer class, and works out which of the two items really stops the tame.

## Where Pumpkins Come From Now, and Why Old Guides Still Say Trees

There is one answer and it is short: **a Farm Plot.** The fandom's Pumpkin page states it in the flat form — pumpkins are obtained only by growing on Farm Plots with a low chance, which the Farmer class increases, and it recommends the Farmer class on that basis. Dino Kid lists Pumpkin under "Farm Plots (low chance)" and calls it the best crop in the game. Sports Rant says the same thing from the other direction: pumpkins are grown in farm plots, and farming is now the only way to get them.

The history behind that sentence is what makes the old guides wrong, and two sources tell it. The fandom's Pumpkin page records that **in the Halloween Update, pumpkins spawned under trees and in random areas, and that they were then nerfed** — currently farm plots only. Sports Rant repeats it: pumpkins used to appear around the map during the Halloween event, but farming is now the only way to get them. Two sources, same direction, same event.

That is worth knowing before you trust a search result, because the "find pumpkins in the forest" articles are exactly the ones that rank well for people asking the question. They answer a version of the game that shipped before the nerf.

One more detail that belongs to this section: a Pumpkin can be put in your Sack and carried away from camp, which the fandom notes with a caveat — a Stew does the same job without occupying Sack space, so carrying raw pumpkins matters mostly on runs that never build a kitchen. The stockpiling side of that argument is on our [Hunger](/guides/hunger) page, which points out that food does not spoil outside Hard Mode, so a pumpkin harvested on day 5 is still a pumpkin on day 20.

Pumpkin is the farm that funds the tames which then eat Steak, and our [Steak](/items/steak) page is where that Steak bill is written out.

A Farm Plot's six plants every 2 days are not all the same crop, and [Carrot](/items/carrot) is the most likely of them, which our Carrot page prints as four published answers.

A plot is a Pumpkin engine and a Carrot backstop and nothing at all for a [Chili](/items/chili), which is why the Chili has to be ordered separately.

Those plots are the same ones the [Fairy](/entities/the-fairy)'s seed packets fill, so her page is the sibling read.

## What One Farm Plot Actually Yields

Here is the number that makes the rest of the page work, and it comes from a single source. Placed on the map, a Farm Plot **produces 6 plants every 2 days**. They are not all the same plant: the crop pool is **Carrot (most likely), Corn, Pumpkin, and Cake (least likely)** — that is the fandom's ordering of the four, restated by rblxguide, which is the only source we found that prints the count and the pool together.

Two things have to be unpacked from that, because both are load-bearing later.

**First: the pool has four entries, and the rarest is the one that matters.** Cake sits at the bottom of the ranking in the same pool, grown by the same plots, harvested on the same two-day cycle as the Pumpkin that everybody talks about. That is the only reason the Mammoth section below needs a calculation at all.

**Second: the count and the odds come from different sources and they do not agree.** Rblxguide gives you the denominator — 6 plants — but not the split. The fandom, Dino Kid and Sports Rant all give you the qualitative odds — "low chance", "quite rare" — but not the numerator. Nothing anywhere publishes the probability. So the honest range looks like this:

| Reading | Pumpkin per plot per 2 days | Across 20 plots per 2 days |
|---|---|---|
| **Flat:** Pumpkin is one of four, evenly | 6 ÷ 4 = **1.5** | **30** |
| **"Low chance":** Pumpkin is about one in ten | 6 ÷ 10 = **0.6** | **12** |

Both numbers are defensible from something a source actually says. Neither is the truth, and the gap between them is the difference between a farm that funds three Mammoths and one that funds slightly more than one. This is the single most useful thing a Pumpkin page can tell you, and no source tells it.

Two structural bonuses belong here rather than on the [Crafting Bench](/locations/crafting-bench) page, because both are about getting more pumpkins out of the plots you already own. **Plots stack**: you can place them on top of each other, provided you do not move or get pushed between placements, which is how 20 plots fit into a base that would not hold 20 individual structures. And the **Enchanted Farm Plot** upgrade — revive the Giant Tree in the Fairy Biome, open the Fairy Chest, discard the Farm Plot Tablet at the Crafting Bench — exists, with the sharp caveat that **it only affects blueprints you buy afterwards**, so a plot already in the ground is unchanged. Neither is a route to more pumpkins specifically, but both are why "20 plots" is a bigger number than it sounds.

## The Farmer Class: the Only Guaranteed Route

If the split is unpublished, the class that removes the uncertainty is the one thing a player can actually buy. The Farmer costs **80 Diamonds**, and our own [All Classes](/classes/all-classes) page summarises the perk in four words: *watering can for rarer crops and pumpkins*. That is the on-site statement. The per-level version comes from Dino Kid alone: **level 1 waters plots for rarer crops, level 2 grows apple trees from saplings, and level 3 guarantees a Pumpkin from every plot you water.**

Set that against the section above and the class stops looking like a flavour perk. At the flat reading a plot returns 1.5 pumpkins per cycle on a dice roll, and at the low-chance reading 0.6. A guarantee of one per watered plot is better than the flat reading and roughly double the low-chance one, and — the part that matters — it is a **floor rather than a median**. For a five-cycle apex tame, the difference between "I hope" and "I know" is worth 80 Diamonds on its own.

What you pay for it is on the [Class Levels](/guides/class-levels) page, which prices the Farmer's rungs and flags them honestly: **level 2 is 150 plants harvested and 50 plots watered, level 3 is 500 plants and 80 plots — and both pairs rest on a single source.** The Farmer is one of seven classes with published requirements but no page of its own on this site yet; it is listed there rather than omitted, with the source count attached. Treat the rungs as indicative until you have measured your own farm.

For contrast, the [Berry](/items/berry) has one sink a Pumpkin never gets: it is the taming currency for the cheapest pet in the game.

## What a Pumpkin Is For: Every Sink, Ranked

Five things spend pumpkins. Two of them are trivial and three of them are walls, which is the useful shape of the list.

| Sink | Cost | What gates it | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pumpkin Soup** | 1 Pumpkin + Any + Any | [Chef](/classes/chef-class) level 1 | 4 forks, and the **only** four-fork dish that keeps you warm |
| **Thanksgiving Challenge** | **2 Pumpkins, total** | The event | Sweet Potato Pie and BBQ Ribs, one pumpkin each |
| **The Bear tame** | 5 Pumpkin (most commonly reported) | [Strong Flute](/items/taming-flute) | The Bear's bill shifts between runs — read your own bubble |
| **The Mammoth tame** | **10 Pumpkin + 5 Cake** | [Strong Flute](/items/taming-flute) | The same bill a [Hellephant](/taming/how-to-tame-a-hellephant) wants |
| **Trick-or-treat candy** | — | Seasonal | Pumpkin Pie, Cotton Candy and friends give **no** hunger |

The first row is the one people overlook. Pumpkin Soup is a level-1 Chef dish that costs one pumpkin and two slots you were going to fill with anything, and it is the only four-fork dish in the game with a warmth clause attached. Our [Hunger](/guides/hunger) page notes that exactly two foods warm you, and our [Snow Biome](/locations/snow-biome) page notes that the two are a Stew and Pumpkin Soup. If your run goes into the cold, the cheapest possible use for a pumpkin is the one that also solves the other meter. The caveat is the one our Chef page already gives: the warmth only means anything while the snow is active, and in a run that never enters the cold, Pumpkin Soup is a Steak Dinner with worse flavour.

The second row is a relief rather than a wall, and it is worth reading twice because the number that gets passed around is wildly wrong. The fandom's Pumpkin page says the Thanksgiving Challenge needs pumpkins for **two** dishes — Sweet Potato Pie and BBQ Ribs — and that **2 pumpkins in total** are required to finish the whole challenge. A second, independent list of the ten Thanksgiving dishes agrees: Sweet Potato Pie takes a Pumpkin, BBQ Ribs take a Pumpkin, and those are the only two. Ten dishes, five of which are event food, and **two** of them want pumpkins. If you are farming plots for an event, that is not a construction project.

The last row is not really a sink, and it is the one that quietly eats a farm's output. Pumpkin Pie, Candy Corn, Candied Apple and Cotton Candy appear in every Halloween food round-up, and our own [Hunger](/guides/hunger) page lists them as temporary speed boosts with **no** fork value. They are event candy that happens to share a word with the crop. Do not count a Halloween run as a pumpkin harvest just because the pot is full of pies.

One more sink this month is event progress, and our [Scythe](/weapons/how-to-get-scythe) page covers the weapon tied to that event.

## Funding a Mammoth: the Arithmetic Behind the Bill

Now the part nobody publishes. The Mammoth and the Hellephant want **10 Pumpkin and 5 Cake** each — one and the same bill, per animal, on the [Strong Flute](/items/taming-flute), which is exactly what our [Mammoth](/entities/the-mammoth) and [Hellephant](/entities/the-hellephant) pages record.

Most guides copy that number across and then say "you need a lot of pumpkins." The interesting question is which of the two items stops you first, because the answer decides whether you build more plots or go and raid a building.

The structure of the problem is simple. Across 20 plots and one two-day cycle you harvest some number of Pumpkin, call it P, and some number of Cake, call it C. You can therefore fund **min(P ÷ 10, C ÷ 5)** animals. Between those two terms, the Cake side is satisfied by five per animal and the Pumpkin side by ten, so **Cake only becomes the thing you are short of if it is at least half as common per plot as the Pumpkin.** Below that line, the Pumpkin is what runs out first, and above it, the Cake does.

Here is where the two readings from the yield section land:

| Reading | P (Pumpkin) | C (Cake, "least likely" of the four) | Mammoths you can fund |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flat pool | 30 | 30 | **3 on Pumpkin, 6 on Cake** — Pumpkin binds |
| "Low chance" pool | 12 | 6 | **1.2 on Pumpkin, 1.2 on Cake** — break-even |

So the published sources sit on **opposite sides of the break-even line**, and that is a clean summary of how much is actually known about this. If the plot pool is roughly even, you can expect to finish off a Pumpkin surplus before the Cake does, and to be throwing away pumpkins for every Mammoth you tame. If Pumpkin is genuinely a low-chance drop and Cake the rarest of all, the two run level and the outcome is decided by noise on the day. Neither has been measured by anybody we can cite.

There is a resolution to the whole question, and it is not "build more plots." **Cake is not farm-only and Pumpkin is.** Our [Hunger](/guides/hunger) page lists Cake from four places — structures, the Clinic, Farm Plots and update parties — and our [Chef](/classes/chef-class) page repeats it. Pumpkin has exactly one source and one location. So if Cake is the scarce side, the fix is a walk to the Clinic, not a twenty-first farm plot; and the Farm Plot cap makes the plot route a hard wall anyway, which the [Crafting Bench](/locations/crafting-bench) page states as 20. If Pumpkin is the scarce side, plots are the only answer and the Farmer's level-3 guarantee is the only reliable one. Our [Best Pet](/taming/best-pet) page already reaches the same conclusion from the other end and says plainly that **Cake is the bottleneck and you should not eat your Cake for healing** if you are saving for one.

## Where Sources Disagree

**1. How many crops a plot grows.** The community wiki's corn page says a placed plot randomly grows one of **three** crops — Carrots, Pumpkins or Corn — with roughly a 30% chance of Corn, and does not mention Cake. Rblxguide's Farm Plot page lists **four** — Carrot most likely, then Corn and Pumpkin, then Cake as the rarest. Our own [Pet Food](/taming/pet-food) page lists five, including Chili and Cake from seeds. The difference is not cosmetic: if there are three crops, the flat reading is 2 pumpkins per plot per cycle rather than 1.5, and the whole table above shifts.

**2. The odds, which cannot coexist with the count.** Rblxguide gives the denominator, 6 plants; nobody gives the numerator. The fandom calls pumpkins "quite rare", Dino Kid says "low chance", and Sports Rant says "one of the strongest basic foods". If every plot really rolled evenly among four crops, the first two would be describing something that is not happening; if pumpkins are as rare as they sound, the flat row of the table above is fantasy. We print both rather than pick.

**3. What a Farm Plot costs.** 10 Wood, corroborated across our [Crafting Bench](/locations/crafting-bench) page, the community wiki and rblxguide. Dino Kid's food page says "20 Wood ×2 to build" — the only source we found that disagrees, and the only one that prints a figure at all on that topic.

**4. The Farmer's level-3 guarantee.** Dino Kid states that level 3 guarantees a Pumpkin from every watered plot. That is one source, and it is the only published statement of an odds figure anywhere on this topic, which makes it both the most useful line on the internet about pumpkins and the most fragile.

**5. The Thanksgiving list.** The fandom names Sweet Potato Pie and BBQ Ribs as the two pumpkin dishes and totals 2 pumpkins for the entire challenge; a second list of the same ten dishes agrees on both dishes and on the total. A third, GamerKe, publishes a different ten-dish table in which **Pumpkin Pie is the only pumpkin item**, at 2 Pumpkins plus Sugar and Milk — and says nothing about BBQ Ribs or Sweet Potato Pie. The one thing all three agree on is that Pumpkin appears in the event, which is not much of a consensus.

**6. Inside this site.** Our [Crafting Bench](/locations/crafting-bench) tier table describes a Farm Plot as growing **carrots** automatically every 2 days, and its FAQ says plots give 20 carrots. Our [Hunger](/guides/hunger), [Chef](/classes/chef-class) and [Pet Food](/taming/pet-food) pages all list Pumpkin as a plot crop. The weight of the site is clearly with the second group — three pages against one, and the single dissenting page is describing the plot in the narrowest possible terms — and the crop pool section above is written to that reading. The two-day cycle itself is agreed everywhere, including on the page that mentions only carrots.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is the Pumpkin the best food in the game?**
At four forks it is the maximum, and it is the best thing a farm plot produces. It is not uniquely the best per fork — cooked Ribs, cooked Shark and every Crock Pot dish are also four — it is uniquely the best per *plot*, because it is a four-fork crop on a renewable structure that costs nothing after the 10 Wood. That is the claim worth making, and it is the one the fandom makes when it calls Pumpkin the best crop from plots.

**Can pumpkins still spawn in the woods?**
Not any more, since the Halloween Update nerfed the wild spawn. If a guide tells you to search under trees, it is describing the game as it shipped during that event. Farm plots are the only source now.

**How many farm plots do I need for a Mammoth?**
Between one and three two-day cycles' worth of a full 20-plot farm, depending on where the truth sits in the table above — and you need the Cake to arrive with it, or a route to Cake that is not a plot. Measure your own runs rather than trusting either row.

**Do I have to cook a pumpkin?**
No. Raw, it is already four forks; the fandom records 4 Hunger Points for eating one directly. Cooking it into Pumpkin Soup costs you a Chef slot and buys you a two-minute warmth rather than more food.

**Which class should I buy for pumpkins?**
The Farmer, at 80 Diamonds, for the level-3 guarantee. Full detail on [All Classes](/classes/all-classes), and what the rungs cost on [Class Levels](/guides/class-levels).

**Can I raise pumpkins in the Snow Biome or the Jungle?**
We found nothing that says location changes the crop pool. Every source describes Farm Plots as a single universal structure, and our [Best Base Design](/guides/best-base-design) page treats them as portable infrastructure — placeable anywhere, which is the point of them.
