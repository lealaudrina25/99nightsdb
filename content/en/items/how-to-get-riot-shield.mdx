The Riot Shield is the only defensive item in 99 Nights in the Forest you hold rather than wear — and that single fact is where almost everyone goes wrong with it. It does not replace your armor. It stacks on top of it.

Last verified: **2026-09-30**.

## Quick Answer

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What it is | A held shield — **not a weapon**, and not body armor |
| What it does | Cuts incoming damage while raised, and **blocks all arrows** |
| How to get it | **Ruby Chests**, or craft it at the **Anvil in the Tool Workshop** |
| Recipe | **4 Cultist Gem + 4 Bear Pelt + 6 Wolf Pelt + 6 Bunny Foot + 25 Scrap** |
| The catch | You **cannot attack while holding it** |
| Worth it? | Yes, if you play melee or tank for a team |

## What It Actually Does — Read This Part

Three things are stated the same way by every source that covers the shield, so treat them as fact:

1. **It reduces the damage you take — but only while it is raised.** Held down, the shield absorbs a large share of incoming hits. Lowered or stowed, it does nothing.
2. **It blocks arrows outright.** The fandom wiki is explicit: it "blocks all arrows shot at its holder." This is the shield's single best feature, and it is why it counters the ranged enemies in the game.
3. **It works in tandem with your armor.** The shield is a *held* item, not a body slot, so a Riot Shield plus an Iron Body or [Thorn Body](/items/how-to-get-thorn-body) is the intended combination — not a choice between them.

The frontier of the protection is your **front**. Sources that go into detail describe the reduction as covering the direction you are facing, which means anything that gets behind you — the Deer, Bears, a flanking Wolf — lands at full damage. The shield makes you durable going forward, not durable in a circle.

**And the rule that catches people out: you cannot attack with any weapon while holding the shield.** This is stated plainly by the fandom wiki and it is the whole tactical shape of the item. You raise it to survive a window, then drop it to swing. If you try to hold it permanently, you are a very tough player who never kills anything.

A practical detail worth knowing: when you are not using it, **put the shield in your backpack**. A held item occupies a hotbar slot, and sources describe experienced players losing firepower because a shield sat in hand "just in case."

## How to Get It

There are two routes, and they are independent — you never have to do both.

### Route 1 — Ruby Chests

The Riot Shield is a possible drop from **Ruby Chests**. Sources that list its locations name the Ruby Chest first. Ruby Chests are the mid-high chest tier; the most repeatable supply of them is the **Cultist Stronghold** loop, which is also where the [Cultist Gems](/items/cultist-gem) for the crafting route come from. The full farming loop, including how the Stronghold resets, is on [Cultist Stronghold](/locations/cultist-stronghold).

It is a loot roll, not a guarantee — a Ruby Chest that can drop a Riot Shield can also drop other equipment. Expect to open several. Riot Shields come from the Ruby tier specifically; the other six tiers are on [Chest Tiers](/items/chest-tiers).

### Route 2 — Craft It at the Tool Workshop

The shield is one of the Tool Workshop's exclusive recipes. Craft it at the **Anvil inside the [Tool Workshop](/locations/tool-workshop)** — the large stone-brick building with vines and an anvil icon on the map. That is the same station that builds the Infernal Helmet and the Chainsaw, so the setup work is identical. For the build itself, see [How to Get the Chainsaw](/weapons/how-to-get-chainsaw).

To use the Anvil you first have to assemble it: defeat the cultists guarding the building, collect the anvil pieces scattered across its floors, and place them on the blueprint.

Six of the materials in that recipe are [Bunny](/entities/the-bunny) feet, and our Bunny page is the farming plan behind them.

## The Recipe

Every source that publishes a Riot Shield recipe publishes the same one:

| Material | Amount | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **Cultist Gem** | 4 | Cultist kills, or Volcano sacrifices — see [Cultist Stronghold](/locations/cultist-stronghold) |
| **Bear Pelt** | 4 | Bears — see [The Bear](/entities/the-bear) |
| **Wolf Pelt** | 6 | Wolves — see [The Wolf](/entities/the-wolf) |
| **Bunny Foot** | 6 | Bunnies — the small wildlife around the map (the same foot also buys [warm clothing](/items/warm-clothing)) |
| **Scrap** | 25 | Ground-up metal — see [How to Get Scrap](/items/how-to-get-scrap) |

The **25 Scrap** is the real cost, not the gems. Four Cultist Gems is a short Stronghold run, but 25 Scrap means feeding a lot of metal into the Grinder first. If Scrap is your bottleneck, [How to Get Scrap](/items/how-to-get-scrap) covers the fastest sources and which junk items grind into the most.

Give yourself a margin on the pelts: **take 6–8 Wolf Pelts and 8 Bunny Feet rather than exactly 6 each**, because those two come from small mobs you kill incidentally and running one short after a workshop run is a wasted trip.

## This Is Not a Weapon — the Mistake to Avoid

It is easy to file the Riot Shield next to the Morningstar or the Ice Sword because it is craftable at the same bench as the workshop weapons. It is not one.

- **It deals no damage.** There is no attack while it is raised.
- **It is not a body armor.** It does not go in the armor slot, which is exactly why it stacks with the armor you are already wearing.
- **Its job is to buy a window, not to fight.** You raise it to cross open ground under arrow fire, to open a chest in a storm, or to close distance on a ranged enemy — then you drop it and swing.

Because of that, the shield's best partner is a **slow melee weapon**. The [Morningstar](/weapons/how-to-get-morningstar) is the classic example: its one-second swing roots you in place, and the shield covers exactly that gap. If you are a ranged player, the shield is far less useful to you.

Real melee weapons are the other side of that warning, and our [Scythe](/weapons/how-to-get-scythe) page covers one that has a live and an expired version.

## Is It Worth Building?

**Yes, for anyone playing melee or acting as the team's tank.** It is the difference between advancing under a Cultist Crossbow volley and dying to it, and it works from the moment you craft it. In multiplayer, one player holding a shield and drawing aggro makes the whole fight easier for everyone else.

**Skip it if you only fight at range.** The 25 Scrap and four gems are better spent elsewhere, and a shield you never raise is just a hotbar slot you cannot use.

One honest limit: the shield reduces damage, it does not remove it, and it does nothing for your back. It softens mistakes — it does not make you invincible. See [Best Weapons](/weapons/best-weapons) for what to pair it with, and [All Classes](/classes/all-classes) if you are picking a class around a tanky frontline role.

## FAQ

**Is the Riot Shield a weapon?**
No. You cannot attack while holding it, and it deals no damage. It is a defensive held item.

**Does it replace my armor?**
No — that is the point. It is held, not worn, so it stacks with whatever body armor you have on. Riot Shield plus [Thorn Body](/items/how-to-get-thorn-body) or Iron Body is the intended setup.

**Can I just hold it all the time?**
You can, but you will not kill anything. Raise it for the dangerous window, then stow it and fight. When you are not using it, keep it in your backpack so it does not eat a hotbar slot.

**Does it stop arrows?**
Yes — all arrows fired at the holder, per the fandom wiki. That is its strongest use against ranged enemies.

**Can I get it without crafting?**
Yes. It is a Ruby Chest drop as well as a Tool Workshop recipe, so a lucky chest can save you the 25 Scrap entirely.

**Does it protect my back?**
No. The reduction covers the front. Enemies that flank you — the Deer, Bears, Wolves — hit at full damage.
