## Why Wood Comes Before Everything Else

Wood goes into the campfire, and the campfire's level gates the map. Every level you add opens more terrain, which is where the better chests — and the better axes — actually are. Levelling the fire is not a side task; it is the progression system.

Hard Mode puts a ceiling on that loop. From 60% Corruption most stored food rots in ninety seconds and cannot be cooked back, which is the single biggest reason a Hard Mode run ends. The counterplay is on [Hard Mode](/guides/hard-mode).

That is also why the two jobs reinforce each other: **better axe → more wood → higher campfire → deeper map → better axe.**

## The Axes, and Where Each One Comes From

Your starting axe cannot fell large trees, and large trees are where saplings come from. Getting past it is the first real goal.

| Axe | How to get | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Old Axe | Starting tool | Small trees only — not enough to start a farm |
| Good Axe | Chests; or the Pelt Trader for two Bunny Feet | Comfortable step up, and the Lumberjack's starting tool |
| Ice Axe | Ice Chests | Also applies a brief chill to enemies |
| Strong Axe | Pelt Trader's fourth trade; Gold and Legendary chests | **One hit per tree** — this is the one that makes a sapling farm possible |
| Chainsaw | Ruby Chests; or crafted at the Tool Workshop | Fully automatic hold-to-cut; see [How to Get the Chainsaw](/weapons/how-to-get-chainsaw) |
| Admin Axe | Update Party events only | Exclusive; cannot be obtained outside those events |

**The Strong Axe is the one that matters.** Everything below assumes you have it, a Good Axe equivalent, or better.

## The Sapling Loop

This is the whole method.

1. **Fell at least four large trees.** They drop wood and, often, a **Sapling** each.
2. **Pick a flat open plot near camp.** Not scattered around the forest — one dedicated plot.
3. **Plant the saplings there and let them grow.** Growth takes roughly a day–night cycle. Spend that time looting.
4. **Chop the young trees.** A grown sapling goes down in **one swing**, where a wild large tree takes several.
5. **Collect the new saplings and replant immediately.** The loop now sustains itself.

Why it beats foraging: one-hit chops instead of multi-hit, zero travel time, and it never runs out. Each cycle you plant more than you started with, so production grows.

## How to Plant Them: Sources Disagree

Worth flagging, because you will see both instructions online and they are opposites.

**Most sources say spread them out, in a U or V shape**, with the two ends pointing towards you. The reason is mechanical: an axe swing can catch several adjacent trees at once, so a curved row lets one swing fell a cluster. Planted directly on top of each other, the swing only connects with one.

**One popular guide says the opposite** — stack the saplings on the same spot, claiming the resulting clump yields more.

Our reading: the U/V layout is described by more sources and comes with a reason attached, so we recommend it. But if you have the patience, test both on a couple of plots and keep whichever your axe actually connects with more often. This is the kind of thing that shifts with patches.

- **Whoever builds the station pays.** [Chef and Cook](/classes/chef-class) — the Chef's Station wants 25 Wood on top of a fire that wants far more than that.

That bottleneck is a capacity problem rather than an axe problem, and it is priced sack by sack on our [sacks and inventory capacity](/guides/inventory-capacity) guide.

## The Bottleneck Nobody Plans For: Your Sack

Chopping speed is rarely the limit. **Carrying capacity is.** A slow axe with a big sack beats a fast axe with a small one, because the real cost is walking back and forth.

| Sack | Capacity |
|---|---|
| Old Sack | 5 slots |
| Good Sack | 15 slots |
| Giant Sack | 25 slots |
| Infernal Sack | 20 slots (22 as Scavenger); cooks raw food stored inside |

**The Good Sack triples your capacity** and is the single biggest quality-of-life jump in the early game. Get it before you obsess over axe upgrades.

The Ammo Crate's second ingredient is Wood, so the wood route our [Ammo and Guns](/guides/ammo-guide) guide has sits directly behind the crate's cost.


If wood is what you want to be good at, Lumberjack costs **70 Diamonds** and is one of the cheaper classes.

- Starts with a **Good Axe**, skipping the worst stage entirely.
- Chance for a chopped tree to drop an **extra Log**.
- At full level: a **25% chance** of dropping an extra Sapling, and the bonus log becomes 1–2 Logs instead of one.

The trade-off is real, though: Lumberjack has **no combat ability** and levels slowly. It is a specialist, and on a team it is at its best when someone else handles the fighting. See [Best Class](/classes/best-class) for how it compares if you are deciding what to spend Diamonds on.

## The Team Method

With three or four players, split the jobs rather than everyone doing everything:

- **Player 1 chops** — nothing else, just keeps felling.
- **Player 2 collects logs** and feeds the campfire.
- **Player 3 collects only saplings** and plants them in the plot.

A crew running this properly can push the campfire to max level in well under a week, which unlocks the deep chests everyone else is still walking towards. One player can then run the fire and crafting bench alone while the rest explore.

## Mistakes That Waste Time

- **Chopping wild trees far from camp.** Travel is the cost, not the chopping.
- **Forgetting to replant.** Break the loop once and you are back to foraging.
- **Ignoring the sack.** More swings into a 5-slot bag gets you nowhere.
- **Using the Chainsaw as a weapon.** It has low damage and short range. It is a logging tool, not a way to fight.
- **Being out at night for wood.** Night is when [The Deer](/entities/the-deer) hunts, and a heavy sack makes you slow. Daylight is for wood.
