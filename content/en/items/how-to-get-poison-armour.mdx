The Poison Armour is the **counter-attack** body piece: it poisons anything that hits you in melee, so a wolf or a cultist that keeps swinging at you slowly kills itself. It is the signature armour of the **Poison Master** class, and the defensive half of the game's poison build alongside the [Poison Spear](/weapons/how-to-get-poison-spear).

Last verified: **2026-09-30**.

## What It Actually Does — Read This Part

**Its signature is offense disguised as defence.** Anything that lands a **melee** hit on you takes poison damage over the next few seconds. It works exactly like the [Thorn Body](/items/how-to-get-thorn-body)'s damage reflection — same trigger, same "only melee" rule — except that instead of bouncing damage back instantly, it applies a **poison damage-over-time** that keeps ticking after the hit.

Two things follow from that:

- **It is brutal against melee swarms.** Wolves, cultists and [Boars](/entities/the-boar) that crowd you poison themselves to death while attacking. One write-up (Sportskeeda) puts it concretely: a wolf takes about **15 poison damage per strike** on the armour and dies after roughly **five hits**.
- **It does nothing against ranged attackers.** An arrow or a crossbow bolt never touches you in melee, so it never triggers. Against a Crossbow Cultist this armour might as well be a T-shirt.

**The poison stacks with other poison sources.** The armour's poison ticks alongside the poison from a Blowpipe or [Poison Spear](/weapons/how-to-get-poison-spear), so a poison build layers several damage-over-times on the same target at once.

### The one thing every guide disagrees about

**Does the Poison Armour also reduce incoming damage?** This is where the sources split, and it is worth being straight about it:

- **Several say yes.** 99-nights-in-the-forest.com rates its damage reduction **"medium-high"**; Pocket Tactics and 99nights-intheforest.net both list it as reducing incoming damage; Roonby calls it a piece that "provides defence along with poison resistance." One source even publishes a number — the Namu wiki (cited on our [Thorn Body](/items/how-to-get-thorn-body) page) puts the Poison Armour at roughly **39% damage reduction**, below Iron's ~62% — but no other source gives figures, so treat that number as unverified rather than a stat.
- **Two say no, flatly.** Sportskeeda states the armour **does not reduce damage at all** — you take the full hit and only the attacker gets poisoned — and Deltia's Gaming agrees, calling it "less defensive than the Iron Body or Alien Armour."
- **The fandom wiki stays silent.** Its Armour page records a protection figure for every other body (Leather "slightly protects," Iron "protects greatly," Thorn "greater than Iron and Alien," Obsidiron "greatly reduces") — but for the Poison Armour it lists **only the poison effect**, no protection claim at all.

**The practical read:** do not buy this armour for its damage reduction. The one thing all sources agree on is the poison, so treat the Poison Armour as **an offensive piece whose defensive value is disputed and at best modest** — not as the mid-game tank body that the [Iron Body](/items/how-to-get-iron-body) is.

## Where the Poison Armour Sits

There are **seven wearable armour variants** in the game (plus the held [Riot Shield](/items/how-to-get-riot-shield)). The Poison Armour is **not a rung on the protection ladder** — it is a specialist side-grade that sits off to one side of it:

| Tier | Armour | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Early | Leather Body | Starter set (Brawler spawns with it) |
| Mid | [Iron Body](/items/how-to-get-iron-body) | The standard mid-game target |
| Mid-high | [Alien Armour](/items/how-to-get-alien-armour) | Above Iron; feeds Alien Tech Energy |
| High | [Thorn Body](/items/how-to-get-thorn-body) | Above Iron *and* Alien; reflects melee damage |
| End | Obsidiron Body | The toughest; Meteor event only |
| **Side-grade** | **Poison Armour** | **This page** — poisons melee attackers instead of adding much protection |

**The permanent-armour rule applies here too.** The fandom wiki and rblxguide both state that armour **cannot be unequipped once worn** and can only be swapped for a *strictly better* piece. For the Poison Armour that cuts in your favour: **the game itself ranks it below the Iron Body**, so picking up an Iron Body (or an [Alien Armour](/items/how-to-get-alien-armour)) later will replace it automatically — wearing it does not lock you out of a real defensive upgrade.

The pond version of that route is worth understanding before you commit an afternoon to it: the frog pools roll the same table as the chest, at the same rates, after the boss is already dead. [**Fishing in 99 Nights in the Forest**](/guides/fishing-guide) explains the mechanics.

Poison Armour sits on the same meteor bench line as several armours, and our [Weather and Night Events](/guides/weather-and-night-events) guide maps the material chain.


### Route 1 — The Poison Master Class (guaranteed)

Buy the **Poison Master** for **200 Diamonds** and it **spawns with the Poison Armour equipped** (and a Blowpipe in hand, according to Deltia's Gaming). This is the **only deterministic source**, and at 200 Diamonds it is one of the cheaper classes — which makes it the cheapest guaranteed armour in the game short of the free ones. For farming the diamonds, see [How to Get Diamonds Fast](/items/how-to-get-diamonds-fast), and for where the class sits against the others, see [All Classes](/classes/all-classes).

What the class's three levels actually do to that poison — stronger ticks, the 25% spread, and the damage buff at Level 3 — and the one claim nobody has confirmed about whether the armour's own poison scales with them, are on the [Poison Master class page](/classes/poison-master-class).

### Route 2 — The Frog Chest (Frog King, ~1%)

The **Frog Chest** that the [Frog King](/entities/the-frog-king) drops in the Frog Cave can contain the Poison Armour — but the drop rate is **about 1%**, making it one of the rarer items in his loot pool (the same chest gives the Poison Spear, Blowdart and Frog Boots, all at similarly low rates). The route: collect **8 Frog Keys** (one per forest pond), jump the **giant whirlpool** into the cave, clear the enemies and the Frog King, then open the chest. The full step-by-step is on [How to Get the Poison Spear](/weapons/how-to-get-poison-spear).

### Route 3 — Fishing the Frog Cave Pond (~1%)

The ponds **inside the Frog Cave** use the same loot pool as the boss chest. Sportskeeda and Deltia's Gaming both put the fishing chance at **1%**, so if the chest roll did not give you the armour, you can fish for it while you are already down there. Bring an upgraded rod and food — it can take a long while.

### Route 4 — The Overlord Jungle Chest (Mother Temple, "high" chance)

The **Jungle Biome** added a faster route. Clear the four **Jungle Temples**, collect the **4 Crystal Skull Keys** from their altars, and place them on the **Mother Temple** to open its hidden treasure room. That room's chest — called the **Overlord Jungle Chest** (or simply the Jungle Chest) — is listed by the fandom wiki, 99-nights-in-the-forest.com and several Jungle guides as a **high-chance** source of the Poison Armour, alongside the Poison Claws and Blowpipe.

Two practical notes: the Jungle Biome needs your **campfire at Level 3** before it appears, and the treasure-room chest **opens once per player** (the gems respawn, the chest does not). For the map side of this, see [All Locations](/locations/all-locations).

**The honest read:** the class is the only way to get it *on demand*. The Frog King, the frog pond and the Mother Temple are all dice rolls — but the Mother Temple roll is the friendliest of the three.

Worth weighing against the much cheaper option of simply being revivable, which costs forty Diamonds and a handful of rabbit feet. See [**Death and Respawn**](/guides/death-and-respawn).

## Is It Worth Getting?

**Yes — if you are running the poison build.** The Poison Master class hands you this armour for free, its poison stacks with the Blowpipe and the [Poison Spear](/weapons/how-to-get-poison-spear), and against melee-heavy nights it turns every attacker into a slow bleed. It is also the one body piece that keeps working while you do nothing.

**No — if what you actually want is protection.** This is the part most guides gloss over. Every source agrees it poisons attackers; they do **not** agree that it reduces damage, and two say it does not. If you want to take less damage, the [Iron Body](/items/how-to-get-iron-body) is the mid-game answer and the [Thorn Body](/items/how-to-get-thorn-body) reflects melee *and* protects better. Take the Poison Armour for the poison, not for the armour.

## FAQ

**What does the Poison Armour actually do?**
It applies a poison damage-over-time to anything that hits you with a **melee** attack. Ranged attackers are unaffected.

**Does it reduce incoming damage?**
Sources disagree — several say it does (one rates it "medium-high"), two say it does not at all, and the fandom entry lists no protection figure. Do not rely on it for defence; its real job is the poison.

**Can I craft it?**
No. There is no recipe — it comes from the class, chests or fishing.

**Which class starts with it?**
Only the **Poison Master** (200 Diamonds). No other class spawns wearing it.

**Can I take it off later?**
Armour cannot be unequipped once worn; it can only be replaced by a better piece. Since the game ranks it below the Iron Body, picking up an Iron or Alien body replaces it automatically.

**Does the poison stack with my Blowpipe?**
Yes — Sportskeeda confirms the armour's poison stacks with the Blowpipe's, so a poison build adds several damage-over-times to the same target.

**Is it better than the Iron Body?**
For protection, no — two sources state it does not reduce damage and the game treats it as inferior to Iron. For a poison build it does a different job entirely.
