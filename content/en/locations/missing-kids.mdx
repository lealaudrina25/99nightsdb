There are four missing kids, and rescuing them is the main quest of 99 Nights in the Forest. Each one is locked in a coloured-key cave guarded by a pack. Clear the guards, take the key they drop, open the door, carry the kid home.

Those rescues are also the account gate on Hard Mode for the one source that states one, which pairs 20 days survived with a single kid rescued. What the unlock is claimed to require is spelled out on [Hard Mode](/guides/hard-mode).

**Every rescue permanently adds +1 to your day-counter multiplier for that run.** That is the whole reason to prioritise them: four kids is four days of progress gained without surviving four extra nights.

Last verified: **2026-09-29**.

Rescued children eventually produce food at camp, which is the same job one cheap class does with a rod. See [**Fisherman Class**](/classes/fisherman-class) for that comparison.

## The Four Kids at a Glance

| Kid | Guardians | Key | Campfire needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dino Kid | 5 Wolves (red collars) | Red | Level 2–3 |
| Kraken Kid | 4 Alpha Wolves (blue collars) | Blue | Level 4 (some sources say 3–5) |
| Squid Kid | 2 Bears (yellow collars) | Yellow | Level 5 |
| Koala Kid | 6 Bears (black collars) | Grey, sometimes called Black | Level 6 |

Two details that trip people up:

- **The last guard killed drops the key.** Leave one wolf alive and you get nothing.
- **Cave placement is procedural.** There is no fixed map to memorise — the caves are in different places every run, so the arrow is the only reliable guide.

That +1 is the multiplier half of [speedrun in 99 Nights in the Forest](/guides/speedrun-guide), and the reason the caves are a speedrun objective rather than a sidetrack.

Orienteering is what the detour pays for, and it cannot share a run with Self-Preservation — the two are listed side by side on our [All Badges](/guides/all-badges) page.

## Why the Detour Pays

**The day counter.** Each rescue is +1 multiplier, permanently. Stacked with beds, all four kids push a run towards the 9x maximum — the difference between crawling to night 99 and getting there comfortably.

**Cave loot scales with difficulty.** The Dino cave holds Common, Good and Iron chests. By the Koala cave you are looking at Gold and Ruby, with a Ruby Chest close to guaranteed. The tiers behind those chests, and their campfire cost, are on [Chest Tiers](/items/chest-tiers). The later caves are among the best loot rooms in the game.

**Two badges, and they oppose each other.** Rescuing all four earns **Orienteering** (plus 4 Diamonds). Finishing a run with **zero** rescues earns **Self-Preservation** (also 4 Diamonds). You cannot get both in one run — pick which one you are chasing before you start.

## How to Actually Find Them

1. **Use the Missing Kids board** next to your campfire. Click a child's poster and a **red arrow** appears at the top of your screen pointing to their location.
2. **Craft a Compass** if you have one — it marks the same spot, and saves you re-clicking the board.
3. **Listen.** The guarding pack is audible before you see it, and you can hear the children crying softly just outside the cave door.
4. **Clear every guard,** take the key from the last one.
5. **Open the door, loot the chests, pick up the kid** and carry them back in your sack. They settle at the edge of the firelight and pitch a tent.

A small detail worth knowing: **the tent colours are tied to rescue order, not to which child.** The sequence is always Red, Blue, Yellow, Purple. Save Koala Kid first and she still gets the red tent.

## The Four Caves

**Dino Kid — Level 2–3, Red Key.** Five wolves with red collars, on a fenced hill. This is the easy one and the correct first target: do it as soon as your campfire reaches the level. Recommended for a first run even with starter gear.

**Kraken Kid — Level 4, Blue Key.** Four Alpha Wolves, noticeably tougher than the red-collared pack. Bring a ranged weapon and pick them off from a vantage point rather than trading hits in the open. Some sources put the unlock anywhere from Level 3 to 5, so check the board.

**Squid Kid — Level 5, Yellow Key.** Two Bears. Reaching Level 5 also unlocks the full map, which is its own reward. Bears are tanky — **craft bear traps and bait them in** — the kill order and the reset rule are on our [Bear Trap](/items/bear-trap) page — rather than standing and shooting. Morning Star or Tactical Shotgun if you have one. Going in with one or two teammates is a very different fight.

**Koala Kid — Level 6, Grey Key.** Six Bears, and the hardest encounter in the main quest. Upgrade armour, stock ammo and bandages, and be willing to **clear it across multiple days** — disengage at dusk and come back. Pushing on after dark into six bears is how runs end.

## Fighting Notes

- **Wolves: aim for the head.** Headshots save you bandages over a long run.
- **Bears: traps, not trades.** They survive far more damage than you want to absorb. Lay traps and pull them through.
- **Keep a strong ranged weapon** — revolver, shotgun, raygun — for the later caves specifically.
- **Night is the deadline.** [The Deer](/entities/the-deer) hunts after dark, and its pathing is bad enough that a cave mouth is a bad place to be cornered.

Every rescue on this page is behind a campfire level, so this is a fuel ladder more than a combat one. The six levels and their costs are on [Campfire](/guides/campfire).

It is also the hard ceiling on a [speedrun in 99 Nights in the Forest](/guides/speedrun-guide), which cannot reach 9x without this rescue.

## Reaching Level 6 Is the Real Work

All four rescues are gated behind campfire levels, and Koala Kid needs Level 6. That is a wood problem before it is a combat problem — see [How to Get Wood Fast](/items/how-to-get-wood-fast), because a run that fails to raise the fire never sees the last two caves at all.

Once they are home, the kids are not just decoration. Since the **Kids Have Jobs** update they each take a passive camp job and generate resources in the background; the details are on [Update Log & Patch Notes](/guides/updates-patch-notes).
