The single most-cited speed fact in 99 Nights in the Forest is that the [Assassin](/classes/assassin-class) has the fastest sprint in the game. It costs 500 Diamonds and it is wrong — or at least incomplete, because the same class that beats it gives the bonus away for three rabbit feet.

Sprint speed is not a stat any source tabulates, so it lives in fragments: a line in a class perk table, a row in a Pelt List, a footnote on the hunger page. This page collects all of them into one place for the first time — every class boost, every food and structure boost, the hidden cost of sprinting, and the two states where the game takes your sprint away entirely.

Last verified: **2026-10-08**.

## The Fastest Sprint Is Not the Assassin

The Assassin's +10% is described by rblxguide as "the fastest sprinting speed in the game", and that is the line nearly every guide repeats. The fandom wiki disagrees with it in its own Trivia section: the Assassin is **second**, behind a fully-levelled **Alien Scientist** by 3%.

Both are beaten by something neither page is talking about. The [Big Game Hunter](/classes/big-game-hunter-class)'s Pelt List has a row that reads **"Bunny Foot ×3 — Sprint 15% faster"**, and it is not level-gated, biome-gated, or paid for in Diamonds beyond the class itself. The class's own page says it plainly: "Three Bunny Feet beat the Assassin's sprint, with no health penalty attached."

So the ladder is:

1. **Big Game Hunter, 3 Bunny Feet — +15%**, no HP cost. The catch is bureaucratic, not economic: you need the 600-Diamond class, and you need to have killed three bunnies.
2. **Alien Scientist, Level 3 — ~+13%**. The most expensive route and the slowest to turn on, since it wants the class levelled.
3. **Assassin, Level 1 — +10% and −15% HP.** The fastest sprint **you can buy on day one** with no setup, which is why it still owns the reputation.

The point is not that the Assassin is bad. It is that if you are choosing a class *for the sprint*, the +10% is the third-best number in the game and the only one that also cuts your health. The reason players keep naming it is that it is the only one that is active the second you spawn.

## The Class Boosts, Line by Line

Four classes touch sprint directly, and they do it in three different ways — a flat percentage, a doubled state, and a penalty you have to manage.

| Class | The sprint rule | The cost |
|---|---|---|
| [Assassin](/classes/assassin-class) | **+10%**, always on, from Level 1 | **−15% max HP** (85 HP total) |
| [Big Game Hunter](/classes/big-game-hunter-class) | **+15% after 3 Bunny Feet** on the Pelt List | 600 Diamonds + the kills |
| [Berserker](/classes/berserker-class) | **×2 sprint** while its Level-2 damage boost is up | 100 Diamonds; only during the boost |
| [Alien](/classes/alien-class) / [Cyborg](/classes/cyborg-class) | **Faster at a full charge bar, slower while overheating** | Firing the alien tech trades your sprint for damage |

The Alien and Cyborg are the interesting ones, because their sprint is a **resource you spend**. Firing drops the charge bar; a low bar slows your sprint; a full bar speeds it up. The Alien page calls the overheating penalty "what gets Alien players killed", and the correct play — stop firing before the bar empties if anything hostile is near — is a sprint decision, not a damage one.

The Berserker's ×2 is the largest multiplier any single source reports, but flag it: it comes from **one** community breakdown of the fandom wiki, it lasts only as long as the boost, and no other source repeats the figure.

Class bonuses also apply to the weapon you swing, and our [Scythe](/weapons/how-to-get-scythe) page covers the class-locked melee weapon they multiply.

## The Food and Structure Boosts

If you do not want to buy a class, three consumables and one structure do the job.

- **[Chili](/items/chili)** — the cheapest repeatable boost. One Chili for a short speed spike, and the reason it shows up in [speedrun](/guides/speedrun-guide) plans: eat it the moment a [Deer](/entities/the-deer) closes, not after the gap is already lost.
- **[Ice Temple](/locations/ice-temple)** — the strongest single boost in the game at **roughly triple normal sprint speed**, and it charges for free on the spot. Our Ice Temple page benchmarks it against the Chili directly: the Temple is bigger but a location you visit; the Chili is smaller but repeatable.
- **Jar o' Jelly** — a Chef-only dish that pairs a **full heal** with a **5-minute speed boost**. It is the longest-lasting boost on this list, which makes it the one to carry into a long night.
- **Hard Mode candy** — Candy Apple, Candy Corn, Cotton Candy and Pumpkin Pie all give a temporary speed boost, from Trick-or-Treating or the Spooky Crock Pot.

The trade between them is simple: the Temple is a one-shot you plan a route around, the Chili is a spike you keep in a sack slot, and the Jelly is a commitment that costs a Chef slot.

## Sprint Is Not Free: The Hunger Drain

Every speed source above shares one cost that none of them mentions: **sprinting drains the hunger bar much faster than walking.**

Our [Hunger](/guides/hunger) page calls it "the hidden drain" — every source agrees sprinting is "much faster", and not one publishes a multiplier. The practical consequence is that the number you see quoted for a hunger bar (twelve to seventeen minutes) is a **standing-still floor**, not an average. A player who sprints everywhere is spending that budget considerably faster, and the run home from a far objective costs food as well as time.

That is why the Chili is priced the way it is. It is not just a speed spike — it is a spike that costs one item instead of a sustained drain, which matters most in a run that is already short on food.

## When You Cannot Sprint at All

Sprint is a resource in one more sense: two states in the game switch it off, and both are lethal for the same reason.

**The Snow Biome freeze meter.** Warm clothing exists because the Snow Biome drains a **freeze meter** the whole time you stand in it, and a full meter kills you. Our [Warm Clothing](/items/warm-clothing) page records the part players miss: **a depleted freeze meter means no sprint.** In a biome full of animals that outrun a walking player, losing sprint is the real threat — not the cold itself.

**The Jungle.** Our [Jungle Biome](/locations/jungle-biome) page notes that movement there is faster than in the Forest, which cuts both ways: you close distance on the Cat faster, and the extra speed carries you over cliff edges that were not there a minute ago.

The pattern is that the game's speed sources and the game's threats are the same mechanic seen from two sides. The [Deer](/entities/the-deer) is beaten by running to a fire, the [Ram](/entities/the-ram)'s charge is faster than a sprint, and the [Cat](/entities/the-cat) punishes standing still — so sprint is worth buying, but the [Bosses](/guides/boss-guide) page is right that none of the five unkillable monsters is a fight you win by being fast alone.

## Where Sources Disagree

Four places the record conflicts. We list them rather than average them.

1. **Who has the fastest sprint?** rblxguide says the **Assassin** at +10%. The fandom wiki says the **Alien Scientist** at Level 3, three percent ahead of the Assassin. Neither mentions the **Big Game Hunter's +15%** from three Bunny Feet, which the class's own page states outright. Three answers; the Big Game Hunter figure is the largest but the least widely reported.
2. **Is the Assassin's bonus always on?** Yes — every source agrees the +10% is active from purchase, with no level requirement. That is the one point that is not contested, and it is why the class keeps the reputation.
3. **How big is the sprint hunger multiplier?** Nobody publishes a number. Hunger sources say "much faster" and stop; our own page records the absence rather than inventing a figure.
4. **Does the Berserker's ×2 sprint exist?** A single community breakdown on the fandom wiki gives it. No other source we found repeats the "2× sprint speed" figure, so we carry it as single-source.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the fastest sprint in 99 Nights in the Forest?**
The largest published figure is the **Big Game Hunter's +15%** from consuming three Bunny Feet — no health penalty. The **Alien Scientist** at Level 3 is about +13%, and the **Assassin** is +10% but active the moment you buy it.

**Is the Assassin worth it just for the sprint?**
For day-one mobility, yes — it is the only speed bonus that applies before you do anything. But it costs 500 Diamonds and cuts your max HP by 15%, and the Big Game Hunter's +15% is larger and costs no health. If you are choosing purely on speed, the Assassin is the third-best number in the game.

**How do I get the 15% sprint without buying the Assassin?**
Take the [Big Game Hunter](/classes/big-game-hunter-class) and kill three [Bunnies](/entities/the-bunny). The row is on the Pelt List and is not level-gated.

**Does sprinting cost me anything?**
Yes — it drains the hunger bar much faster than walking, and no source publishes the multiplier. Plan the run home as a food cost, not just a time cost.

**Can I lose my sprint entirely?**
Yes. A depleted **freeze meter** in the Snow Biome means no sprint at all, which is what makes [warm clothing](/items/warm-clothing) a survival item rather than a comfort one.

**Do Chili and the Ice Temple stack?**
No source tests it. The Temple is a one-visit ~×3 boost and the Chili is a short spike; treat them as alternatives rather than a stack, because no source documents the interaction.
