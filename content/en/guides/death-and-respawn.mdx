# Death and Respawn in 99 Nights in the Forest

Your health hits zero in the middle of a [cultist raid](/entities/the-cultist), and the screen does not end. That surprises people, because almost every guide written about this game treats death as a single event with a single outcome. It is not. What happens next depends on who is standing near you, what is in their hand, what your camp has built, and — in one case — how fast you can click a button that costs real money.

This page covers the whole chain: what going down actually does, how long you have, the four ways back, what leaves your sack when nobody reaches you, and the parts of it that no two sources describe the same way.

## Quick Answer

You do not die the instant your health bar empties. You go into a **downed state**, and that state is a window, not a verdict.

- **A teammate can put you back on your feet.** With a Bandage or Medkit equipped, they walk to your body, hold the interact key, and you stand up after a few seconds. Any class can do this — it is not a Medic-only ability.
- **How long the window lasts is where sources split.** Three of them publish a figure of about **15 seconds**, and they do not agree on what happens when it runs out: two say you are revived automatically, one says you die for good.
- **If nobody reaches you**, you become a spectator for the rest of the night and rejoin your team at camp when the next night begins.
- **Four things can reverse a death without a teammate:** the **Respawn Capsule** (a Tier 5 Crafting Bench structure that revives the most recent dead player after being charged with a Cultist Gem), the **Berserker** class (auto-revives once per run), a **45 Robux** self-revive button, and — for one class — simply being revived a lot.
- **In solo play there is no save system.** If you go down and nothing catches you, the run is over.

The single most important thing to understand is that **revive and respawn are two different endings to the same event**. A revive puts you back where you fell, with your items. A respawn sends you to camp and may leave your gear on the ground where you dropped it.

## The Two Death Models Nobody Reconciles

Before anything else on this page makes sense, you have to know that the guides covering this game describe **two incompatible things**, and almost none of them acknowledge the other exists.

**Model A — the downed window.** Your health reaches zero, you collapse rather than leaving, and a short timer starts. A living teammate who reaches your body and holds the interact key puts you back on your feet. This is the model used by the revival guides at Beebom, TechWiser, BloxBoom, Stealthy Gaming, Sports Rant, Rosenberry Rooms and Of Zen and Computing.

**Model B — spectate until dawn.** You die, you become a spectator who can watch and chat but not act, and you rejoin at camp with basic equipment when the next night starts. This is the model on 99nights.net, which publishes it in several languages.

These read as contradictions, and on a literal reading they are. But there is a way to hold both that costs nothing and matches the in-game detail other pages report:

> **Our reading, flagged as ours:** Model A describes the first phase and Model B describes the second. You go down with a window; if someone fills that window you are revived; if nobody does, you fall through to the spectator state and rejoin at camp. Nothing we found states this explicitly, but it is the only reading under which both descriptions are true, and it is consistent with the Undead class's Level 2 perk — which is described as removing the waiting time after death, something that only makes sense if a wait exists in the first place.

Treat that as a framework, not as a published fact. What matters practically is that **the window exists and someone has to use it**, which is true on both readings.

## At Zero HP: The Downed State

When your health bar empties, three things are consistent across sources.

**You are not removed from the round.** You are on the ground at the spot where you fell, and your body is a physical object other players interact with.

**You keep the ability to communicate.** On the spectator reading you also keep free camera movement and chat. Nothing suggests you are muted or blind during the window.

**In multiplayer, going down is routine rather than fatal.** The revival guide at 99nightsintheforest.net states this plainly: as long as the players do not go down at the same time, the team can keep reviving each other. The failure state is **simultaneous**, not sequential. That single detail reframes the whole mechanic — the danger is not that somebody falls, it is that everybody falls together, which is why the advice further down this page is about where you choose to fall.

One consequence deserves its own mention, because it is the most underrated tactic in the game:

**Your corpse can be picked up.** Multiple guides describe storing a downed teammate's body in your sack and carrying it somewhere safer before reviving them there. See [Moving the Body: The Sack Trick](#moving-the-body-the-sack-trick) below.

## How Long You Have

Three sources publish a number, and they publish the same number: **about 15 seconds**.

| Source | Figure | What happens when it expires |
|---|---|---|
| 99nightsintheforest.net | ~15 seconds | Automatic revival |
| Rosenberry Rooms | ~15 seconds | Automatic revival "in certain situations" |
| Of Zen and Computing | ~15 seconds | You **permanently die** and must restart |

That third row is the problem. Two sources say the timer ending puts you back on your feet; one says it ends your run. Those are not reconcilable by careful reading — they are different claims about the same mechanic.

Two things make it harder to settle:

- Rosenberry Rooms hedges with "in certain situations" without saying which situations.
- Of Zen and Computing's own summary table gives Bandages a **~75% health** revival and Medkits **100%**, a set of numbers no other source publishes and that reads like an estimate rather than a measurement.

**What we would actually plan around:** treat the window as short and the cost of missing it as high. Whether the timer ends in an automatic revive or a lost run, the correct response is identical — get to the body fast, or do not get to it at all. Rosenberry Rooms adds that the base timer does not appear to change with night count or difficulty, and that what actually shrinks the window on later nights is enemy pressure around the body rather than the timer itself.

## How to Revive a Teammate

The procedure is the same on every source that describes it, with one disagreement about the key.

1. **Equip a Bandage or Medkit in your hand.** Several guides are explicit that it has to be the active item, not merely somewhere in your sack.
2. **Find the body.** Beebom notes you can use the map in the top right of the screen to locate it.
3. **Walk to the body and hold the interact key.** Every source agrees this is a **hold**, not a tap, and that it takes several seconds.
4. **You are exposed the entire time.** Reviving does not pause the game around you. TechWiser's phrasing is that the process takes a few seconds and you should be somewhere safe before you start it.

**The key is disputed.** Six sources — Beebom, TechWiser, BloxBoom, Stealthy Gaming, Rosenberry Rooms and Of Zen and Computing — say **E** on PC. One source, 99nightsintheforest.net, says **B**. We are going with E because six against one is not a close call, but we are recording the dissent rather than hiding it. If E does nothing on your setup, B is the only other key anyone has named.

**Any class can do this.** The Medic does it faster, but the ability is not locked to a class. TechWiser states this directly: any player can revive a fallen teammate regardless of their class.

This section gives the revival numbers and leaves the question of what the supply costs; our [Healing, Bandages and Medkits](/guides/healing-guide) guide prices those two items against every other heal in the game.

## Bandages and Medkits: What They Are Worth and Where They Come From

These two items do double duty — they heal you and they put other people back on their feet — and the gap between them is the difference between a revive that holds and one that immediately fails.

| | **Bandage** | **Medkit** |
|---|---|---|
| **Crafting cost** | 2 Bunny Foot + 2 Wolf Pelt | Not craftable on any source we found |
| **Made at** | The [Tool Workshop](/locations/tool-workshop) (the anvil building) | — |
| **Craft limit** | Unlimited | — |
| **Health on revive** | Partial — the amount is disputed | Full, on every source that states it |
| **Health when used on yourself** | ~40 HP, per one source, unconfirmed | More than a Bandage |
| **Practical role** | The thing you actually use | The thing you save for Night 70+ |

**The Bandage recipe is one of the safest numbers on this page.** 2 Bunny Foot and 2 [Wolf Pelt](/entities/the-wolf) at the [Tool Workshop](/locations/tool-workshop) appears identically at Finding Dulcinea, BloxBoom, Six Storeys, TechWiser and 99nightsintheforest.net. Crafting is unlimited once the anvil is rebuilt, which is what turns Bandages from a scarce drop into a renewable supply.

**How much health a Bandage revives with is genuinely unsettled.** Three sources give three answers: "low HP" (Stealthy Gaming), "minimal health" (TechWiser), "around 40–50% of maximum" (Rosenberry Rooms), and ~75% (Of Zen and Computing). Only Medkits get a consistent answer — **full health** — and even that has one dissent, from Beebom, which lists Medkits under items obtained from "chests, crafting, or starting items", implying a crafting recipe no other source publishes.

**Where they come from:**

- **The Medic class** starts with two Bandages, and at Tier 3 one of them becomes a Medkit.
- **The Berserker class** starts with one Medkit — which is why people mistake it for a healer.
- **Chests**, at a low rate. Rosenberry Rooms puts the drop rate at roughly 5–10% outside high-tier areas.
- **Hospital and clinic buildings**, which appear across the [map](/locations/all-locations) as the [campfire](/guides/campfire) expands. Of Zen and Computing says these show up more frequently after day 20.
- **The Tool Workshop**, for Bandages, once the anvil is up.

Given that Medkits only reliably arrive through two classes and rare chests, the practical rule most sources converge on is to **run on Bandages and hold Medkits for the late nights**, when being revived at partial health means being revived into something that kills you again.

## The Respawn Capsule

This is the strongest anti-death item in the game and the one most runs never build.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Unlocks at** | Crafting Bench **Tier 5** |
| **Cost** | **40 [Wood](/items/how-to-get-wood-fast) + 40 [Scrap](/items/how-to-get-scrap) + 1 Gem of the Forest** |
| **What it does** | Revives the **most recent dead player** |
| **Requires** | Charging with a Cultist Gem after crafting |
| **Reuse** | Must be charged again after every use |

The cost is unusually well corroborated — **40 Wood, 40 Scrap and one Gem of the Forest at Tier 5** appears identically at Finding Dulcinea, SimpleGameGuide, BloxBoom, Six Storeys, WebComicsWeekend, Stealthy Gaming, Beebom and Sports Rant. Eight sources agreeing on a crafting recipe is about as solid as this game's documentation gets. (Backyard Drunkard writes "40 Logs + 40 Bolts", which is the same recipe under that site's naming convention.)

Three details matter more than the cost:

**It is Tier 5.** That is the final bench tier, gated behind a Gem of the Forest — the same gem that buys the Weather Machine and the Temporal Accelerometer. A capsule is not an early-game purchase; it competes directly with those two for the same gem. See [the Crafting Bench page](/locations/crafting-bench) for the full upgrade ladder.

**It revives the most recent dead player — not you specifically.** This is stated as "the most recent dead player" by several sources and as "any fallen player" or "any defeated players within range" by others. Backyard Drunkard's version is the most specific: you respawn **right where the capsule was placed**. So the capsule is a camp fixture, not a personal insurance policy, and where you build it is a real decision — see [best base design](/guides/best-base-design) for where that tends to be.

**It has to be charged before you die.** Every source says the same thing: craft it, charge it with a Cultist Gem, and only then does it catch a death. An uncharged capsule does nothing. See [the Cultist Gem page](/items/cultist-gem) for where those come from — mostly cultists and the [Cultist King](/entities/the-cultist-king) — and how many a run realistically produces.

## Charging It: The Cultist Gem Ladder

Here is a number that exists on exactly one website.

Backyard Drunkard states that charging the capsule costs Cultist Gems on a rising ladder:

- First activation: **1** Cultist Gem
- Second activation: **2** Cultist Gems
- Third activation: **3** Cultist Gems, and so on

No other source publishes a figure at all — the rest say only "charge it with a Cultist Gem" and move on. So either this ladder is real and everybody else skipped it, or it is an invention. We are publishing it **labelled as single-source**, and the practical advice does not change either way: Cultist Gems are the running cost of the capsule, not just the entry fee, and a camp that builds one without a gem supply has built a decoration.

The same source adds a limitation worth knowing: **the capsule does not protect you from leaving.** Exiting the game still ends the run.

## The Three Classes That Touch Death

Four classes interact with this system, and one of them is widely misread.

| Class | Cost | What it does to death |
|---|---|---|
| **Berserker** | 100 [Diamonds](/items/how-to-get-diamonds-fast) | Auto-revives **once per run**, on your first death |
| **Undead** | 100 Diamonds | Gains a **permanent buff every time someone revives you** |
| **Medic** | 40 | Starts with 2 Bandages, revives **2× faster**, one Bandage becomes a Medkit at Tier 3 |
| **Necromancer** | 600 | Resurrects **cultists** — not players |

**Berserker.** The auto-revive fires on your first death of the run and does not repeat. You come back with **low HP and low hunger**, which is why the advice on the [Berserker page](/classes/berserker-class) is to treat it as an escape window rather than a second life. Its Level 2 perk extends that window — roughly five seconds of being unseen by entities, plus a speed buff for about thirty seconds.

**Undead.** Every revive grants one permanent buff, drawn from a five-entry list. Level 2 requires being revived **30 times** and removes the wait after death; Level 3 requires **60** and lets you move for eight seconds after dying. Whether the buff list caps at five or keeps rolling is disputed, and the [Undead page](/classes/undead-class) lays out both readings. Note the interaction nobody confirms: **whether a Respawn Capsule revive counts toward the 30/60 counter is stated nowhere.**

**Medic.** Two Bandages at the start, double-speed revives, and a Medkit at Tier 3. This is the cheapest way to make the whole system work, and on the Undead page it is the reason the Undead is worth buying at all.

**Necromancer — and this is the one people get wrong.** It costs 600, it starts with a Cultist Staff, and its perks are about **collecting souls from dead cultists and resurrecting those cultists as summons**, up to six of them. It does not revive players. If you are reading it as a support class that rescues teammates, you have the wrong class. One source, VGTimes, adds that it takes three souls to resurrect one cultist; no other source gives a number.

## The Robux Button

When you go down, a **Self Revive** button appears. It costs **45 Robux**, and every source that mentions it gives that same figure.

- You return **at the spot where you died**, with full health.
- It can be used to revive **the whole team** if everyone goes down together — but BloxBoom and Stealthy Gaming both note the offer only exists while the match is still active, so it has to be clicked before the server returns everyone to the lobby.

TechWiser's verdict is the honest one: at 45 Robux per death this gets expensive fast, and with competent teammates you would have been revived anyway. Treat it as the option for the specific case where the alternative is losing a Night 80 run, not as a routine part of the loop.

A dead player leaves their reserve behind, and our [Ammo and Guns](/guides/ammo-guide) guide is the page that counts what that costs a gun run.


This is the part with the most confusion, because "what happens to your stuff" has three different answers depending on how the death resolves.

**If a teammate revives you**, your inventory is intact. Rosenberry Rooms states this directly. This is why the window matters so much — it is not only about staying in the round, it is about keeping your gear.

**If the timer expires or you are forced to respawn**, you may drop items where you fell and have to go back for them. This is stated by Rosenberry Rooms and implied by the Berserker page's own advice to use the post-revive window to "run back to your dropped items". 99nights.net's version is vaguer — "some resources", with a penalty — which is consistent but not specific.

**If the run ends**, the loss is total. OzoGames describes the permadeath model in the bluntest terms: base, inventory and gathered resources are all lost. What survives is **your account currency, your unlocked classes, and your nights-survived record**.

99nights.net publishes the cleanest keep/lose list for the mid-run case, and it is worth reproducing because nothing else does:

| **You keep** | **You lose** |
|---|---|
| Character level and XP | The current night's progress |
| Unlocked classes | Some resources |
| Purchased items | Active temporary buffs |
| Talents and ability upgrades | Your position — you rejoin at camp |
| Statistics and progress tracking | |

Our reading of the whole picture: **the severity of a death is a function of how fast someone reaches you.** Fast revive costs you nothing. Slow revive costs you a walk back to your gear. No revive costs you the run.

## Solo Play: There Is No Save

If you play alone, the revive system largely does not apply, and this is where expectations break.

**There is no save system.** Backyard Drunkard is unambiguous: progress is not saved in single-player or solo sessions. Leaving mid-run loses the survivor, the structures and the progress. The design is a continuous-session roguelike, and your only permanent record is the nights-survived counter shown in the lobby — which is also why the [speedrun route](/guides/speedrun-guide) is built around day multipliers rather than survival time.

**Multiplayer works the same way.** The host's session ends when the host leaves, and guests do not keep their inventory unless the session continues uninterrupted.

**The capsule does not fix this.** It protects against dying. It does not protect against quitting.

What that means in practice: the only self-revive tools that matter solo are the **Berserker's** one auto-revive and the **45 Robux** button, and the only structural one is a charged **Respawn Capsule** — which Backyard Drunkard frames correctly as an insurance policy against a lethal mistake rather than a save feature.

## Moving the Body: The Sack Trick

This is the single most useful technique on this page, and it is buried in the middle of most revival guides.

If a teammate falls somewhere dangerous, **do not revive them there.** Instead:

1. Press the interact key on the body to **store the corpse in your sack**.
2. Walk or run back to camp, or anywhere with defences and a campfire.
3. Take the body back out of the sack.
4. Equip a Bandage or Medkit and revive them there.

Stealthy Gaming, Beebom, Rosenberry Rooms, Of Zen and Computing and Sports Rant all describe this. Beebom's wording is worth quoting in spirit: reviving in the open is the risky option, so carry the body first and revive second.

The tactic works because the revive animation is several seconds of standing still, and on the nights that matter the thing that did the killing — often [the Deer](/entities/the-deer) — is usually still standing over the body. Moving the body converts a revive under pressure into a revive behind your own walls. It also pairs with the Undead's Level 3 perk, which lets a dead player walk for eight seconds — the [Undead page](/classes/undead-class) notes that aggro drops during that window, which means a dead Undead can walk *toward* the person coming to carry them.

## Death in Hard Mode

Hard Mode does not appear to change the revive rules themselves. Nothing we found states that Bandages work differently, that the window is shorter, or that the Respawn Capsule behaves otherwise when Corruption is running.

What it changes is how often you will need the system. [Hard Mode](/guides/hard-mode) layers thresholds onto the run — slower healing, Juggernaut enemies, food that spoils on a seconds timer, poison spores at full Corruption — and every one of those raises the odds of the bar reaching zero in the first place.

The interaction that actually matters is strategic rather than mechanical. Entering Hard Mode is **irreversible**, and the 99-day runs needed for badges like Teamwork have to be finished before you switch it on. If your plan involves a group pushing for Day 99, the revive chain and the Hard Mode decision are the same decision. See [All Badges](/guides/all-badges) for how those routes conflict.

## What This Means for a Team Plan

Putting the mechanics together, here is the shape of a camp that rarely loses anyone.

**One person carries the Bandages.** The [beginner guide](/guides/beginner-guide) covers the night rules that keep a group out of this situation in the first place; this is the fallback for when they fail. Stealthy Gaming recommends a designated medic; Sports Rant's version is that a team without a Medic is making survival harder than it needs to be. At 40, the Medic is the cheapest unit of revive capacity in the game.

**Bandages are a crafting problem, not a luck problem.** Once the anvil is up, Bandages cost 2 Bunny Feet and 2 Wolf Pelts with no limit. A team that treats them as a rare chest drop will run short; a team that farms rabbits and wolves will not.

**Build the capsule near the campfire and food, not near the fight.** Its revive returns you to where it stands. A capsule at the edge of the map gives you a second life in the worst place on the map.

**Agree on where you fall.** This sounds strange and it is the most practical advice here. The Undead page makes the point directly: a reviver standing ten metres away revives you many more times than one sprinting across the base. Death location is a resource decision, and dying near your camp is worth more than surviving slightly longer in a corner nobody can reach.

**Do not revive into a losing fight.** Every source with an opinion says the same, and it applies hardest during [Cultist Stronghold](/locations/cultist-stronghold) raids: if the body is in the open and the thing that killed them is still there, carry the body out instead.

## Where Sources Disagree

1. **Two entire models of death.** The downed-window model (Beebom, TechWiser, BloxBoom, Stealthy Gaming, Sports Rant, Rosenberry Rooms, Of Zen and Computing, 99nightsintheforest.net) against the spectate-until-dawn model (99nights.net). We propose they are sequential phases; no source states this.
2. **What the 15-second timer actually ends in.** Automatic revival on two sources, permanent death on a third.
3. **The interact key.** E on six sources, B on one.
4. **Health restored by a Bandage revive.** "Low", "minimal", "around 40–50%", and "~75%" are all published. Medkits are consistently full health, with Beebom as the lone dissent implying they can be crafted.
5. **Bandage healing on yourself.** ~40 HP appears once, explicitly unconfirmed by its own source.
6. **Berserker's price currency.** 100 Diamonds on most sources, 100 Gems on Rosenberry Rooms.
7. **Medic and Support prices.** 40 and 45 — but sources split between calling the currency Gems and Diamonds.
8. **Whether Medkits can be crafted.** Beebom implies yes; every other source says they come only from rare chests, hospitals, or class progression.
9. **The capsule's charging ladder.** 1, then 2, then 3 Cultist Gems — Backyard Drunkard only. No other source publishes any figure.
10. **Who the capsule revives.** "Most recent dead player" (most sources) against "any defeated players within range" (BloxBoom). The distinction matters for whether two capsules cover a team.
11. **Whether the capsule revives you at the capsule or where you died.** Backyard Drunkard says where it was placed; BloxBoom's Robux section says the *paid* revive returns you to your death spot. These may be different mechanics rather than a disagreement.
12. **Necromancer's soul cost.** Three souls per resurrected cultist, from VGTimes only.
13. **Necromancer's Level 3 requirement.** 5,000 damage plus 200 resurrections on two wikis, 15,000 damage plus 200 on a third.
14. **Single-player death.** Most sources say it means restarting; Rosenberry Rooms says you restart from the latest checkpoint or saved night, which contradicts the no-save model Backyard Drunkard describes in detail.
15. **Items that may not exist.** 99nightsintheforest.net lists a **Revival Totem** and a **Diamond Revival**. No other source mentions either. We have not adopted them.

## FAQ

**Do I die instantly when my health hits zero?**
No. You enter a downed state with a window in which a teammate can revive you. Sources disagree on what happens if that window closes — automatic revival or a permanent death — but all agree the window exists.

**Can any class revive a teammate?**
Yes. The Medic does it twice as fast, but the ability is not class-locked. You need a Bandage or Medkit in your hand.

**How long does the revive take?**
Several seconds, and you cannot move or defend during it. That is why the sack trick exists.

**What is the Respawn Capsule and is it worth it?**
A Tier 5 Crafting Bench structure costing 40 Wood, 40 Scrap and 1 Gem of the Forest. It revives the most recent dead player, once per charge, and must be recharged with Cultist Gems. It is the only non-Robux way to reverse a death with no teammate involved.

**Does the Berserker's auto-revive work more than once?**
No. It fires on your first death of the run and does not repeat. You return with low HP and low hunger.

**Do I lose my items when I die?**
A teammate revive keeps your inventory. A forced respawn may drop items where you fell. A run that ends loses everything except your account currency, unlocked classes and nights-survived record.

**Can I save and come back later?**
No. There is no save system. Leaving mid-run ends it, and the Respawn Capsule does not change that.

**Is the 45 Robux revive worth it?**
Only in the specific case where the alternative is losing a deep run. It returns you at your death spot with full health, but the cost per death adds up.

**Does the Necromancer revive players?**
No. It resurrects dead cultists as summons, up to six. It is frequently mistaken for a support class.

**What happens if everyone goes down at once?**
On the multiplayer reading, that ends the run. The Revival button can rescue a full-team wipe, but only while the match is still active.
