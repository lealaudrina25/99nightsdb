# Hard Mode

## Quick Answer

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What it is | An optional, one-way difficulty mode added **24 January 2026** with The Forest Is Angry |
| The engine behind it | One number: **Corruption, 0–100%** |
| How it starts | A **vote** at the Research Outpost basement — every player in the lobby pulls their own lever |
| The deadline | Sources split between **Night 2** and **the end of Day 3** — after that the run's opportunity is gone |
| What fights the meter | **Rifts**, which spawn corrupted trees or purple-eyed wolves |
| Fastest possible climb | **0 to 100% in roughly 15 real minutes** if every rift is left alone |
| The payout | **4 Gems of the Forest at the day-45 milestone, 8 at day 99** (normal run: 2 and 3) |
| The reward for running it | Corrupted Chests, five Corrupted Items, Juggernaut and Brute raid waves |

Hard Mode is worth its own page because it is not a stat multiplier. Nothing about it is "the same game but enemies hit harder". It is a second resource curve bolted onto the map, and almost every other system you already read about on this site behaves differently once it is on: the food in your sack rots in ninety seconds, your passive healing stops entirely, and the Corruption you fail to hold keeps a permanent score of its own.

That last point is why the page exists. Our own How to Get Diamonds Fast page says Hard Mode pays more, our Best Weapons page has a Hard Mode section listing four Corrupted Items, and our Cultist page says Juggernauts show up in Hard Mode night raids. Each of those is correct and none of them is complete. What none of them can say is what the meter does between those two sentences, because the answers to that are spread across a voting table, seven effect thresholds, three rift states and a permanent penalty that nobody publishes a number for until it happens to you.

## How to Unlock It

This is the first place the sources disagree, so it is worth being precise about which claim rests on what.

**The account gate.** One source — BloxSwaps — is the only one we found that states a personal unlock condition: **your account must have survived at least 20 days and rescued 1 kid** before the Hard Mode option even becomes selectable in the lobby. Everything else we read treats Hard Mode as something any lobby can choose. That is a real gap, not a rounding error: the fandom wiki's Hard Mode page describes only the lobby toggle, and Bloxodes instead describes a **campfire level 2** requirement to build the map that reveals the Research Outpost in the first place. Those three are not the same claim and they cannot all be the whole story, so treat the 20-day-and-one-kid version as a report from a single source until someone confirms it in game.

**Getting to the lever.** Once a lobby wants to try it, Hard Mode does not start from a menu. You go to the **Research Outpost**, which sits to the north of your campfire once you have crafted a map for it, you find the steel door on the left, and you go **downstairs into the basement**. There is one lever per player in the server, each labelled with a player name, and **every player has to pull their own**. The fandom wiki is explicit that all levers must be activated before the cutscene fires.

**One-way.** One guide states plainly that Hard Mode cannot be switched off once it starts, and that you should be certain before pulling. Nothing we read contradicts that, and the cost of being wrong is a whole run, so take it as fact and plan around it.

## How It Starts, and the Deadline

The vote itself runs on a simple majority, per the fandom wiki's table. Worth learning before you walk down those stairs, because a lobby that does not understand the threshold will pull a lever and then watch nothing happen:

| Players in the lobby | Votes needed to start Hard Mode |
|---|---|
| 1 | 1 out of 1 |
| 2 | 2 out of 2 |
| 3 | 2 out of 3 |
| 4 | 3 out of 4 |
| 5 | 3 out of 5 |

That is a majority of the lobby, not a unanimous vote, and the table only runs to five players.

**When it takes effect is the second disagreement, and it is a bigger one than the unlock.** Four different answers turned up:

| Claim | Source | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| Night 2 — the vote is pulled on day one and Hard Mode goes live when the night ends | BloxSwaps | Fastest; roughly a 9-minute window before the vote wins |
| Night 2, but the window closes about **7.5 minutes before night 2** | Escorenews | Similar end point, different deadline maths |
| **Day 3 must end before you vote**; from Day 4 the chance for that run is gone for good | Desinema | Slowest, and the one that matters most |
| Before the third night, visit the Research Outpost | a Chinese-language guide, 17173 | Closer to the Day 3 reading |

The honest reconciliation is that these probably describe the same deadline from two ends. Desinema's "Day 3 ends" is a hard stop on *voting*. BloxSwaps's "Night 2" is when the mode *becomes active* once the vote has already gone through. A lobby that wants safety should treat **the end of Day 3** as the deadline and Night 2 as the expected start, because only one of those two protects you if the vote happens late.

A detail both the wiki and BloxSwaps give, and which is the single most recognisable sensory signature of Hard Mode: the cutscene that plays when containment fails, the day briefly turns purple, the first Rift opens, and the **Research Outpost is covered in a purple aura that flickers with lightning for the rest of the run**.

## The Corruption Meter

Once Hard Mode is on, almost everything that happens is a symptom of one percentage. There is no number on screen. That surprises people, so it is worth saying loudly: **you never see "73% Corruption"**. What you get instead are two tools, both of which come out of the Research Outpost's own chests and both of which only become openable after Hard Mode has started:

- The **Research Monitor Blueprint** — a large infoboard you craft and place at campfire, which prints the current debuff state, the active rift count and what is currently affecting you. Its blueprint sits in one of the outpost chests.
- The **Corruption Scanner** — a hotbar item for one player to carry, which skips the readout entirely and just points an arrow at the nearest rift. Its chest is the one exception to the once-per-game rule described below.

The rest of those chests are worth looting while you are down there: **3 MREs** (super-stews that do not rot, which matters a great deal at 60% Corruption), **1 medkit** and **2 bandages**. Everything in the Research Outpost chests except the Scanner chest is **once per game, not once per player** — so do not stand around expecting the person who opened the medkit to open it again.

If you are playing with a teammate, the **ping system** added in the same update (Q on PC, the bell icon on mobile) is what makes rift-clearing survivable at all. Mark the rift, mark the corrupted tree, mark the Brute that just walked into your base.

## The Seven Thresholds

Corruption effect thresholds, from the fandom wiki, matched almost word for word by BloxSwaps:

| Level | What it does |
|---|---|
| **All levels** | Cultist attacks simply bring more enemies, whatever the percentage |
| **15%** | **Hungry Deer every night.** You get no on-screen warning for it — not even on the nights normal mode would have warned you |
| **25%** | Passive healing is slowed. Medkits and bandages still work normally |
| **35%** | Raids start **including Juggernaut Cultists** more often, and more cultists overall |
| **60%** | Most stored food rots after **90 seconds**, even in your sack or hotbar. Cooking does not save it. Map-spawned food — carrots, berries, strawberries, apples — never rots |
| **75%** | Raids add **Brute Cultists and Darkstring Cultists** on top of everything already listed, more often |
| **80%** | Passive healing **stops completely**. Medkits and bandages are now your entire heal budget |
| **100%** | **Poison Spores** damage every player anywhere on the map, with a purple cloud around your torso |

Two things about that table are easy to read wrongly.

**They stack, they do not replace.** A run sitting at 75% is eating the 15%, 25%, 35%, 60%, 75% effects all at once. There is no relief tier, no "the worst has passed".

**At 60%, "most stored food" is a specific claim, not flavour text.** The detail the wiki gives under it is unusually useful: rotting applies to stored food and to food dropped by corrupted animals and farmplot crops, but food that spawns on the map never rots. That combination is what makes a long Hard Mode run survivable — you stop being a farmer and become a forager. It also makes the cooking classes the single most valuable thing in the run, a point we come back to below.

And the 100% row is not a soft failure state. Poison Spores hit you through distance and through walls. There is no corner of the map that is safe at 100%.

## Rifts: Quiet, Open, Raging

Rifts are the thing you actually fight. Visually they are a large crack in the ground with a glowing purple aura and tentacle-like branches pushing out of it. In gameplay they are either a **tree rift** or a **wolf rift**, and each one spawns its own corrupted entities until you clear them.

| State | How it looks | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| **Quiet** | Tentacles dark purple, glow only deep in the ground | All nearby corrupted trees or wolves are gone. This state is reached **only** by a full clear |
| **Open** | Tentacles bright purple, glow lifting off the crack | Spawning and spreading |
| **Raging** | Bigger, brighter glow, flickering with lightning | The worst of the three, and the map icon changes to match |

A Rift that goes Quiet **reopens after one to two days** unless you keep it cleared. So a Hard Mode run is not a one-time sprint, it is a rotation: whichever rift is closest to the campfire, go and clean it, then move on.

Two mechanics about the meter's speed are worth memorising, because together they define the difficulty curve:

- **Unchecked rifts can take Corruption from 0 to 100% in about 15 real-world minutes** — the fandom wiki phrases the same figure as roughly 3.5 days of in-game night time. Same number, two clocks.
- **Independently of rifts, Corruption drifts up about 1% per day and stops at 20%.** So even a team that never touches a rift cannot sit at zero forever.

Clearing a rift lowers its contribution, and **a partial clear is enough** — killing some of the corrupted wolves or chopping some of the corrupted trees already helps. You do not need to sweep it spotless. That is the single most important permission on this page: you are not required to win every rift, only to take the edge off the ones you cannot fully clear in the time you have.

**Corrupted trees have a growth stage you should not ignore.** If a tree rift is left open too long it spawns a **corrupted large tree**, and those need a **Strong Axe, a Corrupted Axe or a Chainsaw** to bring down. Cut the small ones before the big one is an option.

## Corrupted Chests and the Five Corrupted Items

**Corrupted Chests** are the reward for running Hard Mode. They look like **Ruby Chests with the red replaced by a rough purple**, and while Hard Mode is active they can **replace an ordinary chest by chance** on the map. They are a roll on the same chest system our Chest Tiers page itemises — the same ladder underneath, a different skin, and a different penalty if you misuse what comes out.

**Corrupted Items** are more powerful than their normal equivalents, and they carry their own use meter. When the meter reaches 100% the item **explodes**, and when it is used up it **permanently adds 10% to the map-wide Corruption**. That last clause is the trap: the punished not for losing the item, but for having used it at all.

| Item | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| **Corrupted Axe** | Chop speed equal to a **Strong Axe**. It will replace a Strong Axe if you already have one — and when it explodes you are left **with no axe at all**. Its corruption bar fills very slowly, so there is time to find a replacement and carry it home |
| **Corrupted Revolver** | Infinite ammo, long reload. The wiki's own note on this one is self-contradictory — it is described both as having a long reload and as having zero reload and shoot time — so treat the reload figure as unresolved |
| **Corrupted Shotgun** | Infinite ammo, long reload, hits harder than a Tactical Shotgun and can **one-shot a Cultist Brute** |
| **Corrupted Thrown Axe** | Functions like the **Kunai** — infinite ammo, no reload — but its corruption meter fills faster than either gun. It detonates somewhere between **80% and 90%**, a little differently depending on your latency |
| **Corrupted Thorn Armour** | The one exception: **no corruption bar, cannot explode.** Survives the whole run and lasts as protection comparable to ordinary Thorn Armour |

That is **five** items. Our own Best Weapons page lists only four, because it omits the Corrupted Thorn Armour — worth flagging here rather than quietly fixing, since a reader of that page would reasonably conclude armour was never part of the set.

Two pieces of practical advice from the wiki on how to spend them. Corrupted Items are for the fights that are otherwise unwinnable — the Stronghold, a kid rescue, the deep caves — **not** for the cultist wave that walks past your door at night. And the Corrupted Shotgun in particular should not be wasted on a bear or a mammoth; its damage is far too high for that. Conversely, if you find the Corrupted Armour, **put it on**, unless you are a Vampire relying on the cloak to heal, because armour does not have uses in the normal sense and this one is free to keep.

## What Corruption Does to Your Run Plan

Beyond the raw thresholds, three shifts decide whether a Hard Mode run is comfortable or brutal.

**The heal budget collapses.** At 25% passive healing slows; at 80% it stops. Between those two lines you go from having a regeneration cushion to running on medkits and bandages alone, which is a hard ceiling on how long a run can last. That is the clock, more than any enemy, that ends most Hard Mode attempts.

**Food stops being a storage problem.** At 60%, ninety seconds. The counterplay the sources keep pointing at is not better storage, it is either **the cooking classes** — healing food sidesteps the rot entirely — or the **Volcanic Biome**, where cooking what you find offsets the loss, plus the fact that extra raids mean extra volcano rewards over a long run. The wiki's recommended list is **Cyborg** first and foremost, then **Assassin**, then **Alien Scientist** at level 2 for food off enemies and extra sack space, then **Camper** for reduced hunger, and **Chef** or **Cook** for the healing food. Fire Bandit and Pyromaniac also rank well, because they cook meat straight off a kill. If those names are new, the full rundown is on our classes pages.

**The biome choice quietly matters.** Volcanic Biome as the main pick and Fairy Forest as a secondary one, for the early flower-shop rewards and a Giant Tree you can finish fast. Nothing about that is a hard rule, but it is the kind of pre-run decision that decides whether day 40 is fun.

## The Diamond Payoff, and a Day Disagreement

Hard Mode pays better for the same objectives, which is why it ends up on our diamond page at all:

| Milestone | Normal run | Hard Mode |
|---|---|---|
| Day 45 (or 50 — see below) | 2 Gems of the Forest | 4 |
| Day 99 | 3 Gems of the Forest | 8 |

The number itself is well corroborated. The **day** is not. The fandom wiki and BloxSwaps both say the first milestone sits at **day 45**; Desinema and a Chinese-language guide both say **night 50**. We have no way to tell which is the live figure, so both are printed rather than picking one, and the disagreement is listed below.

What is not in dispute is the shape: Hard Mode roughly doubles the early payout and nearly triples the day-99 one. It is also why our diamond page carries the warning it does — **do not grind Hard Mode you cannot finish**. An abandoned Hard Mode run pays nothing, and unlike a normal run you cannot close it out and quietly re-queue.

## Where Sources Disagree

Seven open questions. Where a claim rests on a single source, it is named rather than repeated as fact.

1. **The account unlock.** BloxSwaps alone says 20 days survived plus 1 kid rescued. The fandom wiki says nothing, Bloxodes says campfire level 2 to build the map. Not one of them is corroborated.
2. **When the vote has to happen.** Night 2 (BloxSwaps, roughly 9 minutes), 7.5 minutes before night 2 (Escorenews), end of Day 3 (Desinema), before the third night (17173). These may describe a deadline and an activation, but nothing on our side settles it.
3. **The milestone day.** Day 45 (fandom, BloxSwaps) against day 50 (Desinema, 17173).
4. **How many Corrupted Items there are.** Five is the number both fandom pages and Bloxodes give. Our own Best Weapons page lists four. No source we read enumerates a sixth.
5. **The Corrupted Revolver's reload.** The wiki claims a long reload and simultaneously zero reload time in the same bullet. One of the two sentences is wrong and we cannot tell which.
6. **Events that disable Hard Mode.** One source reports that a Meteor, Frog or UFO event will disable Hard Mode mid-run, with the Carnival update as the exception. Single source, and a serious claim if true.
7. **The exact 0→100% clock.** 15 real minutes and 3.5 in-game days are the same source's two phrasings for one figure; BloxSwaps gives 15 minutes as well. Nobody we read explains the conversion.

## What to Actually Do With This

In the order that matters, for a lobby that has decided to run it:

1. **Decide before you pull the lever.** Hard Mode is one-way for the run. Everyone in the lobby has to understand the seven thresholds, not just the damage numbers.
2. **Vote on Day 2, not Day 3.** The deadline is the one thing all sources agree is permanent. Earlier is cheaper.
3. **Build the Research Monitor first thing.** There is no number on screen otherwise, and you cannot prioritise rifts you cannot see or measure.
4. **Clear rifts on rotation, do not purge them.** Partial clears lower Corruption. Sweeping every rift fully while the day burns is how a run dies.
5. **Chop small corrupted trees before a large one grows.** The large ones need a Strong Axe or a Chainsaw and are a time sink you cannot recover.
6. **Become a forager after 60%.** Map-spawned food never rots. Farming and stockpiling stop working, and the cooking classes become the run.
7. **Spend Corrupted Items at the Stronghold, not at your doorstep.** Their use meters are permanent Corruption on the map once they go.
8. **Never let a Corrupted Axe explode with no axe in the pack.** That is not a lost tool, it is a lost slot.

The one limit worth stating plainly: no source we read publishes a rate for anything on this page — no corruption-per-rift value, no Corrupted Item drop chance, no estimate of how much a partial clear actually reduces the meter. Everything above is a threshold, a state or a cap. Treat the arithmetic as unknown, and measure it in game with the Research Monitor rather than trusting a table you did not build.

## FAQ

**Can I turn Hard Mode off?**
One guide says it cannot be switched off once it starts, and nothing contradicts that. Do not treat it as reversible.

**Why did my team vote and nothing happened?**
Every player has to pull their own lever. One lever pulled in a four-player lobby is not a vote, and the cutscene will not start until all of them are down.

**How do I know the Corruption percentage?**
You do not, as a number. The Research Outpost status board and the placeable Research Monitor print the *state* in text. There is no bar on screen.

**Do bandages and medkits still heal at high Corruption?**
Yes. They work normally at 25% and at 80%. It is only *passive* healing that slows and then stops.

**Does the food rot affect me if the food is in the campfire crockpot?**
No — it rots in your sack, your hotbar, from corrupted animals and from farmplots. It does not rot on the map. It also cannot be decontaminated by cooking, which is the part that catches people out.

**Is the Corrupted Thorn Armour worth taking?**
It is the only Corrupted Item that cannot explode, and it lasts the run. Put it on, unless you are a Vampire who needs the cloak to heal.

**Is Hard Mode the best way to farm Diamonds?**
On paper yes — roughly double at the first milestone and nearly triple at day 99. In practice it only pays if you reach those days, and the mode makes reaching them harder. Our diamond page puts it as a switch you make when the group can reliably reach day 99, not earlier.

**Do corrupted wolves behave differently from normal ones?**
They spawn from wolf rifts with a purple palette, glowing eyes and tentacles on their backs, and they are more aggressive than the normal versions. See the wolf page for the base numbers.
