# Hunger in 99 Nights in the Forest

## Quick Answer

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What it is | A bar on the middle-left of the screen that falls from the moment you spawn |
| How long you have from full | **12 minutes 40 seconds** on one version of the wiki, **17 minutes 20 seconds** on another version of the same page |
| What empties it faster | **Sprinting**, which drains it much faster than standing still |
| What happens at zero | Your health starts dropping, and if nothing changes you starve to death |
| The unit food is measured in | **Forks**, written by the wikis as a fork symbol. A carrot is one fork, a Pumpkin is four |
| The single rule everyone learns the hard way | **Raw meat damages you.** Cook it at the campfire first |
| What slows the drain | The Camper class, the Bat blessing from the Caverns, and the Explorer class |
| The longest a bar can last | **21 minutes 54 seconds**, with all three effects stacked |

Something on this site mentions hunger across **101 pages** — every taming recipe, every class review, every fishing route, every base plan. Nothing explains the bar itself. This page explains the bar.

It also does something no other guide does: it tells you which of the numbers you can trust. Hunger is the single mechanic in this game with the most confidently published wrong information around it, including a thirst system that does not exist.

## What the Bar Is and Where It Sits

The hunger bar appears on the **middle-left of the screen** the moment a run starts, and it falls continuously from there. There is no difficulty setting, no warm-up period and no starting buffer — the clock is running while you read your first objective.

Three properties define it, and all three are unanimous across every source:

**It only goes up from eating.** Food is the only input. There is no regeneration, no passive fill near the campfire, and no item that slows the drain without a specific perk, class or blessing behind it.

**Sprinting accelerates it sharply.** Every source says "much faster" and none publishes a multiplier. There is no walk-sprint-jog tier; there is moving normally and there is sprinting, and sprinting costs you food.

**Empty does not mean dead.** Zero is the start of a second phase, not the end. Health begins to fall only after the bar has already emptied, which is why the bar is a warning system rather than a death timer.

One clarification that saves new players real time: **there is no thirst meter.** Several guide sites describe one, along with water purification, calorie counts and temperature in degrees. None of that is in the game. The only other survival drain you have to plan around is cold, and that belongs to the [Snow Biome](/locations/snow-biome) and [Warm Clothing](/items/warm-clothing).

## How Fast It Drains — and the Two Numbers One Wiki Publishes

This is where it gets strange, and worth reading carefully because both numbers are in wide circulation.

The fandom wiki's Hunger page states that a player standing still or idle survives **17 minutes and 20 seconds** before dying of hunger.

The same wiki's editable source for the same page gives a base figure of **12 minutes and 40 seconds**, and then builds an entire arithmetic table on top of it.

Those cannot both be right. Everything below follows the **12 minutes 40 seconds** version, because it is the only one that comes with supporting arithmetic — the reduction percentages published alongside it produce exactly the totals listed. The 17 minute 20 second figure stands alone with nothing derived from it. Treat 12:40 as the working number and 17:20 as an unexplained alternative.

| Condition | Drain reduction | Total bar time |
|---|---|---|
| Standing still, no effects | — | **12:40** |
| Camper class (second talent) | 10% | **14:05** |
| Bat blessing, once | 10% | **14:05** |
| Bat blessing, twice | 20% | **15:12** |
| Explorer class (second talent), away from camp or fire out | 43% | **18:14** |

Two caveats on that table. The Camper figure is described two ways — as a class effect and specifically as the class's **second talent** — so a fresh Camper does not start with the reduction. And the Explorer figure is **conditional**: it applies when you are away from camp, or when the fire is out. Standing at your own fire, it does nothing.

## Sprinting Is the Hidden Drain

Every source agrees sprinting drains the bar "much faster," and not one publishes a figure. That absence matters more than it looks, because sprinting is how you escape the [Deer](/entities/the-deer), how you cross the map at night, and how you get back before the fire dies.

The practical reading: **the published times are a floor, not an average.** Twelve minutes and forty seconds is what you get by standing still. A player who runs everywhere is spending that budget considerably faster, and the same applies to any reduction effect — a 10% saving on a bar you are draining at sprint rate buys you less wall-clock time than it appears to.

There is no published interaction between sprinting and any of the reduction effects, so there is no way to tell whether the Camper saving applies proportionally. Assume it does and treat the total as optimistic.

Starvation is one of the two things that empties a bar the revive system then has to deal with. What happens at zero is on the [**death and respawn**](/guides/death-and-respawn) page.

## What Starvation Actually Does

When the bar empties, **health begins to drop**. It is a drain, not an instant loss, and the gap between empty and dead is the window in which a single carrot saves the run.

The sources agree on the shape and disagree on the specifics of the second phase, which is typical for this game:

- The wiki says the player will "eventually" starve to death, without a rate.
- One guide states plainly that health starts dropping once hunger hits zero.
- One site adds a movement penalty while starving, quantified at around a quarter of your speed — from the same source that publishes hunger percentages nobody else carries, so treat the number as unverified while accepting that a penalty probably exists.
- The same site claims it takes **five days** to starve. Against published bar times of twelve to seventeen minutes, that is not reconcilable with any other source and should be ignored.

What is not in dispute: **a full bar is free insurance and an empty one compounds.** Starvation damage stacks with cold, with poison, and with whatever else the night throws at you, which is why hunger is rarely what kills you directly and often what leaves you one hit short.

## The Fork System

Every wiki and most guides measure food in **forks**, written as a fork symbol. The scale runs from one fork to four, and it is worth internalising because it is the only unit the game shares with the community:

- **One fork** is a snack. Carrots, berries, apples, cake, and most raw meat.
- **Two forks** is a real meal. Corn, and most cooked meat.
- **Four forks** is the top of the scale. Pumpkin, cooked Ribs, every Crock Pot dish, and cooked Shark.

The pattern that runs through the whole food table is **cooking roughly doubles it**: one fork raw becomes two cooked, two becomes four. That is the single highest-value habit in the game, and it costs you nothing but a walk to the fire.

Two items break the pattern in opposite directions. **Ribs** start at two forks raw, which is already better than most cooked meat, and reach four when cooked. **Shark** is the only fish that does the same: two raw, four cooked.

Two items give **no hunger at all** and instead produce an effect: **Chili** and **Jellyfish** both trade their hunger value for a speed or energy boost, which is why they appear in [speedrun](/guides/speedrun-guide) and escape plans rather than meal plans.

Food heals as well as feeds, and Jar o' Jelly is a full heal with five minutes of speed attached; our [Healing, Bandages and Medkits](/guides/healing-guide) guide puts that dish beside the consumables it competes with.

## Raw Food Hurts You — the Rule That Catches Everyone

**Morsel, Steak and Ribs all damage you when eaten raw.** This is stated identically on the fandom, the wiki and the guide sites, and it is the mistake nearly every new player makes once, because raw meat is sitting right there and the campfire is forty metres away.

The rule is not a penalty you can plan around — it is a reason to route differently. Cooked meat is double the forks **and** no damage, so eating raw is never a trade-off in your favour. It is strictly worse on both axes.

The exception is genuine emergencies: if the bar is empty and health is already falling, the damage from raw meat is smaller than the damage from starvation. Eat it, then cook the next one.

There is one related claim worth flagging as unverified: one guide says raw **Clownfish** slightly costs health. No other source repeats it, and the fish tables otherwise treat all non-Shark fish identically.

Worth knowing, because it is the fastest shortcut in the game: animals killed by fire drop **already-cooked** meat. The [Fire Bandit](/classes/fire-bandit-class) and the [Flamethrower](/weapons/how-to-get-flamethrower) both turn that into a food pipeline that never touches the campfire at all.

there is a second way to cook on the move — raw food placed in an Infernal Sack cooks itself — which is the tradeoff our [sacks and inventory capacity](/guides/inventory-capacity) guide sets against the campfire.

## Cooking and the Crock Pot

There are two cooking systems and they are not the same thing.

**The campfire** turns raw meat into cooked meat. Nothing more. Every run has one, it costs nothing, and it is where the doubling happens.

**The Crock Pot** is where the four-fork dishes live. It takes the ingredients you have already gathered — carrots, corn, pumpkins, apples, morsels, steaks, ribs, cakes, fish — and returns a Stew worth four forks that also **warms you if you are cold**. That warmth clause is easy to miss and it is the reason a Stew is worth carrying into the Snow Biome rather than eating at camp.

The Crock Pot has a throughput property worth knowing: each time you take a stew out, a new one starts cooking automatically. Stacked ingredients beside it therefore become a standing order rather than a series of errands.

One class changes this whole tier. The [Chef](/classes/chef-class) unlocks the six dishes in the table above — Seafood Chowder, Steak Dinner, Pumpkin Soup, BBQ Ribs, Carrot Cake and Jar o' Jelly — and the [Cook](/classes/chef-class) at a fifth of the price makes every ordinary dish restore more. If you want the full recipe breakdown with buff durations, that is the Chef page; this page is only about what hunger needs from it.

One of those four routes is a weather event, and our [Weather and Night Events](/guides/weather-and-night-events) guide says exactly which one.


Per the fandom's own framing, there are exactly four sources, and every food strategy is a mix of them:

**1. Killing things.** Everything that drops Morsel, Steak or Ribs. Reliable, but it costs ammo or risk, and it produces raw food you then have to cook.

**2. Foraging.** Carrots, berries, apples and chili scattered across the map. Free and immediate, and the reason nobody starves on day one. The catch is that berries and chili **do not grow back** on their own — you need their seeds.

**3. Farming.** [Farm Plots](/locations/crafting-bench) produce Corn and Pumpkin, and Pumpkin is the only four-fork food that does not require an animal to die. This is the difference between a camp that eats and a camp that hunts.

**4. Fishing.** Free, infinitely repeatable, and produces cooked food at two forks with no ammo spent. See [the fishing guide](/guides/fishing-guide) for why this is the cheapest of the four to run at scale.

The fifth route exists but is seasonal: Update Parties and trick-or-treating hand out cake and the candy items.

## What Slows the Drain: Camper, the Bat Blessing, Explorer

Three things reduce how fast the bar empties, and only three. All of them are published on one wiki and none of them appear on any guide site, which is why most players do not know they exist.

**The Camper class, at its second talent.** A 10% reduction, taking the bar from 12:40 to 14:05. Camper costs **10 Diamonds** — the cheapest class in the game — which makes this the cheapest hunger solution available. Note the caveat: it is the second talent, not the starting perk. The class starts with reduced hunger drain already listed, so read the level requirement in your own run.

**The Bat blessing, from the Caverns.** Also 10%, also taking the bar to 14:05. The important detail is that **it can be taken twice**, for a total of 20% and a bar of 15:12. See [The Caverns](/locations/the-caverns) for the blessing system it belongs to.

**The Explorer class, at its second talent.** A **43%** reduction — far larger than either of the others — taking the bar to 18:14. The condition is severe: it applies **away from camp, or when the fire is out**. It is a roaming perk, not a base perk, and it does nothing while you stand at your own fire.

Stacking behaviour, per the same source:

- Camper plus Bat = **15:12**
- Camper plus Explorer, or Bat plus Explorer = **19:22**
- Camper plus one Bat plus Explorer = **21:54**, the maximum

Our site has no [Camper](/classes/all-classes) or Explorer page yet, and several class lists disagree on whether they exist at all. [Best Class](/classes/best-class) walks through how the cheap end of the roster is usually evaluated. The [All Classes](/classes/all-classes) page tracks which entries are disputed.

## The Maximum You Can Stretch It To

**21 minutes and 54 seconds**, with all three effects running at once.

That number is worth a paragraph, because it reframes what hunger management is. Getting from 12:40 to 21:54 is a 73% increase on the base bar, achieved without eating a single extra fork. Put differently: **the biggest hunger upgrade in the game is not a food, it is a set of three unlocks**, and two of them are one-time purchases rather than ongoing effort.

It is also worth noting what that stack costs you in opportunity. The Camper is 10 Diamonds and changes nothing else about how you play. The Bat blessing requires clearing the Caverns. The Explorer is a roaming class whose value depends entirely on how much time you spend away from the fire — which, in a game about defending a fire, may be very little.

For most players the honest recommendation is the cheapest one: **Camper at 10 Diamonds, for a permanent 10%.** Everything beyond that trades a class slot for a marginal bar extension.

## Hunger and the Two Diet Badges

Two of the game's 99-day restriction badges are diet rules, and both hinge on technicalities that only make sense once you know the food table. See [All Badges](/guides/all-badges) for the full badge roster.

**Vegetarian** asks you to survive 99 days without eating anything classified as meat. The trap is the Stew: **any meat in the recipe makes the Stew meat**, even though the dish itself is a vegetable-and-meat blend. One careless ingredient voids a 99-day run. The same applies to the **Meat? Sandwich**, which the wiki flags explicitly as non-vegetarian.

**Carnivory** asks the reverse — 99 days eating only meat — and the Stew rule works in your favour here, because a Stew that contains meat counts as meat.

Both interact with hunger arithmetic in an unglamorous way: a 99-day run is a long time to be choosing from half the food table, and the fork values above are what tell you whether the restriction is survivable. Vegetarian runs lean on Pumpkin at four forks; see [Pet Food](/taming/pet-food) for how much of that crop is also claimed by the taming economy. Carnivory runs lean on Ribs and cooked Shark, also at four.

## Hunger in Hard Mode

Hard Mode adds a mechanic no other difficulty has: **food spoils.**

At the 60% Corruption threshold, stored provisions rot on a timer measured in seconds rather than days. The one thing that does not rot is the carrot. See [Hard Mode](/guides/hard-mode) for the full Corruption ladder and where the other thresholds sit.

The consequence for hunger planning is direct. Everything in this page assumes food can be stockpiled — that a Pumpkin you harvested on day 5 is still a Pumpkin on day 20. Under Hard Mode that assumption breaks, and the four routes in the section above re-rank themselves: **foraging and fishing, which produce on demand, become strictly better than farming and stockpiling.** A fridge full of Corn is a liability; a pond is not.

## What This Means for a Base Plan

Hunger is not an emergency, and that is the most useful thing to know about it. It is a twelve-to-seventeen-minute bar that a single carrot resets, which means it is a logistics problem rather than a survival crisis.

Three conclusions follow:

**Cook, always.** The doubling is free and the raw damage is real. Nothing else in this page is worth as much per second spent.

**Farm early, hunt later.** Pumpkin at four forks from a Farm Plot beats anything that requires you to win a fight. See [Best Base Design](/guides/best-base-design) for where plots fit.

**Fish if the map allows it.** It is the only route with no input cost and no depletion, and it produces at two forks cooked with no animal and no ammo.

And the one that saves runs: **carry four forks, not ten ones.** A single Pumpkin or a single Stew replaces four carrots, and inventory space is the real constraint on every long run — the same reason [Best Pet](/taming/best-pet) ends up being a decision about carrying capacity.

## Where Sources Disagree

Twelve open contradictions, listed rather than resolved.

1. **The base bar time.** **12 minutes 40 seconds** on the wiki's editable source against **17 minutes 20 seconds** on the rendered page — two versions of the same page.
2. **The sprint multiplier.** Everyone says sprinting is much faster; nobody publishes a number.
3. **Starvation damage rate.** "Eventually," across every source. No figure.
4. **The movement penalty while starving.** Around 25% on one site, absent everywhere else.
5. **Time to starve.** Twelve to seventeen minutes on the wikis, against **five days** on one site.
6. **Hunger as a percentage.** One site gives every food as a percentage of the bar — raw meat 40%, cooked 70% — while five other sources use forks. The two systems do not convert cleanly.
7. **Food spoilage.** One site publishes spoilage times in days for every food. Our own [Hard Mode](/guides/hard-mode) page documents spoilage in seconds at a specific Corruption threshold. Those describe different games.
8. **The Bat blessing's direction.** One site says it makes hunger drain **40% faster**; the wiki says it reduces drain by 10%. Opposite effects, same blessing.
9. **Jellyfish hunger value.** No hunger value with an effect, against one fork, against one raw and two cooked.
10. **Stew restoration.** "Roughly half" of the bar against a specific **45%**.
11. **Stew requirements.** Crock Pot for most sources, Camper level 3 for others, and the fandom lists both.
12. **Whether thirst exists.** It does not. Several guide sites describe a full hydration system with water purification and infection chances, none of which is in the game.

## FAQ

**How long can I go without eating?**
Between twelve and seventeen minutes standing still, depending on which version of the wiki you believe, and considerably less if you sprint.

**What is a fork?**
The unit every wiki uses for food value. One fork is a carrot; four is the maximum, held by Pumpkin, cooked Ribs, cooked Shark and every Crock Pot dish.

**Why did eating raw meat hurt me?**
Because Morsel, Steak and Ribs all damage you raw. Cook them at the campfire — it doubles the value and removes the damage.

**What is the best food in the game?**
By forks, anything at four: Pumpkin, cooked Ribs, cooked Shark, or a Stew. By utility, Jar o' Jelly, which is a full heal with a five-minute speed boost attached.

**Does standing near the campfire slow it?**
No. Nothing slows the drain except the Camper class, the Bat blessing and the Explorer class.

**Is there a thirst meter?**
No. Any guide describing hydration, water purification or calorie counts is describing a different game.

**Does eating warm me up?**
Two things do: a **Stew** warms you if cold, and **Pumpkin Soup** keeps you warm for two minutes. Nothing else in the food table mentions it.

**What happens if I ignore hunger entirely?**
Your bar empties, your health starts dropping, and you die — usually not from the starvation itself but from whatever else arrives while your health is already falling.

**Does food matter on a 99-day run?**
More, not less. Two badges restrict what you may eat for the entire run, and the fork table is what decides whether either restriction is survivable.

**Is the Camper worth 10 Diamonds just for the hunger perk?**
For most players, yes — it is the cheapest permanent drain reduction in the game and it costs a twentieth of the next option.
