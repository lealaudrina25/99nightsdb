Nothing in the game tutorial tells you what finishing looks like. You get a campfire, four children somewhere out in the dark, and a number in the corner that climbs. Halfway through a run most players can tell you what kills them, and almost nobody can tell you what wins — because the win is not a fight, it is a schedule.

This page is the first one in this wiki to answer that question outright. The campfire page owns fuel costs, the gate table owns the 59 gates, the speedrun page owns the day multiplier, and the badges page owns the badge list. None of them says what a completed run is. This one does.

## Quick Answer

**You win by reaching the end of Day 99 with all four Missing Children already in your camp.** That is two conditions, not one, and both of them have to hold at the same moment:

1. **The counter has to reach 99** — not 99 nights survived, 99 days on the counter. See the clock section below for why that distinction matters.
2. **All four children have to be home before the run resolves** — Dino Kid, Kraken Kid, Squid Kid and Koala Kid. Rescuing one on the 99th day itself still counts.

Reaching Day 99 without all four gets you a cutscene and an end screen, and it is a real completion by the game's own accounting — just not the one the game is about. It is the safe way to play if you are not chasing the true ending, and it is what most first-time runs accidentally do.

There are **four ways a run can end**, only one of which is a win:

| # | How it ends | The trigger | A win |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **The true ending** | Day 99 resolves with all four children in camp | **Yes** |
| **2** | **The incomplete ending** | Day 99 resolves with at least one child still out there | No |
| **3** | **A death the run does not come back from** | Health hits zero and nothing reverses it | No |
| **4** | **Leaving** | You exit mid-run, even with a charged Respawn Capsule in camp | No |

Row 3 is fuzzier than it looks and is the reason sources disagree about how many endings exist at all. Row 4 is not contested anywhere, but it is also not in any guide we read — which is a strange thing about a game whose own death page already documents it.

One step further out: everything tier 5 asks for beyond this also wants the **Gem of the Forest**. The spend plan that spreads it across the tier-5 machines is on the [Gem of the Forest](/items/gem-of-the-forest) page.

## The True Ending, Stated Exactly

Four independent sources describe the same win condition, and they phrase it the same way: survive the full counter **and** bring all four children home. Delias Gaming calls it the good ending, Of Zen and Computing calls the same thing the true ending, New Web Play calls it the true ending, and the game's own badge table lists Orienteering — "rescue all four children" — as a Day 99 requirement.

What happens when you get there is the part nobody can verify, because it is a cutscene and no source publishes the dialogue:

- The **Deer Monster** watches from a distance rather than engaging.
- It disappears into an **underground facility**.
- An end screen prints how long the run took, in the form *"reached Day 99 in [minutes:seconds]"*.
- The closing line reads, across the two sources that quote it: **"More story and an ending will be revealed soon."**

That last line matters more than it looks. The true ending is deliberately not resolved, and the standing advice from the sources that quote it is to treat the win as a schedule to finish rather than a boss to kill. There is no final fight waiting on Day 99.

One practical note that only one source publishes and that every source agrees is safe: **New Web Play reports Night 99 as an ordinary night** — no special boss, no unique event, nothing scripted. If you enter the last night with all four children safe, the correct plan is to survive until dawn and take no risks at all. That is single-source, so treat it as a strong hint rather than a documented rule.

## The Clock Behind the 99

The number in the corner is a **day counter**, and the game's title is describing it slightly wrong. Each day runs **3 minutes**, each night runs **1 minute and 30 seconds**, and one full cycle is **4 minutes and 30 seconds**. At the base rate of one day per night, 99 days is 99 cycles, and the whole run is **445.5 minutes — about 7 hours and 25 minutes** of continuous play with the multiplier never raised.

That is the number that makes the win legible. Nobody is going to sit through seven and a half hours, so the counter has to be pushed, and there is exactly one mechanism that pushes it: the day multiplier, which rises **+1 for every bed you craft at the bench** and **+1 for every child you rescue**. Both are permanent for that run. At the ceiling — which sources place at 8x or 9x — 99 days is reachable in **11 nights**, about **49 minutes and 30 seconds** of pure cycle time.

Everything about the arithmetic, the four bed recipes, the Temporal Accelerometer and the 8x-versus-9x fight lives on the [speedrun](/guides/speedrun-guide) page. The point here is only this: **the win condition is cheap to state and expensive to hit**, and the multiplier is the entire gap between the two.

There is one hard wall on the opening that no multiplier gets around. Events on **real days 1 through 10** fire on a real clock regardless of the multiplier, so the first ten days cannot be skipped — the first Cultist Raid lands on real day 4 and the Deer wakes on real day 2 whether or not your counter is moving fast.

The mid-game answer to this is [Pumpkin](/items/pumpkin) — the four-fork crop behind Pumpkin Soup, the Thanksgiving event, and every five-cycle tame that costs ten of them.

Level 6 sits on the critical path of that win and one of its eleven rewards is the [Kiwi](/entities/the-kiwi); ours is the page that says plainly whether to stop for it.

Rescuing the kids is a fight schedule, and the Steak feeding the pets that clear those caves is the input our [Steak](/items/steak) page budgets.

Rescues take a long walk, and a [Carrot](/items/carrot) is the cheapest way to keep the bar up while you do it.

## The Four Rescues Are the Win Condition

Surviving is the easy half. The four rescues are what actually decide whether you get the true ending, and each one is a separate latch:

| Child | Cave | Campfire gate | The guard that drops their key |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Dino Kid** | Wolf cave | **2** | the last of **five Wolves**, red collars |
| **Kraken Kid** | Kraken cave | **4** | the last of **four Alpha Wolves**, blue collars |
| **Squid Kid** | Squid cave | **5** | the last of **two Bears**, yellow collars |
| **Koala Kid** | Koala cave | **6** | the last of **six Bears**, black collars |

Three things follow from that table, and none of them is obvious from the badge list.

**The fire is a win-condition prerequisite, not a convenience.** Level 6 is what unlocks the last child. A run that never gets the fire past 5 has a smaller game and cannot reach the true ending at all — it will end as row 2 of the table at the top of this page. The complete gate list, including chests, caves, ponds and buildings, is on our [Campfire Level Gates](/guides/campfire-level-gates) page.

**Getting past the grate is only half of it.** Each rescue is a locked cave, a key and a pack to clear, and the four coloured keys come off four guardian packs — **5 + 4 + 2 + 6 = 17 creature kills** across a single winning run. That is a great deal of combat, and it is combat nobody counts when they describe the win condition as "survive and bring the kids home". Where each key comes from, which cave it opens and which guard has to die last are all on our [Keys and Locked Doors](/guides/keys-and-locked-doors) page.

**The keys are drop-locked, so a partial clear is worth exactly nothing.** The key falls off the *last* guard alive, which means a rescue you abandon halfway is a rescue you cannot half-finish later in the same sweep.

So the honest shape of a winning run is not "survive and explore". It is **Level 2 for Dino Kid as early as possible, Level 4 for the Kraken Kid through the middle, Level 6 for the Koala Kid in time for the last rescue** — with wood competing for the campfire and for the bench the whole way.

## The Badge Trap: You Cannot Win and Take Self-Preservation

This is the finding that the badges page states and never connects to winning, so it belongs here.

**Orienteering and Self-Preservation cannot share a run.** One requires all four children home; the other requires you to bring home none of them. Since the four rescues are the win condition, **a run that wins is a run that forfeits Self-Preservation**, and any guide that offers you a "complete everything in one run" checklist is quietly handing you one of those two badges as the price of the other.

The same trap runs through the diet pair — **Vegetarian and Carnivory** are two conditions on the same 99 days that cannot both hold.

What that means in practice is a decision you should make deliberately, before night 20 rather than at day 99:

| If you want | Then the true ending is | And you are giving up |
|---|---|---|
| **The true ending** | Mandatory — all four children | Self-Preservation, Carnivory, and every cave chest that needs a key |
| **Self-Preservation** | Impossible | The true ending itself |
| **A fast run** | Still possible, but the multiplier is capped lower for the whole run | Most of the loot in the game |

The full badge table, the diamond totals and the routes are on our [All Badges](/guides/all-badges) page. The arithmetic of the multiplier is on the [speedrun](/guides/speedrun-guide) page. What is new here is only the connection: **winning closes one badge door by definition.**

On Hard Mode the [Chili](/items/chili) you banked for a tame rots exactly like any plot crop, and the win plan has to allow for that, on our Chili page.

The Farm Plot cannot grow every crop; the rest come from Seed Packs on our [Seed Packs](/items/seed-packs) page.

In Hard Mode the food you carry decides the run, and [Berries](/items/berry) are one of only four foods the rot spores cannot touch.

## Winning on Hard Mode

Hard Mode does not change the win condition. It changes what it costs. You survive the same 99 days with the same four children, and the five-star achievement is still awarded for reaching the end of the run — but the Corruption meter runs against you the whole way through seven effect thresholds, and one of the four rescues is a genuine negotiation rather than a fence to climb. The unlock, the one-way switch and every corruption stage are on our [Hard Mode](/guides/hard-mode) page.

The single cross-page warning worth carrying: Hard Mode pays more for the same objectives, but **an abandoned Hard Mode attempt is worth less than a completed normal run**. Reach Day 99 in the mode you can actually finish.

## Where Sources Disagree

Three genuine conflicts, in descending order of how much they change your plan.

**1. How many endings are there — two, three or four?** Deltias Gaming and 99nightsintheforest.org both describe exactly two: a good ending for finishing properly, and a bad ending for not rescuing in time. Neither of them treats dying as an ending. Of Zen and Computing lists four — good, bad, incomplete and death-specific — and counts "die to any cause" as the bad ending. Gamermovil's Spanish guide implies a further set of secret branches and even a fifth ghost child, but it is a single non-English source contradicting four English ones, so it is not actionable.

Our own reading, stated as ours: the four-row table at the top is a reconciliation rather than a fifth claim. Rows 1 and 2 are the two-endings camp; row 3 is death, which the same sites disagree about; row 4 is the one they all forget. **Nobody we read publishes which of those rows the game treats as an ending versus a restart**, so treat the table as the arrangement that matches the evidence, not as official.

**2. Is death an ending or a setback?** This is the deepest disagreement and it decides whether row 3 exists at all. Delias Gaming's model has no death ending — dying simply means you start again. Of Zen and Computing's model has dying as *the* bad ending. Our [death and respawn](/guides/death-and-respawn) page sits in a third place that the other sources never admit: in a team, zero health opens a **downed window** with four ways back, and only a run where nobody can reach you actually ends. In solo play there is no save system at all, and going down with nothing catching you is the end. Both models can be true at once, and which one you are living under depends entirely on who is standing near your body.

**3. Ninety-nine nights or ninety-nine days?** The title says nights, the badge says Day 99, and the fandom wiki's Days page is explicit that the counter counts days with one night per day unless the multiplier rises — so a "99-night" run at 5x is over twenty nights long and a "99-day" run at 9x is eleven. Nobody has published the exact moment the 99th night resolves, and that is why "rescue the last child on the final night" is reported as safe by two sources without either of them saying at what instant the run ends.

**4. Our own wiki used to say two.** The beginner guide currently states that runs end in one of two ways — the fire goes out, or someone wanders into the dark. That line is not wrong, it is just scoped: it describes the two failures that end most of the first twenty nights, which is true and worth saying to a new player. It is not the whole list. The four rows at the top of this page are the complete one, and row 3 of them is the row the beginner guide's two-way model has no room for. Anything that needs a revive, a downed body or a charged capsule exists inside the gap between those two descriptions.

## FAQ

**So what actually counts as winning?**
Reaching the end of Day 99 with all four Missing Children in camp. Surviving Day 99 without them is a finish, but not the true ending.

**Can I rescue the last child on the 99th night itself?**
Yes, by every source that addresses it. Delias Gaming explicitly says a player who completes all four rescues on the 99th night still gets the good ending by keeping the rest of that night risk-free, and New Web Play says the same. Nobody publishes the exact resolution moment, so plan the whole last night as safe play.

**Do I have to win with the fire still burning?**
Nothing we read says the campfire has to be lit when the run resolves, and no source makes it a condition. Keep it lit anyway — it is the only safe zone you have during the night you are trying to survive.

**What is the fastest anyone says a win takes?**
The badge itself sets the bar: **Swiftness**, Day 99 in under 90 minutes, which works out to a day every 55 seconds against a 4-minute-30-second cycle — arithmetically impossible without the multiplier at its ceiling. The diamond reward is itself disputed at 4 or 8, and the speedrun page prints that argument rather than settling it.

**Does dying end my run?**
In solo play, usually yes. In a team, only if nobody reaches you inside the downed window — and there are four ways to reverse a death without a teammate, theRespawn Capsule being the only one available in camp. All of that is on the [death and respawn](/guides/death-and-respawn) page.

**Is there a final boss on Night 99?**
New Web Play says it is an ordinary night with nothing scripted; that is one source. The sources that quote the ending all describe the Deer watching from a distance rather than fighting, which agrees with it in spirit.

**Can I get every badge in one winning run?**
No. Orienteering and Self-Preservation cannot share a run, and neither can Vegetarian and Carnivory. A winning run takes Orienteering by definition, which closes Self-Preservation.

**Is the true ending finished?**
No, and the game says so on screen. The closing line, quoted by two sources, is "More story and an ending will be revealed soon."
