# Fishing in 99 Nights in the Forest

If you want the whole ladder in one place rather than one gate per page, [Campfire Level Gates](/guides/campfire-level-gates) lists all fifty-nine campfire level gates by level, and flags the ones the sources disagree about.

## Quick Answer

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Added when | The Fishing Update on **15 August 2025** |
| Do I need anything to start | Yes — a rod. There is no hand-fishing and no crafting route |
| Where the rod comes from now | The **Tool Trader** next to your campfire, **20 Mossy Coins**, from Day 1. Free rods also sit inside the Fishing Cabin |
| How many rod tiers | Three: **Old Rod**, **Good Rod**, **Strong Rod**. You cannot buy or loot the upper two; you upgrade into them |
| Can I catch anything besides fish | Yes. 15 documented non-fish items, including the Cultist Gem and the only obtainable **Trident** |
| How much hunger does it solve | Cooked fish restore roughly double what raw fish do, and the Shark is the single best food in the water |
| Badges tied to it | Four, paying **25 Diamonds** in total, plus two furniture items |
| The one-line version | Fishing is a zero-ammo food and loot pipeline that costs 20 coins to start and pays for itself before the second night |

Fishing arrived quietly and then became load-bearing. Something on this site mentions fish, a rod or a pond across **25 pages** — the bear you feed salmon, the Trident you cannot buy, the gems you can grind instead of raiding for — and none of them explain the system underneath. This page does.

It also warns you about something no single guide will: most published fishing instructions are now pointing at a building that may no longer exist.

If you want the whole crop in one place, [Pumpkin](/items/pumpkin) covers where pumpkins come from now, what a farm plot yields every two days, and which item really gates an apex tame.

## What Fishing Is Actually For

Three currencies come out of one pond, and very few other activities pay more than one at a time.

**Food that does not run out.** Hunting costs ammo, pulls you away from the fire, and sometimes costs you the fight. A pond near camp produces indefinitely for free. Once cooked, fish sit near the top of the food table — see the [Campfire](/guides/campfire) page for how the fire has to be levelled to unlock the better water, which is the real gate on all of this.

**Ammo and medical supplies without opening anything.** Bandages, Medkits, Rifle Ammo and Revolver Ammo all come out of the water. If you are short on rounds, fishing beats raiding, because raiding spends rounds to get rounds.

**Materials you otherwise fight for.** The [Cultist Gem](/items/cultist-gem) has eight documented sources, and the fishing route is one of the three the community treats as sustainable. Nobody enjoys the alternative of last-hitting a raid boss for one.

And then there is the tail: four [badges](/guides/all-badges) worth 25 Diamonds, two pieces of furniture you cannot get any other way, and one weapon that has no second route at all.

The cost is attention. You cannot move during the mini-game. Every guide says some version of the same thing — fish in daylight, fish with someone watching your back, or fish while the [Deer](/entities/the-deer) is somewhere else.

## Getting Your First Rod — and the Building Everyone Names Wrong

Every guide tells you to walk into a specific building and pick up an Old Rod. The guides do not agree on what that building is called, whether it still exists, or which campfire level unlocks it. There are at least **ten** names in circulation for one or two structures:

| Name used | Who uses it |
|---|---|
| Fishing Hut | Fandom wiki Tools page, GladiatorBoost, gggamer, Pro Game Guides, rblxguide |
| Fishing Cabin | Techwiser, Game Geek Fusion, Pocket Tactics, our own codes pages |
| Fishing Shack | Miraheze wiki, our own codes pages |
| Fishing Gear Hut | Pro Game Guides, describing the same building with a giant blue fish sign |
| Dock Cabin | rblxguide, as the structure the Fishing Hut merged into |
| Upgrading Cabin | Our own [Taming Flute](/items/taming-flute) page records this one for the same bench |
| Skills Building | Fandom fishing page and Miraheze, for the place you upgrade rods |
| Upgrade Station | Techwiser and BloxBoom, as the building the Modifier Flames update deleted |
| Tool Workbench | The replacement bench beside the Tool Trader |
| Upgrade Bench | Pro Game Guides, describing the bench inside the hut |

Two of these are genuinely different buildings and eight are naming drift, which is why the instructions read like they contradict each other.

**There is a harder problem than naming.** rblxguide's own Fishing Hut page states plainly that the standalone Fishing Hut **has been removed**, folded together with the Dock Cabin into the combined Fishing Cabin found in current versions, and says directly that older guides sending you hunting for it are out of date. Almost every other source on this page was written before that change and still describes the hut as standing. If you walked to where a guide said the hut would be and found a different building, that is why.

The campfire level is contested too:

| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| The building appears once your campfire hits **Level 2** | Pro Game Guides, GladiatorBoost, gggamer, dinokid wiki |
| It used to spawn around Level 2, but appears at **Level 3–4** in its later form | rblxguide |
| Fresh Ponds are fishable from **Campfire Level 1** | Fandom fishing page |

A separate, uncontested data point supports something in the Level 2 region: the `yay fishing` chat code documented on our own [How to Redeem Codes](/codes/how-to-redeem-codes) page requires Campfire Level 2 and a rod. Treat Level 2 as the working assumption and do not be surprised either way.

One clarification worth internalising, because two of our own pages had to make it: the [Tool Workshop](/locations/tool-workshop) is not the Tool Trader. The Workshop is the anvil building for one crafted item at a time. The Trader is an NPC with a bench beside him. There is no anvil anywhere in the rod transaction.

## The Tool Trader: 20 Mossy Coins, Day One

This is the current answer, and it is the one most fishing guides predate.

The **Modifier Flames update of 21 February 2026** introduced the Tool Trader, an NPC who **replaced the old Upgrade Station building entirely**. BloxBoom, Techwiser, Game Geek Fusion and Moyens all describe the same arrangement:

- **Location.** Right beside your campfire, on the edge of whichever biome spawned near it. His workshop is marked on the map with a hammer-and-screwdriver icon.
- **Availability.** **Day 1**, no unlock required. This is the part that changes early game play: you do not wait three days to start fishing.
- **Fixed.** Unlike the [Pelt Trader](/items/pelt-trader), who arrives on a schedule and wanders, the Tool Trader stays put for the whole run.
- **Stock.** Exactly two items, both priced at **20 Mossy Coins**: the Old Fishing Rod and the Old Taming Flute. **Each can be bought once per run**, so losing one costs you real money to replace.
- **Upgrades.** The Upgrade Station is gone. Upgrades now happen at the **Tool Workbench beside the NPC**: equip the tool, interact with the bench, spend accumulated Tool XP, confirm.

**Mossy Coins** are the currency, and farming them early is the actual bottleneck. They drop from killing cultists — consistently, and in the largest amounts — and also sit on buildings, structures and throughout the forest floor. If a raid is happening, the raid is your coin run.

There is a free alternative to buying: Techwiser and Game Geek Fusion both list the free **Old Rod inside the Fishing Cabin behind the camp** as the zero-cost route to the same item. Take the free rods first, keep the 20 coins for the flute, and only buy a rod if you have already lost one.

That purchase is not only a quality-of-life spend. Buying an upgradable tool from the Tool Trader is the **Apprenticeship badge**, worth 4 Diamonds — see the badge table on our [All Badges](/guides/all-badges) page. The rod pays you back immediately.

Other documented ways to get hold of an Old Rod, roughly in order of how reliable they still are:

| Route | Status |
|---|---|
| Tool Trader, 20 Mossy Coins | Current and guaranteed from Day 1 |
| Free rods inside the Fishing Cabin | Current, four sources |
| Starting tool of the Fisherman class (50 Diamonds) | Current |
| **Common and Good Chests** | The wiki's Tools page; corroborated by our own [Chest Tiers](/items/chest-tiers) tables, which list the Old Rod in both the Common and Good pools |
| **Pelt Trader stock** | Disputed — see the note below |

**The Pelt Trader disagreement is the one to be careful with.** Pro Game Guides says the Pelt Trader will "sometimes" have an Old Rod, GladiatorBoost says he sells them occasionally, and the fandom fishing page lists him first. Our own [Pelt Trader](/items/pelt-trader) page investigated exactly this and concluded the Old Rod is **no longer obtainable from him** — it appears on old snapshots of the trade tables and not in the current one, in the same way the Old Taming Flute was quietly removed. Plan around that. Do not spend three days waiting for a trade that the current table does not contain.

## The Three Rods and the XP Nobody Counts

There are exactly three tiers and exactly one way through them:

**Old Rod, then Good Rod, then Strong Rod.** There is no source anywhere describing a Good Rod or Strong Rod that can be bought, looted or crafted. You must start with an Old Rod, earn XP on it, and upgrade twice, and no source publishes an XP number for either threshold.

How XP works, per the fandom and Miraheze versions, which agree here:

- **Every successful catch awards XP**, not every fish. The non-fish items on the catch table count too.
- **The harder the item, the more XP it gives.** This is why endgame fishing targets the Bone Pond rather than the easy water.
- **When the bar fills, the rod starts glowing**, which is your cue to go upgrade. Pro Game Guides adds that the upgrade itself is free — the XP is the whole cost.
- Upgrading then happens at the Tool Workbench beside the Tool Trader, or, in older guides, at the bench inside the fishing building. Same action, two locations depending on when your source was written.

**What the rod tier actually changes is where the sources go vague.** All of them say higher tiers make harder fish easier; none publish a catch-rate number, a durability figure, or a tier requirement for any individual fish. If you are asking "can this rod catch a Shark," the honest answer from the published material is that every shark pool is fishable with any rod, and the rod changes your odds rather than unlocking the fish.

One practical consequence: the [Cultist Gem](/items/cultist-gem) page on this site already found that the fandom names the **Algal Pond with a Strong Rod** as the most efficient gem pipeline, and notes the Bone Pond drops gems less often than the Algal Pond despite being the higher-tier water. Rod tier and water tier are two different levers, and they do not always point the same way.

Rain and blizzards change which waters are safe to fish, and our [Weather and Night Events](/guides/weather-and-night-events) guide handles that in the weather section.


The loop is four steps, and only the third one has any skill in it.

**1. Equip the rod and click a body of water.** Any pond works at any time. There is no time-of-day restriction and no bait system in the published material.

**2. Wait for the bite — this is where the ripples matter.** Some ponds show white, transparent ripples on the surface. Those are a **fast fishing spot**: casting into one produces a near-instant bite.

- Ripples **run out**. The fandom page says roughly **six catches**, after which they disappear; Miraheze hedges to "a certain number of catches." Either way, a hotspot is a window, not a permanent fixture.
- **Without ripples, expect to wait up to 15 seconds** per bite. That is the single biggest time lever in the whole system.
- **Algal Ponds, Bone Ponds and Cold Ponds frequently have no ripples at all** but remain fishable. This is the part players misread as a bug: the best fish do not live in the fastest water.

**3. Play the mini-game.** The descriptions differ in wording and agree in shape. Two bars appear:

- Pro Game Guides describes a **right bar showing hook strength** and a **left bar with a moving cursor and a static green area** — click when the cursor crosses the green area to strengthen the hook, repeating until the right bar fills.
- Fandom and Miraheze describe the same mechanic as clicking when the **red line sits over the green rectangle**.

Wrong clicks **lose progress**, and if the bar empties the mini-game ends and you have to cast again. **The rarer the fish, the harder the mini-game.** Two sources add a detail the others omit entirely: easy fish **reel in automatically**, with no mini-game at all.

**4. Cook it.** Everything fishable lands behind you on the bank. Drag it onto the campfire like any other cookable item.

There is no fillet, no sushi, no fish recipe of any kind. Two independent wikis say so explicitly and no source offers a counter-example: cooking is the only transformation available, and even the [Chef](/classes/chef-class) station's seafood recipes work on whole fish rather than processed parts.

## The Seven Waters and Their Campfire Gates

Every fish has an address, and the address is a water type rather than a map coordinate. Note that this table and the next section describe **two incompatible models** — see Where Sources Disagree.

| Water | Where / campfire gate | What it looks like | What lives in it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fresh Pond** | Campfire Level 1+, scattered around the forest | Light blue, one of the smallest ponds | Mackerel, Salmon, Clownfish, Swordfish |
| **Algal Pond** | **Campfire Level 4–5** | Green; described as looking like saltwater | Salmon, Clownfish, Swordfish, **Jellyfish**, **Shark** |
| **Bone Pond** | **Campfire Level 6** | Dark blue, one of the largest pools, skeletons in it | Swordfish, Jellyfish, **Shark** |
| **Cold Pond** | The Snow Biome, including inside the Cold Cabin | Frozen | **Char**, **Eel** — found nowhere else |
| **Lava Pond** | The [Volcanic Biome](/locations/volcanic-biome), **Campfire Level 2–3** | Pool of lava | **Lava Eel**, **Lionfish** — found nowhere else |
| **Frog Pond** | The purple water of the [Frog Cave](/locations/frog-cave), reachable after the boss via the whirlpool | Purple, dim, lit by luminescent mushrooms instead of cattails | Mackerel, Salmon, poison gear, **Frog Boots** |
| **Alien Ponds** | UFO crash / [Mothership](/locations/the-alien-mothership) areas | Glowing green water | Normal catches plus **Alien Batteries** |

The Frog Pond deserves a flag. It is the only water where fishing produces equipment rather than consumables, and per our own [Poison Armour](/items/how-to-get-poison-armour) and [Poison Spear](/weapons/how-to-get-poison-spear) pages, the frog pools use the **same loot table and the same rates** as the Frog King's chest — meaning a roughly 1% roll on the armour and 3% on the spear, both fishable after you have already killed the boss. That is a real second chance on a boss drop, and almost nobody fishes it.

The counting itself is contested: the fandom lists six water types, one translated guide says five, and the Alien Ponds exist in the game and on our own Mothership page but appear in no fishing table at all.

## Every Fish You Can Catch

Eleven things come out of the water on a hook. Ten are fish and one is rubbish. Hunger is given in "forks," the unit the wikis use, and the pattern is consistent: **cooked is worth about double raw**.

| Catch | Forks (raw, cooked) | Water it comes from | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Mackerel** | 1, 2 | Fresh Pond, Frog Pond | The taming fish — see below |
| **Salmon** | 1, 2 | Fresh Pond, Algal Pond, Frog Pond | Also a taming food for bears and wolves |
| **Clownfish** | 1, 2 | Fresh, Algal and Bone Ponds | GladiatorBoost claims eating it raw slightly costs health |
| **Swordfish** | 1, 2 | Fresh, Algal and Bone Ponds | Polar Bear taming food |
| **Jellyfish** | No hunger value listed; gives a short speed boost | Algal and Bone Ponds | Cooked into Jar o' Jelly by the Chef for a full heal |
| **Char** | 1, 2 | **Cold Pond only** | Snow Biome exclusive |
| **Eel** | 1, 2 | **Cold Pond only** | Snow Biome exclusive |
| **Shark** | **2, 4** | Algal and Bone Ponds | The best food in the water and the grind behind the hardest badge |
| **Lava Eel** | 1, 2 | **Lava Pool only** | Volcanic Biome exclusive |
| **Lionfish** | 1, 2 | **Lava Pool only** | Volcanic Biome exclusive |
| **Old Boot** | **None** | Bone Pond | Junk. No hunger value, no use — and its status is genuinely unsettled (see below) |

The **Shark** is worth dwelling on because it is the only fish that breaks the 1-to-2 pattern: 2 forks raw and 4 cooked, which makes it the single highest-yield food in the water by a distance. It is also the only fish anyone grinds deliberately, because 30 of them is the Deep Sea Fishing badge.

Every fish you land for the first time **permanently registers on the Fish Index board** inside the fishing building. That board is the checklist for the Trophy Fishing badge, and it is the best progress tracker in the game — there is no equivalent UI for any other badge chain.

The Bone Pond Medkit is one of only four things that pond is the sole route to, and it is a heal; our [Healing, Bandages and Medkits](/guides/healing-guide) guide sets it beside the three Medkits a clinic gives you for nothing.

One thing this route does not answer: what tier 5 costs. The **Gem of the Forest** is that gate, and more sources lead to it than any single page lists. The [Gem of the Forest](/items/gem-of-the-forest) page collects them.

Three sources disagree about how long this list is. The fandom publishes the full set including the Trident, the Gem of the Forest Fragment and the Frog Key. Pocket Tactics publishes a version without those three at all. Pro Game Guides publishes one without a Trident entry alongside text elsewhere saying the Trident comes from fishing. **The fandom list is the one to plan around**, because it is the only version that matches what our own item pages independently confirm.

One consequence worth spelling out: this table makes the Bone Pond the single most valuable body of water in the game. Four of its entries are Campfire Level 6 exclusive, and one of them is the rarest weapon in the game. Everything before Level 6 is practice.

The doubling is not a fishing rule, it is the whole food economy: one fork raw becomes two cooked, and the Shark is the only catch that starts at two. The full fork scale and every food value are on the [**hunger**](/guides/hunger) page.

## Cooking: Twice the Hunger, and No Fillets

Drag the fish onto the campfire. That is the whole system, and it doubles your return.

The restriction is real and worth knowing before you plan a menu: **there is no filleting, no sushi, and no fish-specific recipe anywhere in the published material.** Two independent wikis state this outright. Fish go in whole and come off cooked.

Two edge cases sit in the sources and conflict with each other:

- **Raw Clownfish** is claimed by GladiatorBoost to slightly reduce health when eaten raw. Nothing else corroborates it.
- **Raw Shark** is claimed by one guide to actively drain hunger, while the fork tables put raw shark at 2 forks — the highest raw value of any fish. These cannot both be true, and the fork table is the better-sourced claim.

Fish also feed the [Chef](/classes/chef-class) economy rather than competing with it. The Seafood Chowder at level 1 restores about 140 hunger, and Jar o' Jelly at level 3 gives a full heal alongside roughly five minutes of speed. The Chef works best alongside Hunter, Farmer and Fisherman precisely because all three exist to hand over ingredients.

## The Four Badges and the Event Window Nobody Dates the Same

Fishing carries four badges paying **25 Diamonds** between them, plus two pieces of furniture that exist nowhere else. Badges pay once and are non-renewable — see [All Badges](/guides/all-badges) for how that works.

| Badge | Requirement | Stars | Reward |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Baiting** | Upgrade your rod to max level | **0.5** | 4 Diamonds |
| **Fishing** | Catch 100 fish | 1 | 5 Diamonds, plus the **Rainbow Fish Lounger** |
| **Trophy Fishing** | Complete the Fish Index board | 2 | 6 Diamonds |
| **Deep Sea Fishing** | Catch 30 sharks | **5** | 10 Diamonds, plus the **Fishbowl** |

Two things make this table unusual.

**Baiting is one of the two badges the fandom wiki currently calls unobtainable.** It sits at half a star alongside Reconstruction, and the wiki's own badge page says both cannot be earned right now — while the same wiki's table lists Baiting's requirement normally, and several contemporaneous guides walk through doing it. Our own badge page flagged the contradiction; nothing in the fishing material resolves it. **Check it before you grind.** If it currently awards nothing, that is 4 Diamonds of effort better spent elsewhere.

**The star scale itself has two versions.** rblxguide publishes the same four badges at 0.5, 1, 2 and 5 stars, matching the five-star maximum the game uses. Two guide sites publish them at 1, 2 and 3 stars instead. The four-star-per-badge spread matters because Deep Sea Fishing at five stars is at or near the ceiling of what the game asks for, and none of the other badge families reach it.

The event window these four launched inside has **three different end dates** in circulation: **30 August**, **29 August** and **22 August 2025**, all with a 15 August start. The introduction date itself is solid — three sources give 15 August 2025 for the Fishing Update, and rblxguide gives the same date for the Fishing Hut's addition. Only the closing date is unsettled, and the badges are understood to have stayed available afterwards.

One class starts each run already holding what this table needs you to fetch, and it is cheaper than every other food economy on the board. [**Fisherman Class in 99 Nights in the Forest**](/classes/fisherman-class) covers the cost, the three perks and the level requirements nobody agrees on.

## The Fisherman Class: 50 Diamonds for Speed

The Fisherman costs **50 Diamonds**, making it one of the cheapest classes on the board, and it starts every run holding a rod. Per the [All Classes](/classes/all-classes) table on this site, it is the food-economy class — the counterpart to Hunter's pelts and the Farmer's plots, and the reason the [Chef](/classes/chef-class) has anything to cook.

It also arrived in the same update as fishing: our own [Flamethrower](/weapons/how-to-get-flamethrower) page records that the Pyromaniac was added in the Fishing Update alongside the Fisherman, which is the cleanest dating available for the class.

There is no Fisherman page on this site yet, because the published perks do not survive cross-checking. Every perk attacks one friction point — speed — but **even one wiki contradicts itself on the order and the requirements**:

| Version | Perk 1 | Perk 2 | Perk 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| The Fisherman wiki page | Rods level up faster | Casts are instant and reach further | Fish hook on faster |
| The wiki's own Classes table | Rods level up faster | Instant and further casts | Faster fish hooking |

That much agrees. The requirements do not. The Fisherman page lists three thresholds — 350 fish and 5 sharks, then 500 and 15, then 800 and 25 — while the same wiki's Classes page attaches **350 fish** to perk 2 and **5 sharks** to perk 3. Those cannot both be right, and the practical consequence is real: you cannot plan a route to max level when nobody publishes which catch counts toward which tier.

**One source should be discarded outright.** `99nightsintheforest.net` publishes six Fisherman abilities that appear nowhere else in any language — Expert Angler at 50% faster catches, Water Sense giving 25% more water in rain, River Navigation giving 30% movement speed in water, a once-a-day Bountiful Catch, Weather Intuition predicting weather to 80% accuracy, and Aquatic Hunter adding 25% damage to aquatic enemies. No other source corroborates any of the six, and several are mechanically implausible for the class. Nothing on this page uses them.

What survives cross-checking is narrower and still useful: cheaper than every other food class, starts with the tool, and makes rod progression faster. Everything beyond that is unquantified.

## The Chat Code That Only Works Mid-Cast

`yay fishing` is a real reward, worth **2 Diamonds**, and it fails for almost everyone who tries it — because typing it into the Codes box will never work, from now on, in every circumstance.

It is a hidden in-run activation. The sequence, which our own pages document identically across four places:

1. Get the campfire to **Level 2**.
2. Get an Old Rod from the Fishing Shack.
3. **Begin the fishing mini-game.**
4. Open chat with `/`, type `yay fishing`, press Enter.

Rewards fall from the sky. See [Active Codes](/codes/active-codes) for what else is currently paying, [How to Redeem Codes](/codes/how-to-redeem-codes) for the four separate redemption paths the game actually uses, and [Codes Not Working](/codes/codes-not-working) if it failed — the chat-versus-shop confusion is the single most common reason.

One source says the code pays 2 to 3 Diamonds rather than a flat 2. Every source that publishes a number alongside the diamond economy says 2; plan on 2.

Fishing has a second product most guides bury: the Frog Key. Our [Keys and Locked Doors](/guides/keys-and-locked-doors) guide covers the eight-key route this section only touches.

fish fill the sack that has to carry them, and the cost of that space is worked out in our [sacks and inventory capacity](/guides/inventory-capacity) guide.

## What Fishing Is the Only Way to Get

Filtered for routes with no substitute, fishing is the sole source of:

- The **[Trident](/weapons/how-to-get-trident)** — not craftable, not lootable, not tradeable. Bone Pond, Campfire Level 6. Our Trident page treats it as tied with the Morningstar as the rarest weapon in the game.
- **Char, Eel, Lava Eel and Lionfish** — four fish that exist in exactly one water type each, three of them behind a biome rather than a campfire level.
- The **Rainbow Fish Lounger** and the **Fishbowl**, via their badges.
- The **Fish Index board** itself, which is the only in-game progress tracker for any badge family.
- A **renewable** [Cultist Gem](/items/cultist-gem) pipeline that does not require winning a raid.
- A **second roll** at the [Poison Armour](/items/how-to-get-poison-armour) and [Poison Spear](/weapons/how-to-get-poison-spear) after the Frog King is already dead, at the chest's own rates.
- **Frog Keys** from the water, as the reliable alternative to farming them off frog corpses.

Set against that list, the 20 Mossy Coins and the Tool Trader asking price are not the interesting cost. The interesting cost is the [Campfire](/guides/campfire) level you need before the good water exists at all — Level 6 for the Bone Pond, which is the same wall that gates the endgame beds and the last firemaking badge. Fishing does not have its own progression; it borrows the campfire's.

## Where Sources Disagree

Fifteen open contradictions, listed rather than resolved, because most of them cannot be settled from outside the game.

1. **Fish count.** Ten fish plus a boot (fandom, and Pocket Tactics' list, which also totals ten). Eight plus a boot (Miraheze's snapshot, missing both lava fish). Eight types (Pro Game Guides). **Nine** types (one guide, including **Carp** and **Trout** — two fish no other source anywhere has ever listed).
2. **The gating model itself.** The fandom gates **water types** by campfire level and lets the fish follow. Pro Game Guides gates **individual species**: Mackerel needs Level 2, Salmon Level 3, Swordfish Level 4, and Clownfish, Jellyfish, Shark, Char and Eel all need Level 6. These are structurally different games and only one can be right.
3. **Campfire level for the first rod.** Level 1 (fandom's Fresh Pond) against Level 2 (four sources) against Level 3–4 (rblxguide describing the building's later form).
4. **Does the building exist.** rblxguide says the Fishing Hut was **removed** and merged with the Dock Cabin into the Fishing Cabin. Nearly every other source still sends players to it.
5. **What the building is called.** Ten names across the sources, listed above, with two of them being genuinely separate structures.
6. **Where upgrades happen.** The Upgrade Bench inside the hut against the Tool Workbench beside the Tool Trader after 21 February 2026. Both are correct for some version of the game.
7. **The Pelt Trader's Old Rod.** Still stocked occasionally per three sources; removed per our own investigation and the current trade tables.
8. **Ripple lifespan.** Roughly six catches (fandom) against "a certain number" (Miraheze). Neither gives a unit you could time.
9. **Fisherman perk requirements.** Two incompatible sets inside one wiki — 350 fish and 5 sharks for perk 2, against a ladder of 350/500/800 fish and 5/15/25 sharks.
10. **Fisherman abilities beyond the three perks.** Six abilities published on exactly one site, none corroborated, none used here.
11. **Event window closing date.** 30 August, 29 August, or 22 August 2025, all from a solid 15 August start.
12. **Badge star scale.** Half-star to five stars (rblxguide and the wiki) against a one-to-three scale on two guide sites.
13. **Does the Old Boot count for the Fish Index.** The fandom calls it junk with no hunger value but includes it in the catch totals, while Trophy Fishing asks you to fill the index. None of them say whether the boot is a required entry.
14. **Raw fish effects.** GladiatorBoost says raw Clownfish costs health; one guide says raw Shark drains hunger while the fork tables put it highest of any raw fish.
15. **How many water types there are.** Five (one guide), six (fandom), or seven counting the Alien Ponds that appear on no fishing table but do appear on our own Mothership page.

## FAQ

**Do I need bait?**
No bait system appears anywhere in the published material. The rod, a body of water and patience are the whole requirement.

**Can I fish at night?**
Yes, and several sources recommend it as the safer window — but this cuts both ways. Night is when the Deer is active, and you cannot move during the mini-game. Two guides say to use the safer night cycles; one says to avoid them. Pick based on whether your camp is under pressure.

**Why is my fishing rod glowing?**
Its XP bar is full. Take it to the Tool Workbench beside the Tool Trader, equip it and upgrade. The upgrade itself is free.

**Can I buy a Good Rod or a Strong Rod?**
No source anywhere describes a direct purchase, drop or recipe for either. The Old Rod is the only entry point.

**Where is the best fishing spot?**
Bone Pond at Campfire Level 6, by a distance. It holds the Trident, the Medkit, the Old Car Engine, the Gem of the Forest Fragment and the highest density of Sharks. Before Level 6, an Algal Pond with a Strong Rod is the best known pipeline for anything specific, per the Cultist Gem material.

**Is fishing worth it if I do not care about badges?**
Yes, and for two reasons that have nothing to do with achievements: it is the only renewable source of ammo and medical supplies that does not spend resources to get them, and four taming recipes need specific fish. See [Best Pet](/taming/best-pet) for whether the tames are worth the trouble.

**Does the rain change anything?**
One guide claims rain boosts fish spawns by 20%. It is the only source that says so and no mechanism is offered. The [Campfire](/guides/campfire) page documents rain and thunderstorms as real weather events, so the premise is plausible and the number is not.

**Is the Old Boot a mistake?**
Probably deliberate, but nobody has said what it is for. It has no hunger value, it does not appear in any recipe, and it comes out of the deepest water in the game.

**Why did the instructions I read not work?**
Most likely the building. If a guide told you to find a standalone Fishing Hut and upgrade at its Upgrade Bench, that guide predates the Modifier Flames update of 21 February 2026. Go to the Tool Trader beside the campfire instead, or take the free rods from inside the Fishing Cabin.
