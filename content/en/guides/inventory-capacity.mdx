There are five sacks in 99 Nights in the Forest, they hold five different amounts, and almost every guide on the game lists them as a table of numbers with no opinion attached. That is fine while you are looting. It stops being fine the moment you have to choose one.

This page does the part nobody does: it prices each sack by what you actually give up to get it, counts how much room each upgrade really buys, and answers the one question the slot numbers get wrong — **which sack should you be carrying on night thirty.**

Last verified: **2026-10-05**.

The Infernal Sack is the one storage upgrade that cooks your raw Steak for you, and our [Steak](/items/steak) page lists it among the four things a raw Steak can still be worth.

## Quick Answer

The section we would read first if you only read one.

- **The Good Sack is the whole early game, and it costs one Bunny Foot.** Five slots to fifteen is a **3.0× jump**, and the cheapest of the four upgrade routes in the game delivers it. Every later sack is a smaller number on a bigger base.
- **The Giant Sack is not the best sack, and the numbers are the reason nobody notices.** Twenty-five slots against the Infernal Sack's twenty reads as a win. It is not: the Infernal Sack cooks raw food for free, and the [Fandom wiki's own tips section](/items/how-to-get-wood-fast) says so in as many words — if you do not already hold an [Infernal Sword](/weapons/how-to-get-infernal-sword) or [Infernal Crossbow](/weapons/how-to-get-infernal-crossbow), keep the Infernal Sack.
- **The Giant Sack has a prerequisite that is not printed on the reward table.** At the Alpha Wolf Pelt rung it is offered *only if you already own an Old Flashlight*. No other source surfaces that. It sits on our [Pelt Trader](/items/pelt-trader) page; it is repeated here because it changes the price of the sack.
- **The Infernal Sack is the only sack you cannot buy or trade for.** It lives in the [Volcanic Biome](/locations/volcanic-biome), in exactly two Red Spiked Chests, and hands you an Infernal Sack around **80% of the time**.
- **The class bonus is worth forty percent on day 1 and eight percent on day 30.** Scavenger's +2 and Explorer's +2 apply to every sack you own, so the same perk shrinks as the sack grows.
- **Above 60% Corruption, the sack stops being a pantry.** Stored food rots in about ninety seconds *even in your sack and hotbar* in [Hard Mode](/guides/hard-mode). Capacity stops mattering at exactly the point you would think it matters most.

## The Five Sacks, Ranked by What They Actually Cost

| Sack | Slots | With Scavenger | What it costs you | Slots per pelt |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Old Sack](/items/how-to-get-wood-fast) | **5** | 7 | Nothing — you spawn holding it | — |
| [Good Sack](#the-good-sack-is-the-whole-early-game) | **15** | 17 | **1 Bunny Foot**, at the first trade | **15.0** |
| [Infernal Sack](#infernal-versus-giant-the-only-capacity-decision-that-matters) | **20** | 22 | A [Volcanic Biome](/locations/volcanic-biome) run — no pelts at all | Not for sale |
| [Giant Sack](#the-giant-sack-has-a-prerequisite-nobody-announces) | **25** | 27 | **1 Alpha Wolf Pelt** at the third rung, *and* you must already be holding an Old Flashlight | **12.5** |
| Admin Sack | **99,999** | 100,001 | An [Update Party](/guides/weather-and-night-events) | Not for sale |

Read the two rates in that table again, because they are the whole argument. **The Good Sack buys fifteen slots for one pelt. The Giant Sack buys twelve and a half.** The bigger sack costs you sixteen percent more pelts per slot, and the reason is not the pelt — it is the rung it sits on.

The Admin Sack is the one entry that is not a decision. It is [event-only](/guides/weather-and-night-events), its capacity figure appears in three sources and nowhere is it presented as obtainable in a normal run, and two of our own guides still call the Giant Sack the largest sack a player can carry. We record the disagreement rather than pick one.

The single [Bunny](/entities/the-bunny) foot that buys this sack comes off the easiest animal on the map, and our Bunny page follows it through every one of its sinks.

## The Good Sack Is the Whole Early Game

Five to fifteen is not an upgrade. It is the difference between making three trips and making one, and it arrives before the first night is over.

The Fandom wiki puts the three percentages next to the sack names, and they are the clearest statement of this in the game: the Good Sack is a **300% upgrade**, the Infernal Sack a **133% upgrade**, the Giant Sack a **167% upgrade**. Those are measured off the Good Sack, which is exactly the point — everything after the first sack is a game of diminishing returns, and the one upgrade that beats 3× costs you a single bunny.

The practical version, and our own [wood page](/items/how-to-get-wood-fast) says it in one sentence: chopping speed is rarely the limit, **carrying capacity is** — a slow axe with a big sack beats a fast axe with a small one. It is the same reason the [speedrun](/guides/speedrun-guide) plan is a wood problem long before it is a combat problem.

The route to the Good Sack is the cheapest of the four, and there are two of them:

- **Trade 1 at the [Pelt Trader](/items/pelt-trader).** He asks for a **Bunny Foot** and one of the three rewards is a Good Sack. Bunnies are the easiest animal on the map to farm, which is why the first trade is never the bottleneck.
- **Common, Good and Iron chests.** The Good Sack appears in all three tiers of the [chest ladder](/items/chest-tiers), so even a run that never sees the trader can double its capacity on day one.

The Fandom wiki's own scheduling advice is worth copying: **if you have not looted one by the time the first night is almost over, go back to base and start killing bunnies**, because the trader may not be back for three to four days.

## What a Slot Costs: Pricing the Sack Ladder

Capacity is usually quoted as a number. It is better quoted as a ratio between what you have and what you want, because the step you are standing on changes what the next step is worth.

| You are holding | Upgrade to | Extra slots | Multiplier | What you are really buying |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Old (5) | Good (15) | **+10** | **3.0×** | Two thirds of your walking, gone |
| Good (15) | Giant (25) | **+10** | 1.67× | The same +10 as before, for two pelts and a prerequisite |
| Good (15) | Infernal (20) | +5 | 1.33× | The smallest raw gain, plus a cooking rig |
| Infernal (20) | Giant (25) | +5 | **1.25×** | The smallest step on the whole ladder, and it costs you cooked food |
| Old (5) | Any sack, as Scavenger | +2 | **1.4×** | Free, permanent, and it shrinks as you upgrade |

That last row is the sleeper. The Scavenger's +2 and the Explorer's +2 are **flat additions to whatever sack you are holding**, not percentage bonuses — so the identical perk is 40% of an Old Sack and 8% of a Giant Sack. A player who buys the class on day 1 and never upgrades their sack is carrying the best inventory in the game by a wide margin. A player who buys it on day 30 and already has a Giant Sack paid roughly one extra slot's worth.

## The Giant Sack Has a Prerequisite Nobody Announces

The Giant Sack is the top of the [Pelt Trader](/items/pelt-trader) chain, which makes it the most written-about sack and the least understood one.

The chain runs Bunny Foot → Wolf Pelt → Alpha Wolf Pelt → Bear Pelt, and the Giant Sack sits on the **third** rung, behind the Alpha Wolf Pelt. Two things about that rung are not on the reward table:

- **You must already own an Old Flashlight.** Hand over an Alpha Wolf Pelt and the Giant Sack option appears only if you are carrying one; the Fandom wiki's exact framing is that without the flashlight "you won't get the option for the Giant Sack." Everything else on that rung — the Rifle, the Strong Flashlight, a spare Old Flashlight — is still offered. The sack is the only reward that disappears.
- **You are spending the chain, not the pelt.** Reaching rung three means you already spent a Bunny Foot and a Wolf Pelt, and on the way you turned down a Good Axe, a Spear, a Revolver and a Leather Body. Call the Giant Sack **two pelts deep and conditional**, and it reads as a much more expensive object.

Counting it honestly, the Giant Sack costs **2 pelts for 25 slots: 12.5 slots per pelt** — against the Good Sack's **15**. It is the better sack in a straight line and the worse one on a price-per-slot basis, and the only reason to buy it is that the Infernal Sack is not on the table.

There is also a failure mode worth knowing before you spend the Alpha Wolf Pelt at all: our [Pelt Trader](/items/pelt-trader) page records a bug reported identically by both wikis — **after an Alpha Wolf Pelt, he may never ask for the Bear Pelt**, which closes the chain before the Iron Body rung. If that happens, the Giant Sack is probably gone for that visit and you fall back on a Legendary or Gold chest.

## Infernal versus Giant: the Only Capacity Decision That Matters

Twenty slots with a cooking function, or twenty-five slots with none. This is the only genuine capacity choice in the game, and the slot count points the wrong way.

The Infernal Sack exists only in the [Volcanic Biome](/locations/volcanic-biome), in the biome's two Red Spiked Chests, and it has a second job that no other sack has: **raw food placed inside it cooks automatically.** A Morsel becomes a cooked Morsel, a Steak becomes a Steak, without a campfire and without a stop. The Fandom wiki singles this out as the reason to keep it, and the sentence is worth having in full — **if you do not have an Infernal Sword or Crossbow, it is better to keep the Infernal Sack for this purpose than to upgrade to a Giant Sack and lose your access to cooked food.**

That flips the decision in the situation it actually comes up in. Runs do not carry an Infernal Sack and then wonder whether to swap up; they hold one from a volcanic chest and then ask what to replace it with.

| | Infernal Sack | Giant Sack |
|---|---|---|
| Slots | 20 (22 as Scavenger) | **25** (27 as Scavenger) |
| Cooks raw food | **Yes, automatically** | No |
| Where it comes from | Two Red Spiked Chests (~80%) or a [Cultist King](/entities/the-cultist-king) roll | Legendary and Gold chests, Pelt Trader rung 3 (with an Old Flashlight) |
| Costs you | A volcanic run, in a biome that is not guaranteed | Two pelts and an Old Flashlight held in advance |
| Keeps working in Hard Mode | **No** — stored food rots at 60% Corruption either way | No |

**Take the Giant Sack if** you already killed an Infernal Sword or Crossbow, you have a campfire and a [Chef](/classes/chef-class) or spare fuel, and five slots is a real difference on a logging run.

**Keep the Infernal Sack if** you do not. Five slots is a sixth of the gain the Good Sack gave you, and cooking is the difference between eating raw meat for damage and not — our own [hunger guide](/guides/hunger) puts it plainly: cooked meat is double the forks *and* costs no damage, so eating raw is never a trade in your favour. Giving that up to carry five extra items is a bad trade almost every time.

One more volcanic detail that belongs to this section rather than the biome page: the Red Spiked Chest on the **damaged church roof** sits over lava, and [the biome page](/locations/volcanic-biome) records that nearly everything dropped into it is destroyed for good. The wiki's advice here is a capacity rule: **do not open the roof chest with a full sack and nowhere to move.** That is a planning problem, not a looting one.

## Where Every Sack Comes From

| Sack | Chests | Pelt Trader | Other |
|---|---|---|---|
| Old Sack | — | — | You spawn with it |
| Good Sack | [Common](/items/chest-tiers), Good, Iron | **Rung 1**, for a Bunny Foot | — |
| Giant Sack | Legendary, Gold | **Rung 3**, for an Alpha Wolf Pelt (needs an Old Flashlight) | — |
| Infernal Sack | [Red Spiked](/locations/volcanic-biome) Infernal Chests — **2 per run** | No | A chance drop from the [Cultist King](/entities/the-cultist-king) |
| Admin Sack | No | No | Update Party events only |

The chest tiers are the route that does not depend on the trader at all, and the reason the Infernal Sack is the hardest to replace is that **no chest tier gives you one reliably at the tier you can reach.** It is 80% out of two chests in a biome that one source gives roughly a 50% chance of appearing in a run at all, with the [Snow Biome](/locations/snow-biome) taking the slot instead.

That last fact is the quiet reason the Infernal Sack decision is real. If the volcano does not spawn, you are holding whatever the [Legendary and Gold chests](/items/chest-tiers) gave you — and on that path the Giant Sack is the better answer even without the cooking.

## Capacity Beats Axe Speed — the Same Bottleneck, Stated Once

The argument belongs to the [wood](/items/how-to-get-wood-fast) page, so it gets one paragraph here and a link there.

Every published guide ranks sacks by slots and axes by speed, and treats the two as separate upgrade lines. They are the same line. If a logging run is thirty items of wood to fill the campfire, the trip count is just the loot divided by capacity:

| Sack you are holding | Trips to move 30 items |
|---|---|
| Old (5) | **6.0** |
| Old with Scavenger (7) | 4.3 |
| Good (15) | **2.0** |
| Good with Scavenger (17) | 1.8 |
| Infernal (20) | 1.5 |
| Giant (25) | 1.2 |

Those are ratios, not distances — we are not going to invent a map distance no source publishes. But the shape is the point: **the Good Sack removes four of those six trips, and the entire ladder from Good to Giant removes less than one.** Spending a Wolf Pelt on a Good Axe rather than a Good Sack is choosing 5 hits per tree at the cost of two extra walks per run, and on a map where [The Deer](/entities/the-deer) hunts after dark, a walk is not a neutral act.

The same shape: which step gives the most per unit. Our [class levels guide](/guides/class-levels) divides Level 3 by Level 2 on all twenty class ladders and finds the mode is 2.0x.

## The Class Bonus Peaks on Day 1, Not Day 30

| Class | Cost | What it does to capacity |
|---|---|---|
| [Scavenger](/classes/all-classes) | 25 Diamonds | **+2 slots on every sack you own**, from the Old Sack up |
| [Explorer](/classes/all-classes) | 80 Diamonds | +2 slots, plus faster fog-clearing and chests visible through walls |
| [Alien Scientist](/classes/all-classes) | 600 | Listed as "extra sack space"; see the disagreement below |

The Scavenger is the cheapest class in the game at **25 Diamonds** and its level 1 perk is the one that never gets quoted in the class reviews. What the [Alien](/classes/alien-class) page notices by contrast is a different mechanism worth stating separately: night vision lets you **stop carrying a Flashlight**, which frees a slot without giving you a bigger bag.

Because the +2 is flat, the perk's value is a function of when you buy it:

| Sack | With +2 | Gain |
|---|---|---|
| Old (5) | 7 | **+40%** |
| Good (15) | 17 | +13% |
| Infernal (20) | 22 | +10% |
| Giant (25) | 27 | **+8%** |

Buy the Scavenger on day 1 and you are carrying a 7-slot Old Sack into the first night, which is the strongest early inventory in the game. Buy it on day 30 with a Giant Sack and you paid 25 Diamonds for two slots — cheap in absolute terms, and a rounding error in relative ones.

No tutorial tells you what completing a run looks like, so it is worth saying plainly: surviving to the end of Day 99 gets you a cutscene, but the true ending also needs all four children home. Every way a run can end is on [how to win 99 Nights in the Forest](/guides/how-to-win-99-nights).

That is more of a [Pumpkin](/items/pumpkin) question than a fuel one, and the crop page carries the yield nobody publishes plus the break-even point between Pumpkin and Cake.

A [Carrot](/items/carrot) is one fork in one slot, which is why wild food beats a cooked dish when the sack is nearly full.

A [Chili](/items/chili) is a slot that buys an escape rather than health, and the slot it competes with changes completely once the sack is nearly full.

## Two Rules for What Goes In First

The sack is not a container in the ordinary sense, and the one mechanical quirk every source mentions is stated by exactly one wiki and then never picked up again.

**It empties from the top.** Items come out last-in, first-out — the Fandom wiki's words are that items are taken out "from the top," so the last thing you put in is the first thing you get back. This seems like trivia until the run where you needed the thing at the bottom of your bag and had to dump everything to reach it. The wiki's own counter-advice is the rule: **if there is something you want to keep, put it in first, and try to remember never to fully empty it.**

**Filling is not the same as stopping.** Two behaviours show up in the same source and both matter at the moment you are most likely to be short on room:

- **Holding the interact key or the unstore button drops the entire sack contents in at least two seconds.** That is a deliberate eviction with a two-second cost, not an instant press.
- **You can pick up items while a chest is open**, which means a full sack is a soft limit rather than a hard one — but only for as long as the chest is open.

Our [hunger guide](/guides/hunger) lands the same point from the food side: carry four forks rather than ten ones, because a single Pumpkin or Stew replaces four carrots, and **inventory space is the real constraint on every long run.** The wildest version of that rule is the [Chef's](/classes/chef-class) — Stews equip to your toolbar and ride along without eating a sack slot at all.

## When the Sack Stops Being a Pantry: Hard Mode

Every table on this page assumes stored food stays stored. [Hard Mode](/guides/hard-mode) withdraws that assumption on a schedule.

| Corruption | What happens to anything you are carrying |
|---|---|
| 25% | Nothing yet — this is the slow tier |
| 60% | **Most stored food rots in about ninety seconds, even in your sack or hotbar** |
| 80% and above | The [Corruption](/guides/hard-mode) ladder reaches the tiers that change what food does at all |

The 60% line is the one to plan around, and it is not in any capacity guide because it is a survival-guide fact that gets written in a different document. Its capacity consequence is blunt: **above 60% Corruption your sack is a ship's pocket, not a larder.** You can carry ten Morsels to the volcano and find four of them rotted on the way back. Carrying food to a long trip stops being a capacity question and becomes a schedule question — and the [Infernal Sack](/locations/volcanic-biome)'s cooking stops being a convenience, because it converts raw meat on the spot rather than storing it.

The [Chef](/classes/chef-class) page states the same rule from the other end: at the 60% threshold most stored food rots even in the sack, which is also why stockpiling and cooking lines stop paying off at the same moment.

Pet slots run on their own ladder, and [Kiwi](/entities/the-kiwi) is a pet that holds a slot for the whole run without ever paying a slot back.

## Capacity Is Not One Number: Pet Slots Run on a Separate Ladder

Before the sources section, one correction that saves a lot of confusion — this game has **two capacity systems and they are priced nothing alike.**

| System | Default | Cheapest route | Most expensive route | Cost per slot |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sack slots** (items, food, ammo) | 5 | **1 Bunny Foot** at the trader | Red Spiked Chests | Effectively free |
| **Pet slots** (tamed animals) | 2 | **3** by holding a [Strong Taming Flute](/items/taming-flute) — two full XP bars, no Diamonds | [Beastmaster](/classes/beastmaster-class) at **400 Diamonds for 5 slots** | 80 Diamonds, plus the animals |

A pet slot costs either two grind bars or eighty Diamonds. A sack slot costs one bunny or nothing. The [Taming Flute](/items/taming-flute) page is the authority on the right-hand column and already does this arithmetic; it is worth reading only to confirm that capacity in this game is not a single economy with one price.

The one place the two touch is the [Toolfinding badge](/guides/all-badges), which is really an inventory check: **hold a Giant Sack, a Strong Axe or Chainsaw, a Strong Flashlight, and high-level armour.** It pays 6 Diamonds and it can only fire once you are carrying the biggest sack in the game. If you are chasing it, the Giant Sack is not an optimisation — it is the gate.

## Where Sources Disagree

**Who gets +2 — Scavenger only, or Scavenger and Explorer?** The Fandom wiki's Sacks page and rblxguide both say *Scavenger and Explorer*, and the Sacks page's capacity lines are written as "7 items" for both classes. The Fandom Items table and the BloxGuides database name only the Scavenger. Our own class table lists Explorer with "sack space" and no number at all. The safe reading is that both classes carry the bonus and the item tables simply omit one.

**Is the Giant Sack the biggest sack a player can carry?** Two of the guides we checked call it the maximum and stop there. The Fandom wiki lists a fifth entry — the Admin Sack at 99,999, from Update Party events — and a fourth site lists it as well. Whether "biggest" is meant within a run or across the game is the disagreement.

**What an Infernal Chest contains.** rblxguide still lists the Infernal Chest table as coming soon. Other write-ups say an Infernal Sack, or an Infernal Sack *or* an Infernal Sword. Our own [Volcanic Biome](/locations/volcanic-biome) page is the most specific figure published anywhere: ~80% Infernal Sack, ~10% Infernal Sword, ~10% Infernal Crossbow, across exactly two chests. Three partial answers and one complete one, and only one of them is a percentage.

**Whether the Infernal Sack can drop from the Cultist King.** Both Fandom pages list it, alongside the Infernal Chests. Our own [Cultist King](/entities/the-cultist-king) page records the Infernal Sack at 20 slots with its cooking property without naming the drop. We treat the King as a secondary route rather than a source.

**Whether a stack counts as one slot.** Nobody we found states it. If twenty logs are one slot or twenty is not determines whether every number on this page means anything, and every capacity table in the game leaves it unsaid. We are flagging the gap rather than guessing at it.

**The Alien Scientist's capacity perk.** Our class table lists "extra sack space" with no figure attached, while the [Alien](/classes/alien-class) page describes the same benefit in a completely different way — night vision that lets you stop carrying a Flashlight, which frees a slot. Those are two different mechanics (a bigger bag versus not having to haul an item) and only the second is corroborated by any external source. Treat the +4 figure that circulates in unpublished write-ups as unevidenced; we could not source it.

**One source we could not use.** The Fandom Sacks page carries a storage list that renders as Old Sack = 0 items, Good Sack = 1, Infernal = 2, Giant = 3, Admin = 4. It contradicts the same page's capacity table in every row, and the numbers are almost certainly an ordered list of names rather than capacities. We used the capacity table and set the other aside.

## FAQ

**What is the best sack in 99 Nights in the Forest?**
By slots, the Giant Sack at 25. By usefulness, the Infernal Sack, because it carries twenty slots and cooks raw food for you at the same time — and the wiki's own advice is to keep it unless you already hold an Infernal Sword or Crossbow.

**Is the Infernal Sack better than the Giant Sack?**
For a food-heavy run, yes. It is five slots smaller and it converts raw meat into cooked meat with no campfire, which matters far more than five slots once you are living off wolf kills at night.

**How do I get a Giant Sack?**
Either a Legendary or Gold chest, or the third trade at the [Pelt Trader](/items/pelt-trader), which asks for an Alpha Wolf Pelt. That third rung only offers the sack if you are already carrying an Old Flashlight.

**Is the Old Flashlight really required for the Giant Sack?**
Yes, per the Fandom wiki, and our own [Pelt Trader](/items/pelt-trader) page reaches the same conditional from the other direction — the Giant Sack and Good Sack options both require you to already own one. If you are unsure, grab the Old Flashlight early; it is an option at the first rung anyway.

**What is the cheapest way to increase my capacity?**
The Good Sack, at one Bunny Foot, or free out of any Common chest. It is a 3× jump and nothing else on the ladder comes close in ratio.

**Does the Scavenger's +2 apply to every sack?**
Yes. It is a flat +2 to whatever sack you are holding, which is why it is worth far more on day 1 than on day 30.

**Does the sack capacity apply to stacks?**
No source states it, so we do not know. Treat the published slot numbers as slots, not items.

**Does the Infernal Sack still cook in Hard Mode?**
It cooks, but it no longer stores — at 60% Corruption most food in your sack rots within about ninety seconds either way. See [Hard Mode](/guides/hard-mode).

**Can I carry more than the sack shows?**
You can keep picking items up while a chest is open, and the sack empties from the top rather than blocking the bottom. Beyond that, the limit is the slot count.
