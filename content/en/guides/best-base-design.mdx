A good base in 99 Nights in the Forest is not a fortress. It is a **funnel**.

The mistake almost every player makes is building walls to keep enemies out. Walls do not do that here — cultists climb, ranged ones shoot over obstacles, and their health climbs every day until whatever you built stops mattering. What a base actually does is decide **where** the fight happens. Get that right and even a cheap base outperforms an expensive one.

Last verified: **2026-09-29**.

## Your Base Has Exactly Two Jobs

**Keep the campfire lit.** Large entities stay outside the campfire's radius while it burns. The fire is not a decoration — it is the only thing standing between you and the thing that walks through walls. If it goes out at night, you have a much bigger problem than any structure can solve. Wood supply is its own topic: see [How to Get Wood Fast](/items/how-to-get-wood-fast).

**Control where cultists walk.** Cultists raid every few nights, they do not steal your items or the rescued kids, and their real threat is burning through your campfire faster while they are alive. You do not need to stop them entering. You need them to arrive somewhere you chose.

Everything below follows from those two jobs.

## Build the Perimeter Out of Log Walls, Not Shelves

This is the most common wrong turn, and it costs you the game's scarcest resource.

**Log Walls** cost wood — around 12 pieces each, according to the sources that list a number. Wood is renewable: the [sapling loop](/items/how-to-get-wood-fast) gives you more every single day, and a Strong Axe drops a full-grown tree in one hit.

**Shelves** cost bolts, and bolts come from **Scrap** — which is scarce, scattered, and needed for crafting upgrades. Using shelves as your outer wall means spending a limited resource on something enemies will eventually break.

And shelves are worse at the job anyway: cultists climb them, and ranged enemies hit you behind them. Their real value is as a **second layer and a shooting platform**, which is what the layout below uses them for.

## The Layout: Ring, Ring, Ring, Corridor

The setup that recurs across every credible guide, from the inside out:

1. **Log Wall ring** tight around the campfire and crafting area
2. **Shelf ring** just inside it — placed against each other with a gap in the middle
3. **Second Log Wall ring** outside the shelves
4. **A ring of planted trees** as the outermost layer

Then leave **one** gap. That gap is your entrance, and you design it rather than leaving it open.

**Inside the corridor, alternate [bear trap](/items/bear-trap) and shelf.** Bear trap, then a shelf, then another bear trap, all the way down the passage. Cultists funnel in single file, get caught, and die in a space where they cannot surround you.

Two reasons this beats "more walls":

**It turns numbers into singles.** Twelve cultists in the open are twelve health bars. Twelve cultists in a one-wide corridor are one at a time.

**It gives you high ground for free.** Cultists can climb shelves, but if one of you stands inside the shelf ring's gap, they have to climb up and drop in to reach you. That is a free window to shoot the crossbow cultists first, then the ones climbing.

## Traps Are Consumables. Budget Accordingly

Spikes, barbed wire and similar defences **break** — and they break on a schedule you cannot control, because cultist health scales upward with the day count while your traps do not.

So do not build a perimeter out of them. Build your perimeter out of wood, and use traps as **ammo inside the corridor**: placed two deep at each entrance, behind the walls, where they slow and damage whoever has already made it through.

Bear traps have one more quirk worth knowing: **they damage you too** — the self-damage range and the badge that comes with it are on our [Bear Trap](/items/bear-trap) page. If you step on your own trap mid-fight, you take the hit. Place them where your own feet do not go, and remember where they are when you are retreating.

The loop that makes a base run itself is campfire fuel, not defence. [Campfire](/guides/campfire) covers what each of the six levels costs, and why logs stop being fuel after level 4.

## The Base That Runs Itself

This is the part almost nobody builds, and it is the difference between babysitting a fire and actually going out to explore.

**Point a Biofuel Processor at the campfire and feed it crops.** With enough farm plots nearby, the processor converts produce into fuel and feeds the fire on its own — no more hauling wood back every night. Check that no wall blocks its output.

**Put a cooking pot on top of the crop plots.** Placing a pot where the crops spawn lets ingredients go in with almost no manual work; pull each finished stew out and the pot starts again.

Together these two turn your farm plots into a **fuel and food pipeline**. The farm plots stop being a chore and start being infrastructure.

If you are running this loop, your campfire stops being the thing you panic about and becomes something that is simply on. That matters more than any wall on this page — a lit fire does more for your safety than a perfect perimeter with a dead fire inside it.

## Picking the Spot

Before any of the above, four things to look for in a location:

**Flat ground.** Walls on a slope leave gaps.

**Close to trees and rocks** — you will be making this trip constantly.

**Near a cave.** Stone and Scrap come from caves, and a short run means you actually make it.

**Two visible approaches.** You want to see things coming. A base tucked into a corner with one sightline gets surprised.

## What a Base Cannot Do

Worth being honest about, because it changes how much you invest:

**No design keeps you safe while you are away.** Long absences let enemies accumulate, and enough of them will pile on top of each other to get over things. A base buys you time; it does not buy you absence.

**Cultists do not steal.** They will not take your items or the kids. What they do is accelerate the campfire's burn while they are alive — which is why leaving them standing around is the real threat, and why the corridor matters more than the wall.

**The campfire is the actual wall.** Large entities stay out while it burns. Spend your resources keeping it fed before spending them on a bigger fence.

## Common Mistakes

**Building shelf walls first.** Costs Scrap, breaks anyway, and blocks your own sightlines.

**Making two entrances.** One corridor you can defend beats two you cannot. If you need an emergency exit, make it a tight one you can wall up.

**Ignoring the fire for the fence.** A level 3–4 campfire on day one and level 6 by around night 10 does more for survival than any structure. Keep a reserve of at least 50 wood.

**Trapping your own path.** Bear traps and spike fields in your own walking route will get you killed faster than the cultists will.

**Leaving the farm plots outside the walls.** If the processor and plots sit outside your perimeter, your fuel supply is the first thing a raid walks through.

## Where Sources Disagree

**Shelves as walls.** One guide recommends circling shelves as your main protection; another says they cost Scrap, defend poorly against ranged enemies and should never be the perimeter. We have taken the second reading, for two reasons: it comes with a mechanism (climbing and ranged fire), and both guides still end up using shelves as a **second layer and a platform**. If your Scrap is flowing and wood is not, the first reading is defensible.

**Spikes and barbed wire.** One guide leans on them heavily as a primary defence; another points out they break as cultist health scales with the day count. The mechanism favours the second — traps that expire are ammunition, not infrastructure.

**The Biofuel Processor loop** comes from a single guide. Nothing contradicts it, and it fits the game's crafting economy, but treat it as worth testing rather than established. The rest of this page is multi-source.

## FAQ

**What is the best base design in 99 Nights in the Forest?**
A Log Wall ring, a shelf ring inside it, a second Log Wall ring outside, planted trees on the outside, and one narrow entrance lined with alternating bear traps and shelves. See the full layout above.

**Should I use shelves as walls?**
As an inner layer and a shooting platform, yes. As your outer wall, no — they cost Scrap instead of wood, and cultists climb them.

**Do cultists steal my items or the kids?**
No. Their threat is burning campfire fuel faster while they are alive.

**Do traps last?**
No. They break over time as enemy health rises. Treat them as consumables placed inside your corridor.

**How do I keep the fire lit without hauling wood all night?**
A Biofuel Processor pointed at the campfire and fed by nearby farm plots. Reported to work as a full auto-loop — see "The Base That Runs Itself" above.
