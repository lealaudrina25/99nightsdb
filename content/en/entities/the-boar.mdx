Most of the Forest teaches you one habit: reach your fire before the Deer does. The Jungle Biome breaks that habit, because its common animal threat does not wait for you to reach anything — it charges. [The Boar](/locations/jungle-biome) is the reason our Jungle page names Boars first among the biome's hazards, and it is the only animal in the game that both **takes damage** and **hits hard enough to matter** in the same package.

This page is about The Boar as an entity: how its attack loop actually runs, why the way it chases is not the way it behaves in the Forest, what the published numbers do and do not include, and — the part almost nothing covers — **where its tusks go**. The biome rules themselves (tar, the four temples, the Skull Trial Stone, the Fighting Pit wave table) are on [Jungle Biome](/locations/jungle-biome) and are not repeated here.

## Where It Lives, and What It Takes to Get There

Boars spawn **exclusively in the Jungle**, in large groups, which is what makes them the biome's pacing problem rather than its boss. One source places the spawn zone **northwest of your camp** and adds the practical gate that fits with everything else we know: the **campfire has to be upgraded to Level 3–4** before the Jungle is a place you can safely work in. That number is a single source, but it agrees with the broader shape of the jungle route our own [Jungle Biome](/locations/jungle-biome) page describes, so we carry it as guidance rather than fact.

Community pages additionally list **Jungle Temple** and the **Mother Temple** as places where Boars turn up, and an entity list we checked puts them at "Jungle Biome, Temple". None of these are hard spawn points — the fandom wiki's own line is blunt about it: the Boar does not spawn in any specifically named location.

Two environment facts from our Jungle page are worth carrying here because they change how you fight Boars rather than just where you meet them:

- **Tar is a Boar enabler.** Tar slows you, and a slowed player standing in a tar pool is exactly what Boars are looking for. Getting out of the pool recovers most of your speed; standing in it does not.
- **Sightlines cut both ways.** Jungle plants and big rocks block vision harder than the Forest does, which hides you from Boars and hides Boars from you. That is a reason to move first and shoot second.

## The Attack Loop: Chase, Hit, Dart Away

The behaviour is consistent across the sources that describe it, and unlike several entity pages here it is not a mystery loop — it is a short one.

**1. Wander.** A Boar moves aimlessly until a player enters its detection radius or deals damage to it. There is no aggro timer, no wind-up telegraph on the chase itself.

**2. Chase.** Once it has seen you, it chases until either you die or you get **outside its detection radius**. That second clause is the escape condition, and it is the same rule the Wolf and the Mammoth run on: distance, not time, ends the encounter.

**3. Hit, then dart.** This is the part that surprises people who expect a wolf-style bite loop. When a Boar gets close it **attacks, then darts away, then resumes the chase**. So the fight is not a steady melee trade — it is a series of short commitments with gaps in them, and those gaps are when you are meant to be dealing damage back or repositioning.

**4. Come home.** Like [The Mammoth](/entities/the-mammoth), a Boar that has drifted **too far from its original spawn point and dropped back to passive** will **teleport back to that spawn point with a puff of smoke**. If you are farming a spot and a Boar disappears in smoke, this is why — it is not despawning, and it is not dead.

## The Pet Rule That Changes Everything

One interaction deserves its own section because it is the most misplayed part of a Boar fight.

**If a pet hits a Boar first, the Boar switches to the pet** and stops targeting you. It stays on the pet until **you** attack it again, at which point it flips back to you.

That single line explains a lot of the advice you will read, and it lets us state plainly what does and does not follow from it:

- **Use the pet as a fuse, not a tank.** Let the wolf or cat start the contact, take the first swings, then walk in yourself and take the Boar back. You trade a little damage for a much better angle.
- **Do not let a pet open a fight you cannot finish.** Once the Boar is on the pet, your damage still works — but if you walk away, the Boar keeps chewing the pet, not you.
- **What we could not confirm:** whether a *tamed* Boar follows the same pet-aggro rule when it is on your side. Nothing we found states it, and the reverse case (your pet fighting your own tamed Boar) is not documented either, so do not build a composition around it.

There is a second consequence that catches people camp-side: **Boars can enter the campfire area if you attack them.** Pulling one toward your base does not get you a safe meat farm — it gets a Boar in your camp.

## Numbers: 125 Health, and a Damage Nobody Publishes

| Stat | Figure | How it is sourced |
|---|---|---|
| **Health** | **125** | fandom lists it as `125?`; rblxguide prints **125** |
| **Damage** | **Not published (TBA)** | fandom and rblxguide both leave it as TBA |
| **Walk speed** | Implied **fast** | one source calls it fast and says you cannot outrun it once aggroed; both other sources list it TBA |
| **Charge damage** | **"Very high"** | one source, qualitative only |
| **Added** | **28 February 2026** | one source says Jungle Biome Part 1 on 28 Feb; fandom and rblxguide both date the same update **1 March 2026** |

**On health, and why we still print 125.** The fandom wiki writes `125?` — with the question mark in the article itself. rblxguide prints the same figure without one. That looks like two sources and is closer to **one source and one restatement of it**, which is not enough for a hard number in a game where health decides whether a fight is survivable. We print 125 because it is the only figure anyone publishes and because it is at least consistent with the one kill-count claim we have (below), but treat it as **unconfirmed** rather than known.

**On damage, the honest answer is that no source we found publishes it.** Two of the three list it TBA, and the third only says the charge is "very high". So this page will not tell you a Boar does 20 or 30 or 40 damage. What we can give you instead is the community estimate, flagged as one estimate: a single source states that **an unarmoured player can be killed in roughly four hits**, and separately that about **four direct Revolver shots kill one Boar**.

## Where Those Two Numbers Fit (Our Arithmetic)

Our own [Revolver](/weapons/how-to-get-revolver) page records a published damage figure of roughly **25–37** for that gun, and notes the figure comes from a single source. Put it next to the Boar numbers above and the arithmetic closes neatly:

- At **25** damage per shot, 125 health needs **5 shots**.
- At **37** damage per shot, 125 health needs **4 shots** (3.4, so 4).
- Mid-range (about 31), it needs **4 shots**.

So **"about four Revolver shots"** is consistent with **125 health** — and inconsistent with any much lower health pool. That is the most we will claim: our arithmetic, using two single-source numbers, lands on the same figure as the one source that says it outright. We are not independently confirming either number; we are showing they do not contradict each other.

The Boar is the biome's pacing problem rather than its boss, a distinction our [Bosses](/guides/boss-guide) guide is built on.

One thing that is not in dispute: **the Boar Tusk is recent.** One source states it twice — on its Boar Tusk page and its Boar Helmet page — that both the tusk and the helmet arrived with the **Biome Refresher** update on **22 August 2026**, which is the same patch our [patch notes](/guides/updates-patch-notes) page records as adding *"Boar tusks and a defensive helmet recipe"*. That line in our own update log is currently the only place on this site that mentions the tusk at all — which makes the next section the actual reason this page exists.

## The Boar Tusk and the Boar Helmet

This is the part no source we found has written up in a way a player can act on, so here it is whole.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Item** | **Boar Helmet** — a clothing/armour piece, obtained by crafting |
| **Effect** | **Significant defence increase** |
| **Recipe** | **4 Boar Tusk + 2 Mammoth Tusk** |
| **Made at** | The **Tool Workshop** |
| **Added with** | The **Biome Refresher** update, 22 August 2026 |
| **Sourcing** | **One source** (99-nights-in-the-forest), stated twice on its own site |

**How it is sourced, and why we are not inflating that.** The recipe appears on two pages of the same site — a Boar Helmet page and a Boar Tusk page — and the two are consistent with each other. Consistency within a single site is not corroboration, so this is a **single source** by our standards: nothing we found anywhere else lists the Boar Helmet at all, including the large crafting-recipe roundups that do list the Mammoth Helmet, the Infernal Helmet and the Riot Shield. We are publishing the recipe because a single source that is internally consistent and not contradicted by any other source is still more useful than a blank, and we are labelling it as one source so you can decide for yourself.

**The conflict nobody mentions:** the Boar Helmet needs **2 Mammoth Tusk**, and so does the [Mammoth Helmet](/entities/the-mammoth). Both are Tool Workshop helmets, and our Tool Workshop coverage lives on the [Infernal Helmet](/items/how-to-get-infernal-helmet) page — not the place for either recipe — but the material overlap is real:

- Wanting both means hunting **4 Mammoth Tusk**, and Mammoths are [neutral until you hit them](/entities/the-mammoth), walk into your shots, and are not something you farm casually.
- The Workshop **makes one item at a time**, so the Boar Helmet and the Mammoth Helmet compete for a slot, not just for tusks.
- **Our read, flagged as ours:** if your Mammoths are also your warm-clothing plan, the Mammoth Helmet is the cheaper of the two to finish (2 Mammoth Tusk covers it completely), while the Boar Helmet needs the tusk farm *and* still leaves you with tusks left over. If you are building Jungle defence, the Boar Helmet is the one whose key material is farmable in a biome you already have to survive in.

**Two things we could not establish:** whether the Boar Helmet obeys the same rule as every other armour piece in the game — **once equipped you cannot take it off**, only swap it for a strictly better one — and whether it counts as a helmet for any cold-biome or Volcanic damage rule. Nothing we found states either, and both would change how you plan a run, so we are leaving them open rather than assuming.

The Boar's tame is priced in Corn and Morsel; the [Kiwi](/entities/the-kiwi)'s is priced in a single Berry and a campfire ladder nobody has added up before.

The Boar is the one tame whose bill opens with Corn instead of a [Carrot](/items/carrot), and our Carrot page carries the full comparison.

The Boar swaps a Carrot for Corn, and the Arctic Fox is the animal that swaps it for [Chili](/items/chili) instead, on our Chili page.

Chili only ever arrives from a Seed Pack, as our [Seed Packs](/items/seed-packs) page explains.

Not every bill is this heavy, and the [Berry](/items/berry) one on the Kiwi is the floor of the whole taming economy.

## Taming One: Corn, Morsel, and One Prerequisite

Boars are tameable, and this is the cheapest taming route in the Jungle — which is why our Jungle page flags that **Corn tames Boars more easily than it tames Wolves**. The full food economy is on [Taming Guide](/taming/taming-guide); the Boar-specific version is short.

**1. Find an unhurt Boar.** This is the prerequisite the sources state and most guides skip: **taming can only start on a Boar that has not taken damage.** If you have already traded blows with it, it cannot be tamed. That single clause is why players who fire one shot at a Boar to "see what it does" end up unable to tame it.

**2. Start the minigame.** With an **[Old Taming Flute](/items/taming-flute) or better**, click the Boar. It goes **docile for the duration of the minigame** — one source puts the minigame at about **30 seconds**, which is worth knowing before you commit mid-fight.

**3. Feed it twice.** The cost is **2 hearts**, and per fandom and rblxguide the feed is **Corn ×1 + Morsel ×1 per stage, two stages**.

**4. The feed dispute.** One source says the post-minigame feeding is "commonly **Morsel + Steak**", and adds that some variants reportedly want **Corn + Morsel**. So two sources say Corn + Morsel and one says Morsel + Steak with Corn as a possible variant. We print **Corn + Morsel** because it is the majority, and we note the alternative rather than pretending the disagreement is not there.

There is also an inconsistency in the taming itself worth knowing before you plan a route: one source describes the Boar as **not tameable at all** in its summary line while simultaneously listing taming requirements in detail two sections later. That is an internal contradiction, and the detailed version is the one that matches the other two sources.

**What a tamed Boar is for:** per one source, tamed Boars follow you and fight as **high-health frontline pets**. Pair that with the pet-aggro rule above and the shape of a Jungle pet comp suggests itself — but the pet-rankings on [Best Pet](/taming/best-pet) and the class bonuses on [Beastmaster](/classes/beastmaster-class) are the pages that actually settle comps, and we are not re-deriving them here.

That badge is a single row in a much longer table: every badge, its star rating and its diamond reward, on our [All Badges](/guides/all-badges) page.

## Boar King: What We Are Not Going To Carry

**One** source states that in Wild West mode variants, Boars — **including a "Boar King"** — may appear with enhanced visuals such as **red eyes or flame effects**.

We are not publishing that as fact, and here is the specific reason rather than a general shrug: the only other thing we found that mentions a Boar King sits on a fandom-style wiki for what is clearly **a different IP** — its character box lists relations to Kraken Kid, Squid Kid, Koala Kid, Dino Kid and "Maya's Minion", and an episode titled *SURVIVING 99 NIGHTS in the WILD WEST!*. That is fan fiction of another franchise wearing our game's name. When a claim that interesting is supported by exactly one plausible source and one obvious nonsense source, the correct move for a site with our rules is to **name the claim, mark it single-source, and decline to carry it**. So: it is claimed, it is one source, and we do not know what a Boar King is or does.

One hundred and twenty-five health splits cleanly between four revolver shots and two heavy swings, which is the whole fight in one division. The arithmetic for every weapon is in our [weapon damage and time to kill guide](/guides/weapon-damage-and-time-to-kill).

## How To Deal With Them

The advice below is mostly single-source and is marked that way. Where we have done arithmetic on top of a source number, we say so.

| Approach | What the sources say |
|---|---|
| **Ranged, at an angle** | One source recommends the Revolver, a Bow, Poison Claws, or "high-DPS ranged"; the practical version is to shoot the flank, not the face |
| **Kite, do not trade** | "Use ranged kiting tactics to avoid direct melee trades" — one source |
| **Use terrain** | Trees, temple stairs and elevation to **limit the charge path** — one source |
| **Split the aggro** | Bring a pet or a teammate so the Boar is not on you; the pet rule above is what makes this work |
| **Poison armour** | Not Boar-specific, but our [Poison Armour](/items/how-to-get-poison-armour) page documents that wolves, cultists and **boars** all kill themselves on it while attacking — roughly 15 poison damage per wolf strike, about five hits to kill one |
| **Do not go at night** | One source advises avoiding Boar zones at night for visibility reasons — consistent with the sightline problem above |

**The one we would add, and it is ours:** the hit-then-dart-away loop means the gap right after a Boar lands its hit is the widest opening you get. That follows from the behaviour all three sources describe, not from a new source. Walk in on the gap, not on the chase.

## FAQ

**Can you kill a Boar?** Yes. Unlike [The Ram](/entities/the-ram), it has a health value and dies.

**How much health does it have?** **125** per the only source that publishes a number, itself marked with a question mark elsewhere. Unconfirmed.

**How much damage does it do?** Nobody we found publishes it. One source estimates an unarmoured player dies in about four hits.

**Why did it disappear in a puff of smoke?** It drifted too far from its spawn point, dropped to passive, and teleported home. See the walk-through above; the same rule applies to [The Mammoth](/entities/the-mammoth).

**Can a Boar follow me into camp?** Yes, if you attack it first. It can enter the campfire area.

**Why can't I tame the Boar I just shot at?** Taming only works on an **undamaged** Boar. One stray shot closes that door.

**What do Boars drop?** Morsel 1–2 across all sources; Steak and Boar Tusk 0–1 per a single source.

**What is the Boar Tusk for?** The **Boar Helmet** at the Tool Workshop — 4 Boar Tusk + 2 Mammoth Tusk. Single source.

**Does the Boar Helmet conflict with the Mammoth Helmet?** Both need 2 Mammoth Tusk and the Workshop makes one item at a time. Our read on which to prioritise is above and flagged as ours.

**Is there a Boar King?** One source claims it with red eyes and flame effects. Single source, contradicted by nothing and supported by nothing we trust. We are not carrying it.
