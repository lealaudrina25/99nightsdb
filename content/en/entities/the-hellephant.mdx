The Hellephant is the [Volcanic Biome](/locations/volcanic-biome)'s apex animal, and it is the one thing in that biome you are meant to *take* rather than merely survive. It is the Mammoth with the neutrality removed: same shape, same appetite, same food bill — but it charges you on sight and it does not let you stand still afterwards.

It is also, in a way most guides miss, a **choice with an expiry**. The first thing you do to it decides whether you end the encounter with food or with a pet, and the animal must be **undamaged** to be tamed. Shoot to clear the cultists around the Ritual Circle and the Hellephant in front of you is no longer taming material.

The full tame route — Strong Flute, five cycles, the campfire reset — is on [How to Tame a Hellephant](/taming/how-to-tame-a-hellephant). This page is the other half: what it is as a threat, what it does to you, what it leaves behind, and where it sits next to the three other things that share its biome.

Last verified: **2026-10-03**.

## Where It Lives

**The Volcanic Biome, at the Hellephant Ritual Circle.** That is the spawn every source agrees on, and the circle is the biome's own landmark — the same structure family as the [Cultist King](/entities/the-cultist-king)'s pedestals, not a cave or a dungeon.

The detail that changes how you plan a run is not the location. It is that **your run may not have a Volcanic Biome at all.** Snow and Volcanic are one-or-the-other per run, and the Jungle is always present; the biomes have to be seen on your map from a **Level 2+ campfire** to be visible at all. So the Hellephant is not a creature you can route to — it is a creature you hope appeared. Our [Taming Guide](/taming/taming-guide) lists it among the apex animals you can tame, and that list is a *catalogue*, not a promise about any single run.

What is consistent wherever it does appear: **cultists guard it.** Every guide that mentions its spawn puts a cultist group near it, which is why the practical advice in every source is the same — **clear the cultists first.** Fighting the Hellephant means sharing the arena with crossbow fire, and the two threats do not interact politely.

The campfire reset is the counter to that loop — stand near a lit fire and the Hellephant drops you. What a lit campfire does, and the levels that make the radius worth standing in, is on [Campfire](/guides/campfire).

## The Loop It Runs

The Hellephant behaves like the game's standard hostile animal, and knowing the loop is most of the fight:

1. **It wanders** aimlessly until you enter its detection radius or damage it. Nothing happens until then.
2. **It commits.** Once it has you, it charges and attacks in range — a **melee charge** listed at **30 damage** that **ragdolls** you.
3. **Speed 20 means you cannot simply outrun it.** That is the number to remember: at 20 it matches walking pace, so once it starts charging, running is not a plan on its own.
4. **It loses interest at a lit campfire** — the same rule the [Scorpion](/entities/the-scorpion) obeys. Get near a fire and it breaks off.
5. **It gets stuck on large structures.** Trees and buildings stop it targeting you *even while you are still inside its detection radius*, which is the gap every source tells you to exploit: put something solid between you and it, then take the side it cannot path around.
6. **It teleports home** if it ends up too far from its spawn with no target. You cannot farm it by dragging it across the biome.

Steps 4 to 6 are the reset tools. Step 3 is the reason the fight is a fight rather than a dodge.

Being ragdolled across the map is how people end up dying somewhere nobody can reach, which is the worst possible place to fall. See [**Death and Respawn**](/guides/death-and-respawn).

## The Ragdoll, Not the 30

Here is the read most stat tables get wrong, and the sources hand it to us almost in writing.

**The Hellephant's damage is 30. The Polar Bear's is 50.** By damage alone the Polar Bear is the worse animal, and one source's own animal table makes exactly that comparison — then adds the sentence that changes the ranking: the Polar Bear **does not ragdoll** you, which "gives you time to escape," while the Hellephant's charge does exactly the opposite.

So 30 here is not a mid-table number you can shrug at.

- The **hit costs you the use of your own character** for a moment, in a biome whose floor is also damaging you.
- It is the **only Volcanic threat documented as ragdolling** you. The Scorpion's problem is poison over time; The Ram's problem is that it is unkillable; the lava's problem is that it eats your loot. None of those take you off your feet.
- Two hits of 30 plus a lava tick is a very different walk home from two hits of 30 with your feet under you.

**What the ragdoll does not do is cost you your inventory.** No source we found states that being ragdolled makes you drop items, and we are not going to assume it. What the sources do say is that **ranged weapons are almost a necessity** — that is the wiki's wording — and that you should **arrive with hunger covered**, because food is scarce in the Volcanic Biome and the fight is long. That hunger note is worth more than it looks: you are about to fight a 500-health animal, and killing it is the biome's best meal ticket.

## Where It Sits: Mammoth, Scorpion, Ram

Three other things get compared to the Hellephant. None of them is it.

| | Hellephant | Mammoth | The Ram | The Scorpion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Biome | Volcanic | Snow | Volcanic | Volcanic |
| Temperament | **Hostile** | Neutral | Hazard | Hostile |
| Health | **500** | 800 | **Unkillable** | 50 |
| Damage | 30 | 30 | Reported 25 | 20 + poison |
| Standout | **Ragdoll** | 800 HP, drops uncooked food | Flattens trees, cannot be stopped | Poison over time |
| Drops | Cooked Steak 8–9, Cooked Ribs 0–2, Mammoth Tusk 0–1 | Steak 8–9, Ribs 2, Tusk 0–1 | **Nothing** | Morsel 0–1, Scorpion Shell 0–1 |

**Against the Mammoth, the counter-intuitive part is that the hostile one is the softer one.** The Hellephant has **less health than the peaceful Snow Mammoth** (500 against 800) while dealing identical 30 damage. If you read "Hellephant" as "the tougher Mammoth because it is in the harder biome," you get the direction backwards. What the Hellephant trades away in health, it buys in aggression — the Mammoth lets you play a flute at it; the Hellephant does not let you do anything.

**Against The Ram, the practical part is that one of these two is answerable.** Both are big Volcanic animals that charge in a straight line at walking-plus speed, and players confuse them from the sound of an engine alone. They are not siblings. The Ram is **unkillable**, has no drop table, and is a hazard to route around. The Hellephant **dies, pays 8–9 Cooked Steak, and can be recruited**. If it is the Ram, leave; if it is the Hellephant, it is worth the trip.

**Against the Scorpion, the difference is scale.** 50 HP versus 500 is not a harder fight, it is a different activity. See [The Scorpion](/entities/the-scorpion) for the poison route.

## The Decision Your First Shot Makes

The Hellephant is the rare entity with two good endings, and the game does not let you have both.

**An animal must be undamaged to be tamed.** Every source on the tame states it, and the Hellephant is the animal where it stings: you will be standing in a Volcanic arena with a group of cultists shooting at you, and the natural move is to shoot the cultists *or* the Hellephant to make the space quiet. Both work. Only one leaves you a pet.

So, before the first round goes down the barrel:

- **Want the pet?** You need a **[Strong Taming Flute](/items/taming-flute)** (Tier 3), which means you have not levelled to it in this run — and you cannot soften it first. Pull the cultists with something that is not aimed at the Mammoth, or accept that you will do the mini-game while something else shoots at you.
- **Want the food?** Shoot it. 8–9 Cooked Steak is the only cooked drop in the game, and no flute level is required.

One more thing on the kill side: **the Volcanic floor may help you.** Lava damages enemies that are not invincible, and the [Volcanic Biome](/locations/volcanic-biome) page documents that as a general mechanic. The Hellephant is not documented as invincible — unlike The Ram — so it should take lava damage the same way the [Cultist King](/entities/the-cultist-king) does. **That last step is our inference, not a published claim:** no source states that lava damages the Hellephant, and we have not tested it. Treat it as a possibility worth trying in a run you were going to lose anyway.

- **The one entity that skips the fire.** [Chef and Cook](/classes/chef-class) — its cooked drops go straight into the station that makes Steak Dinner and BBQ Ribs.

It shares the Mammoth's drop, which is the highest-value meat in the game at four forks cooked. See [**Hunger in 99 Nights in the Forest**](/guides/hunger).

Worth knowing before you spend a day here: the **Gem of the Forest** has to exist first, and no source agrees on the night fragments start dropping. The [Gem of the Forest](/items/gem-of-the-forest) page walks that argument through.

## What Killing It Pays

| Drop | Amount | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Cooked Steak** | **8–9** | Guaranteed |
| **Cooked Ribs** | 0–2 | Chance |
| **Mammoth Tusk** | 0–1 | Chance |

The unusual part is a single word: **cooked**. The Hellephant is described as **the only entity that drops cooked loot with no fire required** — the Mammoth drops the same quantities of *uncooked* Steak and Ribs. In a biome where the floor is lava, being handed ready-to-eat food is not a cosmetic difference, and it is the reason a Volcanic run should include a Hellephant kill even for players who will never own a Strong Flute.

On the Mammoth Tusk: it is **not** an Infernal Helmet ingredient — that recipe takes Scorpion Shells and a Cultist King Antler. It does have a documented use, which our older text here missed: the **Mammoth Helmet** is made at the Tool Workshop from **2 Mammoth Tusk and 2 Wolf Pelt**, which puts it above the Polar Bear Hat (see the [Warm Clothing](/items/warm-clothing) page). One discrepancy worth flagging: our [Owl](/entities/the-owl) page describes an older Mammoth Helmet recipe built from **owl feathers plus coal**. Both are internal to this wiki, and we have not seen the conflict settled by an outside source, so treat the tusk-and-pelt version as the current one and the feather version as the older.

And on availability: one source reports the Hellephant **respawns after roughly two days**. That is a single source and no one else confirms a timer, so do not build a farming loop around it.

## Health: Three Sources Say 500, Two Say 800

The number our own pages use is **500**, and the split has shifted since the tame page was written.

| Source | Health |
|---|---|
| 99-nights-in-the-forest.com | **500** |
| Pocket Tactics | **500** |
| Sportskeeda | **500** |
| rblxguide | 800 |
| A lookalike content site we exclude | 800 |

**500 is now the three-source figure**, and it is consistent with the design pattern too: the Hellephant has less health than the neutral Mammoth it replaces. The 800 crowd is small but not irrelevant, and we show it rather than bury it. Damage (**30**) and walk speed (**20**) are unanimous across every source that lists them.

Worth knowing why this is unresolved at all: the Hellephant **was never mentioned in the official update log**, so its stat sheet rests on community reporting rather than a developer statement. That is the same hole The [Ram](/entities/the-ram) fell into.

## FAQ

**Where does the Hellephant spawn?**
The Volcanic Biome, at the **Hellephant Ritual Circle**, with a cultist group nearby. The Volcanic Biome itself is a one-or-the-other roll per run, so some runs never have one.

**How much health does it have?**
500, by three sources. rblxguide says 800. We use 500 and show the disagreement.

**Is it the Lava Mammoth?**
Yes — same animal. Players said Lava Mammoth first; a tester later reported the in-game name as Hellephant. See the [tame page](/taming/how-to-tame-a-hellephant) for the naming history.

**Why is the ragdoll worse than the 30 damage?**
Because it takes your character away from you while the rest of the biome keeps running. One source's comparison says it outright: the Polar Bear hits for 50 and does not ragdoll, which is why escape is possible there and not here.

**Can I tame it after fighting it?**
No. A damaged animal cannot be tamed. If you want the pet, nothing may damage it first — including your own opening shot.

**What do I need to tame it?**
Strong Taming Flute (Tier 3), 5 cycles, **10 Pumpkin + 5 Cake** — the exact same bill the Mammoth needs. The step-by-step is on [How to Tame a Hellephant](/taming/how-to-tame-a-hellephant).

**Does it drop cooked food?**
8–9 Cooked Steak guaranteed, plus 0–2 Cooked Ribs and a chance of a Mammoth Tusk. It is the only entity that drops cooked loot without cooking it first.

**How do I make it stop chasing me?**
Get close to a **lit campfire**, or leave its detection radius. It also gets stuck on trees and buildings, and it teleports back to its spawn if you pull it too far.

**Is it The Ram?**
No. The Ram is unkillable and drops nothing; the Hellephant dies and pays out. See [The Ram](/entities/the-ram).
