The Kiwi is the cheapest bird in 99 Nights in the Forest and the hardest animal to reach. Taming it takes **one cycle and one Berry** on the Old Taming Flute — the flute you spawn with — which makes it tied with the Bunny for the least expensive tame in the game. Getting to the animal that those one cycle and one Berry are spent on takes **Campfire Level 6**, which is the single most expensive step in the entire ladder: roughly **275 logs and up, 170 coal, 35 canisters, 8 oil barrels and 80 biofuel**, or about four times what the level below it costs.

Every guide that covers the Kiwi covers the first half of that sentence and stops. The second half is the one that answers the question people are actually asking, which is whether the wall is worth climbing for an animal that cannot fight and cannot die. This page prices the wall, counts what else Level 6 buys you, and separates the two readings of "Campfire Level 6" that the sources are arguing about.

Last verified: **2026-10-05**.

The Kiwi and the Bunny are the two tames whose bill is not Steak at all, and our [Steak](/items/steak) page prices them against the Wolf so the difference is a number rather than an opinion.

The [Bunny](/entities/the-bunny) is the same one-cycle bill without the door: one Carrot instead of one Berry and no campfire level at all.

The bill itself is one [Berry](/items/berry), which we price against every other food on our Berry page.

## The Cheapest Tame Sits Behind the Most Expensive Door

Read the two tables the game's own material hands you and the strange shape of this animal falls out immediately.

Every other Old-Flute animal is gated by a biome or by nothing at all. The Bunny needs no campfire level and no biome. The [Wolf](/entities/the-wolf) shows up in the Forest at night around Level 2. The [Green Frog](/entities/the-frog) lives in ponds and the Jungle. The [Scorpion](/entities/the-scorpion) lives in the Volcanic Biome. Only the Kiwi asks for the top of the fire ladder.

| Animal | Flute | Cycles | Food | Gate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bunny | Old | 1 | Carrot ×1 | None |
| **Kiwi** | **Old** | **1** | **Berry ×1** | **Campfire 6** |
| Green Frog | Old | 2 | Mackerel ×2 | Ponds and the Jungle |
| Scorpion | Old | 2–3 | Steak + Morsel | Volcanic Biome |
| Wolf | Old | 3 | Steak ×6 | Forest, night, Level 2 onward |

Three independent dimensions — flute tier, cycles, and food — all point the same way here, and the fourth points the other way. **The Kiwi wants the lowest flute in the game and the highest campfire level.** That is a genuinely inverted difficulty curve, and the only published entity spawn ladder in the game says it outright. Namu's ladder runs **rabbit 0, wolf 2, Alpha Wolf 4, bear 5, kiwi 6** — which places the Kiwi, a one-cycle passive bird on the entry flute, *above the Bear*: a five-cycle apex animal on the Strong Flute. Our own [Campfire Level Gates](/guides/campfire-level-gates) page quotes that ladder and refuses to average it with anything, and that refusal is the correct instinct, because the ladder is the only evidence for the claim and it is one source.

The comparison that makes the point without the ladder is the [Alpha Wolf](/entities/the-alpha-wolf). It needs the **Good** Flute — the second tier, the one our [Taming Flute](/items/taming-flute) page argues is the worst value in the chain — and it needs **Level 4**. The Kiwi needs the **Old** Flute and it needs **Level 6**. If you sort every pet in the game by how much it asks of you, the Kiwi is near the top of one column and at the very bottom of the other.

## Level 6 Is Not One Reward. It Is Eleven, And The Kiwi Is The Only Empty One

Here is the count that no source has run. Our [Campfire Level Gates](/guides/campfire-level-gates) page documents eleven separate things that open at Campfire Level 6. Six of them are the game's back half. One of them is a bait.

| Level 6 gate | What you actually get |
|---|---|
| Full map reveal | The whole map, and the floor the speedrun ladder runs into |
| [Bone Pond](/guides/fishing-guide) | The only water producing the Medkit, the Old Car Engine and the Gem Fragment |
| [The Trident](/weapons/how-to-get-trident) | A weapon with no craft route and no trade route |
| Medkit | The best revive item in the game |
| Old Car Engine, Gem Fragment | High-tier crafting materials |
| [Koala Kid](/locations/missing-kids) | +1 day multiplier, and the last of the four rescues |
| The Grey Key cave | The highest campfire level on any lock in the game |
| The speedrun +1 | The last day-multiplier step |
| Tameable Bear, deep Forest | The upper edge of the bear spawn window |
| Firemaking IV | The fourth and last campfire badge |
| **Kiwi Burrow** | **A bird that cannot attack, cannot be killed, and does nothing** |

Put that last row next to the fuel bill and the shape of the argument is complete. If you are already climbing to Level 6 — and a winning run needs the Koala Kid, the Grey Key and the last speedrun multiplier anyway — the Kiwi costs you nothing extra but a perimeter patrol. If you are *not* going to Level 6, then the Kiwi is the most expensive animal in the game to reach, and everything else you could have spent that wood on is behind the same door.

That is the whole decision, and it is a two-line one. What it does to the [Best Pet](/taming/best-pet) page's verdict — Kiwi listed as a *collector's trophy* with *no practical value* — is to show that the verdict is right for a different reason than the page states. The value problem is not the animal. It is the coincidence of the animal with the most expensive gate in the game.

Campfire gates are a shared theme, and the [Fairy](/entities/the-fairy)'s level-2 unlock is the friendlier one, covered on her page.

## Where The Kiwi Is, And Why "Campfire Level 6" Means Two Different Things

The spawn point is described consistently by five sources and given three names. It is **the edge of the map, near the Campfire Level 6 area, at a tree trunk with a hole in it**. The fandom says the Kiwi "can be located in the Campfire level 6 area, at the edge of the map near a tree trunk with a hole in it, which is where the Kiwi spawns from." The mirror wiki says the same thing and renders the landmark as a *tree stump* with a hole. Finding Dulcinea says a "large tree trunk with a visible hole or cavity near its base." Rosenberry Rooms calls it "a distinctive tree trunk with a hole." Deltia's Gaming, more briefly, says you will "notice tree trunks with holes in them."

Our own [Taming Guide](/taming/taming-guide) and Bloxodes both use a fourth wording, **Kiwi Burrow**, which is the name that gets attached to the campfire gate in our [Campfire Level Gates](/guides/campfire-level-gates) table. Four names, one structure, no source claiming they are different places.

The argument is about what the level does, not where the tree is, and it splits cleanly:

| Reading | Sources | What they say |
|---|---|---|
| **Hard gate** | Deltia's Gaming, fandom, the mirror wiki, Bloxodes, and this site's three pages | You can only find one *after* Level 6. Deltia's states it in both directions: "only after upgrading your campfire to level 6," and "you can only find them near their spawn points" |
| **Rate boost** | Finding Dulcinea, Rosenberry Rooms | Level 6 is not required to spawn, only optimal. Finding Dulcinea: "While not strictly necessary for the Kiwi to spawn, many players report increased spawn rates with a max-level campfire." Rosenberry lists Level 6 as *Optimal* with the *Benefit* of "increased Kiwi spawn chance" |

Five sources against two is not a close vote, but the split is not random: **the two sources that talk about rate are the only two that talk to players about rate.** The five on the left are stating a location. The two on the right are describing an observation. Nobody we can find publishes the mechanism, and our own [Taming Flute](/items/taming-flute) page already lists this as its sixth unresolved disagreement — this page is where that item gets answered as well as it can be, which is to say it gets counted rather than settled.

Both wikis add the same footnote independently: Kiwis turn up in other biomes "due to an occasional glitch," with the fandom putting the odds at "almost 0." So the border spawns are real and negligible, which is the same shape of answer the game gives for most of its rare spawns.

The Kiwi shares its food row with the Bunny, which is tamed for a single [Carrot](/items/carrot), and our Carrot page carries that row.

The Kiwi costs nothing to feed, which puts it in direct contrast with the one pet whose bill carries a [Chili](/items/chili).

Chili only ever arrives from a Seed Pack, as our [Seed Packs](/items/seed-packs) page explains.
## The Gate Is The Patrol, Not The Fire

This section is the one that changes whether you should plan around the Kiwi at all.

Two sources estimate the chance of running into a Kiwi at a given spawn point, and they give the same figure. Finding Dulcinea: "the spawn rate appears to be less than 5% under optimal conditions." Rosenberry Rooms: "Base Rate: Extremely low (estimated under 5% chance per patrol)." Neither measured anything — both say *estimated* — and no other source we found offers a number in either direction. Print it as what it is: a guess that two independent writers reached from the same experience of searching.

What both of them say alongside the guess is the useful part, and they agree on the method:

- **Patrol the perimeter systematically.** The spawn point is fixed; the search is the work.
- **Session-hop.** Both explicitly recommend checking across multiple game sessions rather than one long session.
- **Server population matters to them.** Rosenberry names crowded servers as a factor; Finding Dulcinea repeats it.

And one fact that makes the search bearable: **the Kiwi does not move once it has spawned.** Finding Dulcinea states it plainly — "it doesn't move around like other animals, staying stationary once spawned" — and the fandom agrees that it "does not move." So a Kiwi you find does not wander off, does not chase you, and cannot be lost to a night event. The search is repeatable and, once successful, permanent for that run.

The conclusion follows from that combination. **The campfire is a floor, not the wall.** Level 6 removes you from the search; it does not end it. Anyone who tells you the Kiwi is a Level 6 content is describing the shortest possible version of a two-part job, and the second part is the one that eats the afternoon.

## What A Tamed Kiwi Does: Nothing, On Purpose

This is the part every source agrees on, which in this topic means every source states it in passing and none of them develops it.

**It does not fight.** Deltia's Gaming: "Unlike Wolf or Bear pets, this one does not attack enemies." Finding Dulcinea's summary table lists combat usefulness as *none — cosmetic only*.

**It cannot be killed.** The fandom calls it "the first passive entity that you can't kill." The mirror wiki says "immune to all forms of damage" and calls it "the first invincible passive animal." Finding Dulcinea: "the only passive entity in the game that cannot be killed." Three sources, three different rankings of the same fact, and the ranking is where they disagree — see below.

**It is not hurt by lava,** in the Volcanic Biome or the Volcano itself, per both wikis.

**It is a bird, and not the only one.** The fandom records that the Kiwi is "the second bird entity, after [The Owl](/entities/the-owl), but the first normal bird, as The Owl in-game is considered a monster." That single line is the whole relationship between the two birds in this game, and it is why the Kiwi is a pet you can walk up to while the Owl is a thing that punishes you for moving.

**It is one of five things you can carry.** The fandom notes the Kiwi is "the only entity apart from the four Missing Children you can pick up and put inside a Sack" — and both the fandom and Finding Dulcinea add the detail that it carries no name label until it has been tamed. That last line is also the one piece of reported behaviour on this list that smells like a bug rather than a design choice, and we are recording it as reported rather than corroborated.

So what you get for the wall is a follower that cannot die, cannot help, and cannot be eaten. The one thing it might do is not in this paragraph.

## Why Bother: The Only Published Reason Is The Flute Bar

The only source that names a use names a small one. The fandom: "The Kiwi currently has no evident uses, except that when tamed it increases the level up bar by a lot." That is a single source, it is qualitative, and it is the entire case for taming the animal. Our own [Best Pet](/taming/best-pet) page reaches the same conclusion from the other direction and gives the same recipe as a workaround — Kiwi listed as *collector's trophy / no practical value*, and in the page's own taming order, "Bunny and Kiwi — one cycle each, minimal food. Purely to level the [Taming Flute](/items/taming-flute)."

That is a coherent answer, and it is the honest one: **the Kiwi is the cheapest way to push one flank of the flute XP bar**, and it is worth doing only because the bar is a shared resource you are filling anyway. If you were going to fill it, the Kiwi is a marginal improvement on the Bunny — same cycle count, one Berry instead of one Carrot, and no advantage beyond the count itself.

Where the advance goes rather than the Kiwi is worth stating, because the [Taming Flute](/items/taming-flute) page already does the arithmetic: **a [Wolf](/entities/the-wolf) at three cycles of six Steak gives you a pet that fights, and the Alpha Wolf beyond it gives you the one your run actually wants.** Spending the same bar on a Kiwi spends it on nothing. And the Kiwi is per-run — pets do not carry over, which our [Taming Guide](/taming/taming-guide) page states outright — so the flute XP is the only thing the animal returns to you, and it returns the same day.

The one situation where the Kiwi is strictly correct: **you are already Level 6, you are already patrolling the edge for the Koala Kid, and you have a spare Sack slot and a spare Berry.** In that case the Kiwi costs you a detour, and the only cost is search time you were going to spend anyway.

## Where Sources Disagree

**1. Whether Level 6 is a gate or a multiplier.** Five sources call it a hard gate; two call it an optimal level that raises the spawn chance. Nobody publishes the mechanism. Our own three pages take the gate reading, which is also the reading that lets this page put the Kiwi Burrow in the Level 6 table above.

**2. The odds, which are estimates rather than measurements.** Finding Dulcinea says under 5% under optimal conditions; Rosenberry Rooms says under 5% per patrol; both use the word *estimated*. No source we found has measured the spawn rate, and the two estimates were reached independently from the same kind of experience, which makes them two observations rather than one observation told twice.

**3. What a tamed Kiwi is *for*.** The fandom names one use — it moves the flute XP bar a lot. The mirror wiki says "the Kiwi does not serve a functional purpose. It does not attack enemies, provide buffs, or affect gameplay mechanics." Finding Dulcinea files it under cosmetics. Three sources, and the fandom is the only one that finds any function at all.

**4. Where it sits in the invincibility list.** The fandom calls it both "the first passive entity that you can't kill" and "the fourth creature to be unkillable," running backwards through The Ram, The Owl and The Deer to get there. The mirror wiki calls it "the first invincible passive animal." Finding Dulcinea calls it "the only passive entity." All three are classifying by passive status and arriving at three different positions, which means the classification itself is doing more work than the fact.

**5. The roster size.** Our [The Cat](/entities/the-cat) page describes "the published list of the game's eleven tameable animals," and our [Taming Flute](/items/taming-flute) page agrees: eleven animal rows every source shares, with the Bee, the Mushroom Wolf and an Easter Chick at the edges. Bloxodes totals thirteen. The difference is which edge creatures get counted, not a disagreement about the eleven.

**6. Names for one tree.** Kiwi Burrow (our Taming Guide, Bloxodes); "tree trunk with a hole" (the fandom, Finding Dulcinea, Rosenberry); "tree stump with a hole" (the mirror wiki). Our [Campfire Level Gates](/guides/campfire-level-gates) page uses the first and our [Taming Guide](/taming/taming-guide) repeats it with the campfire level attached.

**7. Inside this site, and this is the one worth flagging.** Our [Taming Guide](/taming/taming-guide) lists the Kiwi's location as "Kiwi Burrow (needs campfire Level 6)" and our [Taming Flute](/items/taming-flute) page numbers the Kiwi's location as its sixth open disagreement. Our [Campfire Level Gates](/guides/campfire-level-gates) table, however, lists the Kiwi Burrow as *one of eleven Level 6 gates*, which is a weaker claim than the first two: a gate table says the burrow becomes available at Level 6, not that the bird refuses to appear at Level 5. We do not think those sentences contradict each other — they are the same fact in different tenses — but the difference matters if you are planning a run and have not reached the fire yet, so it is worth being precise about which one you are relying on. In neither version does anything publish a mechanism.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Is the Kiwi worth taming?**
Only under two conditions: you are going to Campfire Level 6 anyway, and you are going to patrol the map edge regardless. Outside that, you are paying the most expensive step in the game for an animal that cannot fight, cannot die, and has no published use apart from moving the flute XP bar. The [Best Pet](/taming/best-pet) page reaches the same conclusion and calls it a collector's item.

**Do I really need Campfire Level 6?**
Under the majority reading, yes — five sources including this site's own three pages. Under the minority reading, Level 6 raises the spawn chance rather than causing it, which means you could find one earlier and would simply be searching longer. Nothing we can cite settles it. If you are Level 5 with the fuel for one more big push, that push is worth more than the bird.

**Is the Kiwi the hardest animal to tame?**
No, and this is the detail most guides invert. It is the hardest animal to *reach*. Once you are standing in front of it, it is one cycle and one Berry — tied with the Bunny for the easiest tame in the game, with a [fishing-style mini-game](/guides/fishing-guide) that one mood level does not make hard.

**Why can't I find one?**
Two independent sources put the chance at under 5% per patrol or per optimal-condition check, and both advise the same two things: patrol the perimeter systematically, and check across multiple sessions rather than one long one. The animal also does not move once spawned, so a Kiwi you have found stays put until you tame it.

**Can the Kiwi die?**
Not from anything. It is immune to all damage including lava, in the Volcanic Biome and inside the Volcano. It is also the second bird entity after [The Owl](/entities/the-owl), which is classified as a monster rather than a bird.

**Can I tame it with a Strong Flute?**
Yes. The fandom states the Kiwi "can be tamed using all current Flutes as of the Taming Update." One Berry and one cycle either way — the flute tier does not change the cost, only which animals you can reach when you are not at the Kiwi.

**Does the Kiwi carry over between runs?**
No. Taming resets every session, which our [Taming Guide](/taming/taming-guide) page states, so a Kiwi is worth the flute XP it returns on the day you get it and nothing after.

**How many pets can a Kiwi take up?**
Two by default, three holding a Strong Flute, three as a [Zookeeper](/classes/zookeeper-class) and five as a [Beastmaster](/classes/beastmaster-class). The cap tables are on the [Taming Flute](/items/taming-flute) page.
