# Brute Cultist

## Quick Answer

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What is it | A human cultist variant that lives underground, not on the surface |
| Health | 400, or 400–450, depending on the source — see below |
| Damage | 40 per swing (shared by three of four sources) |
| Weapon | A large battleaxe |
| Where | The Caverns and the Final Cave, alongside Shadow Cultists |
| How many | Around 10–12 in the Final Cave |
| Drops | Nothing published — there is no loot table |
| Tameable | No |
| Added | 22 November 2025, with The Bat |
| One-line read | Take the first hit, then it is slow enough to work around |

That last row is the part almost every guide skips. The Brute's damage is high and its health is enormous, but three separate write-ups note the same counterweight: the attack cooldown is very long. Survive one swing and the fight opens up.

Hard Mode is how they reach you outside the Caverns: Brute Cultists join the raid pool from 75% Corruption onwards, and [Hard Mode](/guides/hard-mode) sets out the ladder that lets them.

## The Name Problem: Brute Cultist and the Brute Class Are Not the Same Thing

Before the numbers: there are two things called Brute in this game, and conflating them will mislead you.

- The **Brute Cultist** is an enemy. It spawns underground in the [Caverns](/locations/the-caverns), it has a stat line, and it drops nothing.
- The **Brute class** is a purchasable class in the lobby, listed at 50 diamonds in the class tables, described there as a taunt shield that soaks enemy damage. Our own page on [all classes](/classes/all-classes) carries that row, and [best class](/classes/best-class) recommends it as cheap value.

They share a name and nothing else. The class page also notes that the Brute class unlocks an obsidian shield at level 3, which is what speeds the Obsidiron explosion bar on the [Obsidiron Hammer](/weapons/how-to-get-obsidiron-hammer) page. None of that is true of the enemy, and no amount of killing the enemy will give you the class.

## What the Numbers Are, and Which Source Says What

The enemy's stat line is small enough to tabulate, and that is exactly why it is worth tabulating: the sources do not just differ slightly, they differ in kind.

| Source | Health | Damage | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| rblxguide | 400 | 40 | One figure, no range |
| BreezeWiki (fandom mirror) | 400–450 | 40 | Ranges it; drop field reads "Nothing" |
| BloxSwaps | 400–450 | 40 | Same range, cites no further source |
| 99-nights-in-the-forest.com | "Extremely High (TBA)" | "Extremely High", reported as one-shot | Refuses to publish a number |

Two things follow from that table.

First, **40 damage is the consensus** — three of the four sources agree on it, and the outlier (99-nights-in-the-forest.com) does not contradict it so much as decline to answer, calling the hit "reported as one-shot". Whether a Brute hit ends you outright depends on your armor and health at that moment, so read the one-shot claim as a report, not a stat.

Second, **the health figure is a range in half the sources and a refuse-to-answer in the other half**. The 400 floor is well attested; the 450 ceiling rests on two sources that almost certainly read the same place. Treat 400 as the number you can plan against and treat anything above it as soft.

## Where It Spawns, and How the Cave Is Layered

The Brute arrived with The Bat on **22 November 2025**, in the same update as the Shadow Cultist, and followed the Jungle Cultist onto the list by two months. Both of those dates are recorded on our [Cultist](/entities/the-cultist) page.

Inside the underground, the layout matters more than the stats:

- **The Caverns proper** hold The Bat plus the Shadow, Brute and Darkstring cultists together.
- **The Final Cave** is where the numbers pile up: around **10–12 Brutes at once**, and in the last stretch of the cave the Shadow Cultists are swapped out for Brutes entirely. If you walked in expecting a mixed room, the final leg is a Brute room.
- **The side chambers** hold Darkstring and Shadow only. No Bat, no Brute. That is what makes the third-statue ladder worth climbing, and it is also why Brutes appearing between you and the next statue are self-inflicted.

Everything in this section — the 10–12 count, the side-chamber rule, the Final Cave substitution — traces to a single source (BreezeWiki). The fandom wiki corroborates that these cave variants exist, but publishes no health figure for them. We record both facts on our [Caverns](/locations/the-caverns) page as well.

400 HP of melee you have to out-heal, and a caverns run that runs the same numbers; our [Healing, Bandages and Medkits](/guides/healing-guide) guide has the consumable budget.

Four hundred to four hundred and fifty health at the end of the Caverns is a genuine wall for melee and a slog for rifles. Both, in seconds, are in our [weapon damage and time to kill guide](/guides/weapon-damage-and-time-to-kill).

## How to Survive It

This is the part the stat table cannot tell you, and it comes from one guide that actually describes the fight.

- **Take the first hit, then move.** The Brute's attack cooldowns are described as incredibly long. After a swing, it is vulnerable. A fight that looks unwinnable at 400 HP is a fight you can lose the first exchange and win the second.
- **Melee answer:** a heavy melee weapon such as the [Morningstar](/weapons/how-to-get-morningstar).
- **Ranged answers:** the [Rifle](/weapons/how-to-get-rifle), the [Raygun](/weapons/how-to-get-raygun), or a [shotgun](/weapons/how-to-get-shotgun). One guide specifically flags the Raygun as farmable here — you can wedge yourself on scenery and shoot from a fixed angle, with the usual warning that overheating while your walkspeed is reduced is what gets you killed.
- **Armor:** an [Iron Body](/items/how-to-get-iron-body) is named as the sensible armor against 40-damage swings.
- **Consumables:** bring roughly **4 to 6 bandages** for a full Caverns run.
- **Shape of the room:** caves are narrow and escape routes are short. A 400 HP enemy in a corridor pins you against a wall. Keep an exit lane open and fight it at a door if you can.

## It Drops Nothing — So Killing It Is Not a Farm

Both the fandom wiki and rblxguide publish the same fact: the Brute's drop table is empty. There is no published loot reward, and it cannot be tamed.

That single fact reframes the fight. Brutes are not a source of anything — they are a toll. The only way you end a Caverns run with more than you carried in is by clearing the route, not by grinding the room. If you were planning to farm the Final Cave for gems, the Brutes are what slow you down, not what feed you.

## The Corpse: Totem Charges, and the Necromancer That Gives It Back

The corpse is where the Brute becomes genuinely useful, and there are two competing accounts of it.

**The totem route.** At the Volcanic Biome altar, a normal cultist body is worth 1 charge and a large cultist is worth 2. Brute and Juggernaut are named as the large ones, which puts a Brute corpse at **2 charges** — the same as a Sacrifice Totem itself. Both rules are recorded on our [Volcanic Biome](/locations/volcanic-biome) page and again on the [Infernal Sword](/weapons/how-to-get-infernal-sword) page, where the practical advice is to carry the big ones out. A Brute is heavy, slow to kill, and worth more than two small ones once you are at the altar.

**The necromancer route, and the bug.** BreezeWiki records that if you obtain a Brute corpse during a hard-mode cultist wave and raise it with a necromancer staff, it appears to vanish after the resurrection animation — and does **not** consume a soul. If that holds, raising a Brute is strictly better than raising a small cultist: you get a second body on the field and you keep the soul. This is a single source and we have no second one to check it against, so treat it as a report rather than a mechanic. It is also in tension with the totem rule above: the same corpse is worth 2 charges if you sacrifice it and free if you raise it.

Both of these exist to put your health at zero, and Juggernaut arrives on a Hard Mode threshold rather than a biome. What zero health actually leads to is on the [**death and respawn**](/guides/death-and-respawn) page.

## Brute vs Juggernaut

They get grouped constantly, and for a reason: the fandom notes the Brute's damage is similar to a **Juggernaut Cultist's**. Both are the "large" tier at the altar, both cost 2 charges, and neither drops gear.

Where they split: the Juggernaut shows up at the surface in high-level Cultist Stronghold raids and at Volcanic altars from raids, while the Brute is underground with The Bat. Killing one does not help you with the other, and the [Cultist](/entities/the-cultist) page is where we keep the full type table both of them belong to.

## Where Sources Disagree

1. **Health.** 400 (rblxguide) versus 400–450 (BreezeWiki, BloxSwaps) versus no figure at all (99-nights-in-the-forest.com). The floor is solid; the ceiling is one lineage.
2. **Whether a hit kills you.** Three sources give 40 damage. One calls it "reported as one-shot" and refuses a number.
3. **Appearance.** BreezeWiki describes it as taller than a normal cultist, hooded, with a cape behind its back, wielding a Brutal Battleaxe. The other wiki describes a deer antler helmet, white top, blue pants and chains. Same enemy, two very different silhouettes.
4. **The corpse.** Worth 2 Sacrifice Totem charges (the established altar rule) or free to raise with no soul spent (BreezeWiki, single source).
5. **Class cost.** The Brute class is listed at 50 diamonds in our own class tables and by secondary guides, but we have not seen a developer figure for it, so that number stays "reported".

## What 400 HP Actually Costs You

The stat line says "400 health". The fight is about time, so here is the arithmetic, using only numbers the community has published about the [Raygun](/weapons/how-to-get-raygun) — and flagged as ours, not anyone's measurement.

- **10 damage per body shot, about 13 shots per Alien Tech bar.** Taking 10 as the working figure: 400 health divided by 10 is roughly **40 shots**.
- **That is three full bars plus one.** Two bars is 26 shots; you need about 14 more.
- **Three bars means two overheat locks.** A 9.5-second lock between the second and third bar is about **19 seconds of standing still**, before you count a single shot.
- **Our running estimate: roughly 30 to 40 seconds per Brute** if you fire continuously and hit every shot.

So a corridor with two or three Brutes in it is not a skirmish, it is a minute of your run — during which the rest of the cave is still doing something. That is the real reason the side chambers are worth the detour. None of the wall-clock figures above are official; they are our arithmetic on published per-shot numbers, and they assume you land everything.

One limit worth stating: how armor reduces a 40-damage swing is not published anywhere we can verify, so we are not going to put a number on it. What the sources do agree on is that the window after a swing exists, and that a slow walk-speed fight in a dead end is the wrong place to test that.

## FAQ

**Is the Brute Cultist the same as the Brute class?**
No. One is an underground enemy, the other is a lobby class you buy. See above.

**Does it drop anything?**
Nothing published. Both the fandom wiki and rblxguide leave the drop field empty, and it cannot be tamed.

**How hard is it compared to a Bear?**
Health-wise it out-tanks the Bear. Damage-wise, 40 per swing is far more than a Bear's output, and the attack is slower to repeat. Bring armor and a ranged option.

**Do I have to kill them?**
Only if the route requires it. The side chambers skip them, and the Final Cave is where they pile up.

**Is it worth the 2 totem charges?**
If you are already clearing the Volcanic altar, yes — a large cultist outcharges two small ones per sacrifice.
