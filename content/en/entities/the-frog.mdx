Frogs are the reason the water in 99 Nights in the Forest stops being safe. They are small, they hit for almost nothing, and they are genuinely dangerous — because **killing one makes two more appear**.

That split mechanic is the whole subject of this page. Understand it and frogs go from "the most annoying enemy in the game" to a manageable, even farmable, nuisance. This page covers the ordinary frogs; the boss fight, the 8 Frog Keys and the Frog Chest are on [The Frog King](/entities/the-frog-king).

Last verified: **2026-10-02**.

## When and Where They Show Up

Frogs are **not guaranteed every run** — sources compare them to the alien event in that respect. When they do arrive:

- They emerge from **ponds and lakes**, roughly **10 minutes into a run**, and sources most often tie the first appearance to **Day 3**.
- The notification is **"Something has emerged from the ponds"**.
- Once they are out, they populate **nearly every water source** on the map, which is what turns previously safe fishing and watering spots into danger zones.

## The Four Types

From weakest to strongest:

| Type | Role | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Green Frog** | The common one | Low health, minor damage. **Does not split** when killed |
| **Blue Frog** | Mid tier | Larger and tougher; splits into **2 Green** |
| **Purple Frog** | Guard tier | Appears at larger ponds; high health, stronger poison. Splits into **2 Blue** |
| **Frog King / Orange Frog** | The boss | Very high health, fast (walkspeed 24). Splits into **2 Purple**. Full fight on [The Frog King](/entities/the-frog-king) |

All of them attack with a **long tongue** that deals damage **and applies poison**.

## The Split Mechanic — Read This Before You Fight

**When you kill a frog, it spawns two frogs of the next weaker type.** The chain runs all the way down:

```
Frog King / Orange  →  2 Purple
Purple              →  2 Blue
Blue                →  2 Green
Green               →  nothing (drops a Morsel)
```

**The consequence is arithmetic, and it is worth doing once.** If you kill a Frog King, the cascade produces 2 Purple, then 4 Blue, then 8 Green — **14 additional frogs** from that one kill (our calculation from the split rule above; no source states the total). A careless boss kill can leave you swarmed the moment he falls.

Two practical rules fall out of it:

- **Never fight frogs near water.** Sources are explicit that they can respawn there. Lure them to open ground first.
- **Take the small ones out first.** Clear the Greens that are already on the field before you commit to a bigger kill that will add more bodies. And in the Frog Cave, do not rush to kill everything at once — the wave grows as you go. See [Frog Cave](/locations/frog-cave) for the chamber layout and why the ground you fight on matters here.

Split frogs in a swamp with no safe place to drink a bandage, and our [Healing, Bandages and Medkits](/guides/healing-guide) guide is where a poisoned-biome heal order is laid out.

## How to Fight Them

- **Ranged, always.** Their damage is only 5 but the poison stacks, and standing next to a pond while you swing is how it gets applied.
- **The Rifle is the recommended all-rounder** — enough range to stay clean, and it works well on Purple Frogs in particular.
- **The [Laser Cannon](/weapons/how-to-get-laser-cannon) is the pick for waves**, and a shotgun for burst if something closes on you.
- **Carry healing.** Poison is the thing that actually drains you, not the tongue.
- **Fight in the open**, away from ponds and away from corners where a spawning pair can pin you.
- **If you own the [Poison Master](/classes/poison-master-class), this is the fastest grind in the game for it.** Every frog that swings at you eats the Poison Armour's poison, and every kill landed while poisoned counts toward the class's level counter — frogs are 5 damage and slower than wolves, bears and [arctic foxes](/entities/the-arctic-fox), which is what makes them the cheapest route to Level 3.

Frog Keys are fished rather than fought for, each one sitting in a marked pond as a glowing object in the water. Our [**fishing guide**](/guides/fishing-guide) documents which water types produce them and how the ripple hotspots cut the wait.

Whether a frog kill can stand in for eight pond fished keys is the argument our [Keys and Locked Doors](/guides/keys-and-locked-doors) guide lays out, source disagreement included.

Not one frog on this list drops Steak, and our [Steak](/items/steak) page is the reference for what the hunting animals in the game do drop.

## What They Drop

| Drop | From |
|---|---|
| **Morsel** | The reliable one — a **Green Frog** drops one when it dies |
| **Frog Key** | A **chance** drop from frogs |

There is a wrinkle worth knowing: sources disagree slightly on which frogs drop keys. One says **Green drops the Morsel while the others carry the key chance**; another says **all types have a chance**. Either way, **killing frogs is a secondary source** — the reliable route to Frog Keys is **fishing them out of key-marked ponds**, one per pond, and you need **8** to open the Frog Cave. See [The Frog King](/entities/the-frog-king) for that route.

Both frog tames want Mackerel, which is also one fork of your own bar — the two economies compete. Values on the [**hunger**](/guides/hunger) page.

The Frog's answer is yes and it is the cheap yes; [Kiwi](/entities/the-kiwi)'s yes costs eleven Level 6 rewards' worth of fuel before the Berry even matters.

Berry Seeds keep a berry line alive between forages — see our [Seed Packs](/items/seed-packs) page.

The cheap yes is the [Berry](/items/berry)-billed Kiwi, while the Frog's own bill is two cycles of Mackerel.

## Can You Tame One?

Yes — and it is one of the cheapest tames in the game.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Flute** | [Old Taming Flute](/items/taming-flute) |
| **Stages** | **2** |
| **Food** | **2 Mackerel** total (1 per stage) |
| **Where** | Ponds and the Frog Cave |

Hold the flute, walk up to a frog, win the minigame, feed it a Mackerel, repeat once. Full mechanics on the [Taming Guide](/taming/taming-guide).

**Is it worth a slot?** Honestly, no — frogs are not strong allies compared to a Wolf or an Alpha Wolf. But they are cheap, and you can tame **more than one**, so a small group will follow you into a fight. It is a fun, low-cost pet rather than a build-defining one.

## Where Sources Disagree

**When were they added?** **22 August 2025** is given by two independent sources and matches our [Frog King](/entities/the-frog-king) page. One mob list says "August 2026", which is a year out and contradicts everything else. We use 2025.

**No source publishes frog HP.** Every type is described qualitatively — "low", "medium", "high", "very high" — and no figure is given for any of them. We are not going to invent one.

**10 minutes or Day 3?** Sources say both, and they are probably measuring the same thing two ways (a run day is roughly that length early on). Both are stated on this page rather than picking one.

**Which frogs drop keys?** Covered above — Morsel-vs-key by type, or a key chance on all types. The pond route is the reliable one either way.

## FAQ

**When do frogs appear in 99 Nights in the Forest?**
About 10 minutes into a run, commonly around Day 3, after the message *"Something has emerged from the ponds"*. They do not appear in every run.

**How much damage do frogs do?**
5 per hit, plus a poison effect that lingers. The poison is the real cost.

**Why do more frogs keep appearing?**
Because **every frog you kill splits into two of the next weaker type**. Only the Green Frog dies for good.

**How many frogs does one Frog King turn into?**
Two Purple, which become four Blue, which become eight Green — **14 additional frogs** in total (our calculation from the split rule; no source states the total).

**What do frogs drop?**
A Green Frog drops a Morsel. Frogs can also drop Frog Keys, but fishing the key-marked ponds is the reliable way to get those.

**Can you tame a frog?**
Yes — Old Taming Flute, 2 stages, 2 Mackerel.

**Where should you fight them?**
Away from water, in the open. Frogs can respawn near ponds.
